# Working Constitutional Amendments — Unadopted

**Updated:** 20 October 2026  
**Status:** Working draft; agreement in design discussion, **not enacted or merged into the Constitution**.  
**Authority:** The adopted Constitution remains the sole authoritative text. This file collects proposals between review and formal amendment. Add future settled proposals here at reasonable intervals, preserving concise rationale notes for the Constitutional History page.

## Proposed amendment 1 — Fiscal sustainability and independent reporting

**Scope:** Replace §12.1.b and §12.2(2) only. Preserve all other provisions of Article XII, especially the Social State (§12.1), MA independence (§12.1.a), interim appropriation (§12.2(3)–(5)), surplus-year Endowment funding (§12.2(6)), and National Endowment (§12.4).

### Proposed §12.1.b — Monetary Authority Reporting

The Monetary Authority publishes a detailed State of the Economy report to the NRS, the Assembly, the Legat Consul, and the Civic Consul no less than semi-annually, or more frequently as statute requires. The report independently assesses economic conditions, fiscal sustainability, performance against the Republic's fiscal obligations, and areas requiring improvement, including the MA's actions within its authority.

### Proposed §12.2(2) — Fiscal Accountability

The Republic shall conduct its fiscal affairs to preserve the long-term sustainability of public finances, including public debt, while fulfilling its constitutional obligations.

### Rationale and testing notes for future Constitutional History entry

- **Design decision:** State a durable fiscal obligation, not a prescribed economic theory or specific statutory mechanism. Governments may lawfully borrow and run deficits; the aim is sustainable public finances, not zero debt.
- **Alternatives examined and set aside:** Swiss-inspired annual expenditure ceiling, cyclical balancing formula, compensation account, ten-year adjustment paths, supermajority economic-framework statute, and founding on-ramp. These were rejected as constitutional over-specification or as risking rigid fiscal policymaking; legislation may still adopt appropriate fiscal methods.
- **Independent accountability:** Require a substantive MA report on fiscal sustainability and areas for improvement without freezing a list of economic indicators. The MA continues to possess independent monetary authority, but does not acquire power to make fiscal policy.
- **Continuity and rights testing:** Inherited public debt and temporary recession deficits do not automatically violate the proposed duty. Existing budget-continuity, Social State, and National Endowment rules remain operative. Persistent unsustainable policy is reported and remains principally subject to democratic and ordinary constitutional accountability; the text supplies no automatic ceiling or budget invalidation.
- **Tradeoff acknowledged:** This is a principles-based obligation rather than a binding mechanical debt brake. It improves constitutional clarity and public assessment but cannot guarantee prudent elected fiscal decisions.

## Proposed amendment 2 — Article I principles review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Replace §§1.7, 1.11, 1.12, 1.16, and 1.17 as written below. This is the record of agreed drafting decisions, **not an enacted amendment**. All other Article I provisions remain unchanged, including §§1.14, 1.14.a, and 1.16.a.

### Proposed §1.7 — Privacy, Property, and Security of the Home

Every person holds the right to privacy and security in their person, home, communications, and data, and the right to own property. Government may not enter a home, search a person or property, surveil a person or communication, or collect data without consent or prior independent judicial authorization on specific, articulable grounds. This applies within the Republic's territory and to Republic citizens wherever located. Encryption is lawful and may not be prohibited. Property may be taken for public use only upon just compensation determined independently.

**History note:** Clarifies which actions constitute searches, surveillance, and data collection, retaining broad protection for data and the existing prior-authorization standard. No substantive narrowing intended.

### Proposed §1.11 — Rights of the Accused and Fair Trial

Every person accused of a criminal offense has the right to prompt notice of the charges; counsel, at public expense if unable to afford representation; adequate time and means to prepare a defense; examination of witnesses; silence without adverse inference; and a speedy trial. No person may receive a greater penalty solely for exercising the right to trial. Bail may be imposed only as reasonably necessary to ensure appearance or protect public safety and may not be punitive. The accused has the right to examine and challenge the evidence presented against them, including the methods used to produce it.

**History note:** Replaces a technology-specific right to examine automated or systematic methodology with a general evidentiary guarantee covering AI, scientific analysis, and future methods without naming a particular technology.

### Proposed §1.12 — Right to a Public Trial

Every person accused of a criminal offense has the right to a public trial before an independent and impartial court. Proceedings and records may be closed or sealed only to the minimum extent and duration necessary to protect a compelling interest identified by the court. The grounds for closure or sealing must be published to the NRS, and continued secrecy remains subject to independent judicial review.

**History note:** Replaces fixed ten-year sealing and five-year extension limits and automatic-release machinery with a continuing compelling-interest, necessity, duration, publication, and judicial-review standard. §1.1 and the Constitution's other transparency provisions remain available.

### Proposed §1.16 — Emergency Derogation

No emergency suspends the Individual Sovereignty Floor in full. Rights other than those protected by §1.16.a may be temporarily derogated only upon a declaration stating the emergency, rights affected, scope, and duration, published immediately to the NRS.

Either executive may issue a declaration within their domain for no more than 14 days. Continuation requires a statute with a fixed expiration. No declaration or authorization may be renewed or substantially repeated.

All emergency measures lapse automatically with their authorization. Every declaration receives mandatory expedited independent judicial review, and judicial remedies remain available.

The three Monitors retain full constitutional authority throughout any emergency.

**History note:** Keeps the 14-day executive limit, legislative authorization and expiry, automatic lapse, Monitors' authority, and mandatory independent review. Replaces the fixed 48-hour judicial-review deadline and 60-day re-declaration sequence with expedited review and an unqualified prohibition on renewal or substantially repeated declarations. **Substantive consequence for future review:** the absolute no-renewal wording also bars a renewed or substantially similar derogation during a genuinely continuing emergency; it is not limited to evasion.

### Proposed §1.17 — Right to Seek Asylum

Every person who reaches the Republic's jurisdiction has the right to claim asylum and to an independent determination of that claim under §6.3. Manner of entry does not affect this right.

**History note:** Removes the final sentence suggesting that asylum seekers obtain *all* Article I rights upon claiming asylum. Each right retains its own scope, including citizen-only voting under §1.8 and universal protections already held before an asylum claim.

### Article I disposition and review notes

- **Replace:** §§1.7, 1.11, 1.12, 1.16, 1.17, using the agreed wording above.
- **Retain:** All remaining Article I provisions. In particular, §1.14 retains its same-offense and overlapping State/Republic prosecution rules; §1.14.a deliberately protects lawful interstate conduct and associated assistance or information (including travel assistance and medical care); §1.16.a retains its six non-derogable rights and emergency anti-discrimination guarantee.
- **Design basis:** Preserve substantive rights, necessary institutional checks, and failure-state protections while avoiding technology-specific guarantees and unnecessary fixed administrative deadlines. Revisit only a demonstrable gap, not hypothetical loopholes for their own sake.

## Proposed amendment 3 — Article II executive review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Proposed replacements of §§2.2, 2.5(4), 2.12, 2.14.b, 2.16(2)–(4), and 2.18; delete §2.10 and its redundant standalone publication rules; remove only the final appropriation sentence of §2.15. Related consolidation into §§3.10, 9.6, and 10.1(2) is documented in proposed amendment 4 below. **Unadopted working proposals, not effective constitutional text.** All other Article II provisions remain unchanged, including §2.3.a and its ten-business-day veto period.

### Proposed §2.2 — Military Authorization Framework

The Legat Consul may use military force in immediate response to an active or imminent attack on Republic territory, citizens abroad, or treaty allies. Beyond immediate response, military force requires legislative authorization. The Legat Consul must notify both chambers and the Civic Consul without undue delay of any use of force. Legislative authorization must have a defined expiration and may be renewed only by affirmative legislative action; upon expiration, operations must cease. Operations must state and serve a purpose authorized by §14.1.

**History note:** Replaces the 24-hour notice period with a prompt-notification standard and the twelve-month maximum authorization period with required expiration and affirmative legislative renewal. Preserves immediate-response authority and cessation upon expiration. The Legislature determines the period but cannot give legally indefinite authorization.

### §2.3.a — Legat Consul Legislative Veto: retain unchanged

**Review note:** Retain the ten-business-day veto review period as constitutional machinery. Allowing the Legislature to set or alter that period could nullify an independent veto or indefinitely delay enactment. Retain the established veto, override, and single-use mechanisms.

### Proposed §2.5(4) — Domestic Emergencies

The Civic Consul may declare a domestic emergency in response to natural disaster, public health crisis, or infrastructure failure, subject to Article I and Assembly oversight. The Assembly may terminate the declaration at any time. The Legat Consul has no authority in domestic emergencies except assistance under §2.14.b.

**History note:** Replaces renewal at statutory intervals (default 30 days) with ongoing Assembly oversight and a direct Assembly power to terminate the declaration, without Senate concurrence or Civic Consul approval. Rights derogation remains governed independently by Article I §1.16; continuity emergencies remain governed by §2.11.

### §2.10 — Executive Transparency: delete

**History note:** Consolidate constitutional-act publication obligations into §10.1(2), including attribution and the rule against concealing the existence or legal effect of constitutional acts. Avoid an executive-specific duplicate. See proposed amendment 4.

### Proposed §2.12 — Clemency

Each Consul may grant clemency only within their domain: the Legat Consul for military and national-security offenses, the Civic Consul for domestic federal offenses. Self-clemency is void.

Every grant must state its reasons. Clemency does not bar any constitutional, political, civil, or criminal remedy for misconduct connected with its exercise. Group amnesty requires legislation.

**History note:** Removes the two sentences on optional EM factual review, including the statement that this review does not suspend or invalidate a grant. Lawful investigation and judicial processes can be conducted under their ordinary authorities; substantive clemency limits and remedies remain.

### Proposed §2.14.b — Cross-Domain Assistance

Either Consul may request assistance from the other within the responding Consul's domain. The request must state its purpose and scope and be published to the NRS. The requesting Consul controls the purpose; the responding Consul determines whether and how to act, controls the means, and publishes the response. Neither Consul may compel the other. Assistance may provide non-coercive support only. It may not be used to compel, detain, surveil, or collect intelligence on persons within the Republic, or to commandeer civilian resources, except as §1.16 or another express constitutional source allows.

**History note:** Removes an arbitrary 90-day renewal limit and required duration statement. Preserves voluntary cross-domain assistance, separate domain control, public accountability, and explicit limits on coercion.

### Proposed §2.15 — Federal Prosecution

The Civic Consul is responsible for enforcement of federal law and may set general prosecution policy. Statute must establish a Federal Prosecution Service independent in individual prosecutorial decisions. Executive direction concerning a specific prosecution must be in writing and published to the NRS.

**History note:** Removes only the final sentence imposing a two-thirds vote of both chambers to reduce the Service's appropriation below the prior year's level. General institutional capacity and resource-justification obligations are consolidated into §3.10; retained text should be reviewed separately if a later pass identifies an actual independence conflict.

### Proposed §2.16(2)–(3) — Executive Incapacity

(2) The Consul may declare the incapacity ended, restoring authority 24 hours after publication. If incapacity is determined again on substantially the same grounds within 90 days, the renewed determination requires 2/3 of the deciding body. Following that determination, authority may be restored by majority approval of that body.

(3) A renewed determination under subsection (2) remains effective for no more than six months. Continuation thereafter requires a fresh 2/3 determination. The Consul may challenge any involuntary incapacity determination through expedited independent judicial review, limited to lawful invocation and inability to exercise the office. Policy disagreement is not incapacity.

**Implementation note:** Replace present subsections (2)–(4) with these two subsections; renumber the unchanged former subsections (5)–(9) sequentially, adjusting internal references only if necessary. Existing automatic succession and acting-service rules remain intact.

**History note:** Preserves the ordinary self-declared recovery rule, 24-hour restoration delay, repeated-determination two-thirds vote, 90-day window, and a six-month renewed-determination maximum. Removes the separate 30-day heightened-restoration window and legislative extension process. Courts act on disputes, not as routine approval participants; expedited independent judicial review can begin in the ordinary competent court, not exclusively the Supreme Court. Judicial remedies must have operative effect, including reversal and restoration when warranted.

### Proposed §2.18 — Domestic Insurrection

During armed insurrection against the constitutional order, the Civic Consul must report to the Assembly at statutory intervals for the duration of the response, as the Legat Consul reports to the Senate. No required report may be withheld because operations are ongoing.

**History note:** Removes the operational requirement for the Council of Ministers to remain convened throughout an insurrection. Retains mandatory executive reporting and oversight.

### Article II disposition and review notes

- **Revise:** §§2.2, 2.5(4), 2.12, 2.14.b, 2.16(2)–(4), and 2.18 as above.
- **Delete:** §2.10, consolidating its unique safeguards into §10.1(2).
- **Funding consolidation:** Delete the final sentence of §2.15; address institutional resources through §3.10 and remove §9.6.
- **Retain:** All other Article II sections, including §2.3.a and the veto's fixed ten-business-day deadline.
- **Design basis:** Preserve deadlines that secure independent constitutional authority and automatic succession, but leave operational methods, lawful oversight procedures, and policy durations to democratic institutions where doing so creates no failure of continuity or accountability.

## Proposed amendment 4 — General institutional resources and NRS publication

**Scope:** Revise §3.10 by adding a uniform resources principle while preserving its existing legislative-competence text and institutional-capacity obligation; delete §9.6 in full; revise the opening of §10.1(2) while preserving the remainder. Coordinate these changes with the approved Article II deletion of §2.10 and funding sentence removal in §2.15. Unadopted working proposals.

### Proposed addition to §3.10 — Institutional Resources

The Legislature shall provide institutions established by this Constitution or by statute with resources sufficient to perform their assigned duties. Material changes in those resources must be accompanied by a published justification addressing the institution's responsibilities and capacity to fulfill them.

**Integration note:** Add the paragraph to §3.10 without deleting its existing provision that the Legislature must maintain an institution capable of performing each constitutionally assigned function. Statutory institutions remain subject to lawful reorganization, changed duties, and abolition; constitutional duties cannot be abolished by a budget decision.

**History note:** Replaces institution-specific appropriation formulas with a uniform sufficiency-and-explanation rule, applying to both increases and reductions. Retains legislative budget discretion while prohibiting impairment of duties. Tradeoff: removes automatic funding floors and supermajority shields from independent oversight institutions.

### §9.6 — Minimum Funding Guarantee: delete in full

**History note:** Repeal the inflation-adjusted appropriation floor for each Monitor, the Elections Panel, the NRS Panel, and the Monetary Authority; remove the two-thirds reduction threshold, certification procedure, and automatic-floor appropriation. Institutional funding obligations will instead reside in §3.10.

### Proposed opening of §10.1(2) — Constitutional Acts and Records

Every constitutional act, order, finding, certification, designation, declaration, and determination made under this Constitution is published to the NRS as a permanent record, attributed to the responsible officer or institution; no provision need state this obligation individually. The existence and legal effect of a constitutional act may not be concealed by classification.

**Integration note:** Following these opening sentences, preserve unchanged the existing §10.1(2) text beginning “Official records and materials created, received, maintained, or produced in the exercise of official duties are property of the Republic” and continuing through its rules on custody, destruction, publication, access, retention, and classification.

**History note:** Consolidates §2.10's executive-specific publication and accountability rules into the general constitutional-acts rule, expands attribution to responsible officers or institutions, and distinguishes the public existence and legal effect of acts from potentially classified supporting materials.

## Proposed amendment 5 — Article III Legislature review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Replace §3.2(2), §3.2(5), the last sentence of §3.3, the second paragraph of §3.4, §3.5(1), §3.9, and §3.11 with the text below. The agreed §3.10 institutional-resources addition is already recorded under proposed amendment 4 and should be implemented once, not duplicated. Other Article III provisions remain unchanged, including §§3.1, 3.6, 3.7, and 3.8. **Working proposals only; not enacted.**

### Proposed §3.2(2) — Census

The census, conducted by a statutory independent agency at intervals defined by statute not exceeding ten years, is the basis for reapportionment.

**History note:** Changes a rigid ten-year cycle into a maximum interval, allowing more frequent censuses without enabling indefinite postponement of population-based representation.

### Proposed §3.2(5) — Assembly Vacancies

Mid-term Assembly vacancies are filled by special election within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days, unless the remainder of the term is shorter than that period.

**History note:** Preserves the 90-day cap while avoiding mandatory special elections when the unexpired term is shorter than the statutory election period. The exception permits, but does not require, leaving the seat vacant near term-end.

### Proposed final sentence of §3.3 — Electoral Districts

Any citizen or State government may seek judicial remedy for a district map that violates this Constitution.

**History note:** Removes exclusive direct Supreme Court jurisdiction while preserving citizen and State standing, substantive district-map standards, and effective relief through the ordinary judicial system. Retain all earlier sentences of §3.3 unchanged.

### Proposed second paragraph of §3.4 — Legislative Standards

Each chamber establishes and publishes its sitting schedule, and any revision, to the NRS. No recess may be used to defeat or delay a constitutional duty or deadline. Emergency sessions may be called by either Consul or by petition of one-third of either chamber.

**History note:** Removes mandated full-time legislative status; retains publicly accountable calendars, safeguards against recess-based avoidance of constitutional duties, and emergency convening. Leave the first and third paragraphs of §3.4 unchanged.

### Proposed §3.5(1) — Senate Vacancies

The Senate comprises two senators per State regardless of population, elected for six-year staggered terms. A mid-term vacancy is filled as the State provides, within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days, unless the remainder of the term is shorter than that period.

**History note:** Preserves States' choice of vacancy-filling mechanism and the 90-day maximum; permits a vacancy to remain unfilled if the statutory filling period exceeds the remaining term, while allowing a State to fill it voluntarily.

### Proposed §3.9 — Independent Statutory Agencies

The Legislature may establish independent agencies outside the authority of either executive and may alter or abolish agencies established by statute. The Legislature may not impair the independence of a function this Constitution assigns to an independent agency.

Unless this Constitution provides otherwise, statute governs organization, appointments, qualifications, terms, and oversight. The appointed leadership of an independent agency serves fixed terms and may be removed only for conduct, incapacity, or incompetence, not policy disagreement. Qualification requirements may not be designed to exclude candidates because of political or policy views or prior criticism of government.

**History note:** Clarifies that statutory agencies may be altered or abolished by the Legislature, while extending fixed-term and for-cause removal protections from principal decision-makers exercising constitutionally assigned independent functions to **appointed leadership of any independent agency**. Independence protects agency leadership during the agency's existence, not permanent institutional existence or ordinary employees.

### §3.10 — Federal Legislative Competence: apply amendment 4

**Integration note:** Add the already-agreed institutional resource-sufficiency and published-justification paragraph from proposed amendment 4. Preserve existing legislative competence, States' self-government, and constitutionally assigned institutional-capacity language.

### Proposed §3.11 — Delegation of Authority

The Legislature may by statute delegate defined authority within federal legislative competence to either Consul or to an independent agency under §3.9. A delegation must state its scope and conditions. Delegation does not divest the Legislature of that authority: statute prevails over any conflicting exercise of delegated authority, and the Legislature may rescind a delegation at any time. Each exercise of delegated authority is published to the NRS identifying the delegating statute.

**History note:** Removes universal duration requirement for statutory delegations. Legislative control, scope, conditions, statutory supremacy, and revocation remain; sunsets may still be set by statute when appropriate.

### Article III retained provisions and tested alternatives

- **§3.1 — Senate Review: retain unchanged.** Keep the 90-day review period, optional one-time 30-day Senate extension, and distinction between tacit approval and constitutionally required affirmative votes. Defining a legislative session as the period between Assembly elections was considered; a session-end deadline alone could leave too little Senate review for bills passed just before elections. Elections do not truncate the 90-day review.
- **§3.6 — Treaties and Trade Agreements: retain unchanged.** Keep the maximum 90-day Senate affirmation period for proposed treaty withdrawal. Without a definite lapse, Senate inaction could leave foreign-policy decisions unresolved despite the Legat Consul's foreign-affairs responsibility.
- **§§3.7 and 3.8: retain unchanged.** Voting-threshold clarity and Monitor access to legislative information remain important.
- **Remainder:** Retain all Article III text outside the expressly identified replacements and the already-agreed §3.10 addition.
