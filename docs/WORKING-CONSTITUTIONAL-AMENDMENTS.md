# Working Constitutional Amendments — Unadopted

**Updated:** 20 October 2026  
**Status:** Working draft; agreement in design discussion, **not enacted or merged into the Constitution**.  
**Authority:** The adopted Constitution remains the sole authoritative text. This file collects proposals between review and formal amendment. Add future settled proposals here at reasonable intervals, preserving concise rationale notes for the Constitutional History page.

## Proposed amendment 1 — Fiscal sustainability and independent reporting

**Scope:** Replace §12.1.b and §12.2(2) only. Preserve all other provisions of Article XII, especially the Social State (§12.1), MA independence (§12.1.a), interim appropriation (§12.2(3)–(5)), surplus-year Endowment funding (§12.2(6)), and National Endowment (§12.4).

### Proposed §12.1.b — Monetary Authority Reporting

The Monetary Authority publishes a detailed State of the Economy report to the NRS, the Assembly, the Legat Consul, and the Civic Consul no less than semi-annually, or more frequently as statute requires. The report independently assesses economic conditions, fiscal sustainability, performance against the Republic's fiscal obligations, and areas requiring improvement, including the MA's actions within its authority.

### Proposed §12.2(2) — Fiscal Accountability

The Republic shall conduct its fiscal affairs to preserve the long-term sustainability of public finances, including public debt, while fulfilling its constitutional obligations.

### Rationale and testing notes for future Constitutional History entry

- **Design decision:** State a durable fiscal obligation, not a prescribed economic theory or specific statutory mechanism. Governments may lawfully borrow and run deficits; the aim is sustainable public finances, not zero debt.
- **Alternatives examined and set aside:** Swiss-inspired annual expenditure ceiling, cyclical balancing formula, compensation account, ten-year adjustment paths, supermajority economic-framework statute, and founding on-ramp. These were rejected as constitutional over-specification or as risking rigid fiscal policymaking; legislation may still adopt appropriate fiscal methods.
- **Independent accountability:** Require a substantive MA report on fiscal sustainability and areas for improvement without freezing a list of economic indicators. The MA continues to possess independent monetary authority, but does not acquire power to make fiscal policy.
- **Continuity and rights testing:** Inherited public debt and temporary recession deficits do not automatically violate the proposed duty. Existing budget-continuity, Social State, and National Endowment rules remain operative. Persistent unsustainable policy is reported and remains principally subject to democratic and ordinary constitutional accountability; the text supplies no automatic ceiling or budget invalidation.
- **Tradeoff acknowledged:** This is a principles-based obligation rather than a binding mechanical debt brake. It improves constitutional clarity and public assessment but cannot guarantee prudent elected fiscal decisions.

## Proposed amendment 2 — Article I principles review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Replace §§1.7, 1.11, 1.12, 1.16, and 1.17 as written below. This is the record of agreed drafting decisions, **not an enacted amendment**. All other Article I provisions remain unchanged, including §§1.14, 1.14.a, and 1.16.a.

### Proposed §1.7 — Privacy, Property, and Security of the Home

Every person holds the right to privacy and security in their person, home, communications, and data, and the right to own property. Government may not enter a home, search a person or property, surveil a person or communication, or collect data without consent or prior independent judicial authorization on specific, articulable grounds. This applies within the Republic's territory and to Republic citizens wherever located. Encryption is lawful and may not be prohibited. Property may be taken for public use only upon just compensation determined independently.

**History note:** Clarifies which actions constitute searches, surveillance, and data collection, retaining broad protection for data and the existing prior-authorization standard. No substantive narrowing intended.

### Proposed §1.11 — Rights of the Accused and Fair Trial

Every person accused of a criminal offense has the right to prompt notice of the charges; counsel, at public expense if unable to afford representation; adequate time and means to prepare a defense; examination of witnesses; silence without adverse inference; and a speedy trial. No person may receive a greater penalty solely for exercising the right to trial. Bail may be imposed only as reasonably necessary to ensure appearance or protect public safety and may not be punitive. The accused has the right to examine and challenge the evidence presented against them, including the methods used to produce it.

**History note:** Replaces a technology-specific right to examine automated or systematic methodology with a general evidentiary guarantee covering AI, scientific analysis, and future methods without naming a particular technology.

### Proposed §1.12 — Right to a Public Trial

Every person accused of a criminal offense has the right to a public trial before an independent and impartial court. Proceedings and records may be closed or sealed only to the minimum extent and duration necessary to protect a compelling interest identified by the court. The grounds for closure or sealing must be published to the NRS, and continued secrecy remains subject to independent judicial review.

**History note:** Replaces fixed ten-year sealing and five-year extension limits and automatic-release machinery with a continuing compelling-interest, necessity, duration, publication, and judicial-review standard. §1.1 and the Constitution's other transparency provisions remain available.

### Proposed §1.16 — Emergency Derogation

No emergency suspends the Individual Sovereignty Floor in full. Rights other than those protected by §1.16.a may be temporarily derogated only upon a declaration stating the emergency, rights affected, scope, and duration, published immediately to the NRS.

Either executive may issue a declaration within their domain for no more than 14 days. Continuation requires a statute with a fixed expiration. No declaration or authorization may be renewed or substantially repeated.

All emergency measures lapse automatically with their authorization. Every declaration receives mandatory expedited independent judicial review, and judicial remedies remain available.

The three Monitors retain full constitutional authority throughout any emergency.

**History note:** Keeps the 14-day executive limit, legislative authorization and expiry, automatic lapse, Monitors' authority, and mandatory independent review. Replaces the fixed 48-hour judicial-review deadline and 60-day re-declaration sequence with expedited review and an unqualified prohibition on renewal or substantially repeated declarations. **Substantive consequence for future review:** the absolute no-renewal wording also bars a renewed or substantially similar derogation during a genuinely continuing emergency; it is not limited to evasion.

### Proposed §1.17 — Right to Seek Asylum

Every person who reaches the Republic's jurisdiction has the right to claim asylum and to an independent determination of that claim under §6.3. Manner of entry does not affect this right.

**History note:** Removes the final sentence suggesting that asylum seekers obtain *all* Article I rights upon claiming asylum. Each right retains its own scope, including citizen-only voting under §1.8 and universal protections already held before an asylum claim.

### Article I disposition and review notes

- **Replace:** §§1.7, 1.11, 1.12, 1.16, 1.17, using the agreed wording above.
- **Retain:** All remaining Article I provisions. In particular, §1.14 retains its same-offense and overlapping State/Republic prosecution rules; §1.14.a deliberately protects lawful interstate conduct and associated assistance or information (including travel assistance and medical care); §1.16.a retains its six non-derogable rights and emergency anti-discrimination guarantee.
- **Design basis:** Preserve substantive rights, necessary institutional checks, and failure-state protections while avoiding technology-specific guarantees and unnecessary fixed administrative deadlines. Revisit only a demonstrable gap, not hypothetical loopholes for their own sake.

## Proposed amendment 3 — Article II executive review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Proposed replacements of §§2.2, 2.5(4), 2.12, 2.14.b, 2.16(2)–(4), and 2.18; delete §2.10 and its redundant standalone publication rules; remove only the final appropriation sentence of §2.15. Related consolidation into §§3.10, 9.6, and 10.1(2) is documented in proposed amendment 4 below. **Unadopted working proposals, not effective constitutional text.** All other Article II provisions remain unchanged, including §2.3.a and its ten-business-day veto period.

### Proposed §2.2 — Military Authorization Framework

The Legat Consul may use military force in immediate response to an active or imminent attack on Republic territory, citizens abroad, or treaty allies. Beyond immediate response, military force requires legislative authorization. The Legat Consul must notify both chambers and the Civic Consul without undue delay of any use of force. Legislative authorization must have a defined expiration and may be renewed only by affirmative legislative action; upon expiration, operations must cease. Operations must state and serve a purpose authorized by §14.1.

**History note:** Replaces the 24-hour notice period with a prompt-notification standard and the twelve-month maximum authorization period with required expiration and affirmative legislative renewal. Preserves immediate-response authority and cessation upon expiration. The Legislature determines the period but cannot give legally indefinite authorization.

### §2.3.a — Legat Consul Legislative Veto: retain unchanged

**Review note:** Retain the ten-business-day veto review period as constitutional machinery. Allowing the Legislature to set or alter that period could nullify an independent veto or indefinitely delay enactment. Retain the established veto, override, and single-use mechanisms.

### Proposed §2.5(4) — Domestic Emergencies

The Civic Consul may declare a domestic emergency in response to natural disaster, public health crisis, or infrastructure failure, subject to Article I and Assembly oversight. The Assembly may terminate the declaration at any time. The Legat Consul has no authority in domestic emergencies except assistance under §2.14.b.

**History note:** Replaces renewal at statutory intervals (default 30 days) with ongoing Assembly oversight and a direct Assembly power to terminate the declaration, without Senate concurrence or Civic Consul approval. Rights derogation remains governed independently by Article I §1.16; continuity emergencies remain governed by §2.11.

### §2.10 — Executive Transparency: delete

**History note:** Consolidate constitutional-act publication obligations into §10.1(2), including attribution and the rule against concealing the existence or legal effect of constitutional acts. Avoid an executive-specific duplicate. See proposed amendment 4.

### Proposed §2.12 — Clemency

Each Consul may grant clemency only within their domain: the Legat Consul for military and national-security offenses, the Civic Consul for domestic federal offenses. Self-clemency is void.

Every grant must state its reasons. Clemency does not bar any constitutional, political, civil, or criminal remedy for misconduct connected with its exercise. Group amnesty requires legislation.

**History note:** Removes the two sentences on optional EM factual review, including the statement that this review does not suspend or invalidate a grant. Lawful investigation and judicial processes can be conducted under their ordinary authorities; substantive clemency limits and remedies remain.

### Proposed §2.14.b — Cross-Domain Assistance

Either Consul may request assistance from the other within the responding Consul's domain. The request must state its purpose and scope and be published to the NRS. The requesting Consul controls the purpose; the responding Consul determines whether and how to act, controls the means, and publishes the response. Neither Consul may compel the other. Assistance may provide non-coercive support only. It may not be used to compel, detain, surveil, or collect intelligence on persons within the Republic, or to commandeer civilian resources, except as §1.16 or another express constitutional source allows.

**History note:** Removes an arbitrary 90-day renewal limit and required duration statement. Preserves voluntary cross-domain assistance, separate domain control, public accountability, and explicit limits on coercion.

### Proposed §2.15 — Federal Prosecution

The Civic Consul is responsible for enforcement of federal law and may set general prosecution policy. Statute must establish a Federal Prosecution Service independent in individual prosecutorial decisions. Executive direction concerning a specific prosecution must be in writing and published to the NRS.

**History note:** Removes only the final sentence imposing a two-thirds vote of both chambers to reduce the Service's appropriation below the prior year's level. General institutional capacity and resource-justification obligations are consolidated into §3.10; retained text should be reviewed separately if a later pass identifies an actual independence conflict.

### Proposed §2.16(2)–(3) — Executive Incapacity

(2) The Consul may declare the incapacity ended, restoring authority 24 hours after publication. If incapacity is determined again on substantially the same grounds within 90 days, the renewed determination requires 2/3 of the deciding body. Following that determination, authority may be restored by majority approval of that body.

(3) A renewed determination under subsection (2) remains effective for no more than six months. Continuation thereafter requires a fresh 2/3 determination. The Consul may challenge any involuntary incapacity determination through expedited independent judicial review, limited to lawful invocation and inability to exercise the office. Policy disagreement is not incapacity.

**Implementation note:** Replace present subsections (2)–(4) with these two subsections; renumber the unchanged former subsections (5)–(9) sequentially, adjusting internal references only if necessary. Existing automatic succession and acting-service rules remain intact.

**History note:** Preserves the ordinary self-declared recovery rule, 24-hour restoration delay, repeated-determination two-thirds vote, 90-day window, and a six-month renewed-determination maximum. Removes the separate 30-day heightened-restoration window and legislative extension process. Courts act on disputes, not as routine approval participants; expedited independent judicial review can begin in the ordinary competent court, not exclusively the Supreme Court. Judicial remedies must have operative effect, including reversal and restoration when warranted.

### Proposed §2.18 — Domestic Insurrection

During armed insurrection against the constitutional order, the Civic Consul must report to the Assembly at statutory intervals for the duration of the response, as the Legat Consul reports to the Senate. No required report may be withheld because operations are ongoing.

**History note:** Removes the operational requirement for the Council of Ministers to remain convened throughout an insurrection. Retains mandatory executive reporting and oversight.

### Article II disposition and review notes

- **Revise:** §§2.2, 2.5(4), 2.12, 2.14.b, 2.16(2)–(4), and 2.18 as above.
- **Delete:** §2.10, consolidating its unique safeguards into §10.1(2).
- **Funding consolidation:** Delete the final sentence of §2.15; address institutional resources through §3.10 and remove §9.6.
- **Retain:** All other Article II sections, including §2.3.a and the veto's fixed ten-business-day deadline.
- **Design basis:** Preserve deadlines that secure independent constitutional authority and automatic succession, but leave operational methods, lawful oversight procedures, and policy durations to democratic institutions where doing so creates no failure of continuity or accountability.

## Proposed amendment 4 — General institutional resources and NRS publication

**Scope:** Revise §3.10 by adding a uniform resources principle while preserving its existing legislative-competence text and institutional-capacity obligation; delete §9.6 in full; revise the opening of §10.1(2) while preserving the remainder. Coordinate these changes with the approved Article II deletion of §2.10 and funding sentence removal in §2.15. Unadopted working proposals.

### Proposed addition to §3.10 — Institutional Resources

The Legislature shall provide institutions established by this Constitution or by statute with resources sufficient to perform their assigned duties. Material changes in those resources must be accompanied by a published justification addressing the institution's responsibilities and capacity to fulfill them.

**Integration note:** Add the paragraph to §3.10 without deleting its existing provision that the Legislature must maintain an institution capable of performing each constitutionally assigned function. Statutory institutions remain subject to lawful reorganization, changed duties, and abolition; constitutional duties cannot be abolished by a budget decision.

**History note:** Replaces institution-specific appropriation formulas with a uniform sufficiency-and-explanation rule, applying to both increases and reductions. Retains legislative budget discretion while prohibiting impairment of duties. Tradeoff: removes automatic funding floors and supermajority shields from independent oversight institutions.

### §9.6 — Minimum Funding Guarantee: delete in full

**History note:** Repeal the inflation-adjusted appropriation floor for each Monitor, the Elections Panel, the NRS Panel, and the Monetary Authority; remove the two-thirds reduction threshold, certification procedure, and automatic-floor appropriation. Institutional funding obligations will instead reside in §3.10.

### Proposed opening of §10.1(2) — Constitutional Acts and Records

Every constitutional act, order, finding, certification, designation, declaration, and determination made under this Constitution is published to the NRS as a permanent record, attributed to the responsible officer or institution; no provision need state this obligation individually. The existence and legal effect of a constitutional act may not be concealed by classification.

**Integration note:** Following these opening sentences, preserve unchanged the existing §10.1(2) text beginning “Official records and materials created, received, maintained, or produced in the exercise of official duties are property of the Republic” and continuing through its rules on custody, destruction, publication, access, retention, and classification.

**History note:** Consolidates §2.10's executive-specific publication and accountability rules into the general constitutional-acts rule, expands attribution to responsible officers or institutions, and distinguishes the public existence and legal effect of acts from potentially classified supporting materials.

## Proposed amendment 5 — Article III Legislature review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Replace §3.2(2), §3.2(5), the last sentence of §3.3, the second paragraph of §3.4, §3.5(1), §3.9, and §3.11 with the text below. The agreed §3.10 institutional-resources addition is already recorded under proposed amendment 4 and should be implemented once, not duplicated. Other Article III provisions remain unchanged, including §§3.1, 3.6, 3.7, and 3.8. **Working proposals only; not enacted.**

### Proposed §3.2(2) — Census

The census, conducted by a statutory independent agency at intervals defined by statute not exceeding ten years, is the basis for reapportionment.

**History note:** Changes a rigid ten-year cycle into a maximum interval, allowing more frequent censuses without enabling indefinite postponement of population-based representation.

### Proposed §3.2(5) — Assembly Vacancies

Mid-term Assembly vacancies are filled by special election within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days, unless the remainder of the term is shorter than that period.

**History note:** Preserves the 90-day cap while avoiding mandatory special elections when the unexpired term is shorter than the statutory election period. The exception permits, but does not require, leaving the seat vacant near term-end.

### Proposed final sentence of §3.3 — Electoral Districts

Any citizen or State government may seek judicial remedy for a district map that violates this Constitution.

**History note:** Removes exclusive direct Supreme Court jurisdiction while preserving citizen and State standing, substantive district-map standards, and effective relief through the ordinary judicial system. Retain all earlier sentences of §3.3 unchanged.

### Proposed second paragraph of §3.4 — Legislative Standards

Each chamber establishes and publishes its sitting schedule, and any revision, to the NRS. No recess may be used to defeat or delay a constitutional duty or deadline. Emergency sessions may be called by either Consul or by petition of one-third of either chamber.

**History note:** Removes mandated full-time legislative status; retains publicly accountable calendars, safeguards against recess-based avoidance of constitutional duties, and emergency convening. Leave the first and third paragraphs of §3.4 unchanged.

### Proposed §3.5(1) — Senate Vacancies

The Senate comprises two senators per State regardless of population, elected for six-year staggered terms. A mid-term vacancy is filled as the State provides, within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days, unless the remainder of the term is shorter than that period.

**History note:** Preserves States' choice of vacancy-filling mechanism and the 90-day maximum; permits a vacancy to remain unfilled if the statutory filling period exceeds the remaining term, while allowing a State to fill it voluntarily.

### Proposed §3.9 — Independent Statutory Agencies

The Legislature may establish independent agencies outside the authority of either executive and may alter or abolish agencies established by statute. The Legislature may not impair the independence of a function this Constitution assigns to an independent agency.

Unless this Constitution provides otherwise, statute governs organization, appointments, qualifications, terms, and oversight. The appointed leadership of an independent agency serves fixed terms and may be removed only for conduct, incapacity, or incompetence, not policy disagreement. Qualification requirements may not be designed to exclude candidates because of political or policy views or prior criticism of government.

**History note:** Clarifies that statutory agencies may be altered or abolished by the Legislature, while extending fixed-term and for-cause removal protections from principal decision-makers exercising constitutionally assigned independent functions to **appointed leadership of any independent agency**. Independence protects agency leadership during the agency's existence, not permanent institutional existence or ordinary employees.

### §3.10 — Federal Legislative Competence: apply amendment 4

**Integration note:** Add the already-agreed institutional resource-sufficiency and published-justification paragraph from proposed amendment 4. Preserve existing legislative competence, States' self-government, and constitutionally assigned institutional-capacity language.

### Proposed §3.11 — Delegation of Authority

The Legislature may by statute delegate defined authority within federal legislative competence to either Consul or to an independent agency under §3.9. A delegation must state its scope and conditions. Delegation does not divest the Legislature of that authority: statute prevails over any conflicting exercise of delegated authority, and the Legislature may rescind a delegation at any time. Each exercise of delegated authority is published to the NRS identifying the delegating statute.

**History note:** Removes universal duration requirement for statutory delegations. Legislative control, scope, conditions, statutory supremacy, and revocation remain; sunsets may still be set by statute when appropriate.

### Article III retained provisions and tested alternatives

- **§3.1 — Senate Review: retain unchanged.** Keep the 90-day review period, optional one-time 30-day Senate extension, and distinction between tacit approval and constitutionally required affirmative votes. Defining a legislative session as the period between Assembly elections was considered; a session-end deadline alone could leave too little Senate review for bills passed just before elections. Elections do not truncate the 90-day review.
- **§3.6 — Treaties and Trade Agreements: retain unchanged.** Keep the maximum 90-day Senate affirmation period for proposed treaty withdrawal. Without a definite lapse, Senate inaction could leave foreign-policy decisions unresolved despite the Legat Consul's foreign-affairs responsibility.
- **§§3.7 and 3.8: retain unchanged.** Voting-threshold clarity and Monitor access to legislative information remain important.
- **Remainder:** Retain all Article III text outside the expressly identified replacements and the already-agreed §3.10 addition.

## Proposed amendment 6 — Article IV judicial architecture review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Replace §4.1; replace §4.2(1) and §4.2(5); amend only the equal-division and quorum paragraph of §4.3; amend §4.5(3), §4.5(4), the first category of §4.5(5), and §4.5(6); and consolidate §4.5(8)–(9) into one §4.5(8). Preserve all other Article IV text, especially §§4.2(2)–(4), 4.3.a, 4.4, 4.4.a, 4.5(1)–(2), 4.5(7), 4.5.a, and 4.6. **Settled discussion drafts, not enacted.**

### Proposed §4.1 — Court Structure

The Republic's judiciary consists of district courts, appellate courts, and the Supreme Court. The Legislature may establish, abolish, and define the jurisdiction of inferior courts by statute. Abolition of a court takes effect for each occupied judicial seat upon expiration of the sitting judge's term. Constitutional questions arising in any proceeding may be referred to the SC.

**History note:** Replaces redundant retention-of-office, compensation, and temporary-service language with occupied-seat abolition through attrition. Reorganization remains legislative; existing judges' seats survive through their terms. Reassignment and pending-case administration do not require a distinct constitutional succession office. Reflects the attrition safeguard already applied to Supreme Court seat reductions.

### Proposed §4.2(1) — Inferior Judicial Appointments

The Civic Consul shall nominate inferior court judges from the Judicial Pool under §9.8 without undue delay following a vacancy. The Senate confirms by a majority of its full seated membership.

**History note:** Replaces a fixed 90-day nomination deadline with an enforceable prompt-action standard; leaves judicial selection and confirmation with the Civic Consul and Senate. The public-confirmation fallback in §4.4.a applies to Supreme Court appointments only.

### Proposed §4.2(5) — Pending Cases During Vacancies

The Supreme Court may reassign cases among inferior federal courts or qualified judges as necessary to prevent judicial vacancies from delaying proceedings.

**History note:** Replaces automatic temporary judicial-office succession with authority to redistribute cases during vacancies. The safeguard serves both continuity and accountability: proceedings move independently of delayed political appointments, diminishing appointing authorities' leverage over cases until permanent appointments occur. General resources and adequate staffing remain governed by §3.10 under the One Home Rule; do not duplicate that obligation here.

### Proposed second paragraph of §4.3 — Supreme Court Equal Division and Quorum

An equally divided Court leaves the challenged ruling in effect, or, in an original proceeding, grants no relief. An equal division establishes no binding precedent. A majority of seated justices, including Temporary Associate Justices, constitutes ordinary quorum; constitutional questions require at least five participating justices.

**History note:** Clarifies that tied original-jurisdiction proceedings grant no relief and that an equal division creates no binding precedent. Ordinary procedures determine whether a matter can be reheard; no constitutional guarantee of unlimited relitigation. Retain the first paragraph's minimum five justices, twelve-year nonrenewable staggered terms, two-thirds chamber approval for resizing, and attrition protection.

### §§4.4 and 4.4.a — Supreme Court Selection and Public Confirmation: retain unchanged

**Review note:** Retain the 90-day nomination and 120-day Senate-vote maximums, which activate the public-confirmation fallback when officials fail to act; timely Senate rejection is a legitimate exercise of constitutional checking, not inaction. Retain automatic eligible appellate-judge temporary succession and the public confirmation or rejection pathway. Do not create a third fallback merely because no eligible appellate judge is available: an unfilled seat may remain vacant until an eligible successor becomes available, subject to the separate Court quorum requirement. The Judicial Pool under §9.8 provides permanent-nomination candidates, not automatic temporary appellate successors.

### Proposed §4.5(3) — Monitor Findings

Monitor findings published to the NRS are admissible in judicial proceedings.

**History note:** Removes a court's mandatory written explanation for disagreeing with Monitor findings. Admissibility does not give oversight reports conclusive factual or legal weight; courts remain independent in deciding law and evidence.

### Proposed §4.5(4) — Standing and Judicial Forum

Except where this Constitution expressly confers standing, courts adjudicate only cases involving a concrete, articulable legal injury and may not issue advisory opinions. An organization has standing where at least one member would have standing and the claim is germane to the organization's established purposes. The Legislature may not leave any category of legal injury without a judicial forum.

**History note:** Changes “its established constitutional purpose” to “the organization's established purposes,” preserving member injury and germaneness while not excluding otherwise qualifying associations without a specifically constitutional mission.

### Proposed first category of §4.5(5) — Supreme Court Original Jurisdiction

— constitutional disputes between States, and disputes between a State and the Republic concerning their respective constitutional powers or obligations;

**Integration note:** Replace only the first enumerated category. Preserve all other original-jurisdiction categories, including disputes between Consuls, structurally compromised independent-institution disputes, direct emergency rights claims, and amendment-consistency disputes. Ordinary constitutional challenges to federal statutes proceed through the judicial hierarchy unless another original-jurisdiction category applies.

**History note:** Preserves direct SC jurisdiction for constitutional State–State disputes and structural State–Republic allocation-of-authority disputes, but not every constitutional challenge to a federal statute.

### Proposed §4.5(6) — Immediate Defensive Military Authority

Constitutional challenges to the exercise of immediate defensive military authority under §2.2 during active operations receive expedited independent judicial review.

**History note:** Replaces automatic SC jurisdiction and a 48-hour decision maximum with expedited review by a competent independent court. Existing structural-dispute SC jurisdiction remains available where appropriate.

### Proposed §4.5(8) — Interim Protection and Missed Judicial Deadlines

Courts may issue interim protective orders where necessary to prevent irreparable harm pending judgment. Failure to rule within a constitutionally prescribed period does not determine the merits or produce a substantive constitutional outcome. The parties retain all otherwise available judicial remedies.

**Integration note:** Replace existing §4.5(8) with this text and delete §4.5(9). No change to §4.5(7).

**History note:** Removes detailed one-time Supreme Court extension votes and fixed extension caps, while preserving interim protection, binding judicial deadlines, and the principle that missing a deadline does not decide the merits.

### Article IV disposition and implementation checks

- **Replace:** §4.1; §4.2(1), §4.2(5); second paragraph of §4.3; §4.5(3), §4.5(4), first category of §4.5(5), §4.5(6), and §4.5(8).
- **Delete:** Old §4.5(9) after consolidation into §4.5(8).
- **Retain:** §§4.2(2)–(4), 4.3 first paragraph, 4.3.a, 4.4, 4.4.a, 4.5(1)–(2), remaining §4.5(5) categories, §4.5(7), 4.5.a, and 4.6, plus other unchanged text.
- **Cross-references:** Confirm removal of the old §4.1 reference to §4.2(5), and later search the full Constitution for any references to deleted §4.5(9). Do not silently alter other provisions without review.
- **Design basis:** Courts must keep cases moving; control of pending cases should not depend on political appointment delays. Use ordinary courts for ordinary legal disputes and reserve original Supreme Court jurisdiction for structural questions; preserve enforceable rights and judicial independence while minimizing detailed scheduling machinery.

## Proposed amendment 7 — Article V citizenship review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Amend the first, second, and fourth paragraphs of §5.1; establish a new §5.1.b governing civic and constitutional proficiency. Preserve the third paragraph of §5.1 on revocation; retain §§5.1.a, 5.2, and 5.3 unchanged. All changes remain **working proposals, not enacted constitutional text**.

### Proposed §5.1, first paragraph — Citizenship at Birth

A child is a citizen from birth if at least one parent is a citizen, if born within the Republic to a legal-resident parent, or if born within the Republic and otherwise stateless. Citizens acquire full citizenship rights, including voting and eligibility for office, at 18.

**History note:** Preserves descent through a citizen parent regardless of birthplace, while limiting birthright citizenship through a legal-resident parent to births within the Republic. Territorial statelessness protection is retained; note the substantive tradeoff that the revised birthright clause does not automatically protect an otherwise stateless child born abroad to noncitizen parents.

### Proposed §5.1, second paragraph — Naturalization and Service

A legal resident may earn citizenship upon meeting the legal qualifications and continuous residency period established by statute. Qualifying honorable military or national service reduces the required residency period as provided by statute. All naturalization pathways require the civic and constitutional proficiency assessment established under §5.1.b.

**History note:** Recognizes qualifying honorable military or civilian national service by shortening statutory residency rather than exempting an applicant from civic understanding. Retains legal-resident eligibility, legislative authority over service qualifications and the amount of reduction, and a universal assessment for naturalization. The substantive test rules have one home in new §5.1.b.

### §5.1, third paragraph — Revocation: retain unchanged

**Review note:** Preserve prohibition on punitive citizenship revocation, clear-and-convincing judicial fraud findings for revocation of naturalization, protection against statelessness, and protection of previously acquired derivative citizenship.

### Proposed §5.1, fourth paragraph — Derivative Status of Children

A child born before a parent acquired legal residency or citizenship who joins that parent in the Republic within the period defined by statute, which may not be less than five years, receives the parent's status at the time of joining.

**History note:** Clarifies that the child's derivative status follows the parent's status when the child joins, including if the parent has advanced from residency to citizenship. Retains the five-year statutory minimum joining window as a family-reunification safeguard.

### New §5.1.b — Civic and Constitutional Proficiency

The Republic shall maintain a civic and constitutional proficiency assessment administered by an independent agency under §3.9. The assessment must comply with §1.6 and may not evaluate political opinion or cultural loyalty. Its content and administration are governed by statute.

**History note:** Establishes one constitutional home for the citizenship assessment, referenced by every naturalization pathway. Applies §1.6 nondiscrimination and retains the separate bar against evaluating political opinion or cultural loyalty, neither of which is comprehensively covered by §1.6 alone. Details remain statutory. §5.1.a is already occupied by State and Territory Voting Citizenship; new §5.1.b avoids renumbering it.

**Integration note:** Article XIX references the civic proficiency assessment administrator during founding transition; confirm the statutory agency obligation remains coherent after the new §5.1.b is inserted. A child acquiring citizenship at birth is not required to pass the naturalization assessment.

### Retained Article V provisions

- **§5.1.a — State and Territory Voting Citizenship:** retain unchanged. Each citizen has exactly one State or Territory voting citizenship, no gap in affiliation, and statutory resolution of overlaps.
- **§5.2 — Legal Resident Rights:** retain unchanged.
- **§5.3 — Custodial Care of Children:** retain unchanged. The stay of removal until custodial arrangements are secured is an operative child-protection safeguard rather than dispensable administrative detail.

## Proposed amendment 8 — Article VI immigration and residency review (settled working wording)

### §6.1 — Two-Stage Immigration System: retain unchanged

**Review note:** States choose whom to sponsor under constitutional nondiscrimination rules, statute sets federal eligibility and aggregate ordinary application volumes, and the executive administers the law. The Legislature may restrict ordinary immigration, including imposing zero intake, but no ordinary limit extinguishes a protected asylum claim. Once legal requirements are completed, the executive cannot obstruct vested residency.

### Proposed §6.2(2) — Review of Sponsorship and Certification Denials

State sponsorship denials may be challenged on grounds of discrimination contrary to §1.6. Federal certification denials on national security grounds may be challenged through judicial review. Courts determine the appropriate remedy.

An asylum claim must be determined before removal may be executed.

### Proposed §6.3(2) — Judicial Asylum Grants

### Proposed §6.3.a — Unexecuted Removal Orders

## Proposed amendment 9 — Article VII elections and federal office review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** These are proposed drafting changes only, not amendments to the adopted Constitution or public website. Preserve all Article VII provisions not specified below. Decisions made section by section in review.

### Proposed §7.2(7) — Election-result review

The results of a federal election or a direct vote of the citizens are final upon certification, subject to timely judicial review for material error or violation of constitutional electoral requirements, under procedures established by statute.

**History note:** Replaces exclusive Supreme Court review for material certification errors with judicial review for material error or violations of constitutional electoral requirements. Statute establishes procedures; finality and timeliness remain.

### Proposed §7.2.a — Special Elections and Postponements

Special elections shall be conducted under procedures established by statute.

A federal election may be postponed due to a natural disaster by a two-thirds vote of each chamber of the Legislature. A postponed election must be held as soon as practicable. The mandates of affected officeholders continue until certification of the postponed election.

**History note:** Eliminates Elections Panel certification as a legal prerequisite or veto, repeated 90-day authorizations, and constitutionally prescribed disaster/infrastructure procedural thresholds. The Elections Panel informs elected representatives and conducts elections; postponement authority belongs to the Assembly and Senate voting by two-thirds in each chamber. Courts can review overreach under ordinary constitutional judicial authority without a special review clause. The accepted trigger remains *natural disaster* rather than an expanded reference to any security emergency or catastrophe. Office continuity persists until election certification.

### Proposed §7.4(6) — Candidate-disqualification review

A candidate disqualified by the Elections Panel is entitled to expedited judicial review under procedures established by statute. Review must be available in sufficient time to protect the candidate's right to participate in the election or to obtain an effective remedy where the election has already occurred.

**History note:** Removes mandatory Appellate Court forum and default 14-day filing period, preserving meaningful expedited access to the judiciary, with effective pre- or post-election relief.

### §7.6 — Continuity and Acting Service: retain unchanged

**Review note:** General uninterrupted-succession obligation complements office-specific rules and immediate operation of authority in §7.15; no redundant repeal.

### §7.7 — Official Capacity Protection: retain unchanged

**Review note:** Maintain civil protection for lawful official action but no immunity for criminal conduct, unauthorized acts, or constitutional violations. No added constitutional good-faith immunity or judicial liability script.

### Proposed §7.10 — Ethics and Financial Disclosure (consolidates former §§7.10–7.11)

(1) A financial interest is disqualifying where it creates a direct and material conflict with the impartial exercise of official duties.

(2) Except for Monitor Generals as provided in Article IX, constitutional officers must disclose their financial interests, holdings, income sources, and liabilities to the Monitor responsible for their office, within a period defined by statute after taking office and annually thereafter. Disclosures are published to the NRS.

(3) An officer with a direct financial interest in a decision within their authority must recuse themselves. This requirement is self-executing.

(4) A constitutional officer with a personal, financial, or other material connection to a matter within their authority must publicly disclose that connection and state whether they will participate or recuse themselves, with reasons for that decision. The disclosure and explanation are published to the NRS. This provision does not diminish mandatory recusal under subsection (3).

(5) Constitutional officers may not accept gifts exceeding a de minimis threshold established by statute; absent such statute, the threshold is zero. Gifts received in an official capacity belong to the Republic and must be disclosed and transferred to the appropriate public authority regardless of value.

(6) Each Monitor may investigate violations within its mandate and refer matters warranting further action to the appropriate authority.

(7) The Legislature may establish codes of conduct, disclosure requirements, conflict-of-interest standards, and disciplinary procedures for constitutional offices outside the Legislature. Such rules may not diminish constitutional authority, tenure, or institutional independence.

**Integration note:** Replace §7.10 entirely with the preceding consolidated text and delete §7.11. Retain §7.13 separately as applying to federal employees and military personnel rather than constitutional officers.

**History note:** One constitutional home for financial interests, recusal, material-connection explanations, gift limits, Monitor oversight, and supplementary statutory ethics authority. Mandatory recusal applies to direct financial interests; other material connections require public disclosure and a justified participation/recusal decision. Do not automatically extend mandatory recusal to every personal connection.

### Proposed §7.12 — Post-Service Restrictions

Constitutional officers may not exploit non-public information, privileged access, or official influence obtained through their service for private benefit after leaving office. The Legislature may establish post-service restrictions and cooling-off periods by statute. The responsible Monitor must audit compliance; enforcement rests with the appropriate legal authority.

**History note:** Only `must establish` becomes `may establish` for statutory post-service restrictions and cooling-off periods. The directly operative anti-exploitation rule and Monitor oversight remain. Statute can tailor safeguards by risk without compulsory cooling-off for every office.

### §7.13 — Federal Public Service Ethics: retain unchanged

**Review note:** Distinct statutory disclosure and recusal obligations for federal employees and military personnel, subject to Monitor audits; do not fold into constitutional-office rules.

### Proposed §7.14 — Compensation of Federal Officials

Compensation of federal officials is established by statute. No federal official may determine their own compensation or that of their direct supervisors other than through such a statute. Changes to the compensation of elected constitutional officers take effect only after the next election for the affected office following enactment.

**History note:** Delete only the final existing sentence governing functional official benefits and accommodations. Preserve statutory control, protection from personal self-setting outside statute, and delayed changes for elected constitutional officers. Implementation should not let noncash personal benefits circumvent compensation safeguards.

### Article VII disposition and integration checks

- **Revise:** §7.2(7), §7.2.a, §7.4(6), §7.10 (incorporating former §7.11), §7.12, and §7.14.
- **Delete:** Former §7.11 as separate section after its content is integrated into §7.10.
- **Retain:** §7.1, §7.2(1)–(6), §7.3, §7.4(1)–(5), §§7.5–7.9, §7.13, and §7.15, subject only to future explicit review.
- **Cross-reference check:** §8.2's reference to §7.4(6) remains a valid subsection reference but now points to expedited judicial review rather than a named court/period. Search for references to former §7.11 before enacting.
- **Design basis:** Elected representatives exercise political postponement authority under two-thirds checks; independent courts police constitutional overreach. The Elections Panel advises/administers but does not gatekeep parliamentary power. Preserve constitutional rights and ethical accountability without assigning routine procedural details to the Constitution.

## Proposed amendment 11 — Article IX Monitors review (approved decisions and integration dependencies)

**Status:** Approved working directions and exact wording from the Article IX review; NOT enacted. This is a drafting register, not an assertion that all Article IX replacement sections are fully integrated. Do not merge a synthetic complete Article IX until unresolved interdependencies below are tested. Existing §9.6 revisions were already captured in earlier working entries; do not duplicate them.

### Proposed §9.1 — Three Monitors

The Republic maintains three constitutionally independent Monitors — Legislative (LM), Executive (EM), and Judicial (JM) — each selected under §9.2. Monitors hold an information-only mandate: they observe constitutional processes, assess compliance, and publish findings. Findings and assessments are not binding on any constitutional actor and are not a prerequisite for any legal proceeding or governmental function, except for consequences this Constitution expressly attaches to a finding. Each Monitor General serves a 10-year non-renewable term.

Any constitutional officer, chamber, or body may request an assessment from a Monitor within its mandate. Monitors shall publish their findings and assessments to the NRS. Where an investigation or assessment is discontinued, the Monitor shall publish the reasons for discontinuation and the findings obtained before discontinuation.

**History:** Remove prescribed response deadlines, non-responsiveness findings, compulsory completion after work becomes moot, and factually-impossible-only termination. Preserve transparent publication including reasons for discontinued investigations, while allowing investigative judgment.

### Proposed §9.1.a — Legislative Monitor

The Legislative Monitor audits the Legislature for fiscal accuracy and constitutional compliance, including the population census, Assembly districting under §3.3, and all information relating to the Legislature's public business. The Legislative Monitor also audits the Elections Panel's administration of referendum eligibility.

**History:** Expand public-business oversight, preserve specific jurisdiction for census/districting and referendum eligibility, remove annual audit scheduling requirement.

### Proposed §9.1.b — Executive Monitor

The Executive Monitor audits both executives, their ministers and subordinate officers, the Monetary Authority, and federal administrative agencies, including independent agencies, for constitutional compliance and the exercise of their public authority.

The Executive Monitor audits executive officers for dereliction of constitutional duties. Dereliction means failure to perform a constitutionally mandated function within the required period, except where circumstances genuinely beyond the officer's control prevented performance. Political disagreement with a constitutional requirement is not a defense.

**History:** Clarify that independent agencies, ministers, and subordinate executive officers fall within EM oversight. Use Republic terminology rather than “cabinet.” Restrict EM dereliction oversight to executive officers, not all constitutional officers. Preserve distinction between dereliction and policy disagreement; do not confer authority to assess courts' legal reasoning.

### Proposed §9.1.c — Judicial Monitor

The Judicial Monitor audits the courts, judicial officers, the Elections Panel, and compliance with constitutional treaty and compact obligations, including all information relating to the public business of institutions within its mandate.

**History:** Broad JM jurisdiction; no judicial decision-making authority or binding reassessment of court judgments.

### Proposed §9.1.d — Acting Monitor General (approved partial replacement)

Where a Monitor General's office becomes vacant or the Monitor General is temporarily unable to exercise its authority, the most senior eligible member of the affected Monitor's candidate pool by continuous pool tenure who accepts designation serves as Acting Monitor General until the vacancy is filled or the Monitor General's authority is restored.

The Acting Monitor General holds full constitutional authority but may not be appointed as the permanent successor to the Monitor General for whom they act.

**Integration requirement:** Retain original §9.1.d immediate term-expiry, succession, acting disqualification, vacancy, continuity, and NRS recording safeguards as applicable. This partial text alone is **not** a complete integrated §9.1.d. Check special succession conditions, ties, term scope and eligibility.

### Proposed §9.1.e — Temporary Incapacity (approved structure)

A Monitor General may declare themselves temporarily unable to exercise the duties of office.

Where a Monitor General is unable to make such a declaration, the constitutional authority responsible for suspension under §9.7.a may determine temporary incapacity.

During temporary incapacity, the Acting Monitor General assumes authority under §9.1.d. Temporary incapacity does not vacate the office or interrupt its term.

A determination of involuntary temporary incapacity is subject to expedited judicial review.

**Additional agreed restoration safeguard (requires integrated wording):** The affected Monitor General may declare recovery and resume office immediately unless the deciding authority obtains judicial relief to prevent restoration; the burden of continuing involuntary restriction lies with that authority. Remove former 24h/90d/30d incapacity cycles, mandatory public extension referendum and related procedures. Do not allow prolonged temporary incapacity to function as permanent removal.

### §9.4 — Judicial Monitor and judicial candidate pools

- **Delete existing §9.4(1)** detailed annual JM audit list: §9.1.c now establishes jurisdiction. Renumber appropriately at enactment.
- **Replace existing §9.4(2)** with: “Eligibility for the Judicial Monitor Candidate Pool is limited to former State or Republic judges who have served on a court of record at the trial court level or above and have been out of judicial office for at least one year. Former Supreme Court justices must have been out of Supreme Court service for at least five years.”
- **Replace existing §9.4(3)** with: “The Judicial Monitor Candidate Pool shall be audited jointly by the Legislative and Executive Monitors.”
- **Change only first sentence of §9.4.a** to: “Any citizen meeting the following eligibility criteria may apply directly to the Elections Panel for entry into the Judicial Pool, from which all federal judicial appointments are drawn: at least 15 years of legal practice or 10 years of judicial service; no disqualifying financial interest as defined in §7.10.”
- **Retain the remainder of §9.4.a unchanged:** Former Supreme Court justice may never re-enter Judicial Pool; prior permanent service on another federal court bars reappointment to the *same* court but permits movement to other courts; temporary/acting service does not exhaust eligibility.

**History:** Government lawyers, active State judges and other public officials may enter Judicial Pool without resigning first; incompatible service still governed on assumption of office. Fixed, nonrenewable judicial terms require a career path: no repeat term on same court to reduce conflicts, mobility to other courts to enable recruitment and advancement, Supreme Court as final pinnacle with no return. Preserve the trial-court floor and judicial-monitor cooling-off rules.

### Proposed §9.5 — Publication, independent staff disclosures, access, obstruction, public reporting

Monitor findings and assessments shall be published permanently to the NRS, subject to constitutional protections for confidential information and lawful security interests. No authority outside the issuing Monitor may suppress, delay, alter, or classify its findings.

Monitor personnel may independently disclose evidence of improper suppression or alteration of findings to the NRS, subject to the same confidentiality protections, and shall be protected from retaliation for lawful disclosure.

Monitors shall have unrestricted access to records, personnel, facilities, and systems within their constitutional audit mandates. Access to confidential or classified information does not authorize its public disclosure. Obstruction of a Monitor acting within its constitutional mandate shall be documented and published to the NRS.

The three Monitors shall periodically provide the Legislature and the citizens with a joint report on the State of the Republic.

**History:** Staff may reveal suppressed/altered findings but do not themselves issue official institutional findings. Remove detailed processing, required report delivery to Speakers, mandatory binary obstruction Fail designation, joint-work procedural rules, and fixed annual State of the Republic date. Adopt “periodically,” not “from time to time.” This combined §9.5 is assembled from individually approved component drafts and should be checked in final integration.

### Proposed §9.5.a — Audit Findings and Review

Monitors shall publish their audit findings to the NRS and submit them to the Senate for review. Where this Constitution requires a determination of compliance based on an audit, the Senate shall make that determination under procedures established by statute.

A body or officer subject to a Monitor report shall publish a response to the NRS within a period established by statute. The response need not agree with the Monitor's findings.

**History:** Eliminate JMC-mandated universal Pass/Fail classification and related prerequisites. Monitors make independent factual findings; Senate makes consequential compliance determinations where constitutionally required, with judicial review and public attribution. **Article XV companion reform mandatory** before constitutional enactment; no default remedy or inaction rule yet approved.

### §9.5.b — Agency Accountability: retain unchanged

Monitor findings, reports, and assessments do not substitute for a responsible body's independent obligation to perform its constitutional and statutory duties or to act on matters within its operational authority.

### §9.6 — Funding

Prior cross-article working amendment already replaces the inflation-adjusted automatic protected floor with §3.10 sufficient funding obligation and published rationale. No new change here.

### Proposed §9.7 — Joint Monitor Council

The three Monitor Generals constitute the Joint Monitor Council, which coordinates matters crossing Monitor jurisdictions and oversees compliance with constitutional processes assigned to it.

Two Monitor Generals constitute a quorum. Council decisions require a majority of participating members. Dissenting positions shall be published to the NRS.

The Council may establish compliance standards within its constitutional mandate but may not exercise functions assigned to another constitutional institution or override the independent authority of an individual Monitor.

**Integration:** Retain JMC's NRS audit responsibility in §9.7(3), with frequency set by statute rather than mandatory annual. Distinguish Council *procedural* standards from the Senate's consequential Statehood Audit determinations; the JMC may not reinstate a Pass/Fail veto through standards.

### Proposed §9.7.a — Institutional Compromise (approved principal allocation)

Where two Monitor Generals find evidence that the third has engaged in serious misconduct or abuse of office, they may recommend suspension by publishing their findings to the NRS.

Suspension requires approval by a majority of the Supreme Court for the Legislative Monitor General, jointly by the Speakers of both legislative chambers for the Executive Monitor General, or jointly by both executives for the Judicial Monitor General.

Disagreement with a Monitor General's findings or conclusions is not grounds for suspension. Suspension does not constitute removal, which remains governed by §9.9.

**Outstanding integration:** A fixed or statutory suspension duration limit and judicial review were discussed but no complete terminal wording approved. Do not silently import the old incapacity-referendum extensions. Ensure suspension cannot substitute for permanent removal; §9.1.d acting service remains effective during valid suspension.

### §9.8 — Constitutional Pool Framework

**Approved functional division:** Elections Panel maintains candidate registries and pools and *administers* constitutional lotteries; JMC audits compliance; it does not conduct draws or choose eligible individuals by political merit. The following two-sentence wording was approved in principle and must be integrated with statutory/constitutional implementation:

“The Elections Panel maintains constitutional candidate pools and registries and administers constitutional selection lotteries under applicable constitutional and statutory requirements. The Joint Monitor Council audits compliance with those requirements.”

**Replace §9.8(4)–(5)** with: “Each constitutional candidate pool shall contain at least five eligible members. A deficiency shall be published to the NRS, and the Elections Panel shall take action to restore the pool under procedures established by statute.”

**Replace §9.8(6)** with: “Eligibility for constitutional candidate pools shall be determined solely by objective criteria established by this Constitution. No applicant may be excluded on grounds of legal philosophy, judicial temperament, ideological orientation, or political association.”

**Replace §9.8(7)** with: “An applicant excluded or removed from a constitutional candidate pool is entitled to a published statement of reasons and timely judicial review under procedures established by statute.”

**Retain §9.8(8) unchanged.** Existing members protected from retroactive criteria changes, but continuing financial and other constitutional eligibility rules apply.

**History:** Entrench objective eligibility and impartial lotteries, five-person pools, transparent deficiencies and judicial remedies; remove detailed review deadlines, exclusive Supreme Court appeals and administrative roles assigned to the JMC.

### §9.9 — Removal (settled directions, integration required)

**For serious misconduct or abuse of office of a Monitor General:** Public confirmation is matched by public removal. A national removal referendum requires **60% of votes cast** under §7.3 without a 55% eligible-citizen turnout floor. Preserve stated public grounds, no automatic suspension upon filing, finality against repeated allegations from same conduct, and ability to present genuinely new evidence or conduct.

**Initiation by non-audited institution, acting alone:**
- Legislative Monitor: either Consul.
- Executive Monitor: either legislative chamber.
- Judicial Monitor: either legislative chamber or either Consul.

**Approved revised referendum timing/finality component:**

“The Elections Panel shall administer a national removal referendum within 90 days of initiation, or at the next regularly scheduled federal election, whichever occurs first. Failure to conduct the referendum within that period does not terminate the proceeding. The affected Monitor General retains their authority unless separately suspended under this Constitution.

Where a removal referendum fails, another proceeding may not be initiated against the same Monitor General on substantially the same grounds arising from the same conduct during that term. This restriction does not apply to materially new conduct or evidence.”

**Permanent incapacity is a DISTINCT no-fault removal ground:**

“Where two Monitor Generals determine that the third is permanently incapable of performing the duties of office, they may submit a recommendation for removal to the Speaker of the Assembly.

Removal for permanent incapacity requires approval by two-thirds of each chamber of the Legislature and by both Consuls. Political disagreement with a Monitor General's findings or conclusions does not constitute incapacity.”

Remove permanent incapacity from the general misconduct-referendum track when integrating; ensure the incapacity decision and acting service cannot be confused with misconduct suspension.

**Other constitutionally confirmed independent officers (revised §9.9(5)):**

“For constitutionally confirmed independent officers other than Monitor Generals and federal judges, removal may be initiated by a resolution supported by one-third of either legislative chamber. Removal requires a concurrent vote of two-thirds of the full seated membership of each chamber, on grounds established by statute. Initiation does not suspend the officer's authority.”

**Retain §9.9(6)** prospective-only statutory removal categories. Avoid redundant duplication of statutory ground-authority sentence when consolidating.

### §9.9.a — Monitor General Disclosure: retain unchanged

Monitor Generals independently publish §7.10 financial disclosures directly to NRS without another Monitor reviewing them. Concealment of disqualifying interests or failure to recuse remains serious misconduct.
