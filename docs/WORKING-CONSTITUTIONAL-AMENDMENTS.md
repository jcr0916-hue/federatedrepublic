# Working Constitutional Amendments — Unadopted

**Updated:** 23 November 2026  
**Status:** Working draft; agreement in design discussion, **not enacted or merged into the Constitution**.  
**Authority:** The adopted Constitution remains the sole authoritative text. This file collects proposals between review and formal amendment. Add future settled proposals here at reasonable intervals, preserving concise rationale notes for the Constitutional History page.

## Proposed amendment 1 — Fiscal sustainability and independent reporting

**Status (24 October 2026):** Adopted. The principle entered the Constitution with the 23 October release (PR #233); the final wording below, including the budget sustainability assessment, was applied by PR #241 (MA assessment after enactment; either chamber may request one on pending legislation). Retained here for its rationale notes.

**Scope:** Replace §12.1.b and §12.2(2) only. Preserve all other provisions of Article XII, especially the Social State (§12.1), MA independence (§12.1.a), interim appropriation (§12.2(3)–(5)), surplus-year Endowment funding (§12.2(6)), and National Endowment (§12.4).

### Proposed §12.1.b — Monetary Authority Reporting

The Monetary Authority publishes a State of the Economy report to the NRS, the Assembly, the Legat Consul, and the Civic Consul no less than semi-annually, or more frequently as statute requires. The report independently assesses economic conditions, fiscal sustainability, and performance against the Republic's fiscal obligations; identifies areas requiring improvement; and describes the MA's actions within its authority.

### Proposed §12.2(2) — Fiscal Accountability

The Republic conducts its fiscal affairs to sustain public finances, including public debt, over the long term while fulfilling its constitutional obligations. After each budget is enacted, the MA publishes an assessment of its likely effects on the economy and on fiscal sustainability. Either chamber may request an MA assessment of pending legislation.

### Rationale and testing notes for future Constitutional History entry

- **Design decision:** State a durable fiscal obligation, not a prescribed economic theory or specific statutory mechanism. Governments may lawfully borrow and run deficits; the aim is sustainable public finances, not zero debt.
- **Alternatives examined and set aside:** Swiss-inspired annual expenditure ceiling, cyclical balancing formula, compensation account, ten-year adjustment paths, supermajority economic-framework statute, and founding on-ramp. These were rejected as constitutional over-specification or as risking rigid fiscal policymaking; legislation may still adopt appropriate fiscal methods.
- **Independent accountability:** Require a substantive MA report on fiscal sustainability and areas for improvement without freezing a list of economic indicators. The MA continues to possess independent monetary authority, but does not acquire power to make fiscal policy.
- **Continuity and rights testing:** Inherited public debt and temporary recession deficits do not automatically violate the proposed duty. Existing budget-continuity, Social State, and National Endowment rules remain operative. Persistent unsustainable policy is reported and remains principally subject to democratic and ordinary constitutional accountability; the text supplies no automatic ceiling or budget invalidation.
- **Tradeoff acknowledged:** This is a principles-based obligation rather than a binding mechanical debt brake. It improves constitutional clarity and public assessment but cannot guarantee prudent elected fiscal decisions.

## Proposed amendment 2 — Guardians in the education clause

**Status (23 November 2026):** Proposed; settled in design discussion, not yet in the Constitution. It enters the adopted text only with a release.

**Scope:** Replace the last sentence of §12.1 (The Social State) only. Preserve the rest of §12.1, including the three mandatory guarantees, the Legislature's framework and adequacy standards, and the Executive Monitor's annual assessment.

### Proposed §12.1, final sentence

Parents or guardians may direct their children's education within public standards.

*Current text:* Parents may direct their children's education within public standards.

### Rationale and testing notes for future Constitutional History entry

- **Design decision:** The clause protects the person who stands in the parental role from being displaced by the State's educational framework. A child may have no living or available parent: a ward of the State, a child in foster or kin care, or one under a court-appointed guardian. "Parents" alone left that child's caregiver outside the sentence.
- **Wording:** "Parents or guardians" keeps parents first, since they are the common case, and matches the existing phrase "parent or guardian" in §5.3. The change is two words and leaves the clause's limit ("within public standards") and its relation to the Legislature's framework power untouched.
- **Alternatives examined and set aside:** (1) "A child's guardian" alone, which can be read as removing parents' standing; (2) "legal custodian," which turns on custody law and is narrower than the intent; (3) "caregiver," which reaches people with no legal standing. "Guardian" is left as the ordinary legal term, with its content set by statute and by the courts, as elsewhere in the Constitution.
- **Open point for review:** Where the State itself is the guardian, the clause protects less, because the State is also the framework-setter. The text does not try to solve this; it ensures only that the child's lawful guardian, whoever that is, is not excluded from the sentence. A court-appointed guardian other than the State is the case it serves best.
- **Downstream updates at release:** `constitution_data.json`, `annotated.html`, `docs/constitution-current.md`, the print edition and PDF, `search-index.js`, and a Constitutional History entry. The §12.1 annotation that speaks of the "parental direction clause" (`annotations.json`) should be reworded to "parent or guardian direction." The State constitutions are separate texts and are not changed by this proposal.

## Proposed amendment 3 — Sustainability wording in §12.2(2)

**Status (23 November 2026):** Proposed; settled in design discussion, not yet in the Constitution. It enters the adopted text only with a release. This refines the wording adopted under Proposed amendment 1 (PR #241).

**Scope:** Replace the first sentence of §12.2(2) only. Preserve the rest of §12.2(2) (the MA's post-enactment assessment and either chamber's right to request one on pending legislation) and all other provisions of §12.2.

### Proposed §12.2(2), first sentence

The Republic conducts its fiscal affairs to keep its public finances, including public debt, sustainable while fulfilling its constitutional obligations.

*Current text:* The Republic conducts its fiscal affairs to sustain public finances, including public debt, over the long term while fulfilling its constitutional obligations.

### Rationale and testing notes for future Constitutional History entry

- **Design decision:** Two drafting problems, fixed together. First, "sustain public finances, including public debt" can be read as keeping the debt going, when the intent is that the debt stays sustainable. Second, "over the long term" has no anchor: no one can say when the long term arrives, so almost any deficit can be defended as sustainable eventually, and a court has nothing to test.
- **Wording:** "Sustainable" already looks forward: the question is whether the finances can continue, which needs no stated horizon. "Keep … sustainable" makes the object of the duty the finances and the debt, not the debt itself. The text now uses the same term as §12.1.b and the next sentence of §12.2(2) ("fiscal sustainability").
- **Alternatives examined and set aside:** (1) Keeping "over the long term" and moving it, which leaves the horizon unanchored; (2) a numeric or dated horizon (for example ten years), the over-specification Proposed amendment 1 rejected; (3) "responsibly," which is vaguer than the current text; (4) a negative duty not to run unsustainable debt, which frames the obligation as a prohibition and invites mechanical tests.
- **Continuity and rights testing:** Proposed amendment 1's testing notes still hold. Inherited debt and temporary recession deficits do not automatically violate the duty, because the test is whether the finances remain sustainable, not whether any single year balances. The duty remains principles-based, with no automatic ceiling or budget invalidation.
- **Downstream updates at release:** `constitution_data.json`, `annotated.html`, `docs/constitution-current.md`, `search-index.js`, the print edition and PDF, and a Constitutional History entry. The §12.2 annotation that says subsection (2) "ties fiscal practice to a long-term sustainability obligation" (`annotations.json`) should be reworded to "a sustainability obligation."

## Proposed amendment 4 — Monitor findings under §12.6: submission, not compelled response

**Status (23 November 2026):** Proposed; settled in design discussion, not yet in the Constitution. It enters the adopted text only with a release.

**Scope:** Replace the second paragraph of §12.6 (Fiscal Equalization) only. Preserve the first paragraph (the Legislature's duty to establish an equalization mechanism by statute, with its "reasonably sufficient" standard tied to §12.1 and Article I).

### Proposed §12.6, second paragraph

The Executive Monitor must audit and review the mechanism's compliance with constitutional requirements, publish its findings to the NRS, and submit them to the Speakers of both legislative chambers and the Civic Consul.

*Current text:* The Executive Monitor must audit and review the mechanism's compliance with constitutional requirements and publish its findings to the NRS. The Legislature must publish a response to findings of noncompliance within a period established by statute.

### Rationale and testing notes for future Constitutional History entry

- **Design decision:** The Monitor audits and reports; it does not compel. The current second sentence makes a Monitor "finding of noncompliance" the trigger for a mandatory legislative act, which is inconsistent with the information-only mandate of §9.1 and with §9.5.a, where a compliance determination belongs to the institution the Constitution names, not to the Monitor. A "finding of noncompliance" is a determination; an audit and review is an assessment. The paragraph now asks the Monitor for the second and nothing more.
- **Submission replaces the compelled response:** The findings still reach the right desks. They go to the NRS (public record) and to the Speakers of both chambers and the Civic Consul (the people who can act on them). That uses the same transmittal phrase as §2.2, §9.3 and §15.5.a ("the Speakers of both chambers") and the Civic Consul, so no new institutional pattern is introduced.
- **Why the Legislature is not required to answer:** "Must publish" forces an action on a co-equal branch in response to a finding by a body that cannot compel anything. Consequences come from publicity, the chambers' own oversight, and ordinary politics. The Legislature may still answer, and a statute may still set a response practice; the Constitution simply does not require one.
- **Alternatives examined and set aside:** (1) Keep a mandatory legislative response but soften the trigger; this still forces an action on the strength of a Monitor finding. (2) Let the Legislature "request review and remediation by the Monetary Authority"; remediation is fiscal-policy work, which the Monetary Authority does not hold (Proposed amendment 1: no fiscal-policy power). (3) Add a clause allowing the chambers to request an MA review; redundant, because either chamber may already ask for MA assessment of pending legislation under §12.2(2) and may seek review at any point as part of its ordinary prerogative.
- **Tradeoff acknowledged:** The paragraph no longer contains an answering step. A shortfall can be documented, published and delivered to the Speakers and the Civic Consul, but nothing in the text makes anyone respond. The first paragraph still obliges the Legislature to maintain an equalization mechanism meeting the standard, and a court can still hear a concrete rights claim, so the duty itself is untouched; only the compelled reply is removed.
- **Downstream updates at release:** `constitution_data.json`, `annotated.html`, `docs/constitution-current.md`, `search-index.js`, the print edition and PDF, and a Constitutional History entry. The §12.6 annotation (`annotations.json`) must be rewritten where it says the Monitor "publishes findings of noncompliance," the Legislature "must publish a response," and that the loop is "a notice-and-response structure" with "both mandatory." Scenario and world text that treats the response as a constitutional requirement needs a pass: `scenario-the-formula.html` (subtitle, the "A published response is required" beat, the Year 9 beat, and the closing summary) and the matching entry in `_data/scenarioBridges.json` (about line 965). Published world records are not edited silently: `torenthia/nrs/torenthia-nrs-005.html` already attributes its 90-day window to "the reporting statute implementing §12.6," which stays consistent because a statute may still require a response; its sentence that "the Legislature's obligation under §12.6 is to respond" would need a dated correction or an editor's note rather than a quiet rewrite. `scenario-critical-finding.html` concerns the Legislative Monitor under §9.5.a, not §12.6, and is unaffected. The State constitutions are separate texts and are not changed by this proposal.
