# Working Constitutional Amendments — Unadopted

**Updated:** 20 October 2026  
**Status:** Working draft; agreement in design discussion, **not enacted or merged into the Constitution**.  
**Authority:** The adopted Constitution remains the sole authoritative text. This file collects proposals between review and formal amendment. Add future settled proposals here at reasonable intervals, preserving concise rationale notes for the Constitutional History page.

## Proposed amendment 1 — Fiscal sustainability and independent reporting

**Scope:** Replace §12.1.b and §12.2(2) only. Preserve all other provisions of Article XII, especially the Social State (§12.1), MA independence (§12.1.a), interim appropriation (§12.2(3)–(5)), surplus-year Endowment funding (§12.2(6)), and National Endowment (§12.4).

### Proposed §12.1.b — Monetary Authority Reporting

The Monetary Authority publishes a detailed State of the Economy report to the NRS, the Assembly, the Legat Consul, and the Civic Consul no less than semi-annually, or more frequently as statute requires. The report independently assesses economic conditions, fiscal sustainability, performance against the Republic's fiscal obligations, and areas requiring improvement, including the MA's actions within its authority.

### Proposed §12.2(2) — Fiscal Accountability

The Republic shall conduct its fiscal affairs to preserve the long-term sustainability of public finances, including public debt, while fulfilling its constitutional obligations.

### Rationale and testing notes for future Constitutional History entry

- **Design decision:** State a durable fiscal obligation, not a prescribed economic theory or specific statutory mechanism. Governments may lawfully borrow and run deficits; the aim is sustainable public finances, not zero debt.
- **Alternatives examined and set aside:** Swiss-inspired annual expenditure ceiling, cyclical balancing formula, compensation account, ten-year adjustment paths, supermajority economic-framework statute, and founding on-ramp. These were rejected as constitutional over-specification or as risking rigid fiscal policymaking; legislation may still adopt appropriate fiscal methods.
- **Independent accountability:** Require a substantive MA report on fiscal sustainability and areas for improvement without freezing a list of economic indicators. The MA continues to possess independent monetary authority, but does not acquire power to make fiscal policy.
- **Continuity and rights testing:** Inherited public debt and temporary recession deficits do not automatically violate the proposed duty. Existing budget-continuity, Social State, and National Endowment rules remain operative. Persistent unsustainable policy is reported and remains principally subject to democratic and ordinary constitutional accountability; the text supplies no automatic ceiling or budget invalidation.
- **Tradeoff acknowledged:** This is a principles-based obligation rather than a binding mechanical debt brake. It improves constitutional clarity and public assessment but cannot guarantee prudent elected fiscal decisions.

## Proposed amendment 2 — Article I principles review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Replace §§1.7, 1.11, 1.12, 1.16, and 1.17 as written below. This is the record of agreed drafting decisions, **not an enacted amendment**. All other Article I provisions remain unchanged, including §§1.14, 1.14.a, and 1.16.a.

### Proposed §1.7 — Privacy, Property, and Security of the Home

Every person holds the right to privacy and security in their person, home, communications, and data, and the right to own property. Government may not enter a home, search a person or property, surveil a person or communication, or collect data without consent or prior independent judicial authorization on specific, articulable grounds. This applies within the Republic's territory and to Republic citizens wherever located. Encryption is lawful and may not be prohibited. Property may be taken for public use only upon just compensation determined independently.

**History note:** Clarifies which actions constitute searches, surveillance, and data collection, retaining broad protection for data and the existing prior-authorization standard. No substantive narrowing intended.

### Proposed §1.11 — Rights of the Accused and Fair Trial

Every person accused of a criminal offense has the right to prompt notice of the charges; counsel, at public expense if unable to afford representation; adequate time and means to prepare a defense; examination of witnesses; silence without adverse inference; and a speedy trial. No person may receive a greater penalty solely for exercising the right to trial. Bail may be imposed only as reasonably necessary to ensure appearance or protect public safety and may not be punitive. The accused has the right to examine and challenge the evidence presented against them, including the methods used to produce it.

**History note:** Replaces a technology-specific right to examine automated or systematic methodology with a general evidentiary guarantee covering AI, scientific analysis, and future methods without naming a particular technology.

### Proposed §1.12 — Right to a Public Trial

Every person accused of a criminal offense has the right to a public trial before an independent and impartial court. Proceedings and records may be closed or sealed only to the minimum extent and duration necessary to protect a compelling interest identified by the court. The grounds for closure or sealing must be published to the NRS, and continued secrecy remains subject to independent judicial review.

**History note:** Replaces fixed ten-year sealing and five-year extension limits and automatic-release machinery with a continuing compelling-interest, necessity, duration, publication, and judicial-review standard. §1.1 and the Constitution's other transparency provisions remain available.

### Proposed §1.16 — Emergency Derogation

No emergency suspends the Individual Sovereignty Floor in full. Rights other than those protected by §1.16.a may be temporarily derogated only upon a declaration stating the emergency, rights affected, scope, and duration, published immediately to the NRS.

Either executive may issue a declaration within their domain for no more than 14 days. Continuation requires a statute with a fixed expiration. No declaration or authorization may be renewed or substantially repeated.

All emergency measures lapse automatically with their authorization. Every declaration receives mandatory expedited independent judicial review, and judicial remedies remain available.

The three Monitors retain full constitutional authority throughout any emergency.

**History note:** Keeps the 14-day executive limit, legislative authorization and expiry, automatic lapse, Monitors' authority, and mandatory independent review. Replaces the fixed 48-hour judicial-review deadline and 60-day re-declaration sequence with expedited review and an unqualified prohibition on renewal or substantially repeated declarations. **Substantive consequence for future review:** the absolute no-renewal wording also bars a renewed or substantially similar derogation during a genuinely continuing emergency; it is not limited to evasion.

### Proposed §1.17 — Right to Seek Asylum

Every person who reaches the Republic's jurisdiction has the right to claim asylum and to an independent determination of that claim under §6.3. Manner of entry does not affect this right.

**History note:** Removes the final sentence suggesting that asylum seekers obtain *all* Article I rights upon claiming asylum. Each right retains its own scope, including citizen-only voting under §1.8 and universal protections already held before an asylum claim.

### Article I disposition and review notes

- **Replace:** §§1.7, 1.11, 1.12, 1.16, 1.17, using the agreed wording above.
- **Retain:** All remaining Article I provisions. In particular, §1.14 retains its same-offense and overlapping State/Republic prosecution rules; §1.14.a deliberately protects lawful interstate conduct and associated assistance or information (including travel assistance and medical care); §1.16.a retains its six non-derogable rights and emergency anti-discrimination guarantee.
- **Design basis:** Preserve substantive rights, necessary institutional checks, and failure-state protections while avoiding technology-specific guarantees and unnecessary fixed administrative deadlines. Revisit only a demonstrable gap, not hypothetical loopholes for their own sake.

## Proposed amendment 3 — Article II executive review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Proposed replacements of §§2.2, 2.5(4), 2.12, 2.14.b, 2.16(2)–(4), and 2.18; delete §2.10 and its redundant standalone publication rules; remove only the final appropriation sentence of §2.15. Related consolidation into §§3.10, 9.6, and 10.1(2) is documented in proposed amendment 4 below. **Unadopted working proposals, not effective constitutional text.** All other Article II provisions remain unchanged, including §2.3.a and its ten-business-day veto period.

### Proposed §2.2 — Military Authorization Framework

The Legat Consul may use military force in immediate response to an active or imminent attack on Republic territory, citizens abroad, or treaty allies. Beyond immediate response, military force requires legislative authorization. The Legat Consul must notify both chambers and the Civic Consul without undue delay of any use of force. Legislative authorization must have a defined expiration and may be renewed only by affirmative legislative action; upon expiration, operations must cease. Operations must state and serve a purpose authorized by §14.1.

**History note:** Replaces the 24-hour notice period with a prompt-notification standard and the twelve-month maximum authorization period with required expiration and affirmative legislative renewal. Preserves immediate-response authority and cessation upon expiration. The Legislature determines the period but cannot give legally indefinite authorization.

### §2.3.a — Legat Consul Legislative Veto: retain unchanged

**Review note:** Retain the ten-business-day veto review period as constitutional machinery. Allowing the Legislature to set or alter that period could nullify an independent veto or indefinitely delay enactment. Retain the established veto, override, and single-use mechanisms.

### Proposed §2.5(4) — Domestic Emergencies

The Civic Consul may declare a domestic emergency in response to natural disaster, public health crisis, or infrastructure failure, subject to Article I and Assembly oversight. The Assembly may terminate the declaration at any time. The Legat Consul has no authority in domestic emergencies except assistance under §2.14.b.

**History note:** Replaces renewal at statutory intervals (default 30 days) with ongoing Assembly oversight and a direct Assembly power to terminate the declaration, without Senate concurrence or Civic Consul approval. Rights derogation remains governed independently by Article I §1.16; continuity emergencies remain governed by §2.11.

### §2.10 — Executive Transparency: delete

**History note:** Consolidate constitutional-act publication obligations into §10.1(2), including attribution and the rule against concealing the existence or legal effect of constitutional acts. Avoid an executive-specific duplicate. See proposed amendment 4.

### Proposed §2.12 — Clemency

Each Consul may grant clemency only within their domain: the Legat Consul for military and national-security offenses, the Civic Consul for domestic federal offenses. Self-clemency is void.

Every grant must state its reasons. Clemency does not bar any constitutional, political, civil, or criminal remedy for misconduct connected with its exercise. Group amnesty requires legislation.

**History note:** Removes the two sentences on optional EM factual review, including the statement that this review does not suspend or invalidate a grant. Lawful investigation and judicial processes can be conducted under their ordinary authorities; substantive clemency limits and remedies remain.

### Proposed §2.14.b — Cross-Domain Assistance

Either Consul may request assistance from the other within the responding Consul's domain. The request must state its purpose and scope and be published to the NRS. The requesting Consul controls the purpose; the responding Consul determines whether and how to act, controls the means, and publishes the response. Neither Consul may compel the other. Assistance may provide non-coercive support only. It may not be used to compel, detain, surveil, or collect intelligence on persons within the Republic, or to commandeer civilian resources, except as §1.16 or another express constitutional source allows.

**History note:** Removes an arbitrary 90-day renewal limit and required duration statement. Preserves voluntary cross-domain assistance, separate domain control, public accountability, and explicit limits on coercion.

### Proposed §2.15 — Federal Prosecution

The Civic Consul is responsible for enforcement of federal law and may set general prosecution policy. Statute must establish a Federal Prosecution Service independent in individual prosecutorial decisions. Executive direction concerning a specific prosecution must be in writing and published to the NRS.

**History note:** Removes only the final sentence imposing a two-thirds vote of both chambers to reduce the Service's appropriation below the prior year's level. General institutional capacity and resource-justification obligations are consolidated into §3.10; retained text should be reviewed separately if a later pass identifies an actual independence conflict.

### Proposed §2.16(2)–(3) — Executive Incapacity

(2) The Consul may declare the incapacity ended, restoring authority 24 hours after publication. If incapacity is determined again on substantially the same grounds within 90 days, the renewed determination requires 2/3 of the deciding body. Following that determination, authority may be restored by majority approval of that body.

(3) A renewed determination under subsection (2) remains effective for no more than six months. Continuation thereafter requires a fresh 2/3 determination. The Consul may challenge any involuntary incapacity determination through expedited independent judicial review, limited to lawful invocation and inability to exercise the office. Policy disagreement is not incapacity.

**Implementation note:** Replace present subsections (2)–(4) with these two subsections; renumber the unchanged former subsections (5)–(9) sequentially, adjusting internal references only if necessary. Existing automatic succession and acting-service rules remain intact.

**History note:** Preserves the ordinary self-declared recovery rule, 24-hour restoration delay, repeated-determination two-thirds vote, 90-day window, and a six-month renewed-determination maximum. Removes the separate 30-day heightened-restoration window and legislative extension process. Courts act on disputes, not as routine approval participants; expedited independent judicial review can begin in the ordinary competent court, not exclusively the Supreme Court. Judicial remedies must have operative effect, including reversal and restoration when warranted.

### Proposed §2.18 — Domestic Insurrection

During armed insurrection against the constitutional order, the Civic Consul must report to the Assembly at statutory intervals for the duration of the response, as the Legat Consul reports to the Senate. No required report may be withheld because operations are ongoing.

**History note:** Removes the operational requirement for the Council of Ministers to remain convened throughout an insurrection. Retains mandatory executive reporting and oversight.

### Article II disposition and review notes

- **Revise:** §§2.2, 2.5(4), 2.12, 2.14.b, 2.16(2)–(4), and 2.18 as above.
- **Delete:** §2.10, consolidating its unique safeguards into §10.1(2).
- **Funding consolidation:** Delete the final sentence of §2.15; address institutional resources through §3.10 and remove §9.6.
- **Retain:** All other Article II sections, including §2.3.a and the veto's fixed ten-business-day deadline.
- **Design basis:** Preserve deadlines that secure independent constitutional authority and automatic succession, but leave operational methods, lawful oversight procedures, and policy durations to democratic institutions where doing so creates no failure of continuity or accountability.

## Proposed amendment 4 — General institutional resources and NRS publication

**Scope:** Revise §3.10 by adding a uniform resources principle while preserving its existing legislative-competence text and institutional-capacity obligation; delete §9.6 in full; revise the opening of §10.1(2) while preserving the remainder. Coordinate these changes with the approved Article II deletion of §2.10 and funding sentence removal in §2.15. Unadopted working proposals.

### Proposed addition to §3.10 — Institutional Resources

The Legislature shall provide institutions established by this Constitution or by statute with resources sufficient to perform their assigned duties. Material changes in those resources must be accompanied by a published justification addressing the institution's responsibilities and capacity to fulfill them.

**Integration note:** Add the paragraph to §3.10 without deleting its existing provision that the Legislature must maintain an institution capable of performing each constitutionally assigned function. Statutory institutions remain subject to lawful reorganization, changed duties, and abolition; constitutional duties cannot be abolished by a budget decision.

**History note:** Replaces institution-specific appropriation formulas with a uniform sufficiency-and-explanation rule, applying to both increases and reductions. Retains legislative budget discretion while prohibiting impairment of duties. Tradeoff: removes automatic funding floors and supermajority shields from independent oversight institutions.

### §9.6 — Minimum Funding Guarantee: delete in full

**History note:** Repeal the inflation-adjusted appropriation floor for each Monitor, the Elections Panel, the NRS Panel, and the Monetary Authority; remove the two-thirds reduction threshold, certification procedure, and automatic-floor appropriation. Institutional funding obligations will instead reside in §3.10.

### Proposed opening of §10.1(2) — Constitutional Acts and Records

Every constitutional act, order, finding, certification, designation, declaration, and determination made under this Constitution is published to the NRS as a permanent record, attributed to the responsible officer or institution; no provision need state this obligation individually. The existence and legal effect of a constitutional act may not be concealed by classification.

**Integration note:** Following these opening sentences, preserve unchanged the existing §10.1(2) text beginning “Official records and materials created, received, maintained, or produced in the exercise of official duties are property of the Republic” and continuing through its rules on custody, destruction, publication, access, retention, and classification.

**History note:** Consolidates §2.10's executive-specific publication and accountability rules into the general constitutional-acts rule, expands attribution to responsible officers or institutions, and distinguishes the public existence and legal effect of acts from potentially classified supporting materials.

## Proposed amendment 5 — Article III Legislature review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Replace §3.2(2), §3.2(5), the last sentence of §3.3, the second paragraph of §3.4, §3.5(1), §3.9, and §3.11 with the text below. The agreed §3.10 institutional-resources addition is already recorded under proposed amendment 4 and should be implemented once, not duplicated. Other Article III provisions remain unchanged, including §§3.1, 3.6, 3.7, and 3.8. **Working proposals only; not enacted.**

### Proposed §3.2(2) — Census

The census, conducted by a statutory independent agency at intervals defined by statute not exceeding ten years, is the basis for reapportionment.

**History note:** Changes a rigid ten-year cycle into a maximum interval, allowing more frequent censuses without enabling indefinite postponement of population-based representation.

### Proposed §3.2(5) — Assembly Vacancies

Mid-term Assembly vacancies are filled by special election within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days, unless the remainder of the term is shorter than that period.

**History note:** Preserves the 90-day cap while avoiding mandatory special elections when the unexpired term is shorter than the statutory election period. The exception permits, but does not require, leaving the seat vacant near term-end.

### Proposed final sentence of §3.3 — Electoral Districts

Any citizen or State government may seek judicial remedy for a district map that violates this Constitution.

**History note:** Removes exclusive direct Supreme Court jurisdiction while preserving citizen and State standing, substantive district-map standards, and effective relief through the ordinary judicial system. Retain all earlier sentences of §3.3 unchanged.

### Proposed second paragraph of §3.4 — Legislative Standards

Each chamber establishes and publishes its sitting schedule, and any revision, to the NRS. No recess may be used to defeat or delay a constitutional duty or deadline. Emergency sessions may be called by either Consul or by petition of one-third of either chamber.

**History note:** Removes mandated full-time legislative status; retains publicly accountable calendars, safeguards against recess-based avoidance of constitutional duties, and emergency convening. Leave the first and third paragraphs of §3.4 unchanged.

### Proposed §3.5(1) — Senate Vacancies

The Senate comprises two senators per State regardless of population, elected for six-year staggered terms. A mid-term vacancy is filled as the State provides, within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days, unless the remainder of the term is shorter than that period.

**History note:** Preserves States' choice of vacancy-filling mechanism and the 90-day maximum; permits a vacancy to remain unfilled if the statutory filling period exceeds the remaining term, while allowing a State to fill it voluntarily.

### Proposed §3.9 — Independent Statutory Agencies

The Legislature may establish independent agencies outside the authority of either executive and may alter or abolish agencies established by statute. The Legislature may not impair the independence of a function this Constitution assigns to an independent agency.

Unless this Constitution provides otherwise, statute governs organization, appointments, qualifications, terms, and oversight. The appointed leadership of an independent agency serves fixed terms and may be removed only for conduct, incapacity, or incompetence, not policy disagreement. Qualification requirements may not be designed to exclude candidates because of political or policy views or prior criticism of government.

**History note:** Clarifies that statutory agencies may be altered or abolished by the Legislature, while extending fixed-term and for-cause removal protections from principal decision-makers exercising constitutionally assigned independent functions to **appointed leadership of any independent agency**. Independence protects agency leadership during the agency's existence, not permanent institutional existence or ordinary employees.

### §3.10 — Federal Legislative Competence: apply amendment 4

**Integration note:** Add the already-agreed institutional resource-sufficiency and published-justification paragraph from proposed amendment 4. Preserve existing legislative competence, States' self-government, and constitutionally assigned institutional-capacity language.

### Proposed §3.11 — Delegation of Authority

The Legislature may by statute delegate defined authority within federal legislative competence to either Consul or to an independent agency under §3.9. A delegation must state its scope and conditions. Delegation does not divest the Legislature of that authority: statute prevails over any conflicting exercise of delegated authority, and the Legislature may rescind a delegation at any time. Each exercise of delegated authority is published to the NRS identifying the delegating statute.

**History note:** Removes universal duration requirement for statutory delegations. Legislative control, scope, conditions, statutory supremacy, and revocation remain; sunsets may still be set by statute when appropriate.

### Article III retained provisions and tested alternatives

- **§3.1 — Senate Review: retain unchanged.** Keep the 90-day review period, optional one-time 30-day Senate extension, and distinction between tacit approval and constitutionally required affirmative votes. Defining a legislative session as the period between Assembly elections was considered; a session-end deadline alone could leave too little Senate review for bills passed just before elections. Elections do not truncate the 90-day review.
- **§3.6 — Treaties and Trade Agreements: retain unchanged.** Keep the maximum 90-day Senate affirmation period for proposed treaty withdrawal. Without a definite lapse, Senate inaction could leave foreign-policy decisions unresolved despite the Legat Consul's foreign-affairs responsibility.
- **§§3.7 and 3.8: retain unchanged.** Voting-threshold clarity and Monitor access to legislative information remain important.
- **Remainder:** Retain all Article III text outside the expressly identified replacements and the already-agreed §3.10 addition.

## Proposed amendment 6 — Article IV judicial architecture review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Replace §4.1; replace §4.2(1) and §4.2(5); amend only the equal-division and quorum paragraph of §4.3; amend §4.5(3), §4.5(4), the first category of §4.5(5), and §4.5(6); and consolidate §4.5(8)–(9) into one §4.5(8). Preserve all other Article IV text, especially §§4.2(2)–(4), 4.3.a, 4.4, 4.4.a, 4.5(1)–(2), 4.5(7), 4.5.a, and 4.6. **Settled discussion drafts, not enacted.**

### Proposed §4.1 — Court Structure

The Republic's judiciary consists of district courts, appellate courts, and the Supreme Court. The Legislature may establish, abolish, and define the jurisdiction of inferior courts by statute. Abolition of a court takes effect for each occupied judicial seat upon expiration of the sitting judge's term. Constitutional questions arising in any proceeding may be referred to the SC.

**History note:** Replaces redundant retention-of-office, compensation, and temporary-service language with occupied-seat abolition through attrition. Reorganization remains legislative; existing judges' seats survive through their terms. Reassignment and pending-case administration do not require a distinct constitutional succession office. Reflects the attrition safeguard already applied to Supreme Court seat reductions.

### Proposed §4.2(1) — Inferior Judicial Appointments

The Civic Consul shall nominate inferior court judges from the Judicial Pool under §9.8 without undue delay following a vacancy. The Senate confirms by a majority of its full seated membership.

**History note:** Replaces a fixed 90-day nomination deadline with an enforceable prompt-action standard; leaves judicial selection and confirmation with the Civic Consul and Senate. The public-confirmation fallback in §4.4.a applies to Supreme Court appointments only.

### Proposed §4.2(5) — Pending Cases During Vacancies

The Supreme Court may reassign cases among inferior federal courts or qualified judges as necessary to prevent judicial vacancies from delaying proceedings.

**History note:** Replaces automatic temporary judicial-office succession with authority to redistribute cases during vacancies. The safeguard serves both continuity and accountability: proceedings move independently of delayed political appointments, diminishing appointing authorities' leverage over cases until permanent appointments occur. General resources and adequate staffing remain governed by §3.10 under the One Home Rule; do not duplicate that obligation here.

### Proposed second paragraph of §4.3 — Supreme Court Equal Division and Quorum

An equally divided Court leaves the challenged ruling in effect, or, in an original proceeding, grants no relief. An equal division establishes no binding precedent. A majority of seated justices, including Temporary Associate Justices, constitutes ordinary quorum; constitutional questions require at least five participating justices.

**History note:** Clarifies that tied original-jurisdiction proceedings grant no relief and that an equal division creates no binding precedent. Ordinary procedures determine whether a matter can be reheard; no constitutional guarantee of unlimited relitigation. Retain the first paragraph's minimum five justices, twelve-year nonrenewable staggered terms, two-thirds chamber approval for resizing, and attrition protection.

### §§4.4 and 4.4.a — Supreme Court Selection and Public Confirmation: retain unchanged

**Review note:** Retain the 90-day nomination and 120-day Senate-vote maximums, which activate the public-confirmation fallback when officials fail to act; timely Senate rejection is a legitimate exercise of constitutional checking, not inaction. Retain automatic eligible appellate-judge temporary succession and the public confirmation or rejection pathway. Do not create a third fallback merely because no eligible appellate judge is available: an unfilled seat may remain vacant until an eligible successor becomes available, subject to the separate Court quorum requirement. The Judicial Pool under §9.8 provides permanent-nomination candidates, not automatic temporary appellate successors.

### Proposed §4.5(3) — Monitor Findings

Monitor findings published to the NRS are admissible in judicial proceedings.

**History note:** Removes a court's mandatory written explanation for disagreeing with Monitor findings. Admissibility does not give oversight reports conclusive factual or legal weight; courts remain independent in deciding law and evidence.

### Proposed §4.5(4) — Standing and Judicial Forum

Except where this Constitution expressly confers standing, courts adjudicate only cases involving a concrete, articulable legal injury and may not issue advisory opinions. An organization has standing where at least one member would have standing and the claim is germane to the organization's established purposes. The Legislature may not leave any category of legal injury without a judicial forum.

**History note:** Changes “its established constitutional purpose” to “the organization's established purposes,” preserving member injury and germaneness while not excluding otherwise qualifying associations without a specifically constitutional mission.

### Proposed first category of §4.5(5) — Supreme Court Original Jurisdiction

— constitutional disputes between States, and disputes between a State and the Republic concerning their respective constitutional powers or obligations;

**Integration note:** Replace only the first enumerated category. Preserve all other original-jurisdiction categories, including disputes between Consuls, structurally compromised independent-institution disputes, direct emergency rights claims, and amendment-consistency disputes. Ordinary constitutional challenges to federal statutes proceed through the judicial hierarchy unless another original-jurisdiction category applies.

**History note:** Preserves direct SC jurisdiction for constitutional State–State disputes and structural State–Republic allocation-of-authority disputes, but not every constitutional challenge to a federal statute.

### Proposed §4.5(6) — Immediate Defensive Military Authority

Constitutional challenges to the exercise of immediate defensive military authority under §2.2 during active operations receive expedited independent judicial review.

**History note:** Replaces automatic SC jurisdiction and a 48-hour decision maximum with expedited review by a competent independent court. Existing structural-dispute SC jurisdiction remains available where appropriate.

### Proposed §4.5(8) — Interim Protection and Missed Judicial Deadlines

Courts may issue interim protective orders where necessary to prevent irreparable harm pending judgment. Failure to rule within a constitutionally prescribed period does not determine the merits or produce a substantive constitutional outcome. The parties retain all otherwise available judicial remedies.

**Integration note:** Replace existing §4.5(8) with this text and delete §4.5(9). No change to §4.5(7).

**History note:** Removes detailed one-time Supreme Court extension votes and fixed extension caps, while preserving interim protection, binding judicial deadlines, and the principle that missing a deadline does not decide the merits.

### Article IV disposition and implementation checks

- **Replace:** §4.1; §4.2(1), §4.2(5); second paragraph of §4.3; §4.5(3), §4.5(4), first category of §4.5(5), §4.5(6), and §4.5(8).
- **Delete:** Old §4.5(9) after consolidation into §4.5(8).
- **Retain:** §§4.2(2)–(4), 4.3 first paragraph, 4.3.a, 4.4, 4.4.a, 4.5(1)–(2), remaining §4.5(5) categories, §4.5(7), 4.5.a, and 4.6, plus other unchanged text.
- **Cross-references:** Confirm removal of the old §4.1 reference to §4.2(5), and later search the full Constitution for any references to deleted §4.5(9). Do not silently alter other provisions without review.
- **Design basis:** Courts must keep cases moving; control of pending cases should not depend on political appointment delays. Use ordinary courts for ordinary legal disputes and reserve original Supreme Court jurisdiction for structural questions; preserve enforceable rights and judicial independence while minimizing detailed scheduling machinery.

## Proposed amendment 7 — Article V citizenship review (settled working wording)

**Scope:** Amend the first, second, and fourth paragraphs of §5.1; establish a new §5.1.b governing civic and constitutional proficiency. Preserve the third paragraph of §5.1 on revocation; retain §§5.1.a, 5.2, and 5.3 unchanged. All changes remain **working proposals, not enacted constitutional text**.

### Proposed §5.1, first paragraph — Citizenship at Birth

A child is a citizen from birth if at least one parent is a citizen, if born within the Republic to a legal-resident parent, or if born within the Republic and otherwise stateless. Citizens acquire full citizenship rights, including voting and eligibility for office, at 18.

**History note:** Preserves descent through a citizen parent regardless of birthplace, while limiting birthright citizenship through a legal-resident parent to births within the Republic. Territorial statelessness protection is retained; note the substantive tradeoff that the revised birthright clause does not automatically protect an otherwise stateless child born abroad to noncitizen parents.

### Proposed §5.1, second paragraph — Naturalization and Service

A legal resident may earn citizenship upon meeting the legal qualifications and continuous residency period established by statute. Qualifying honorable military or national service reduces the required residency period as provided by statute. All naturalization pathways require the civic and constitutional proficiency assessment established under §5.1.b.

**History note:** Recognizes qualifying honorable military or civilian national service by shortening statutory residency rather than exempting an applicant from civic understanding. Retains legal-resident eligibility, legislative authority over service qualifications and the amount of reduction, and a universal assessment for naturalization. The substantive test rules have one home in new §5.1.b.

### §5.1, third paragraph — Revocation: retain unchanged

**Review note:** Preserve prohibition on punitive citizenship revocation, clear-and-convincing judicial fraud findings for revocation of naturalization, protection against statelessness, and protection of previously acquired derivative citizenship.

### Proposed §5.1, fourth paragraph — Derivative Status of Children

A child born before a parent acquired legal residency or citizenship who joins that parent in the Republic within the period defined by statute, which may not be less than five years, receives the parent's status at the time of joining.

**History note:** Clarifies that the child's derivative status follows the parent's status when the child joins, including if the parent has advanced from residency to citizenship. Retains the five-year statutory minimum joining window as a family-reunification safeguard.

### New §5.1.b — Civic and Constitutional Proficiency

The Republic shall maintain a civic and constitutional proficiency assessment administered by an independent agency under §3.9. The assessment must comply with §1.6 and may not evaluate political opinion or cultural loyalty. Its content and administration are governed by statute.

**History note:** Establishes one constitutional home for the citizenship assessment, referenced by every naturalization pathway. Applies §1.6 nondiscrimination and retains the separate bar against evaluating political opinion or cultural loyalty, neither of which is comprehensively covered by §1.6 alone. Details remain statutory. §5.1.a is already occupied by State and Territory Voting Citizenship; new §5.1.b avoids renumbering it.

**Integration note:** Article XIX references the civic proficiency assessment administrator during founding transition; confirm the statutory agency obligation remains coherent after the new §5.1.b is inserted. A child acquiring citizenship at birth is not required to pass the naturalization assessment.

### Retained Article V provisions

- **§5.1.a — State and Territory Voting Citizenship:** retain unchanged. Each citizen has exactly one State or Territory voting citizenship, no gap in affiliation, and statutory resolution of overlaps.
- **§5.2 — Legal Resident Rights:** retain unchanged.
- **§5.3 — Custodial Care of Children:** retain unchanged. The stay of removal until custodial arrangements are secured is an operative child-protection safeguard rather than dispensable administrative detail.

## Proposed amendment 8 — Article VI immigration and residency review (settled working wording)

- **Territorial admission audit:** Assembly first, as Territories have Assembly representation but not Senate representation. An affirmative determination of Statehood compliance requires a **majority of the full seated membership** of the deciding chamber. The same threshold applies following transfer to Senate.
- **Existing-State biennial audit:** Senate first, as Senate represents States. An affirmative determination of either compliance **or noncompliance** requires a **simple majority of votes cast with a quorum present**. The same standard applies upon transfer to Assembly.
- **Provisional status:** Separate from failed audits and initially decided by Senate. Requires three consecutive failed biennial audits, substantive judgment that deficiencies are serious and persistent, and affirmative **majority of full seated membership**. Not automatic from audit failures. Upon inaction transfer to Assembly, same substantive and voting requirements apply.
- **Inconclusive votes:** Each chamber devises its own tie/reconsideration rules, but completion requires an affirmative decision one way or the other. An inconclusive vote or failed motion is not an adverse determination; inability to determine by the deadline constitutes inaction.
- **Primary chamber:** acts within statutory deadline **not exceeding 90 days** after receipt of complete audit record. **Other chamber:** authority transfers **automatically and exclusively** upon failure to determine; acts within statutory deadline **not exceeding 90 days** after transfer. Original chamber cannot reclaim authority for that determination. Rejection/completed negative determination is not inaction; judicial review remains.
- **Both chambers inactive:** expedited judicial review; courts may enforce the *receiving* chamber's constitutional duty through appropriate remedies but cannot make the substantive determination themselves. Neither inactivity nor missed deadlines automatically approves/rejects audits, changes an existing State's status, or causes a Territorial proceeding to lapse. Inaction-based judicial compulsion as the *first* fallback was discussed then explicitly rejected in favor of automatic transfer.
- Each individual determination shall be recorded to NRS with reasons. **Every existing State** receives an affirmative legislative determination of its biennial audit, but the chamber may consider multiple audit reports together if each State receives a separately identifiable valid determination; no deemed administrative approval.

**Approved candidate language for deadlines:**

“The chamber responsible for a Statehood Audit determination shall reach a determination within the period established by statute, not exceeding 90 days after receipt of the completed audit record.

If authority transfers because of inaction, the receiving chamber shall reach a determination within the period established by statute, not exceeding 90 days after the transfer.

A failure by either chamber to act shall not cause a pending Statehood proceeding to lapse or alter an existing State's constitutional status. The provisions governing transfer of authority and judicial enforcement remain applicable.”

“Upon verification of the required petition signatures, the Legislature shall have 90 days to enact the proposed legislation.

If the Legislature does not enact the proposal within that period, the Elections Panel shall administer a national referendum on the proposal within the following 90 days.

**History:** Preserve first legislative opportunity (90d), default public ballot (another 90d) and 60% vote-cast approval for *enacting* citizen legislation; remove **50% participation floor** to eliminate abstention veto.

## Proposed amendment 17 — Article XIV military authority; §§2.2 and 12.2 conforming changes (approved framework; 2026-10-22)

### Document-wide editorial principle — Positive Enumeration of Authority (approved)

Constitutional institutions exercise **affirmatively granted powers** within their authorized purposes and conditions. Do not attempt to enumerate every prohibited misuse; keep specific prohibitions where independently necessary to protect rights or close real circumvention. This applies to the **whole Constitution**, complementary to **One Home Rule**, not as a new interpretive article. Distinguish public institutions (enumerated power) from citizens (liberties do not require affirmative enumerated permission). During final integration simplify redundant negative language, **without changing approved substantive safeguards**.

### §2.2 — Immediate defense, notification, planned operations, stand-down (approved framework)

- The Legat Consul (LC) may act immediately against active or imminent attacks upon Republic territory, citizens abroad, and treaty allies, **and continue operations necessary to repel or prevent such attacks**. This defensive authority can operate beyond Republic borders; whether an operation is defense turns on the **actual active or imminent threat and the operation's purposes**, not geography alone.
- Upon initiating military action, LC notifies **Civic Consul (CC) and the Speakers of both chambers without undue delay**, giving purposes and grounds. CC receives notice to perform its **domestic role**, not to authorize or command operations. This replaces 24-hour notice and other awkward fixed response periods.
- With **deliberate speed**, LC gives the **Assembly Speaker** a proposed operational timeline and budget for planned action, allowing appropriation proceedings to begin. For action **beyond defensive measures**, an **authorizing law** is required, supported by a proposed plan of action and budget; the **Legislature** authorizes, while each chamber has its distinct review task. Treaty authorization is a separately recognized form of binding law, see §14.4.
- Senate may **by majority vote** determine active defense no longer justified by active or imminent threat and order **return to alert posture**. LC retains military command and can respond to a **new or renewed** active or imminent attack. Alert posture preserves preparedness rather than amounting to unconditional withdrawal.
- LC commands forces and determines strategy, tactics, methods within constitutional defense or lawfully authorized purposes and scope. Do not duplicate broad lists of forbidden missions.
- Supersede the previously registered Article II amendment 3's remaining general authorization-expiry formula **as applicable**; the new agreed scheme does not mandate fixed periodic legislative renewals. Preserve constitutional scope, affirmative authorization for nondefensive operations, and Senate powers. **Draft a unified final §2.2 only on integration**; proposals here supersede inconsistent previously approved §2.2 wording.

**Approved stand-down wording (working draft):**

“The Senate may, by majority vote, direct that defensive military operations return to an alert posture where continued active operations are no longer justified by an active or imminent threat.

Such a determination terminates authority for continued active defensive operations but does not restrict the Legat Consul's authority to respond immediately to a new or renewed active or imminent attack.”

**Approved defense boundary (working draft):**

“The Legat Consul may conduct military operations necessary to repel or prevent an active or imminent attack on the Republic's territory, citizens, or treaty allies.

Defensive authority does not extend to military operations pursuing objectives beyond repelling or preventing such attacks. Those operations require authorization by law under §14.1.”

As a result of later adoption of positive enumeration, **retain the substantive boundary but simplify redundant negative formulations**.

### §14.1 — Authorized purposes (retain and reconcile)

Preserve the current two purposes: defense of Republic territory, citizens and treaty allies; and legislative authorization on specific grounds in response to crimes against humanity defined under international law. Use positive-grant drafting. Existing final sentence limiting Supreme Court review to the **stated** purpose is identified as a potential conflict with enforcing actual constitutional limits and treaty-trigger review; **no final replacement judicial-review standard was adopted**. Do not automatically install the previously proposed “reasonable factual basis” formula (explicitly withdrawn).

Material changes in operations/circumstances reported to Senate, which may require **updated plans**; genuine expansion of statutory purpose/scope requires further law, not merely new tactics.

“A law authorizing military operations shall establish their purposes and scope.

The Legat Consul shall conduct those operations within the authorization and report material changes in their conduct or circumstances to the Senate.

The Senate may require the Legat Consul to submit an updated operational plan for its review. Operations requiring an expansion of the purposes or scope established by law shall be submitted for further legislative authorization.”

“The Legat Consul shall report to the Senate on the conduct, progress, and continuing objectives of military operations authorized by law.

“Expiration or withdrawal of authorization or appropriations for a military operation terminates authority to pursue its objectives.

Upon integration, simplify the final prohibition by positive enumeration **while preserving the substantive constraint**.

“A law authorizing military operations shall provide for the orderly withdrawal of deployed personnel and the fulfillment of obligations arising from the operation.

### §14.4 — Treaty-based authorization (approved)

**Approved working text:**

**History:** Treaties are law of the land; an action actually **required** by a duly ratified treaty needs no duplicative authorization statute, but treaty cannot authorize what Constitution disallows or authorize action exceeding its terms. Upon completion of federal service, command of State forces returns to the respective State.”

**History:** State consent not required for immediate external defense; normal State command and civilian roles are retained, other federal service requires law, defensive necessity bounds federal control, and Senate alert-posture decision applies equally to defensive operations using called-up State forces. ## Proposed amendment 19 — Article XVII constitutional amendment process (approved; 2026-10-22)

**Status:** Working proposals approved section by section and integrated stress-tested. **Not enacted.** This register entry does not amend the adopted Constitution or website.

### §17.1(1) — Amendment paths (retain unchanged)

Retain parliamentary and citizen initiative routes, and publication of each legislative member's amendment vote to the NRS.

### §17.1(2) — Parliamentary amendment and ratification (approved)

**Approved working replacement (§17.1(2), in combination with later approved deletion of turnout floor):**

“A parliamentary amendment requires approval by two-thirds of the full seated membership of both legislative chambers.

The proposing body shall specify whether the amendment proceeds through State Ratification or Popular Ratification and establish a definite period for ratification in accordance with statute.

The proposing body must set a **definite period** in accordance with statute at proposal time, which cannot be changed mid-ratification. The originally proposed draft containing 55% turnout was **superseded** by the later, separately adopted popular-ratification wording: “Popular Ratification requires approval by two-thirds of votes cast in a national referendum.” The finite ratification period must remain compatible with the Judicial Monitor review opportunity; implementing statute can arrange the timetable.

### §17.1(3) — Citizen Initiative Amendments (approved)

“A Citizen Initiative Amendment proceeds through the two-phase petition process established under §13.1, without the filing deadline applicable to optional referendums. Phase Two requires signatures from 15% of eligible voters nationally.

Upon verification of the required signatures, the Elections Panel shall administer a national referendum within 180 days. The amendment is ratified upon certification of approval by 70% of votes cast.

An amendment rejected by the voters may not be reinitiated in substantially the same form within five years, unless materially changed.”

**History:** Keep 15% signature threshold, 70% ratification vote and **180-day referendum guarantee** as an anti-obstruction constraint protecting citizens who completed demanding petitions; remove 65% turnout. Five-year repeat bar applies only to proposals **rejected by voters**, not petitions that fail to qualify, and exempts materially changed proposals. Citizen route bypasses ordinary legislative approval.

Retain existing enumerated protected constitutional categories: Monitor selection/independence, information-only mandate/non-dependency, and funding; NRS architecture/permanence; independent Elections Panel and NRS Panel; designation and protection of non-derogable rights; reversion to States/Territories; and this subsection itself. Preserve mandatory **Popular Ratification** regardless of amendment origin, with citizen-initiative referendum satisfying that requirement. Update cross-section numbers only during final consolidation; do not add categories incidentally.

“An amendment that modifies the substance or legal effect of a provision protected under this subsection requires Popular Ratification, regardless of where the amendment is placed in this Constitution.”

**History:** Section location cannot be used to evade required direct voter ratification. E.g., insertion into an unprotected article granting dismissal authority over an independent Elections Panel still substantively affects protected §11.1, so State Ratification alone is insufficient. ### §17.2 — No executive veto; immediate effectiveness (retain unchanged)

Neither Consul has an amendment veto/signature; certification by LM for State Ratification or Elections Panel otherwise, plus NRS publication, determines effectiveness. Officials do not obtain a policy veto by withholding certification.

### §17.3(1) — Amendment consistency (approved)

“A proposed constitutional amendment shall identify the provisions it repeals or modifies.

An amendment ratified in accordance with this Constitution shall be interpreted consistently with existing provisions wherever reasonably possible. Where an irreconcilable conflict exists, the later amendment governs to the extent of the conflict, subject to the protections established by §17.3(3).”

**History and rejected alternative:** The preexisting strict invalidation of an amendment for omission of express repeal/modification could defeat a duly ratified constitutional change through a drafting omission. Require identification and consistent interpretation first; let later lawful amendment prevail on genuine irreconcilable conflict, while preserving the non-derogable rights floor and mandatory Popular Ratification procedural requirements. Users explicitly noted this trade-off as difficult and chose the recommendation. Avoid using this to implicitly bypass §17.1(4).

### §17.3(2) — Judicial Monitor consistency analysis (approved)

“The JM shall examine each proposed constitutional amendment for consistency with this Constitution and publish its findings to the NRS.

The JM shall have 30 days following parliamentary approval or verification of a citizen initiative's required petition signatures to publish its findings.

Ratification may proceed upon publication of the findings or expiration of that period, whichever occurs first.”

**History:** Mandated independent **information-only** JM analysis, without JM control/veto; a concrete 30-day non-obstruction deadline is necessary constitutional machinery. Align available ratification window with report window through statute/administrative procedures; no need for extra clocks.

### §17.3(3) — Judicial review and entrenched rights (approved)

“The Supreme Court shall adjudicate challenges to the constitutional validity of ratified amendments on petition of any person with standing.

The Court may determine whether an amendment satisfied the requirements for ratification and whether it extinguishes a right designated non-derogable under §1.16.a.

An amendment that extinguishes such a right is void to the extent of the extinguishment.”

**History:** Courts verify all legally required ratification criteria including §17.1(4) mandatory Popular Ratification; they do **not** gain substantive policy veto over properly ratified amendments. Preserve enforceable entrenched non-derogable floor.

### §17.3(4) — Prospective operation (retain unchanged)

Past lawful actions stay lawful; specialized transition of offices, terms, rights, contracts or obligations must be specified where needed in the relevant amendment itself rather than in elaborate generic transitional code.

### §17.3(5) — Withdrawal of proposed amendment (approved)

“A proposed constitutional amendment may be withdrawn by its proposing authority before submission for ratification.

Upon submission for ratification, the amendment shall proceed under the applicable constitutional process until ratified or rejected.”

**History:** Withdrawal is part of the proposer’s authority until the proposal transfers to State or popular ratifiers; Parliament or petition sponsors cannot cancel a proposal after submission. Read “rejected” with the finite expiration of parliamentary ratification period, which terminates unsuccessfully without separate ratifier action.

### §17.4 — Supremacy of Constitution (retain unchanged)

The Constitution is superior to inconsistent legislative, executive, treaty and administrative action; explicit void-to-inconsistency rule is substantive hierarchy, not a redundant list of specific prohibitions.

1. Two-thirds of both chambers propose; States refuse two-thirds approval in fixed period → no amendment; no government suspension (**pass**).
2. Popular Ratification 75% yes of votes cast with 40% turnout → passes revised 2/3 approval, no turnout veto (**pass**).

### §18.1 — Use Doctrine and NRS registration (approved)

**History:** Constitutionalize host relationship and functional federal independence rather than requiring competitive bidding, published minimum criteria, fixed 30-day Supreme Court challenge window, or a special case procedure. **History:** Termination arises from end of *function*, not belated publication, and cannot be prolonged by speculative future demand. Remove fixed 90-day publication and statutory reversion window. ### §18.4(1) — National Trust as public dedication (approved)

“Land designated to the National Trust is held for the permanent benefit and use of the people of the Republic.

Designation requires the consent of the State in which the land is situated and does not alter that State's territorial sovereignty.

Management may be exercised by the State, the Republic, or jointly, under arrangements established in accordance with law.

Land remains protected by the National Trust until its designation is removed under §18.4(2).”

**History:** Substantially revises old provision suggesting National Trust “holds all federal land.” National Trust is an enduring *public guarantee/dedication to the citizenry*, not an automatic federal acquisition or territorial sovereignty. State, Republic or joint managers may administer; designation survives any management change or State's subsequent changed opinion. Operational Use Right and National Trust designation are distinct constitutional relationships.

### §18.4(2) — Designation and removal (approved)

“Designation of land to the National Trust requires approval by two-thirds of both legislative chambers and the consent of the host State.

Removal of land from the National Trust requires approval by two-thirds of both legislative chambers and ratification by 60% of votes cast in a national referendum administered by the Elections Panel.

Where land is subject to an international conservation agreement, withdrawal from that agreement requires approval by two-thirds of the Senate before the removal process proceeds.”

**History:** Preserve high entry/exit thresholds, popular final decision and conservation agreement treatment. Remove 50% referendum participation floor (abstention veto) and constitutional 180-day referendum administration period (statute governs timetable). For Territories, “host State consent” is replaced by special §18.5 compact/statute rule; reconcile cross-reference at integration.

### §18.4(3) — Stewardship (approved concise wording)

“Land designated to the National Trust shall be maintained for its designated public purposes, preserving ecological integrity and public accessibility.”

**History:** Do **not** duplicate the roles of State/Republic/joint manager or EM's existing monitoring mandate. The responsible governments can handle implementation. Preserve stewardship guarantee without overengineering.

### §18.4(4) — Delete (approved)

Delete separate express authorization for State/Indigenous co-management with Civic Consul. Management is covered in §18.4(1), with Indigenous nations' capacity to agree under applicable constitutional/ordinary law. **Do not interpret deletion as barring Indigenous co-management**.

### §18.5 — Territory procedures (approved)

Delete redundant former §18.5(1), repeating §18.1/§18.3 application to Territories. Replace old §18.5(2) with:

“Designation of land within a Territory to the National Trust shall be governed by its Territorial Compact or, where the Compact is silent, by statute, subject to the requirements of §18.4.”

**History:** Compact first; statutory participation where silent; national two-thirds legislative approval remains. State consent under §18.4(2) belongs to States; Territories governed by compact or statute rather than former written-position/60-day default mechanism. Be careful not to read §18.4 “State consent” as an accidental Territorial veto.

### Integrated stress tests (approved)

1. Federal research facility closes; speculative possible future use cannot preserve use right; automatic reversion or new mutually agreed arrangement — **pass**.
2. Lawful courthouse use right omitted from NRS; judicially compel registration, no automatic disruption; no enlargement beyond registered bounds — **pass**.
3. Host attempts to tax or obstruct national constitutional operations; exclusive functional authority inside designated capital footprint protects operations while host retains territorial sovereignty — **pass**.
4. State later wants Trust land for development; original consent's revocation insufficient, must complete two-thirds legislative approval and 60%-of-votes-cast national removal referendum — **pass**.
5. Federal-to-State or joint management change; National Trust public dedication, ecological/access guarantees persist — **pass**.
6. Territory disputes Trust designation; Territorial Compact governs, or statute if silent, always with constitutional national designation approval — **pass**.
7. Cross-article sovereignty test: National Trust dedication benefits all Republic people without transferring State territory; distinguish land dedication from federal Operational Use Rights — **pass**.

**Integration cautions:** Reconcile §18.4(2) host State consent with §18.5 Territorial procedure; do not conflate dedication with federal title or territorial sovereignty. Uphold §18.1 registration accountability and §18.3 enforceability without creating a documentary veto over otherwise lawful federal constitutional operations. Apply **One Home Rule**, **Positive Enumeration of Authority**, and **Constitutional Necessity**: define what must be guaranteed, leave ordinary management, EM monitoring detail, and administrative calendars to competent institutions and statute. Next review: Article XIX.

## Proposed amendment 21 — Article XIX Transition and Ratification (approved; 2026-10-23)

**Status:** Working revisions approved section by section and integrated stress test accepted; **not enacted**. Preserve adopted Constitution and public site. Consolidate provisions using One Home Rule, Constitutional Necessity and Positive Enumeration. Routine drafting, obsolete cross-references and duplication can be corrected during consolidation without individually revisiting policy; substantive changes require a new decision.

**Governing design decision — the light-switch principle:** Every Federated Republic institution assumes its constitutional responsibilities **individually when constitutionally capable**. The corresponding predecessor or temporary authority transfers immediately by constitutional operation; no general simultaneous activation, rigid phase order or universal 24-month Transition Window. Institutions **need not** depend on each other's readiness, except where exercising a particular constitutional function necessarily requires another. "Need not" is deliberately permissive: coordination is allowed; "shall not" or "shall generally not" was expressly rejected. Founding arrangements provide implementation planning and continuity, not permission to delay an already capable constitutional successor.

### §19.1 — Ratification and founding accommodation

* Retain interim Elections Panel certification that the NVS is operational under §11.2 and founding ratification by **60% of votes cast**. **Delete 50% participation requirement**; abstention is not a veto.
* Retain **unchanged** treaty-of-union sentence: "Where multiple sovereign nations simultaneously pool sovereignty through a treaty of union, transition provisions adapt as specified in the treaty of union, subject to the constitutional principles of this Article."
* Replace predecessor-institution accommodation text and remove two-year deadline: "Where existing institutions overlap with institutions established by this Constitution, the Legislature shall determine their relationship by statute.

Existing institutions shall retain their lawful standing until their relationship with the Republic's institutions is established in accordance with this Constitution."
* Reconcile this standing protection with §19.2: no predecessor may keep constitutional authority after the corresponding successor becomes capable.

### §19.2 — Day Zero and independent transfers

§19.2(1) — Day Zero remains when ratification is certified. Laws inconsistent with Individual Sovereignty Floor void immediately; predecessor national government's public assets, liabilities, rights and obligations vest in Republic, subject to Constitution; authoritative records transfer or register to NRS according to law. **Remove 24-month Transition Window.** Founding arrangements establish a definite implementation schedule but **not** an all-institution activation deadline. Initial proposed wording (adapt last sentence to adopted no-window principle during consolidation):

"Day Zero is the date on which constitutional ratification is certified.

Upon Day Zero, laws inconsistent with the Individual Sovereignty Floor are void, and the predecessor national government's public assets, liabilities, rights, and obligations vest in the Republic, subject to this Constitution. Authoritative predecessor records shall be transferred or registered to the NRS in accordance with law.

The founding arrangements submitted for ratification shall establish a definite Transition Window and an implementation schedule consistent with this Article."

**IMPORTANT:** The quoted final paragraph was superseded by the user's later explicit agreement to **eliminate the universal Transition Window**. Retain an implementation schedule in founding arrangements, **not** a definite Transition Window.

§19.2(2) — Replace numbered phases (0–4, Days 1–90, 91–270, 271–450) with user-approved wording:

"The institutions of the Republic shall assume their constitutional authority individually as each becomes capable of exercising its assigned functions.

Upon such capability, the corresponding authority of predecessor or temporary institutions transfers immediately by constitutional operation.

The establishment or readiness of one institution **need not** depend upon the establishment or readiness of another, except where the exercise of its constitutional functions necessarily requires it.

Founding arrangements shall provide for the establishment of constitutional institutions and the continuity of government during the transition."

### §19.3 — Caretakers and independent monitoring

(1) "Officials and institutions of the predecessor national government may continue in caretaker capacity only as necessary to preserve lawful government, existing services, rights, obligations, and public safety until the corresponding institutions of the Republic become constitutionally capable of exercising those functions.

Existing State, territorial, Indigenous, and local governments continue under their own lawful authority except where this Constitution expressly provides otherwise."

(2) "A caretaker legislature may enact only measures reasonably necessary to preserve government continuity, constitutional rights, existing obligations, public safety, or the implementation of this Constitution.

Legislation enacted beyond this authority is void and subject to judicial review.

Legislation validly enacted during the transition remains subject to amendment or repeal by the Legislature of the Republic."

(3) "A joint Monitor panel shall independently monitor the constitutional transition and publish its findings to the NRS.

The interim Elections Panel shall establish the joint Monitor panel before ratification. Its composition and procedures shall be determined by the founding arrangements, with provision for participation by the Republic's Monitors as they become constitutionally operational.

The joint Monitor panel shall dissolve when all predecessor and temporary authorities exercising transitional constitutional functions have been replaced by their corresponding institutions of the Republic."

Monitors **only independently monitor and report**, not direct or certify government operations.

### §19.3.a — Caretaker defense

(1) "Until the institutions of the Republic responsible for national defense become constitutionally capable of exercising their respective functions, predecessor national institutions may exercise only the military and defense authority reasonably necessary to defend the Republic, its territory, population, and forces against actual or imminent invasion or armed attack, or to fulfill military obligations arising from inherited treaties that remain binding under §19.5(4)." Initial proposal separately repeated successor-transfer rule; **omit repetitive transfer sentence** because §19.2 controls.

(2) "Predecessor military command arrangements, defense appropriations, and military authorities remain provisionally effective only to the extent necessary to exercise the defensive authority permitted under subsection (1).

New commitments may be undertaken only where reasonably necessary to conduct or sustain such authorized defensive action.

All continuing arrangements and commitments remain subject to alteration or termination by the competent constitutional authorities of the Republic as they assume their respective functions."

(3) "Until the Legislature of the Republic becomes constitutionally capable of exercising its authority, a predecessor national authority exercising caretaker defense command under subsection (1) may call State forces into federal service without the legislative authorization otherwise required by §14.5, but only to the extent reasonably necessary to defend against invasion or armed attack."

(4) **Retain unchanged:** "Defensive action under subsection (1) may occur outside the Republic's territory where reasonably necessary to repel, disrupt, or terminate the invasion or armed attack."

(5) "The exercise of caretaker defense authority under this section, and its constitutional basis, shall be published to the NRS as soon as operationally practicable.

Operational information may be classified only in accordance with Article X.

The joint Monitor panel shall have access to records necessary to assess compliance with this section and shall report its findings in accordance with §19.3(3)."

(6) "A military operation commenced under caretaker authority may continue under its transitional authorization for no more than **60 days** after the Legislature becomes constitutionally capable of exercising its authority, unless the Legislature authorizes its continuation under §2.2 or directs its cessation.

Within **14 days** of assuming office, the Legat Consul shall submit such an operation for legislative authorization or begin its orderly cessation.

Defense appropriations preserved under subsection (2) remain available for authorized continuing operations until superseded by lawful appropriation."

Two different triggers are deliberate. The Legat Consul and Legislature can activate on different days. The 14-/60-day substantive emergency-power limits survive removal of administrative phase deadlines.

### §19.4 — Predecessor classified material

"Predecessor classified material is subject to Article X from Day Zero.

The Legislature shall establish an independent mechanism to review predecessor classified material under Article X. Until that mechanism becomes operational, a lawful temporary mechanism shall exercise the review authority necessary to preserve constitutional compliance and access to review.

Material that does not satisfy Article X shall be declassified and published to the NRS.

Any Inhabitant may obtain judicial review of a determination concerning predecessor classified material."

Remove 90-day Phase 4 deadline; general judicial review rather than exclusive Supreme Court original review.

### §19.5 — Pre-existing obligations

(1) **Delete** redundant military-operations provision; governed exclusively by §19.3.a. No grandfathering of unauthorized offensive campaigns.

(2) "Pre-existing social insurance programs shall continue without interruption during the constitutional transition, subject to subsequent legislation enacted under this Constitution."

(3) **Delete** separate military/national-service benefit clause and budget-cycle deadline; §19.2 assumes **all** lawful predecessor liabilities and obligations and §19.3 preserves their administration.

(4) "The Republic succeeds to the predecessor government's international legal relationships, including its treaties, financial commitments, and contractual obligations.

Where an inherited international obligation conflicts with this Constitution, the Republic shall seek its renegotiation or lawful termination in accordance with §3.6.

Pending resolution, the Republic shall honor the obligation to the extent required by international law, without requiring any constitutional officer to violate Article I or any non-derogable constitutional right."

(5) "From Day Zero, an independent judicial forum shall remain continuously available to enforce Article I.

A temporary judicial institution may exercise only the authority necessary to preserve constitutional rights until the corresponding judiciary of the Republic becomes constitutionally capable of exercising that jurisdiction."

### §19.6 — Founding States and Territories

(1) "A political entity holding recognized State or equivalent constitutional status before ratification that elects to enter the Republic as a State shall be recognized as a State from Day Zero, with all rights and responsibilities established by this Constitution."

(2) "Predecessor territories that do not enter the Republic as States shall be recognized as Territories from Day Zero.

Predecessor capital districts and special administrative regions shall likewise be recognized as Territories, subject to the provisions of Article XV."

(3) **Delete** special founding Statehood Audit and Phase 2 deadline; ordinary Article XV process applies once competent constitutional institution ready.

(4) "A predecessor political entity may elect to enter the Republic as a Territory by a majority vote of its existing legislative body, regardless of its eligibility for Statehood."

(5) "Before entry into the Republic, a predecessor State or Territory may voluntarily subdivide in accordance with its lawful procedures.

The resulting entities shall be considered separately for admission under this Constitution."

(6) **Delete** special first post-Phase-4 biennial Statehood Audit rule; Article XV already applies to founding States.

* Initial **Candidate Registers prepared by interim Elections Panel**, not joint Monitor panel. First elections subject to relevant eligibility provisions; independent panel oversight.
* "The founding register of recognized Indigenous nations shall be established from the authoritative predecessor records and published to the NRS.

The compilation is ministerial and shall not alter the recognition or standing of any Indigenous nation."
  Administrative responsibility via founding arrangements; eliminate Phase 1 deadline; no new discretion to revisit prior recognition.
* "Until the Monetary Authority becomes constitutionally capable of exercising its functions, a lawful temporary institution may perform the certification functions necessary to preserve monetary continuity.

The Monetary Authority shall be constituted in accordance with §12.1.a."
  No operational certification authority for joint Monitor panel; transfer rule in §19.2.

### §19.8 — Transitional Accountability Mechanism

"Where documented violations by a predecessor government warrant transitional accountability, the Legislature may establish a temporary, non-criminal Transitional Accountability Mechanism by a two-thirds vote of both chambers.

The establishing statute shall define the mechanism's jurisdiction, powers, procedures, and duration. Any expansion of its jurisdiction or powers requires the same legislative approval.

The mechanism shall operate consistently with Article I and shall terminate upon expiration of its statutory mandate."

Remove arbitrary ten-year maximum and ten-year prohibition on new mechanism; retain statutory sunset, two-thirds approval and Article I.

(1) "Where this Constitution requires the establishment of a permanent institution to exercise a constitutional function, the responsible authorities shall provide for its establishment in accordance with law." The initially drafted second sentence referring to continuity via temporary arrangements is **substantively approved** but may be omitted under One Home Rule because (2) covers it. ## Proposed amendment 22 — Article XX Associated Communities (approved; 2026-10-23)

**Status:** Section-by-section working decisions approved; integrated stress test passed following clarification of the Article XVI founding-period cross-reference. **Not enacted; do not change adopted Constitution or website.**

**Underlying distinction:** Associated Communities are fully sovereign and **outside** the Republic, related by consensual compact; Territories are within it. Sovereign equality, choice of domestic governance, non-derogable rights, reciprocal commitments, independent arbitration, and voluntary exit are constitutional principles. Operational representation, review timetables and exit notice periods belong to the compact.

### §20.1 — Nature of status

**Retain unchanged.** Available to recognized Indigenous nations under Article XVI and independent nations outside the Republic.

### §20.2 — Equal sovereign parties and compact-defined representation

"The Republic and an Associated Community are equal sovereign parties to their compact.

The compact may establish representation, consultation, and participation arrangements between the parties.

The Republic may not require an Associated Community to adopt a particular form of government as a condition of association."

**History:** Remove automatic constitutional observer speech/attendance at Assembly and Senate proceedings; these opportunities can be agreed in individual compacts. Do not prescribe an Associated Community's government.

### §20.3 — Compact conditions and enforcement

(1) **Retain unchanged:** Prohibit entry/ratification with entities whose governance practices include, authorize or systematically permit violations of §1.16.a non-derogable rights.

(2) "Allegations of violations of §1.16.a arising under an association compact shall be subject to review through the compact's independent arbitration mechanism.

The compact may be suspended upon a preliminary determination by that mechanism that the violations meet the standard established in subsection (1).

The compact shall provide procedures for urgent review and resolution of such disputes."

**History:** Remove suspension immediately upon a party's allegation or JM finding, which permitted politically motivated interruption. JM may independently monitor/report/refer but **cannot suspend** a compact. Arbitration must make the preliminary suspension determination. Remove constitutional 90-day preliminary decision clock and automatic lifting deadline; urgent review procedures belong to compact.

(3) **Retain unchanged:** Independent arbitration panel may terminate a compact for confirmed violations that remain unremedied for the period stated by compact; termination discretionary, not automatic.

(4) **Retain unchanged:** Existing governance relationships of Indigenous nations inside an Associated Community must be addressed and must not be eliminated or diminished by association compact.

(5) "The effects of a compact's suspension shall be governed by its terms, without prejudice to obligations independently binding upon either party under international law."

**History:** No universal suspension of observer privileges (observer provisions now negotiated); compact specifies suspended/continuing privileges and obligations, but cannot extinguish independent international commitments.

### §20.4 — Sovereign mobility

"An Associated Community may terminate its compact through its own democratic or traditional governance process. The Republic shall neither prevent nor penalize that decision.

Termination shall take effect in accordance with the compact's agreed procedures, upon written notice registered to the NRS.

The termination procedures shall not permit either party to compel the continued association of an unwilling sovereign community."

**History:** Remove one-size-fits-all minimum 12-month notice and obsolete withdrawal-of-notice mechanics; procedures negotiated but may not obstruct continuing sovereign right to leave.

### §20.5 — Reciprocity, arbitration, review

"Each compact shall define the reciprocal obligations of the parties and establish an independent arbitration mechanism.

Either party may request a review of the compact by written notice registered to the NRS. Review procedures shall be established by the compact."

**History:** Delete mandatory regular review schedule; request for review does not itself authorize unilateral amendment.

### §20.6 — Negotiation and ratification

"The Legat Consul negotiates and concludes compacts with Associated Communities, subject to Senate ratification under §3.6."

**History:** Remove mandatory Civic Consul consultation and advisory response transmission, without forbidding ordinary coordination. Maintain Article III treaty powers and two-thirds full-seated Senate threshold.

### §20.7 — Continuity

"An association compact remains binding upon the Republic notwithstanding a change of government.

Amendment or renegotiation of the compact shall proceed in accordance with its terms and the applicable constitutional treaty procedures."

**History:** Prevent a government from unilaterally disregarding commitments without suggesting compacts supersede later constitutional changes. International law and treaty procedures govern conflicts.

### Integrated test (approved)

1. New Legat Consul wants to disregard predecessor's compact — **pass**, remains binding and procedures apply.
2. Unsupported rights accusation — **pass**, JM/party allegations lead to independent review, not automatic suspension.
3. Confirmed and unremedied systematic §1.16.a violations — **pass**, arbitration may terminate.
4. Community voluntarily withdraws — **pass**, no coercion or penalty, compacts establish administration.
5. Parties disagree on performance — **pass**, independent arbitration and mutually accessible review.
6. Indigenous governance arrangements inside Associated Community — **pass**, §20.3(4) preserves.

The recognized Indigenous nation **always possesses sovereign self-determination and complete independence**, with an Associated Community relationship as the *founding default*, never presumed assent to specific unagreed compact terms. **Founding is distinguished by immediate/expedited admission** under ratified founding arrangements; when that opportunity closes, any independent nation (including an Indigenous nation) may pursue ordinary admission processes. Do **not** convert the founding admission procedure into a permanent special fast-track, nor make its expiry extinguish sovereignty or subsequent ordinary admission.

Remove only administrative deadlines, duplicate consultation machinery and unilateral power not supported by compact; preserve all distinct rights-based constraints.
