# Scenario format trial — Duty of Refusal

**Status:** Experimental editorial prototype; **not** canonical history, an approved site feature, or a proposed amendment.

## Original and replacement

- Original unchanged: `scenario-the-order.html` (Year 9 narrative with identified officer, intelligence chain, Monitor report and a fully resolved outcome).
- Alternative preview: `scenario-trial-duty-of-refusal.html` (no historical date, no fictional characters, no official event; stage-based pressure test of §2.8).

## What this trial changes

1. States an objective, test parameters, legal assumptions, controlled variables, exclusions, and success/failure criteria before applying pressure.
2. Changes one cooperation/enforcement assumption per stage.
3. Separates a **rule violated by an official** from a **mechanism that has failed**, and legal invalidity from practical enforcement.
4. Distinguishes demonstrated failure under stipulation from a blanket claim that the Constitution has no other remedy.
5. Analyzes a missing automatic fix without pretending its design rationale is documented.
6. Identifies the next test (mistaken good-faith refusal) and explicit limits.

## Editorial issues for review

- Does the four-stage format teach more than the original narrative?
- Should each stage state "observed", "inferred", and "stipulated" separately?
- Are the test results sufficiently adversarial, not merely illustrative?
- Should every test have one principal provision and a hard cap on supporting citations?
- Do we need structured front matter for an eventual test library? **Not in this trial.**
- Note that §6.2 itself is narrow; do not silently import extra removal procedure not present in its constitutional wording. Distinguish statutory removal proceedings and the §1.1 right of remedy.
- The §2.8 text has three sentences: no compliance with unlawful executive orders; lawful refusal creates no liability and any penalty is void; report to EM permitted. Some older narrative description refers to a fourth sentence. Do not propagate that claim.
