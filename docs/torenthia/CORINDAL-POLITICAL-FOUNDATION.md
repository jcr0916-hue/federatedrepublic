### Correct coalition arithmetic and the Lieutenant Governor

The estimate totals **51**. State §§3.4(f) and 3.5(c) require **26 affirmative State Senate member votes** for ordinary bill passage, calculated from authorized membership.

Each pairing of the three principal factions could meet that threshold **if its members agreed and voted together**:

- Restoration + District Representation: **19 + 18 = 37**.
- Restoration + Institutional Reform: **19 + 11 = 30**.
- District Representation + Institutional Reform: **18 + 11 = 29**.

The three unaligned members cannot independently give any single faction a majority: **19 + 3 = 22; 18 + 3 = 21; 11 + 3 = 14**. Ferrel's faction is influential but is not indispensable if the other two agree. No coalition, agreement on statutory text, or successful division is created by this arithmetic.

Under State §§2.4(b) and 3.4(a), Veth presides and votes only to break an actual tie. She cannot supply the 26th authorized-membership vote on a bill. A **25–26** division is a defeat, not a tie; a **25–25** division still lacks 26 affirmative member votes. Any procedural tie-break remains subject to the applicable rules and thresholds; this record invents no new voting rule.

## Redistricting authority and unresolved law

The [State Constitution](../../State%20Constitutions/corindal-state-constitution.md), especially **§3.2(c)**, controls:

- The **independent redistricting commission** draws State Senate districts following each decennial census, under published neutral criteria and independently of both the State Senate and Governor. Final plans are public and subject to judicial review.
- The **State Senate legislates** within its lawful competence, including the commission's statutory framework and funding. The published legislative deadlock concerns the changes needed to correct noncompliance; it does not make the Senate the map-drawing body or establish a vote to adopt a preferred map.
- The **Governor** may recommend legislation and exercise the ordinary bill-signing/veto powers (State §§2.3(g), 2.5), but cannot draw districts or order the commission to select a map.
- Judicial review remains available under State §§3.2(c) and 4.2(d). The Development Commission's §5.4(c) veto provision is distinct and must not be transposed to the redistricting commission.

**The precise statutory mechanism for a mid-cycle corrective redraw is not established.** Do not infer a particular existing remedial bill, trigger, commission appointment procedure, deadline, legislative map veto or gubernatorial power. A possible statutory explanation is not an enacted law. Leave this gap for separate central review; the political foundation does not resolve it.

A corrected map, remedial law or State election does not by itself restore ordinary federal status. Federal **§§15.1.a, 15.2 and 15.2.a** require two consecutive biennial audits determined compliant through the federal process; restoration then occurs automatically and the Elections Panel administers special elections for vacant federal Senate seats within 90 days.
