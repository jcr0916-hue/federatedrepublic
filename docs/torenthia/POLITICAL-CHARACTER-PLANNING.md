## 1. Governing principles

- Torenthia is a **large federal republic**, not a story involving only two national leaders. Its States, institutions, parties, civil servants, courts, reporters, legislators, and residents should have meaningful lives and conflicts of their own.
- Characters should appear **organically as needed**. Do not prepopulate exhaustive leader lists, invent official rosters, or make every minor appearance a planned national star.
- The constitutional framework, recorded events, and published World/NRS items govern what actually happened. **Plans and likely arcs never supersede canon.** Validate each new development against existing source material before publication.
- Federal and State storylines can evolve independently and eventually intersect. Not every story must connect to a national plot.
- Avoid rushing: **Elin Thoss is only a few fictional months into the Civic Consul role**. Character growth, political alliances, reputations, and conflicts should accumulate gradually.
- Allow people to misjudge, lose, compromise, recover, and sometimes disappear from coverage. Consequences of decisions should endure.
- Maintain the weekend break in routine Torenthia World/NRS news publication as agreed for the weekend of **October 31–November 1, 2026**. Research work and State historical tests are separate from current news releases.

## 2. Party framework — six significant national parties

The following six national parties are the **agreed editorial political landscape**. The descriptive political positions are planning characterization, not invented vote shares, official manifestos, or Assembly seat counts.

| Party | Abbreviation | Broad identity | Storytelling distinctions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Liberal Reform Party** | **LRP** | Broad center; market liberal, fiscally cautious, institutionally reformist | Contains meaningful conservative and progressive factions. Thoss is associated with its **conservative wing**; affiliation does not establish that she leads the party. |
| **Civic Renewal Movement** | **CRM** | Broad center; technocratic and institutional reform, transparency, professional administration | Likely to compete and cooperate with the LRP, especially around institutions and governance. Its members need not share one view on policy. |
| **Social Democratic Alliance** | **SDA** | Center-left; public services, labor and social protections | Potential policy partner or critic of Thoss, depending on issue and actual Assembly dynamics. |
| **National Republican Party** | **NRP** | Center-right; fiscal discipline, traditional civic conservatism, security | May support or oppose parts of an LRP-led governing program; avoid treating it as automatically interchangeable with the LRP conservative wing. |
| **Workers' Solidarity Party** | **WSP** | Democratic socialism; worker participation and cooperatives | Can bring labor, worker-ownership, and organizational questions into local and national stories. |
| **Green and Regional Alliance** | **GRA** | Ecology, conservation, regional autonomy | Particularly relevant to territorial/environmental and State-federal tensions. |

**Relative electoral prominence:** The **LRP and CRM are generally the two largest vote-getting national parties**, but **no numerical party vote shares, seat distributions, or polling baselines have been established** by this planning. Smaller regional parties and independents also exist and should not be erased.

**Regional tendency, not deterministic classification:** **Varek likely leans GRA**, in part because of the Argent Ridge/Riverglow conservation debate. This does *not* mean everyone in Varek supports GRA or that any result has been determined.

**Do not silently promote planning into canon:** When introducing a party name or profile in published news, do so in a plausible context, verify compatibility with any earlier coverage, and update the Story Bible/Status as appropriate.

## 3. Elin Thoss — Civic Consul

### Established position and present situation

- Elin Thoss is the **Civic Consul**, selected by the **Assembly**, **not** directly elected to that office by a national popular ballot.
- She is affiliated with the **conservative wing of the LRP**. She need not be the formal leader of the LRP.
- She is **a few fictional months into her tenure** at this point in the storyline. Resist the temptation to narrate her eventual legacy as if it already exists.
- Her original governing arrangement was broadly LRP + CRM, with support from some center-right/NRP-aligned representatives. The **274** associated with her original Assembly installation is a **historical installation count**. The subsequent **266** was a **dated political estimate of working coalition support**, **not** a live vote or her popularity rating; check primary published records before reusing these counts in new stories.
- The published Stats ledger (`_data/stats.js`) carries **Thoss public approval at 43%**. This is an existing fictional approval indicator, not a newly commissioned poll with established fieldwork details. It is **not** party preference, legislative support, a probability of reelection, or a confidence-vote result. Do not invent retrospective methodology; any future authored poll must identify its fictional fieldwork and methodology.
- The Assembly has **500 seats**; a successful constructive no-confidence motion must **name an eligible serving-member successor** and obtain an **absolute majority of the full seated Assembly (251 when all 500 seats are filled)**. Vacancies affect that denominator. A general election alone does **not** automatically remove the Civic Consul.
- Her existing published priorities include a fiscal equalization committee/report process, housing, backlogs in social-state administration, merit-based appointments, clearer regulations, and an orderly budget. **The fiscal bill / fiscal equalization question is a significant potential accomplishment**; distinguish legislation actually enacted from legislation still being considered.
- **Korda** provides another consequential policy and institutional test that she can speak to. Korda's Northern Lake settlement and affected-electorate issues have specific pending steps: do **not** invent a concluded referendum, approval, or settled outcome.
- The author-approved intention is that she will **at some point nominate Herbert Sole to the Supreme Court (SC)**. This is an **author-approved future story intention**, **not yet a published appointment, nomination date, confirmation, or predetermined outcome**. Preserve constitutional appointment procedure and allow legitimate institutional complications. Section 4.4 requires a qualified Judicial Pool nominee under §9.8, a vacancy and Senate confirmation; no individual eligibility, Pool admission, vacancy or confirmation is inferred from this intention.
- Her published interview with Petra Vend included the remark **“The people choose my bosses, not me.”** Respect her approach of not using official government communications to campaign for her own party.

### Character logic — the quiet political realist

Thoss is a **quiet negotiator and pragmatic institutional realist**, rather than a naturally showy, personality-centered leader. She has recognizable fiscal and institutional commitments, but she is willing to negotiate across party lines to advance durable outcomes. She can let others receive public credit if an agreement produces results. Her effectiveness should appear **through decisions and relationships**, not through the narrator repeatedly announcing that she is skilled.

Her style carries genuine costs. She can misread a faction, lose a legislative contest, offend a necessary ally, compromise too far, or be underappreciated by the public. Low visibility is not equivalent to either political weakness or wisdom.

**Long horizon, not a promise:** The user imagines that she could serve **five years, perhaps eight if circumstances permit**, rather than being removed after an artificially brief three-year arc. These are editorial hopes for development, **not fixed terms, predictions, constitutional deadlines, or guaranteed outcomes**. Verify the legal term and succession framework independently from the governing Constitution before referencing duration in canon.

### Election and coalition direction

There is an **editorial possibility** that a future Assembly emerges **modestly to Thoss's left**, with more influence from the SDA, GRA, WSP, and progressive wings of CRM/LRP. Thoss might remain Civic Consul and negotiate a new, more center-left coalition—**perhaps emerging politically stronger by proving adaptable rather than merely clinging to office**. This is a possible narrative direction, **not a published election result, seat projection, or promise**. Do not predetermine voters' choices or manufacture polling to force it.

### Long-term weakness — the unused public voice

A future constitutional or national confrontation **may** require Thoss to use the public **“bully pulpit”**: appealing beyond parliamentary relationships to a national audience. Her habit of quiet, behind-the-scenes success may have left her without a devoted public audience. A major speech might be ignored, misunderstood, or greeted with indifference. **Do not force this event early**, and do not resolve it with a sudden transformation into a charismatic public celebrity. Her relationship with the public should evolve from accumulated public-facing experience, not a single cinematic moment.

The user wants to learn about developments **when the public in the story learns about them**, without advance plot outlines, foreshadowing announcements, or spoilers in progress summaries.

**Editorial instructions:**

- Preserve Rehn's and Corindal's established published identities and constitutional constraints by reviewing the latest Story Bible, Status, World stories, and Corindal State Constitution before drafting.
- Introduce developments in proportion to their apparent local significance; do not mark them as secret major arc setup in published text.

- Allow genuine contingency, setbacks, and multiple perspectives. The exact future events, timing, and outcomes are **not established in this document**.

## 6. Journalism, polling, and elections

Media outlets in Torenthia may conduct or commission election-season polling. Polls can track **Thoss approval**, **national party preferences across six parties**, **Legat Consul candidate preferences**, **local Assembly races**, or **policy questions**. The question, population, fieldwork date, sample size, method, treatment of undecided responses, weighting where relevant, and uncertainty or limitations must be specified. Publications can interpret the same poll differently according to their perspective, but **the underlying numbers must be internally coherent and not invented simply to validate a favored partisan narrative**.

A poll is **not** a certified election outcome, a seat count, or a constructive-confidence vote. There is **no approved six-party numerical national polling baseline yet**.

The **Legat Consul** is an office distinct from Civic Consul. Four qualified Legat Consul candidates were previously established: **Ines Carrow, Seren Mak, Tobias Vael, and Pell Sandris**. No official ballot order, candidate vote totals, official election date, or outcome should be manufactured. Assembly elections include distinct local State arrangements; coverage should keep those mechanisms clear. The intended dramatic narrative buildup in real-world late November/early December 2026 is a **publishing/storytelling horizon**, **not an in-world election date**.

## 7. State storytelling and editorial-agent architecture

Long-range goal: State storylines should have **real local autonomy** and sometimes intersect the national story organically. This is a substantial next stage, **not** approval to spawn many unsupervised agents or publish unreviewed content.

**Pilot approach:** Begin with **one State editorial agent**, evaluate whether it maintains a distinctive local voice, then consider additional contrasting States such as Varek, Merath, or Arvane. Each State agent requires a concise, maintained packet containing its Constitution, established history, political context, characters, and unresolved matters. A central editor holds continuity and publication responsibility.

**State-agency boundaries:** Agents may propose local stories and characters independently, but they must not change federal canon, publish to main without review, silently rewrite settled history, invent legal rules, or duplicate the authoritative story record. State impacts on federal storylines require central cross-checking.

**Website presentation:** The visitor should experience a **living, orderly republic**, not a jumble of pages or agent outputs. Use **one canonical source for each published article** with State, national, dossier/topic, and publication views linking back to that article. Reuse existing State-test profile and provision-callout mechanisms. Keep consistent site navigation/design while allowing States to feel culturally and politically distinctive. The public should see the fictional country, **not the underlying AI production machinery**.

**Story cadence:** Not every State needs a daily news item. Local developments can emerge slowly, and their consequences can matter long afterward. No artificial ticker or incessant activity merely to simulate dynamism.
