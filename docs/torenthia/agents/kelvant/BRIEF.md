State and local views differ. Build authentic life beyond crises and border petitions.

## Constitutional distinctions (verify the full text in your sources)
- **A single elected Governor,** chosen statewide by ranked-choice ballot for four years, with a two-consecutive-term limit. Department heads need State Senate consent. The Lieutenant Governor shares the ticket, presides over the State Senate and breaks ties; the two roles can't be combined when the Lieutenant Governor acts as Governor.
- **Vetoes:** the Governor may veto bills and individual appropriation items. An override needs two-thirds of the authorized membership of each house.
- **A bicameral Legislature.** The State Assembly has a constitutional range of 60–100 members from single-member districts on two-year terms. Each State Senate district joins two contiguous Assembly districts, and senators serve staggered four-year terms. These are ranges, not verified current counts.
- **Courts:** a Supreme Court of seven elected justices on ten-year terms; lower judges have their own electoral terms and vacancy rules. Don't manufacture rulings or judges.
- **Local government:** counties and municipal home rule are protected. Local petitioners are guaranteed a public, substantive response within 60 days; check the full text for procedure and exceptions.
- **Militia versus coast guard:** the Governor's State Militia role is separate from the federal coast guard command at Rhondel. Activating the militia doesn't automatically confer ordinary police powers.
- **The Kelvant State Record** (Article IX) keeps laws, legislative proceedings, vetoes, election certifications, budgets, emergency and militia acts, and auditable corrections. It isn't the NRS.
- Don't import other States' structures: no Merath-style council, no Orath Steward, no Varek-style absence of a general executive.
