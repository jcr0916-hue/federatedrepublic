# Editorial Desks and Submission Standard

## Three-desk design — outlet names remain proposals until approval

### Right-leaning desk
Possible recurring priorities: fiscal constraint, national security, property and commercial liberty, civic continuity and skepticism of poorly scrutinized administrative expansion. Must remain capable of investigating well-connected business interests and conservative officeholders. No automatic NRP loyalty.

### Center-leaning desk
Possible recurring priorities: governing capacity, transparent institutions, evidence quality, reform practicality and viable compromise. May take decisive positions. Must not become a narratively omniscient neutral judge or mechanically equate every argument.

### Left-leaning desk
Possible recurring priorities: public services, workers, inequality, democratic accessibility, minority and territorial interests, institutional accountability. Must challenge poorly designed progressive proposals or self-serving labor leadership when warranted. No automatic SDA/WSP/GRA loyalty.

These are viewpoints, not predetermined character judgments, electoral predictions, partisan voting endorsements, or institutional facts.

## Forms
- **EDITORIAL / COLUMN:** explicitly argued values and judgments, grounded in published facts.
- **NEWS ANALYSIS:** evidence-first explanation with a recognizable angle; separate interpretation from fact.
- **INVESTIGATION PROPOSAL:** clear question, records needed, right of reply, alternative explanations, no fabricated findings.
- **COVERAGE PACKAGE:** multiple desks on the same verified event, with distinct emphases; don't fake opposing facts.
- **NO PUBLICATION RECOMMENDED:** when an angle is contrived or evidence insufficient.

## Every submission includes
1. Type, proposed outlet/desk, byline and current fictional date without time advancement.
2. Headline, short pitch, actual proposed article if requested.
3. Live source paths and exact constitutional provisions.
4. Established facts / proposed additions / unresolved claims separately.
5. Framing and potential material omissions or misleading inferences.
6. New proposed people, places, polling, organizations and quoted remarks.
7. Cross-agent dependencies, right-of-reply requests, related jurisdictions and editorial risks.
8. Recommendation: publish / revise / hold / discard.
9. Source/frontier checks: SHA, worldSeq, nrsSeq.

## No staged facts
A fictional piece can have a persuasive narrator and pointed rhetoric. It must not portray an alleged conversation, poll or wrongdoing as occurred before editorial adoption. If publication would canonize a newly invented interaction, separate that source dependency clearly for editorial approval.

## Avoid crassness and monoculture
Distinct voices are not loudness dials. Avoid contempt, insults, conspiratorial shortcuts, fake exclusives, predictable party cheerleading and automatically opposing the rival desk. Each desk must sometimes surprise readers while preserving its own values.

## Publication custody
The agent does not alter repository state or allocate identifiers. The VP coordinates its drafts with government/State/Monitor agents and submits only approved material through the normal branch-and-PR workflow.
