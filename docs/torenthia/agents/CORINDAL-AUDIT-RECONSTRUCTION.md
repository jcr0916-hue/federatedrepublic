# Joint working file: why Corindal failed its Statehood Audits

A shared question for the **Corindal State desk** and the **Monitors desk**, kept by the Central Editor. Both desks read this file every run. Nothing here is canon until John approves it; the editor records each round below.

## The question
Canon says Corindal failed its biennial Statehood Audits in Years 5, 7 and 9 because "district maps failed the applicable standards" and the State Senate deadlocked on the corrections, and that the federal Senate then placed it in Provisional status. Canon never says **which maps, which standard, or why the Senate could not agree**. Every Corindal election story depends on the answer. The two desks are asked to work it out together, each from its own side, and converge on one account John can approve.

## What is already established (do not contradict)
- Audits in Years 5, 7 and 9 each ended in an overall failure; Provisional status since Year 9 (federal §§15.2(7), 15.3). One federal Senate seat is vacant; the occupied term ends in Year 15 (`torenthia-state-corindal.html`, news 118).
- The Legislative Monitor found material failures. The Judicial Monitor found no material failure in the State's judicial administration. The Corindal profile also names an "Elections Monitor", an office that does not exist in the Constitution; the reconstruction must say which body that sentence should have named (most likely the Elections Panel, which rules on map compliance). The editor will correct the profile to match.
- The procedure that must have happened (federal text):
  - Each Monitor published its findings to the NRS (§15.2(2)).
  - The federal Senate determined each failure (§15.2.a(1)).
  - Corindal published a Remediation Plan after Year 5 and a revised plan after Year 7 (§15.3(1)).
  - After Year 9 the Monitors submitted the audit history and findings to the Senate, which voted Provisional status by a majority of its full seated membership (§15.3(2)).
- **Two kinds of map exist.** Corindal draws its own **federal Assembly** districts by law under the federal criteria (equal population, contiguity, §1.6 non-discrimination, no minority vote dilution, compactness; no party, voting-history or incumbent-residence data). The Elections Panel rules on compliance and draws an interim map if an election comes first. Its own **State Senate** districts are drawn by an independent commission "independently of the State Senate and Governor" (State §3.2(c)). A State law needs 26 votes, a majority of the 51-seat authorized membership (State §§3.4(f), 3.5(c)).
- Sollis's Dispatch: the findings are public, the failures "were real", and the legislature "deadlocked on the redraw".

## What the desks must settle
1. Which map or maps failed, and which standard(s) each breached, in each of Years 5, 7 and 9. Did the defect stay the same or change?
2. Which Monitor found what, and what the Elections Panel determined. Was an interim map ever drawn for a Corindal election?
3. Why the State Senate could not reach 26 votes: the honest, good-faith reasons on each side. No villains, no corruption, no secret lawbreaking.
4. What the two Remediation Plans promised, and why they did not deliver.
5. What, if anything, the State disputes in the Monitors' account. Disagreement is allowed and useful.

## Limits for both desks
- **Years 5–9 only.** Audits continued after Year 9 (§15.2(7)), but their results are not established: don't decide them, and say so where it matters. No restoration, no compliant audit, no election outcome or date.
- **Casimir Rehn stays out entirely.**
- Don't advance the fictional date (Year 14, Month 2).
- Historical senators, commissioners and officials may be proposed only where the account needs them; list every new name in your notes.
- Keep findings informational. A Monitor finding binds no one; the federal Senate made the determinations.

## The end product
- **Monitors desk:** a retroactive account of the Corindal audit findings that canon can draw on. Propose its form in your notes. Possibilities include reconstructed Year 5/7/9 findings, the Year 9 audit-history submission under §15.3(2), or a present-day consolidated summary published to the NRS; John decides.
- **Corindal desk:** the State's side of the same history, through the Remediation Plans, the Senate's votes and the blocs that formed. Again propose its form in your notes: a Corindal Register entry, a reconstructed plan, or *The Corindal Ledger* coverage of the record.

## Rounds (kept by the editor)
- **Round 1:** each desk files its own account (kind `report`, not for publication) without seeing the other's. The editor records the key points of both here, marking agreements and conflicts.
- **Round 2:** each desk answers the other's account and the open conflicts.
- **Round 3:** the editor proposes a reconciled account; John approves; the desks draft the publishable pieces.
