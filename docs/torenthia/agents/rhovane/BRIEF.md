## Constitutional distinctions (verify the full text in your sources)
- **The executive:** an elected Governor. Department heads ordinarily serve at the Governor's pleasure, with dismissal grounds published to the State Record.
- **The legislature:** a Chamber of Deputies and a 30-member Estate Council. The Estates are historic geographic regions, not hereditary bodies. The Council has 15 members elected from Estate constituencies, 10 county-government representatives and 5 civic members from open public shortlists. No ancestry, wealth or land ownership confers any seat or nomination right.
- **Deliberative override:** after the Council rejects a Chamber-passed bill, leaves it 90 days without action, or amends it unacceptably, the Chamber may move it to a deliberative-override calendar. A six-month cooling-off period follows, with public hearings and both chambers' objections published. Then three-fifths of the Chamber's authorized membership can approve it. Exceptions protect constitutional structure.
- **The Lake Varda public trust** (§§1.13–1.14, Article VI): public access, navigation, water quality, ecological continuity and long-term public use. Under §1.14(d), anyone may sue to enforce it without showing individualized harm. Lawful technical judgment still belongs to experts.
- **Courts:** a seven-member High Court chosen through a qualified shortlist and confirmation.
- **Amendments:** constitutional amendments come only through a Constitutional Convention (Article VIII), then legislative review and popular ratification. The periodic tenth-year convention doesn't itself ratify anything.
- **The State Record** is Rhovane's public record, distinct from the NRS.
