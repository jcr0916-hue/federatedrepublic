# Morantine Territorial Compact — Approved Editorial Reference v0.2

**Status:** President-approved governing editorial reference (v0.2, approved November 3, 2026). The full framework, including the 36-member unicameral Assembly, proportional ranked-choice voting, four-year terms, Assembly-elected Chief Minister (19 votes), constructive no-confidence and 30-day vacancy succession, is approved for agent development. **Not enacted law, not an NRS record, and not a new Year 14 event.**

**Prepared:** November 3, 2026 (real-world editorial date)  
**Fictional reference period:** Year 14, Month 2  
**Federal basis:** Federated Republic Constitution §§15.1, 15.2, 15.8–15.8.a, Article I, Articles III–IV, IX–X, XII and other applicable provisions.  
**Source principle:** Morantine's creation route is not established as a §15.4 devolution or §15.6 incorporation. This draft therefore uses the **standard statutory territorial framework** permitted by §15.1, subject to future evidence of a different constitutional origin.

> **Editorial construction note.** Articles below propose an institutional framework. The chosen numbers, procedures, titles and administrative divisions are not claimed to exist in the published World. Existing Morantine NRS submissions and works records remain independent of this draft and do not become retrospective evidence of a particular officeholder, vote or Compact ratification.

## Article 1 — Status and Governing Law

1. Morantine is a Territory of the Federated Republic. The Constitution, applicable federal law made within federal competence, and this Compact govern its institutions and territorial authority. In this Compact, "this Compact" means the standard statutory framework established under §15.1, not a separately ratified founding instrument.
2. The Republic holds the governing authority over Morantine. This Compact establishes an elected, accountable territorial government over matters of local concern, consistently with §15.1 and the principles of local capacity and subsidiarity in §§15.8–15.8.a.
3. Territorial authority is neither the sovereignty of a State nor an independent power to alter the Republic's external boundaries. Territorial law may not displace the Constitution or valid controlling federal law.
4. All rights in Article I apply throughout Morantine. No provision creates an exception to non-derogable rights or to the lawful jurisdiction of the courts and independent constitutional institutions.
5. No statement in this Compact asserts a particular founding admission, accession treaty, historic referendum, or former independent status of Morantine.

## Article 2 — Territorial Assembly

1. The **Morantine Territorial Assembly** is a unicameral elected local governing authority with **36 members**. This size is an **approved editorial design choice**, selected to support representation across a Territory of approximately 1.6 million residents; it is not an independently published count.
2. Members are chosen for **four-year terms** by proportional ranked-choice voting (single transferable vote) in multi-member territorial constituencies defined by territorial legislation, subject to applicable national voting standards and review. Constituencies should respect communities, population, practical access and fair representation. They are independent of the Territory's federal Assembly district boundaries.
3. The Assembly elects a Speaker by absolute majority of its full membership and establishes its own standing orders, committees and scrutiny processes. Its members are not, by virtue of that office, members of the federal Assembly.
4. The Assembly enacts territorial legislation; appropriates territorial funds; authorizes lawful territorial taxation; reviews administration; may demand documents, hear ministers and investigate public-service performance within territorial competence.
5. The Assembly must publish enacted laws, votes and official instruments where publication is required by Article X and applicable law. It cannot command the federal Legislature, Monitors, Elections Panel, Judiciary, Legat Consul or another jurisdiction.
6. Electoral boundaries and timing are governed by applicable law and must be administered consistently with the constitutional authority of the Elections Panel; this Compact creates no new federal electoral mandate.

## Article 3 — Chief Minister and Territorial Administration

1. The Territorial Administration is responsible for executing territorial law and maintaining ordinary public services. It is headed by a **Chief Minister** who is the political executive of the Territory, not a federal consul.
2. After a general territorial election, the Territorial Assembly elects the Chief Minister by absolute majority of its full membership (**19 votes** while membership is 36). Procedures for nominations and successive ballots are provided by territorial law.
3. The Chief Minister appoints and dismisses ministers responsible for distinct portfolios, subject to lawful qualifications, conflict-of-interest rules and Assembly scrutiny. Portfolios may include local works, rural services, finance, transport, health and education; their exact current titles and incumbents are **not established here**.
4. The Chief Minister proposes territorial budgets, directs administration within law and reports to the Assembly on public-service performance. Neither Chief Minister nor ministers may negotiate or ratify international agreements on the Republic's behalf, control external military operations, certify federal elections, or adjudicate Statehood qualification.
5. The Administration may consult with Valedon-border communities and coordinate routine service delivery within lawful competence, without purporting to conduct foreign policy or make international commitments.

## Article 4 — Confidence, Vacancy and Continuity

1. The Assembly may replace the Chief Minister only through a **constructive vote of no confidence** simultaneously electing a named eligible successor by absolute majority of the full Assembly. A policy disagreement, failed amendment or rejected spending proposal is not a resignation or a vacancy.
2. Upon death, resignation, permanent incapacity or disqualification, an eligible minister designated by a publicly established territorial succession order serves as Acting Chief Minister. If none can act, the Assembly may elect a temporary acting executive by absolute majority.
3. The Assembly must elect a permanent successor within **30 days** of a permanent vacancy. This President-approved editorial design period is a Compact rule, not a pre-existing federal deadline. Acting service remains limited to lawful continuity and does not create new powers.
4. A caretaker administration may preserve essential services and implement existing lawful commitments, but may not use a vacancy to alter the Compact, claim federal power or take irreversible constitutional action.
5. Judicial review remains available for questions concerning a vacancy, election or succession's legality and procedure.

## Article 5 — Local Government and Community Capacity

1. Morantine may establish, recognize and reform municipalities, rural councils and other local subdivisions by territorial law. Local bodies must have reasonable legal, fiscal and administrative capacity to carry out assigned lawful functions under §§15.8–15.8.a.
2. Territorial institutions should assign public functions to the most local level reasonably able to perform them effectively and consistently with the Constitution. Temporary assumption of a function for demonstrated incapacity or cross-jurisdiction effects must be proportionate and reviewable.
3. The names, number, locations and present officeholders of local councils are **left open**. This Article neither creates a named district nor establishes a historical municipal boundary.
4. No territorial authority may invent local incapacity by denying a subordinate body the resources reasonably necessary for functions assigned to it and then cite that incapacity as an unrestricted basis for centralization.

## Article 6 — Public Services and Works

1. Subject to lawful competence, Morantine administers ordinary local roads, drainage, municipal infrastructure, local public buildings, education and health delivery, and other assigned territorial services.
2. The Administration may prepare technical studies, maintenance registers and procurement proposals within existing authority. A study is not an appropriation, a construction authorization, an award of contract or a finding of constitutional failure.
3. New expenditure, taxation and commitments of public money require authorization through applicable budget and appropriations law. Administrative officials may not promise an unappropriated infrastructure outcome.
4. Official works notices and completed studies are published as required by law. An incomplete register may identify factual measurements without establishing an engineering recommendation.
5. **Continuity:** NRS 120 commissioned an engineering survey at the Kessel culvert on the Valedon road outside Tessom; NRS 133 recorded preliminary measurements. Neither authorized construction or a new capital allocation. This note is for agent training and does not create a new Compact duty to enlarge the culvert.

## Article 7 — Border and External Relations

1. Morantine's geographic and cultural relationship with neighboring Valedon may inform lawful territorial administration, commerce, education and local community life.
2. Foreign affairs, treaty-making, international recognition, military command and other constitutionally reserved external functions remain with the Republic, including the Legat Consul where §2.1 assigns those functions.
3. No territorial minister may conclude an international agreement or claim to determine Valedon's constitutional relationship with the Republic. Any proposed international arrangement must be referred through competent federal institutions.
4. This Compact creates no border alteration, independence question, international obligation, immigration exemption or bilateral agreement.

## Article 8 — Local Justice and Administrative Review

1. The federal judicial system and applicable constitutional allocation of jurisdiction control in Morantine.
2. Local courts and administrative tribunals may be organized only to the extent authorized by valid Republic law. Their procedures, selection and tenure must secure Article I rights and independent, impartial review.
3. Morantine shall not claim a separate territorial supreme court or final authority to interpret the federal Constitution.
4. Territorial administrative decisions affecting rights must supply legally required reasons, notice and review opportunities. Federal appellate and Supreme Court jurisdictions remain intact.

## Article 9 — Territorial Finance, Taxation and Equalization

1. The Territorial Assembly may enact territorial budgets, appropriations, taxes and fees within Morantine's lawful jurisdiction, subject to §§12.3 and 12.5, Article I and applicable law.
2. Territorial revenue and funds are accounted for separately from the Republic's National Revenue Fund. Federal appropriations and disbursements require federal legal authority; the Territory cannot command an allocation through its own budget.
3. Morantine may present evidence and advocate before federal institutions concerning the fiscal-equalization mechanism under §12.6, but the federal Legislature establishes any formula by lawful statute. A territorial submission does not itself establish entitlement, allocation priority, breach or a committee recommendation.
4. **Continuity:** NRS 048 (Year 13, Month 12) recorded Morantine's submission identifying old capacity requests, recurring service obligations and the possible treatment of request age. No formula, weight, grant or noncompliance finding follows from that receipt. The present Joint Committee review remains unresolved.
5. Local financial oversight and publication must respect the Executive Monitor's independent constitutional mandate. No expenditure preference alone establishes constitutional dereliction.

## Article 10 — Public Records and Accountability

1. The National Record System is the authoritative publication system for official territorial legal records under Article X. Morantine shall submit its laws, regulations and official acts as required by law.
2. Local administrative offices may maintain technical files, working registers and routine records; these do not supersede the NRS as the authoritative legal record.
3. Territorial government must publish decisions and reasons as law requires, while respecting protected information and due process.
4. This Compact does not create a competing sovereign record system or authorize the Territory to assign national NRS identifiers.

## Article 11 — Statehood

1. Morantine's path from Territory to State is governed solely by the requirements and procedures of §15.2 and applicable law. A Territorial Assembly resolution or eligible citizen petition may initiate an audit proceeding in accordance with §15.2(1), but no local official may declare Statehood unilaterally.
2. The three Monitors provide their independent constitutionally defined portions of each completed Statehood Audit; the responsible legislative chamber determines compliance under §15.2.a. A first affirmative determination does not itself make the Territory a State.
3. The second qualifying audit must independently satisfy §15.2, and Statehood follows immediately by constitutional operation only upon the relevant affirmative legislative determination and any applicable remainder-viability requirement.
4. **Continuity:** Morantine's first audit was affirmatively determined compliant. Its second is pending within the applicable constitutional window. No particular date, finding or outcome of that second audit is established by this Compact.
5. Upon Statehood, the territorial framework yields to the Constitution's provisions for States and to appropriate lawful continuity arrangements. The Compact cannot create Senate seats ahead of that event.

## Article 12 — Amendment

1. Changes to this governing framework require procedures valid under §15.1 and federal law, including federal legislative action where the standard statutory framework requires it, and meaningful participation by the elected territorial governing authority.
2. Ordinary territorial administrative policies, budgets and local statutes may change without a Compact amendment where this framework permits them to change.
3. Nothing in this Article establishes an unverified historical ratification process or impairs the federal Constitution's supremacy.

## Article 13 — Interpretation and Severability

1. The Constitution prevails over any inconsistent part of this Compact. An invalid provision is severed to the extent legally required; the remainder operates where possible.
2. No Article impairs Article I, the Judiciary, the three Monitors, the Elections Panel, the National Record System Panel or the Monetary Authority.
3. This Compact is not evidence of any previously unreported territorial election, appointment, statute, public works contract, external agreement or Statehood finding.
