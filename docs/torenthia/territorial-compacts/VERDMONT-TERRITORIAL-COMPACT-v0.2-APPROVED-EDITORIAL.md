# Verdmont Territorial Compact — v0.2 (Approved Editorial Reference)

**Status:** **President-approved complete editorial governing reference (2026-11-03).** The institutional architecture and operative procedures of v0.2 are approved for Verdmont agent training and editorial continuity: one population-based People's Assembly of 24 members and a separate collective eight-member Territorial Executive Council, elected two from each of four sections. **Not an enacted law, a published NRS record, or a new fictional event.** Section names, boundaries, population figures by section, and election history remain unspecified; constitutional-equality review is required before any future portrayal of enactment.

**Revised:** November 3, 2026 (real-world editorial time). **Fictional setting:** Year 14, Month 2, unchanged.

## Source and continuity guardrails

**Established in published sources:** Verdmont lies in the northwest, from the Northern Alps toward the coast; its economy includes resource extraction. Population approximately **400,000**, **four federal Assembly seats**, **zero federal Senate seats**; **no Statehood Audit initiated**. NRS-Y14-0029 (published in `torenthia/nrs/torenthia-nrs-117.html`, Year 14 Month 1) establishes seasonal high-pass road closures, valley routes remaining subject to weather advisories, and freight rerouting for extraction sites.

**President's accepted design direction:** a rugged coastal/mountain Territory, with much of its population likely outside the mountains; smaller mountain communities deserve meaningful territorial executive representation and may contain people with cultural or migration links to Varek who disagree among themselves. The President has **approved** replacing the earlier bicameral/upper-senate model with **a unicameral population-based Assembly plus a geographically balanced collective executive**. Neither the exact section boundaries nor their names/populations are established.

**Supersession:** This draft replaces v0.1 Articles 2–8's Council-of-Sections-as-upper-legislative-chamber / Territorial-Convenor-as-single-executive model. Any prior v0.1 provision requiring passage by *both chambers* or a lower-house *override* is **withdrawn**. The eight councillors are executives, **not territorial senators**.

## Article 1 — Territorial status and constitutional supremacy

1. Verdmont is a Territory under Federal Constitution §15.1 and applicable Republic law. Its territorial institutions exercise delegated local self-government, not State sovereignty.
2. Federal constitutional rights and exclusive competences prevail, including foreign affairs, federal elections, Statehood Audits, national fiscal mechanisms, federal Monitors and final constitutional adjudication.
3. Editorial approval of this Compact does not imply a fictional enactment, ratification date, officeholder, election, prior legal act or alteration of the published NRS.

## Article 2 — People's Assembly

1. The **People's Assembly** is Verdmont's **sole territorial legislative chamber**, consisting of **24 elected members**.
2. Members serve **four-year terms**, provisionally chosen by **proportional ranked-choice voting (single transferable vote)** in multi-member constituencies apportioned primarily by population, subject to applicable electoral law, equal suffrage and practical accessibility. Constituency boundaries are separate from Verdmont's four **federal** Assembly seats.
3. The Assembly enacts territorial legislation, taxation, budgets and appropriations within delegated competence; questions and investigates the executive; receives public audit reports; and may summon councillors to explain decisions under lawful procedures.
4. Bills pass under the Assembly's published standing orders by majority of votes cast with quorum, except where this Compact lawfully prescribes a higher threshold. The Executive Council has **no legislative veto or upper-house vote**.
5. Elections and certifications follow the applicable constitutional election-administration framework; the Assembly cannot appropriate federal Elections Panel authority.

## Article 3 — Geographic sections and equality

1. For this approved editorial design, Verdmont comprises **four geographic sections**, provisionally labelled **Sections A–D** until names, boundaries and population data receive separate editorial review.
2. Each section elects **two members** to the Territorial Executive Council, regardless of its share of territorial population. Each eligible elector has equal voting rights *within* that elector's section.
3. Boundaries must be coherent and grounded in geography, accessibility, settlement patterns and continuing communities; they must not be manipulated to privilege a party, family, extractive operator or particular incumbent.
4. A regular independent boundary review examines population changes, travel barriers and representational effects. Any actual change to section boundaries requires public consultation and the Compact's enhanced amendment process, together with all applicable federal approvals.
5. The deliberate *between-section* representational imbalance is designed to protect sparsely populated regions. Before final enactment or any fictional history of enactment, it requires constitutional equality and rights review; this Compact does not assert an automatic exemption from equality guarantees.

## Article 4 — Territorial Executive Council: election and composition

1. Verdmont's executive authority within its delegated competence is exercised **collectively** by a **Territorial Executive Council of eight elected councillors**: two from each of the four sections.
2. Councillors are directly elected within each section for **four-year terms** by a locally lawful ranked-choice method designed to fill both seats; the election method, ordinary timing and vacancy-election procedure must be specified in implementing rules consistent with constitutional guarantees.
3. A councillor may not simultaneously sit in the People's Assembly. Councillors owe duties to the *whole Territory*, not merely their section, while remaining directly answerable to electors of that section.
4. The Council is **not a Senate or second legislative chamber**; its eight seats do not convey federal Senate representation, and no councillor may legislate independently by executive resolution.
5. The Council administers territorial departments and implements Assembly legislation and budgets; the exercise of delegated authority must be publicly recorded and judicially reviewable.

## Article 5 — Council Chair and administrative portfolios

1. The Council elects a **Council Chair** from among its eight members by **five affirmative votes of its full membership**. The Chair convenes meetings, manages their agenda, communicates collective decisions and represents the Territory ceremonially or administratively within lawful powers.
2. The Chair is **first among equals**, not a separate Chief Minister, President or unilateral executive. The Chair retains one ordinary Council vote but receives **no casting vote** in ties.
3. The Chair serves a **one-year renewable coordinating term** unless replaced by five Council votes. Chair replacement does not remove the person as a directly elected councillor.
4. Portfolios (for example transport and winter access, community services, land and extraction oversight, finance and public administration) may be allocated among councillors by Council resolution. Portfolio responsibility does **not** independently confer power to enact laws, spend unappropriated funds, bind another government or overrule collective Council decisions.
5. Senior civil servants administer routine operations under published law, delegated authority and ordinary conflict-of-interest rules; they do not become a shadow unelected executive.

## Article 6 — Collective decisions, quorum and stalemate

1. **Five of eight affirmative votes** are required for a substantive Council decision, regardless of absences. An abstention does not count as an affirmative vote.
2. A meeting requires at least **six councillors present**, including at least one from three distinct sections, except for narrowly defined emergency continuity meetings authorized by law. The five-vote substantive threshold remains unchanged.
3. A four–four split, incomplete quorum or failure to secure five affirmative votes means **no new collective decision**. It is not permission for the Chair or a portfolio holder to act alone.
4. When Council agreement fails, lawful existing services and previously authorized decisions continue under ordinary administration. The Council must publicly report the unresolved question and may request a neutral review, further evidence or a reconvened vote. It cannot use stalemate to suspend constitutional rights.
5. Emergency administrative steps may be taken only as existing law specifically permits, for strictly necessary duration, subject to later public Council review, Assembly oversight and judicial remedies. This is **not** a general emergency decree power.

## Article 7 — Legislative and financial accountability

1. The People's Assembly is the **only body** that makes territorial laws, establishes territorial taxes and authorizes appropriations. A budget bill is introduced and debated in the Assembly; the Council may submit a proposed budget, explanatory schedules and nonbinding objections.
2. The Council cannot veto enacted lawful appropriations or independently withdraw funds, impose taxes or force expenditures not authorized by statute.
3. The Assembly may question councillors, hold public committee inquiries, subpoena records within lawful powers, and pass resolutions of censure or requests for policy reconsideration. **An ordinary censure does not remove directly elected councillors.**
4. Members of the Assembly cannot use the budget to dissolve the Council, redraw sections by ordinary law or penalize a section for electing dissenting councillors.
5. If an annual budget is delayed, essential services continue temporarily on a narrowly bounded previous-law basis subject to audit; this creates no new entitlement to federal money or major project approval.

## Article 8 — Terms, vacancies, removal and continuity

1. Vacancies in Council seats are filled by a section-based process specified in electoral law, ordinarily a special election or lawful ranked-ballot succession, **without transferring a section's seat to another section**. The replacement serves the remainder of the term.
2. During a vacancy, the Council continues only to the extent it can satisfy the quorum and **five affirmative vote** requirements; a vacancy never lowers the threshold to four. If temporary incapacity blocks collective action, essential existing services continue through lawful civil-service delegation.
3. The Council elects an acting Chair by five affirmative votes if the chairship is vacant. If five votes cannot be obtained immediately, a published neutral order of meeting-conveners handles only convening and routine continuity, **not substantive unilateral decisions**.
4. A councillor can resign; lawful removal or recall must rest on a specifically established legal procedure with due process and, when elector-initiated recall is authorized, an electorate from **that councillor's own section**. No Assembly simple majority may dismiss a regional seat-holder for mere policy disagreement.
5. Misconduct remains subject to independent investigation and the judiciary. The executive is not immune from federal Monitor review or applicable federal law.

## Article 9 — Regional administration, transport and public works

1. Verdmont may organize municipalities and local or intermunicipal councils for coastal, lowland, valley and mountain communities under applicable territorial and federal law, with meaningful delegated service responsibilities and resources where lawful.
2. Transportation, seasonal access, environmental management, resource-extraction oversight, schooling, health services and ordinary infrastructure are managed under law and appropriation. Published high-pass closure schedules must be respected; valley access is weather-dependent, not permanently guaranteed.
3. Administrative project approval must not be confused with engineering study, procurement, capital appropriation or federal funding. All material public works procurements require auditable records and conflict safeguards.
4. Cooperation with Varek or other States on ordinary matters must respect their autonomy and the Republic's allocation of powers. No one section or heritage group speaks for all Varek-linked residents.

## Article 10 — Individual rights and plural identities

1. All people in Verdmont enjoy the rights secured by the Federal Constitution regardless of section, migration history, occupation, political affiliation or opinion of Varek's earlier institutions.
2. Geographic equality **among the four executive sections** must be balanced with individual voting rights, access to public services and meaningful legal remedies. The population-based Assembly provides a distinct, territorial-wide representative check.
3. Residents from Varek and their descendants may disagree about its institutional history; neither affection for nor opposition to Varek's earlier government is a constitutional prerequisite to office.
4. Elections and public information must be accessible across seasonal road disruptions and remote communities, consistent with applicable law.

## Article 11 — Judiciary, ethics and federal oversight

1. Territorial tribunals and courts exist only under lawful federal or delegated arrangements; the Territory cannot create a final constitutional court independent of the Republic's judiciary.
2. Executive decisions, Council election disputes, and section-boundary changes remain open to the appropriate judicial review; Monitors' constitutionally independent powers remain intact.
3. Councillors disclose conflicts, including interests in resource-extraction and public-works contracts. Recusal rules must not permit a councillor to defeat lawful oversight or allow a chair to reduce the five-vote threshold.

## Article 12 — Federal representation and Statehood

1. Verdmont currently has **four elected federal Assembly representatives** and **no federal Senate representation** because it remains a Territory. The eight-person Executive Council is not a federal chamber.
2. Published Statehood status: **no Statehood Audit initiated**. This Compact establishes no audit, result, Statehood entitlement or Senate election.
3. Future Statehood proceedings remain governed exclusively by Federal Constitution §15.2 and competent independent institutions. This Compact cannot predetermine the outcome.

## Article 13 — Amendment and transition

1. Changes to this governing framework require public notice, consultation, a **two-thirds vote of the People's Assembly (16 of 24)** and **six affirmative votes of the eight-member Executive Council**, plus any federal approval or legal steps required by §15.1 and applicable law.
2. Replacing equal two-per-section executive representation, changing section boundaries, or transferring legislative authority to the Council additionally requires independent constitutional-equality review; an ordinary Assembly statute or Council resolution cannot accomplish those changes.
3. Any lawful transition from a prior territorial administrative arrangement must be separately defined and authorized. This approved editorial Compact does **not** establish that Verdmont already conducted its first Council election or enacted the arrangement in Year 14.
4. Where there is conflict with the Federal Constitution, its requirements prevail.

## Presidential approval and outstanding factual/legal gates

**Approved complete editorial governing design** (reflected here):
- A **unicameral, 24-member, population-apportioned People's Assembly**, not two territorial legislative houses.
- A **collective eight-member Executive Council**, two directly elected from each of four territorial sections.
- A **Council Chair as first among equals**, not an independently powerful Chief Minister; the eight councillors collectively administer the Territory.

**Approved internal procedures and implementation parameters (subject to constitutional compliance before fictional enactment):**
- Council four-year terms; Chair one-year renewable term; five-vote decision threshold and six-person quorum with three sections present.
- Special-election/section-based vacancy replacement, legally bounded regional recall, and continuity if voting locks at four–four.
- Assembly-only legislation and appropriations, public oversight without an ordinary political removal vote, and 16/24 Assembly + 6/8 Council thresholds for amendment.
- Geographic sections' names, precise boundaries, demography and election timetable remain **undecided and are not approved canon**. Section labels A–D are placeholders; examples such as “Coastal” or “Mountain” are not final boundaries.
- Independent constitutional rights/electoral review is still required before fictionally enacting a system with unequal executive electors-per-seat.
