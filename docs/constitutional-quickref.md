## ARTICLE I — INDIVIDUAL SOVEREIGNTY AND RIGHTS

### §1.1 — Right to Courts and Legal Remedy

Every person may seek legal remedy for any violation of their rights under this Constitution, whether by act or omission, before an independent and impartial court. This right is self-executing: where the Legislature has not funded adequate legal aid, courts appoint counsel at public expense.


### §1.2 — Individual Sovereignty Floor

Every person possesses an inviolable sphere of personal sovereignty over their own life, body, and choices. Government authority operates outside this sphere.


### §1.3 — Prohibition of Torture and Cruel Treatment

Torture and cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment are absolutely prohibited. No person, wherever located, may be subjected to such treatment by an agent or officer of the Republic, nor by any person acting at the direction or with the acquiescence of the Republic.


### §1.4 — Prohibition of Slavery and Forced Labor

Slavery, involuntary servitude, and forced labor are absolutely prohibited. Lawful criminal sentences, jury service, military conscription, and civil emergency duties required by law do not constitute forced labor.


### §1.5 — Civil and Political Liberties

Every person holds the rights of expression, thought, conscience, religion, association, assembly, petition, and movement. Government may regulate the time, place, and manner of expression and assembly only as necessary for public order and without viewpoint discrimination.


### §1.6 — Equality and Non-Discrimination

Every person is equal before the law. Government may not discriminate on the basis of race, ethnicity, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, religion, national origin, disability, age, or economic status.


### §1.7 — Privacy, Property, and Security of the Home

Every person holds the right to privacy and security in their person, home, communications, and data, and the right to own property. Government may not enter a home, search a person or property, surveil a person or communication, or collect data without consent or prior independent judicial authorization on specific, articulable grounds.


### §1.8 — Democratic Participation

Every citizen, subject to constitutional eligibility requirements, has the right to vote, stand for office, participate in democratic processes, and have their vote counted. No governmental action whose purpose or substantial effect suppresses or reduces access to the franchise is valid.


### §1.9 — Right to Bear Arms

Every person holds the right to keep and bear arms for lawful purposes, including self-defense. The Legislature and States may regulate the licensing, safety, type, transfer, and carry of arms, provided lawful ownership for self-defense remains available.


### §1.10 — Habeas Corpus

Every person detained by public authority has the right to challenge that detention before an independent and impartial court. The detaining authority must produce the person and justify detention within the period established by statute, not to exceed 72 hours.

- **Time limits:**
  - The detaining authority must produce the person and justify detention within the period established by statute, not to exceed 72 hours

### §1.11 — Rights of the Accused and Fair Trial

Every person accused of a criminal offense has the right to prompt notice of the charges; counsel, at public expense if unable to afford representation; adequate time and means to prepare a defense; examination of witnesses; silence without adverse inference; and a speedy trial. No person may receive a greater penalty solely for exercising the right to trial.


### §1.12 — Right to a Public Trial

Every person accused of a criminal offense has the right to a public trial before an independent and impartial court. Proceedings and records may be closed or sealed only to the minimum extent and duration necessary to protect a compelling interest identified by the court.


### §1.13 — Freedom from Retroactive Criminal Law

No person may be convicted for conduct that was not criminal when committed, and no criminal law may otherwise be applied retroactively to the disadvantage of the accused.


### §1.14 — Double Jeopardy

No person may be tried or punished twice for the same offense following final acquittal or conviction. Offenses are the same where they arise from the same conduct and the later charge requires no element distinct from the earlier charge.


### §1.14.a — Conduct Lawful in Another Jurisdiction

No State or Territory may impose criminal or civil liability on a person for conduct that was lawful where it occurred in another State or Territory, or for assisting such conduct or providing information about it.


### §1.15 — Environmental Obligations

Government may not cause or systematically permit material environmental harm to persons or communities. The Legislature must establish environmental protections.


### §1.16 — Emergency Derogation

No emergency suspends the Individual Sovereignty Floor in full. Rights other than those protected by §1.16.a may be temporarily derogated only upon a declaration stating the emergency, rights affected, scope, and duration, published immediately to the NRS.

- **Time limits:**
  - Either executive may issue a declaration within their domain for no more than 14 days
- **Cross-refs:** §1.16.a

### §1.16.a — Non-Derogable Rights

These six rights are absolute: the prohibition on torture (§1.3); slavery (§1.4); habeas corpus (§1.10); the right to a public trial (§1.12); no retroactive punishment (§1.13); and non-refoulement (§1.18). No emergency measure may be applied in a discriminatory manner on the basis of race, ethnicity, sex, religion, national origin, sexual orientation, or gender identity; any such application is void.

- **Cross-refs:** §1.10, §1.12, §1.13, §1.18, §1.3, §1.4

### §1.17 — Right to Seek Asylum

Every person who reaches the Republic's jurisdiction has the right to claim asylum and to an independent determination of that claim under §6.3. Manner of entry does not affect this right.

- **Cross-refs:** §6.3

### §1.18 — Non-Refoulement

No person, wherever located, may be returned, expelled, or extradited by the Republic, or by anyone acting at its direction or with its acquiescence, to any territory where they face genuine risk of persecution, torture, or deprivation of life. This protection applies regardless of the person's status, conduct, or the receiving government.

## ARTICLE II — THE DUAL EXECUTIVE

### §2.1 — The Legat Consul — Domain and Term

To hold the office of Legat Consul, a person must have been a citizen for at least 7 years at the time of taking office. The Legat Consul holds authority over military command, force employment, foreign affairs, external intelligence (§2.3), trade agreements (§2.4), and indigenous nation relations (Article XVI).

- **Time limits:**
  - To hold the office of Legat Consul, a person must have been a citizen for at least 7 years at the time of taking office
  - No person may serve more than 12 years as Legat Consul
- **Cross-refs:** §2.3, §2.4

### §2.2 — Military Authorization Framework

The Legat Consul may conduct military operations necessary to repel or prevent an active or imminent attack on the Republic's territory, citizens abroad, or treaty allies, including defensive operations beyond the Republic's borders where necessary to that purpose. Military operations pursuing other objectives require authorization by law within the purposes permitted by §14.1.

- **Cross-refs:** §14.1

### §2.3 — Consular Intelligence

The Legat Consul directs external intelligence. No intelligence operation against Republic citizens, wherever located, may be conducted on the Legat Consul's authority; such operations require independent judicial authorization.


### §2.3.a — Legat Consul Legislative Veto

The Legat Consul may veto legislation raising a genuine constitutional concern within the security domain — military operations, external intelligence, treaty obligations, or border security — by returning the bill within 10 business days of final passage with written grounds. The veto may be used once per bill; a bill repassed in a subsequent session proceeds without Legat Consul veto.

- **Thresholds:**
  - The Senate may override by 2/3 during that session

### §2.4 — Legat Consul International Agreements

The Legat Consul negotiates and concludes treaties and trade agreements, subject to §3.6. The Legat Consul must consult the Civic Consul on trade and indigenous agreements bearing on domestic interests; the Civic Consul's response is advisory.

- **Cross-refs:** §3.6

### §2.4.a — Domain Officers

The Legat Consul may appoint domain officers to exercise defined authority. Officers are nominated by the Legat Consul and confirmed by the Senate.


### §2.5 — The Civic Consul — Domain and Term

(1) The Civic Consul holds residual executive authority over functions not enumerated to the Legat Consul. (2) No person may serve more than 12 years as Civic Consul.

- **Time limits:**

### §2.6 — Constructive Vote of No Confidence

(1) The Civic Consul is accountable to the Assembly and may be removed only by a constructive vote of no confidence that simultaneously elects a named successor by absolute majority of the full seated Assembly. A vote not electing a successor has no effect.

- **Thresholds:**

(2) If the Assembly does not elect within that period, the Assembly Speaker becomes Acting Civic Consul by constitutional operation, with authority limited to maintaining existing policy.

### §2.7 — Civic Consul Legislative Instruments

The Civic Consul may, within 10 business days of final passage, return a bill with written grounds. The bill is suspended for one month, during which the Senate may override by 2/3.

- **Thresholds:**
  - The bill is suspended for one month, during which the Senate may override by 2/3

### §2.8 — Duty of Refusal

No civil servant, military member, or person exercising federal authority may comply with an executive order requiring violation of this Constitution or statute. Lawful refusal creates no liability and may not support penalty; any such penalty is void.


### §2.9 — Legat Consul Succession and Incapacity

(1) Full succession activates upon the Legat Consul's death, resignation, or removal. The Senate Speaker holds the office from that moment.


### §2.9.a — State Emergency Council

(1) At each federal electoral period, the Elections Panel shall publish to the NRS a public-lot ordered list of States. Each State is represented for this purpose by its constitutionally designated head of state or, absent one, its presiding legislative officer.

### §2.11 — Emergency Governance

When extraordinary circumstances prevent ordinary constitutional government, either Consul, or a majority of available members of either chamber, may declare a continuity emergency. The declaration states the circumstances, is published to the NRS, and is sent to every State and Territory.

- **Time limits:**
  - Declarations lapse after 30 days unless renewed by the same process
- **Cross-refs:** §1.16, §2.16, §2.9, §2.9.a, §7.2.a

### §2.12 — Clemency

Each Consul may grant clemency only within their domain: the Legat Consul for military and national-security offenses, the Civic Consul for domestic federal offenses. Self-clemency is void.


### §2.13 — Consular Removal

The Legat Consul may be removed through two independent processes. Legislative removal requires a concurrent 2/3 vote of both chambers for serious misconduct, abuse of office, or permanent incapacity; the Legislature's determination that grounds exist is final.

- **Thresholds:**
  - Legislative removal requires a concurrent 2/3 vote of both chambers for serious misconduct, abuse of office, or permanent incapacity

### §2.14 — Council of Ministers

The Council of Ministers consists of the Legat Consul's domain officers and the Civic Consul's ministers, serving as the standing forum for cross-domain coordination and disputes. It exercises no executive authority.


### §2.14.a — Cross-Domain Emergency Lead

In a cross-domain emergency requiring unified operational authority, either Consul may propose a temporary operational lead. Designation requires concurrence of both Consuls and NRS publication.


### §2.14.b — Cross-Domain Assistance

Either Consul may request assistance from the other within the responding Consul's domain. The request must state its purpose and scope and be published to the NRS.

- **Cross-refs:** §1.16

### §2.15 — Federal Prosecution

The Civic Consul is responsible for enforcement of federal law and may set general prosecution policy. Statute must establish a Federal Prosecution Service independent in individual prosecutorial decisions.


### §2.16 — Executive Incapacity

(1) A Consul is temporarily unable to exercise authority upon their declaration or a determination by a majority of the Council of Ministers (at least five participating). Acting authority vests immediately in the Speaker of the Consul's chamber, then in statutory successors, then in the most senior chamber member by continuous service; ties resolved by age.

- **Thresholds:**
  - If incapacity is determined again on substantially the same grounds within 90 days, the renewed determination requires 2/3 of the deciding body
  - Continuation thereafter requires a fresh 2/3 determination
- **Time limits:**
  - (2) The Consul may declare the incapacity ended, restoring authority 24 hours after publication
  - If incapacity is determined again on substantially the same grounds within 90 days, the renewed determination requires 2/3 of the deciding body
  - (5) A person next in succession may decline within 48 hours by NRS publication

### §2.17 — Cooling-Off from Executive Service

During any constitutional cooling-off period barring a person from holding or seeking Consular office, that person may not serve in any federal executive capacity. This is the express exception to §7.4(5) and does not bar service outside the executive branch.

- **Cross-refs:** §7.4

### §2.18 — Domestic Insurrection

During armed insurrection against the constitutional order, the Civic Consul must report to the Assembly at statutory intervals for the duration of the response, as the Legat Consul reports to the Senate. No required report may be withheld because operations are ongoing.

## ARTICLE III — THE LEGISLATURE

### §3.1 — The Legislature

The Legislature comprises the Assembly and the Senate. All legislation originates in the Assembly.

- **Time limits:**
  - The Senate may review and amend an Assembly-passed bill and must act within 90 days, or any shorter period defined by statute
  - By majority of its full seated membership, the Senate may once extend review by up to 30 days

### §3.2 — The Assembly

(1) States determine their own Assembly district arrangements, subject to §3.3. Single-member districts use majority-based Ranked Choice Voting; multi-member districts use proportional Ranked Choice Voting.

- **Time limits:**
  - (5) Mid-term Assembly vacancies are filled by special election within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days, unless the remainder of the term is shorter than that period
- **Cross-refs:** §3.3

### §3.3 — Electoral Districts

States draw their own Assembly district maps subject to five criteria: substantially equal population per representative, accounting for the number of members elected by each district; geographic contiguity; compliance with §1.6 non-discrimination; no material dilution of minority voting power; and reasonable compactness. Voter political affiliation data, voting history data, and incumbent residence may not be…

- **Cross-refs:** §1.6

### §3.4 — Legislative Standards

Every bill must address a single clearly defined subject. The full text of a bill must be published to the NRS for the minimum period defined by statute before final passage; substantial amendment restarts that period.

- **Thresholds:**
  - Each chamber may expel a member by 2/3 of full seated membership, effective immediately

### §3.5 — The Senate

(1) The Senate comprises two senators per State regardless of population, elected for six-year staggered terms. A mid-term vacancy is filled as the State provides, within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days, unless the remainder of the term is shorter than that period.

- **Time limits:**
  - A mid-term vacancy is filled as the State provides, within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days, unless the remainder of the term is shorter than that period

### §3.6 — Treaties and Trade Agreements

Treaties and compact agreements require ratification by 2/3 of the full seated Senate. Trade agreements concluded by the Legat Consul take effect upon approval by an absolute majority of the full seated membership of both chambers.

- **Thresholds:**
  - Treaties and compact agreements require ratification by 2/3 of the full seated Senate
  - Trade agreements concluded by the Legat Consul take effect upon approval by an absolute majority of the full seated membership of both chambers
  - Withdrawal requires affirmation by 2/3 of the full seated Senate within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days of the notice
- **Time limits:**
  - Withdrawal requires affirmation by 2/3 of the full seated Senate within the period defined by statute, not exceeding 90 days of the notice

### §3.7 — Legislative Process and Passage

Legislation requires an absolute majority of the full seated membership of both chambers unless this Constitution provides otherwise. Higher thresholds are counted from the full seated membership of the chamber or chambers specified.

- **Thresholds:**
  - Legislation requires an absolute majority of the full seated membership of both chambers unless this Constitution provides otherwise
  - Where this Constitution requires a simple majority of a chamber, it means a majority of members voting, a quorum being present

### §3.8 — Legislative Oversight

The Legislature may not withhold records, deliberations, votes, or draft legislation from the Monitors. The LM may review pending legislation only upon request by a member.


### §3.9 — Independent Statutory Agencies

The Legislature may establish independent agencies outside the authority of either executive and may alter or abolish agencies established by statute. The Legislature may not impair the independence of a function this Constitution assigns to an independent agency.


### §3.9.a — Operational Independence of Agencies

An independent agency established by this Constitution or by the Legislature is operationally independent of both executives and of the Legislature in the exercise of its functions, subject to this Constitution and applicable law.


### §3.10 — Federal Legislative Competence

The Legislature may legislate only within powers assigned to the Republic by this Constitution and powers reasonably necessary to carry those powers into effect. Powers not assigned to the Republic remain with the States and their local governments.


### §3.11 — Delegation of Authority

The Legislature may by statute delegate defined authority within federal legislative competence to either Consul or to an independent agency under §3.9. A delegation must state its scope and conditions.

- **Cross-refs:** §3.9

## ARTICLE IV — THE JUDICIAL ARCHITECTURE

### §4.1 — Court Structure

The Republic's judiciary consists of district courts, appellate courts, and the Supreme Court. The Legislature may establish, abolish, and define the jurisdiction of inferior courts by statute.


### §4.2 — Judicial Appointments and Independence

(1) The Civic Consul shall nominate inferior court judges from the Judicial Pool under §9.8 without undue delay following a vacancy. The Senate confirms by a majority of its full seated membership.

- **Thresholds:**
  - (3) The Legislature may remove an inferior court judge by 2/3 of both chambers for serious misconduct, abuse of office, or permanent incapacity
- **Cross-refs:** §9.8

### §4.3 — The Supreme Court

The Supreme Court consists of the number of justices established by statute, never fewer than five. Justices serve single non-renewable 12-year terms in staggered classes as nearly equal as practicable.

- **Thresholds:**
  - Altering the number of seats requires 2/3 of both chambers

### §4.3.a — Supreme Court Justice Removal

A Supreme Court justice may be removed by 2/3 of both chambers for serious misconduct, abuse of office, or permanent incapacity; the Legislature's determination that grounds exist is final. Disagreement with a decision is not grounds for removal.

- **Thresholds:**
  - A Supreme Court justice may be removed by 2/3 of both chambers for serious misconduct, abuse of office, or permanent incapacity

### §4.4 — Supreme Court Selection

(1) The Civic Consul nominates SC justices from the Judicial Pool under §9.8. Any pool member meeting the statutory qualifications for the Supreme Court is eligible.

- **Time limits:**
  - (2) The Civic Consul nominates within the period defined by statute following a vacancy, not to exceed 90 days
  - (3) The Senate must vote within the period defined by statute following nomination, not to exceed 120 days
- **Cross-refs:** §9.8

### §4.4.a — Public Confirmation

If the Civic Consul fails to nominate, or the Senate fails to vote, within the applicable period under §4.4, the Temporary Associate Justice is submitted for public confirmation at the next federal electoral period. Confirmation requires 60% of votes cast under §7.3.

- **Cross-refs:** §4.4, §7.3, §8.1

### §4.5 — Judicial Review and Scope

(1) Courts may declare legislative acts, executive orders, and administrative decisions unconstitutional and void. (2) Courts decide constitutional and legal questions, not policy questions.

- **Cross-refs:** §16.2, §17.3, §2.14, §2.2

### §4.5.a — Judicial Decisions and Privacy

Judicial decisions are public. Courts must protect personally identifying information where its disclosure is unnecessary to understand the decision, enforce the law, or preserve public accountability.


### §4.6 — Judgment Recognition

Judgments, official records, and authenticated documents valid in one jurisdiction of the Republic are recognized in all others. A judgment may be denied recognition only where the issuing court lacked jurisdiction or the judgment was obtained by fraud.

- **Cross-refs:** §17.4

## ARTICLE V — CITIZENSHIP AND NATIONAL IDENTITY

### §5.1 — Citizenship

A child is a citizen from birth if at least one parent is a citizen, if born within the Republic to a legal-resident parent, or if born within the Republic and otherwise stateless. Citizens acquire full citizenship rights, including voting and eligibility for office, at 18.

- **Cross-refs:** §5.1.b

### §5.1.a — State and Territory Voting Citizenship

Every citizen is a voting citizen of exactly one State or Territory, determined by residence or domicile under applicable law. No law may leave a citizen without a State or Territory of voting citizenship.


### §5.1.b — Civic and Constitutional Proficiency

The Republic shall maintain a civic and constitutional proficiency assessment administered by an independent agency under §3.9. The assessment must comply with §1.6 and may not evaluate political opinion or cultural loyalty.

- **Cross-refs:** §1.6, §3.9

### §5.2 — Legal Resident Rights

Legal residents may work, enter contracts, own property, and access the social-state systems under Article XII. They do not hold federal voting rights or eligibility for federal office.


### §5.3 — Custodial Care of Children

Where removal of a parent or guardian would deprive a child of their primary caregiver, the Republic must ensure the child's care, housing, and connection to remaining family, and the removal order is stayed until custodial arrangements are resolved. No child of a citizen or legal resident may be deported because of the child's own documentation status.

## ARTICLE VI — IMMIGRATION AND RESIDENCY

### §6.1 — The Two-Stage Immigration System

Legal residency requires sponsorship under the applicable State or federal framework, followed by federal certification, except as otherwise provided by this Constitution. States retain discretion over whom they sponsor but may not apply sponsorship criteria that violate §1.6.

- **Cross-refs:** §1.17, §1.6

### §6.2 — Enforcement, Removal, and Appeals

(1) Only the federal government may remove a person from the Republic. (2) State sponsorship denials may be challenged on grounds of discrimination contrary to §1.6.

- **Cross-refs:** §1.6

### §6.3 — Asylum and Protection

(1) An asylum claim must be determined before removal may be executed. (2) Where a federal court grants asylum, the person acquires legal residency by operation of that determination;

- **Time limits:**
  - A federal legal guardian must be appointed by an independent federal court with jurisdiction over the point of contact within 72 hours
- **Cross-refs:** §1.18

### §6.3.a — Unexecuted Removal Orders

A person whose removal cannot be executed remains at liberty, subject to all applicable laws of the jurisdiction in which they reside.

## ARTICLE VII — ELECTIONS AND FEDERAL OFFICE

### §7.1 — Consular Election System

(1) The Legat Consul is elected through two concurrent requirements: a national RCV majority and a State plurality. A candidate satisfies the State plurality requirement by receiving more first-preference votes than any other candidate in a majority of the Republic's States.


### §7.2 — Federal Elections

(1) Federal elections are held at regular intervals: Assembly elections every two years; Senate elections on the staggered six-year cycle;


### §7.2.a — Special Elections and Postponements

Special elections shall be conducted under procedures established by statute. A federal election may be postponed due to a natural disaster by a two-thirds vote of each chamber of the Legislature.


### §7.3 — Electoral Supermajority Threshold

Except for constitutional-amendment referendums governed by §17.1, any supermajority required by this Constitution in a direct vote of the citizens is 60% of votes cast, in addition to any separately established participation or turnout requirement.

- **Cross-refs:** §17.1

### §7.4 — Eligibility and Disclosure for Federal Office

(1) Every candidate for federal office and every person assuming federal office without election must publicly disclose their criminal history under penalty of perjury. A current and accurate disclosure satisfies this requirement for subsequent appointments or elevations.


### §7.5 — Removal from Federal Office

A federal officeholder whose tenure or removal is governed by this Constitution may be removed only through a mechanism established or expressly authorized by this Constitution. Criminal liability remains unaffected.


### §7.6 — Continuity and Acting Service

Selection and confirmation procedures for fixed-term constitutional offices must provide for uninterrupted succession.


### §7.7 — Official Capacity Protection

Constitutional officers and persons acting within the authority of a constitutional office are protected from personal civil liability for lawful acts performed within their official authority. This protection does not extend to criminal conduct, acts outside official authority, or violations of this Constitution.


### §7.8 — Separation of Branch Authority

No person may simultaneously hold office in more than one branch of government, except when temporarily exercising acting authority under this Constitution. During such acting service, the person retains their original office or mandate but may not exercise any authority of that office.


### §7.9 — Inviolability of Electoral Mandates

An elected constitutional mandate may not be shortened, suspended, or terminated except through a mechanism expressly provided or authorized by this Constitution.


### §7.10 — Ethics and Financial Disclosure

(1) A financial interest is disqualifying where it creates a direct and material conflict with the impartial exercise of official duties. (2) Except for Monitor Generals as provided in Article IX, constitutional officers must disclose their financial interests, holdings, income sources, and liabilities to the Monitor responsible for their office, within a period defined by statute after taking office and annually…


### §7.12 — Post-Service Restrictions

Constitutional officers may not exploit non-public information, privileged access, or official influence obtained through their service for private benefit after leaving office. The Legislature may establish post-service restrictions and cooling-off periods by statute.


### §7.13 — Federal Public Service Ethics

The Legislature must establish disclosure and recusal requirements for federal employees and military personnel whose duties present material conflicts of interest. The responsible Monitor must audit institutional compliance.


### §7.14 — Compensation of Federal Officials

Compensation of federal officials is established by statute. No federal official may determine their own compensation or that of their direct supervisors other than through such a statute.


### §7.15 — Authority by Constitutional Operation

Authority conferred, transferred, or assumed by operation of this Constitution vests immediately upon satisfaction of the constitutional conditions, without oath, acceptance, publication, or other implementing act unless expressly required by this Constitution. The change must be recorded to the NRS as soon as practicable.

## ARTICLE VIII — ELECTORAL FINANCE AND CAMPAIGN CONDUCT

### §8.1 — Electoral Finance

All contributions to electoral campaigns must be disclosed in full to the Elections Panel, including the identity of every contributor and the amount given, without threshold or exception. The Elections Panel publishes contribution records to the NRS; publication may present contributions below a threshold defined by statute in aggregate or generalized form to protect the privacy of individual contributors.

- **Cross-refs:** §1.5

### §8.2 — Campaign Conduct

Candidate impersonation, vote suppression, and interference with election administration are prohibited. Campaign communications must clearly identify their sponsor.


### §8.3 — Political Neutrality of Public Communication Resources

Public communication resources, including those operated by private entities using public resources, shall be made available for electoral purposes on politically neutral and nondiscriminatory terms established by statute.


### §8.4 — Electoral Assistance

The Legislature may provide impartial electoral assistance by statute to support the exercise of the rights guaranteed under §1.8.

- **Cross-refs:** §1.8

## ARTICLE IX — THE MONITORS

### §9.1 — Three Monitors

## ARTICLE XII — SOCIAL STATE AND ECONOMIC RIGHTS

### §12.1 — The Social State

The Republic is a social state. Every Inhabitant, meaning every person present within the Republic's jurisdiction, is entitled to conditions of life consistent with human dignity, which the Republic fulfills through three mandatory guarantees: universally accessible basic healthcare; social assistance sufficient to assist in maintaining basic material conditions; and free compulsory education for every child.


### §12.1.a — The Monetary Authority

The Monetary Authority is a constitutionally mandated independent agency under §3.9.a, governed by §3.9 except as this section provides. It is responsible for monetary policy, currency issuance, fiscal integrity certification, and the National Endowment functions assigned by this Article.

- **Cross-refs:** §3.9, §3.9.a

### §12.1.b — Monetary Authority Reporting

The Monetary Authority publishes a detailed State of the Economy report to the NRS, the Assembly, the Legat Consul, and the Civic Consul no less than semi-annually, or more frequently as statute requires. The report independently assesses economic conditions, fiscal sustainability, performance against the Republic's fiscal obligations, and areas requiring improvement, including the MA's actions within its authority.


### §12.2 — Fiscal Accountability

(1) The annual budget originates in the Assembly. Each Consul prepares and submits to the Assembly a proposed budget for their respective domain, published to the NRS upon submission, by the deadline defined by statute; absent a statutory deadline, no later than 120 days before the start of the fiscal year.

- **Time limits:**
  - absent a statutory deadline, no later than 120 days before the start of the fiscal year
- **Cross-refs:** §12.1, §13.1, §14.2, §3.10

### §12.2.a — Extraordinary Defensive Expenditure

Where an active or imminent attack requires immediate defensive expenditure beyond available appropriations, and the Assembly is unable to act, the Legat Consul may authorize expenditure necessary for the immediate defense of the Republic. The Legat Consul shall report such expenditure to the Speaker of the Assembly without undue delay.


### §12.3 — Taxing Power and National Revenue Fund

The Legislature has exclusive authority to levy taxes, duties, and tariffs by statute. States, Territories, and local governments retain authority to tax within their own jurisdictions, subject to §12.5.

- **Cross-refs:** §12.2, §12.5, §3.6

The National Endowment is a constitutionally established reserve fund that backstops the Republic’s social state obligations under §12.1 during a Severe Revenue Contraction — a decline in national revenue that meets a threshold the Monetary Authority defines and publishes to the NRS before applying it, as certified by the Monetary Authority. ### §12.5 — Internal Commerce

No State or Territory may impose requirements on goods, services, or economic activity originating in another State or Territory that are more burdensome than those applied to its own. The Legislature may establish uniform internal commerce standards by statute.

The Legislature shall establish by statute a fiscal equalization mechanism ensuring that every State and Territory has fiscal capacity reasonably sufficient to fulfill the Social State obligations under §12.1 and the rights guaranteed under Article I. ## ARTICLE XIV — MILITARY AUTHORIZATION AND ACCOUNTABILITY

### §14.1 — Authorized Purposes for Military Force

Military force may be used only for two purposes: protection of the Republic’s territory, citizens, and treaty allies; and, with legislative authorization stating the specific grounds, response to crimes against humanity as defined under international law. The Legislature applies that definition directly in each authorization; no prior international recognition or finding is required.


### §14.2 — Defense Authorization and Allocation

A law authorizing military operations shall establish their purposes and scope and provide for the orderly withdrawal of deployed personnel and fulfillment of obligations arising from the operation. The Legat Consul shall provide a proposed plan of action and budget, including provision for those responsibilities.

- **Cross-refs:** §2.14

### §14.4 — Treaty-Based Military Authorization

A treaty duly ratified under this Constitution constitutes lawful authorization for military action required by its terms, within the purposes permitted by §14.1. Military operations conducted under treaty authority are subject to the command, notification, operational review, and appropriation provisions of §2.2 and Article XIV.

- **Cross-refs:** §14.1, §2.2

### §14.5 — State Forces

States may maintain organized forces for disaster response, civil emergencies, and the preservation of public order within their territories. Such forces are subject to Article I.

## ARTICLE XVIII — FEDERAL PROPERTY AND NATIONAL TRUST

### §18.1 — The Use Doctrine

The federal government holds no territorial sovereignty within any State or Territory. Federal Operational Use Rights confer functional operational control over a designated footprint for an authorized purpose and terminate when that function ends.

- **Cross-refs:** §18.3

### §18.2 — Seat of Government

The Seat of Government shall be hosted by a State or Territory selected through a process established by statute. The hosting arrangement shall establish the Republic's Operational Use Rights over the designated federal footprint in accordance with §18.1.

### §18.3 — Operational Use Rights

The federal government shall publish notice of termination to the NRS in accordance with statute. Upon termination, the designated land and improvements revert to the host State or Territory, unless the Republic and the host establish a new arrangement by mutual agreement.


### §18.4 — The National Trust

(1) Land designated to the National Trust is held for the permanent benefit and use of the people of the Republic. Designation requires the consent of the State in which the land is situated and does not alter that State's territorial sovereignty.

- **Cross-refs:** §18.4, §18.5

### §18.5 — National Trust Land in Territories

Designation of land within a Territory to the National Trust shall be governed by its Territorial Compact or, where the Compact is silent, by statute, subject to the requirements of §18.4.

## ARTICLE XX — ASSOCIATED COMMUNITIES

### §20.1 — Nature of Associated Community Status

An Associated Community is a sovereign political community that remains outside the Republic in a consensual compact relationship with it under this Article. Associated Community status is available to recognized Indigenous nations under Article XVI and to any independent nation outside the Republic.


### §20.2 — Compact Terms and Equal Sovereignty

The Republic and an Associated Community are equal sovereign parties to their compact. The compact may establish representation, consultation, and participation arrangements between the parties.


### §20.3 — Compact Conditions and Compliance

(1) No compact may be entered or ratified with an entity whose governance practices include, authorize, or systematically permit violations of the non-derogable rights in §1.16.a against any person within its jurisdiction. (2) Allegations of violations of §1.16.a arising under an association compact shall be subject to review through the compact's independent arbitration mechanism.

- **Cross-refs:** §1.16.a

### §20.4 — Sovereign Mobility

An Associated Community may terminate its compact through its own democratic or traditional governance process. The Republic shall neither prevent nor penalize that decision.


### §20.5 — Reciprocal Obligations and Review

Each compact shall define the reciprocal obligations of the parties and establish an independent arbitration mechanism. Either party may request a review of the compact by written notice registered to the NRS.


### §20.6 — Compact Ratification

### §20.7 — Compact Continuity

An association compact remains binding upon the Republic notwithstanding a change of government. Amendment or renegotiation of the compact shall proceed in accordance with its terms and the applicable constitutional treaty procedures.
