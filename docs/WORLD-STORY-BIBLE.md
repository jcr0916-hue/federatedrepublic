# World Story Bible

**Purpose:** durable canon and writing constraints for Torenthia World content.

This is the working source for facts that must remain true across stories. It replaces the old
active `world-canon.md`, `CHARACTER-REFERENCE.md`, and individual arc-framework files. Historical
planning notes remain under `docs/archive/world/`.

**Authority order**
1. Published World pieces and their front matter establish what actually happened.
2. The current Constitution controls constitutional mechanism.
3. This file records durable geography, identities, relationships, and story constraints.
4. `WORLD-STORY-STATUS.md` records where live threads currently stand and what remains open.

Do not treat an archived plan as canon when it conflicts with a published piece or this file.

## Founding era

The long-form prequel `before-the-republic.html` is canonical founding history.

### Predecessor order

- The Federated Republic was created by the voluntary union of **three sovereign predecessor countries**: the **Harren Union**, the **Torenthian Commonwealth**, and the **Vardan Confederacy**.
- **Valedon** participated fully in the constitutional negotiations and helped shape parts of the draft but declined to join. This is the origin of its durable identity as **“the nation that almost joined.”**
- Predecessor-country borders do **not** map cleanly onto present State boundaries. The transition, Statehood rules, territorial choices, and later constitutional processes produced the current internal map.
- Exploratory regional talks began in **Year -2**. The formal Convention for the Formation of a Federated Republic began in **Year -1**.
- Ratification creates **Day Zero / Year 0** and begins the Article XIX transition. Full constitutional operation follows through the transition rather than appearing instantaneously on ratification.
- The Republic’s first Assembly was fragmented and failed to elect a Civic Consul within the formation window. The §2.6.a fallback operated: the Assembly Speaker became Acting Civic Consul, the Assembly nominated candidates, and a national election supplied the first full-authority Civic Consul. This event is the in-world founding instance underlying the separate `scenario-the-formation.html` constitutional test.
- By **Year 4**, the Republic was negotiating the Toren River relationship with Caldris as one sovereign counterparty.
- By **Year 8**, Casimir Rehn was Legat Consul and Ines Carrow was serving in Foreign Affairs.
- The founding prequel closes at **Year 12, Month 9**, immediately before `torenthia-news-001.html`.

### Founding-generation people

- **Mira Venn** — Commonwealth administrative coordinator and central constitutional negotiator. Strong advocate for bounded institutions, direct rights enforcement, and a divided executive. She does not become a federal officeholder in the founding transition.
- **Edran Vale** — Harren constitutional/political leader. Publicly names the project as a possible federated republic in Year -2. Later serves in the founding Senate and draws one of the shorter staggered founding terms.
- **Tomas Arven** — Vardan negotiator and persistent skeptic of concentrated federal authority. Helps shape the external/domestic executive split and remains a public critic of the government he helped design.
- **Sela Oran** — Calden municipal records specialist whose cross-border-record problem becomes the practical seed of the National Record System. She does not become a founding political officeholder.
- **Ilyan Seret** — Valedon Foreign Minister and serious Convention participant. Leads a government that ultimately declines the Treaty of Union without becoming hostile to the new Republic.
- **Ilyan Orre** — Korda representative who forces the Convention to confront prior Indigenous sovereignty and consent. His arrival and “fifth chair” become part of founding political memory.

### Founding-story constraints

- The Republic is not born from total state collapse or a single catastrophic war. The predecessor countries are viable governments that choose union because a combined order promises greater security, prosperity, bargaining power, and administrative coherence.
- External pressures matter—Caldris’s scale, Sunderland’s increasing unpredictability, Lake Varda, the Toren, and maritime competition—but no neighbor alone “causes” the Republic.
- Founding institutions emerge from concrete coordination problems rather than from one philosopher’s master plan.
- The NRS originates in conflicting but authentic predecessor records. Its core lesson is that one government cannot safely depend on several competing official memories.
- The Monitors arise from the refusal to let institutions grade their own constitutional compliance, while remaining information-first rather than a coercive fourth branch.
- Indigenous sovereignty is acknowledged as prior to the Republic, not granted by it. Korda’s participation helps force this distinction into the constitutional settlement.
- Valedon’s refusal is principled rather than antagonistic. It remains a close external neighbor and counterfactual to the Republic’s founding bargain.

## Geography

Torenthia is a coastal country on the **western seaboard of the crescent continent**. The
**Western Sea** lies west and southwest. The **Torenthian Alps** run along the northern border.
**Lake Varda** defines much of the eastern frontier. Continental land continues north and south;
Torenthia is not a peninsula.

### Water and neighbors

- **Toren River:** Caldris holds the main-stem headwaters; Torenthia is downstream on that stem.
  An eastern tributary rises in the Torenthian Alps, runs north into Caldris, and carries most
  dry-season flow. The Year 4 framework creates mutual dependence rather than a simple upstream /
  downstream relationship.
- **Lake Varda:** shared with Sunderland. Torenthian shores run through Norvane, Rhovane, Kelvant,
  and Korda. Coast-guard command is in Rhondel; Varenne Station is in Korda.
- **Caldris:** north/northeast beyond the Alps; upstream river power and a major regional actor.
- **Sunderland:** east; fracturing/at war; source of the refugee crossings and border incidents.
- **Valedon:** south; “the nation that almost joined.”
- **Seronne:** southeast buffer state; northwest corner touches Lake Varda.
- **Marisvia:** west across the Western Sea; maritime democracy and closest constitutional peer.

### Geography rules

- The map and this written canon move together; never silently “fix” one against the other.
- Scenario-only place names are not World canon.
- Map detail follows coverage: do not add a named facility, town, border, or landmark before a
  published story earns it.

## Durable story architecture

### Korda Convention

**Premise:** a certified whole-Territory statehood petition collided with the already-scheduled
Kelvant–Korda lake-corridor merger referendum inside §15.5.a’s 90-day window, forcing the
Republic’s first Territory Convention.

**Mechanism:** §15.5.a sends competing petitions affecting different defined portions into one
Convention. The underlying referenda do not proceed. Korda’s Assembly delegates constitute the
Convention; it has 90 active days from first session to produce a resolution. A resolution still
requires the applicable ratification/approval path.

**Locked published law/story:** the JMC applied §15.5.a. The corridor vote was canceled.
The Supreme Court upheld that application 6–3; the whole-Territory petition counts as a different
defined portion. The stay paused only the clock, not the Convention. The Convention continues.

**Conflict:** corridor residents had a certified, scheduled vote that disappeared before they could
cast it; inland/whole-Territory advocates argue the corridor’s departure would structurally weaken
the rest of Korda. Neither side is a villain. Do not turn the Convention into three tidy factions
or predetermine its final settlement.

### Argent Ridge / Riverglow

**Premise:** beloved federal land between Varek and Norvane was assumed protected but was merely
Trust-eligible. A development statute exposed the gap.

**Mechanisms:** §13.1 citizen repeal and §18.4 National Trust designation are separate. Repeal can
stop the disposal statute but cannot itself protect the land. Trust designation needs the federal
supermajority plus host-State consent.

**Locked published position:** Varek consented; Norvane refused. Norvane’s reason is Lake Varda
arrival-zone housing capacity, not simple tax-base politics. The Trust designation is blocked unless
Norvane changes position. The separate repeal campaign continues.

**Emotional rule:** “Argent Ridge” is the official/survey name; **Riverglow** is the local name.
The story works because all safeguards are individually reasonable and collectively produce a
tragic deadlock.

### Corindal Industrial Partners — long game

**Status:** intentionally dormant.

**Origin:** after *Corindal Industrial Partners v. NRS Panel*, the company cannot erase the
lawfully-published procurement records. The permanent record is the wound.

**Long-game motive:** seek a future Court that reads the EM’s §9.1.b publication mandate more
narrowly. The company itself cannot contribute electorally; executives can make capped disclosed
individual contributions and lawful independent expenditures remain possible if uncoordinated.

**Chain:** district races → Assembly composition → Civic Consul selection → SC nominations;
§4.3 court expansion by 2/3 statute is the extreme endgame lever.

**Moral rule:** no cartoon conspiracy and no secret lawbreaking. Everything important should be
legal and disclosed. The constitutional question is whether sunlight constrains patient money or
merely records it. This is the slowest burn in the World and should remain mostly quiet.

## Storytelling rules

1. **Published beats beat plans.** If an old outline and a publication differ, the publication wins.
2. **Local stakes carry the piece.** No single article needs to explain the entire constitutional web.
3. **Do not artificially de-converge events.** Several threads may land in the same period; density
   is normal. Use clear local framing instead of moving events merely for tidiness.
4. **No villain requirement.** The strongest arcs come from sensible provisions colliding with
   sympathetic interests.
5. **Dry records matter.** NRS notices, filings, and routine procedural items can quietly advance an
   arc before journalists or characters understand the pattern.
6. **Mundane life remains mundane.** Not every transit, library, weather, or ordinary-government item
   needs a hidden story hook.
7. **Check names before inventing.** Reuse established characters where appropriate and preserve
   outlet/office continuity.
8. **worldSeq is publication/reading order, not necessarily world-time order.** Use `worldDate` when
   chronology matters.
9. **The published World front matter is the historical index.** Do not maintain a second giant
   hand-written chronology in this file.
