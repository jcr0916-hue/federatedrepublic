# Continuity of Government — Working Draft v2

**Status:** discussion draft. Nothing here is in the Constitution, the repo, or PR #119. It replaces the earlier two-Designee version. Wording is built against the current text on branch `constitution-door-closing-2026-10-10`.

---

## 1. The design in one paragraph

The ordinary chain of succession handles everything short of total failure. Survivors, acting Consuls and the 10-day vacancy rule cover those cases. If nobody in the Legat succession order can be reached, the head of state of a lot-drawn State becomes the **Emergency Designee**, who commands the military and acts to restore a functioning republic. The heads of state of the other reachable States form the **State Emergency Council**, which directs the Designee and can replace them. Authority returns automatically as people can take their offices. Each chamber is restored when members hold a majority of its seats, and the Council and Designee dissolve when both Consular offices can run their ordinary replacement process. If the Republic is not restored in six months, the States and Territories are sovereign and may act as such. That final provision is drafted in section 4 and is not yet placed.

## 2. New section

### §2.9.a — State Emergency Council

(1) At each federal electoral period the Elections Panel draws an ordered list of the States by public lot and publishes it to the NRS. A State's head of state is the officer its constitution designates or, absent a designation, the presiding officer of its legislature.

(2) If no one in the order of succession for the Legat Consul's office under §2.9 and §2.16 can be reached, the head of state of the first reachable State on the list is the Emergency Designee. The Designee commands the military and acts to restore a functioning republic, answerable to the Council, and holds no other federal authority. A head of state may hold this office notwithstanding §7.8, retains their State office, and their State provides for its functions during the service.

(3) While a Designee serves, the heads of state of the other reachable States form the State Emergency Council, each joining by publication to the NRS or its parallel system. The Council acts by majority of its members to address the emergency and to reconstitute constitutional government, directs the Designee, and may replace them with another of its members. The Designee and the Council are bound by Article I.

(4) A person above the Designee in the order of succession who can act holds their office at once on publishing an official act, and the Designee yields command to them. A member elected to fill a seat during a Council period joins the order of succession only when that member's chamber is restored.

(5) A chamber is restored when members holding a majority of its seats have taken them. The Legat Consul's office is restored when the Senate is restored and a senator holds the office under §2.9. The Civic Consul's office is restored when the Assembly is restored and §2.6.a can proceed. When both offices are restored, the Council and the Designee dissolve.

(6) If both offices are not restored within six months after the Designee took office, the Council and the Designee end and the Reversion provision in section 4 applies.

## 3. Changes to existing sections

### §2.11 — Emergency Governance

**Paragraph 1.** Replace the opening sentence:

> When extraordinary circumstances prevent the ordinary functioning of constitutional government, either Consul, or a majority of the available members of either legislative chamber, may declare a continuity emergency, notwithstanding ordinary quorum requirements. The declaration states the circumstances, is published to the NRS, and is sent to every State and Territory.

**Paragraph 2.** Keep PR #119's text and make two changes. Add as the penultimate sentence:

> During a continuity emergency, a threshold this Constitution calculates from the full seated membership of a chamber is calculated from the members able to act.

and replace the final sentence ("Where a Consul is unavailable, acting authority passes under §2.9 and §2.16.") with:

> Where a Consul is unavailable, authority passes under §2.9, §2.16 and §2.9.a.

**New paragraph after paragraph 2:**

> Ten days after a declaration under this section or, absent a declaration, after ten days in which no officer of the federal legislature or executive has published an act to the NRS or its parallel system, any legislative seat or Consular office whose holder has been unavailable throughout is vacant. A Consular office is then filled under §2.9 or §2.6.a. Each State and Territory may fill its vacant federal seats by emergency election under its own law, notwithstanding the ordinary vacancy process, and a member so elected serves the remainder of the term.

The 30-day lapse, expedited SC review and the "yield to ordinary procedures" paragraph from PR #119 stay as they are.

### §2.9 — Legat Consul Succession and Incapacity

**(1)** Current: "Full succession activates upon the Legat Consul's death, resignation, or removal."
Proposed: "Full succession activates upon the Legat Consul's death, resignation, removal, or vacancy under §2.11."

**(4)** Current: "The military chain of command recognizes succession from the constitutional moment of transfer. Any order issued in the name of the prior Legat Consul after transfer is void."
Proposed: "The military chain of command recognizes succession from the constitutional moment of transfer and obeys the first person in the order of succession it can reach. Any order issued in the name of the prior Legat Consul after transfer is void."

### §2.16 — Executive Incapacity (two new subsections)

> (10) A Consul or successor whose whereabouts or condition cannot be established is treated as unable to exercise authority, and authority passes under subsection (1), skipping anyone who cannot be reached. Authority returns to a holder at once when the holder publishes an official act, and orders given in the interim stand.
>
> (11) An acting Legat Consul holds the full authority of the office. An Acting Civic Consul holds the authority described in §2.6.a(2) [and may also act as §2.11 permits during a continuity emergency].

## 4. Reversion provision (placement open)

> **§[ ] — Reversion to the States and Territories**
> If the Republic is not restored within the period in §2.9.a(6), the States and Territories are sovereign governments and may act as such, including by reconstituting the Republic. This is not secession under §15.7. The rights in §1.19.a continue to bind every government.

**Placement options**
- **§15.7.a, in Article XV beside State Immutability.** It sits next to the rule it is an exception to, and a reader checking secession will find it.
- **Article XIX, with Transition and caretaker provisions.** It is the Republic's end state, parallel to its founding transition.
- **Standing at the end of §2.9.a(6) with no separate section.** It is the shortest form, but the most consequential sentence in the Constitution would sit inside a succession section.

My recommendation is the first or second, as a separate section.

**Entrenchment.** Add the reversion section to the §17.1(4) list so no ordinary amendment can remove the end state or shorten the six months. §2.11 already bars amendment during an emergency.

## 5. Notes on how it fits the existing text

- **No new thresholds.** The chambers' own restoration test counts seats, because the Constitution gives the Senate no quorum of its own (§3.5(4) leaves procedure to the Senate), and the Assembly's quorum (§3.2(3)) is a "majority of full seated membership" without saying whether vacancies shrink it. Counting seats removes the ambiguity for this purpose. A one-sentence clarification of "full seated membership" would also help ordinary mass vacancies.
- **§7.15.** Authority vests by constitutional operation. The NRS is evidence of the failure, not its source. Joining the Council by publication is an express requirement, which §7.15 permits.
- **§7.9.** The 10-day vacancy is an express mechanism for ending a mandate, so the bar on other mechanisms is satisfied.
- **§3.2(5).** The 90-day special-election limit and Elections Panel administration are displaced by the State emergency elections in §2.11.
- **Territories.** They hold Assembly seats, so they fill those seats by emergency election and take part in restoring the Civic side. They do not sit on the Council or serve as Designee. Their governments continue under their Compacts, and they are sovereign alongside the States under section 4.
- **Civic gap.** When only the Assembly is destroyed, the Civic Consul's office stays empty until it refills. An Acting Civic Consul already cannot veto and can only maintain existing policy, so the gap costs little. Ministers keep administering, and the Senate may legislate alone under §2.11.

## 6. Open points

1. **A returning member after the 10 days.** The vacancy is final once it takes effect. A senator who reappears on day 11 does not resume the seat even if no replacement has been seated. This is clean but harsh. Alternative: resumption allowed until the seat is actually filled.
2. **Acting Civic Consul scope.** The bracket in §2.16(11) lets an Acting Civic Consul act under §2.11 in a continuity emergency. "Maintaining existing policy" may be too narrow in a catastrophe.
3. **Placement of the reversion section** (section 4).
4. **PR #119.** Its §2.11 wording is kept in this draft. The choice is to update that PR or open a new branch.
