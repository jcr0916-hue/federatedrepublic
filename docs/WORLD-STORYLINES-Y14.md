## A. The Working Majority — Thoss and the spring Assembly election

**Settled (John, 10.10):** Thoss remains Civic Consul until removed by a constructive vote of no confidence (§2.6(1)), resignation, or the 12-year service limit. A general election does not trigger a new formation vote; §2.6.a(1) was corrected to read "following a vacancy."

**Premise.** The spring Year 14 Assembly election cannot remove Thoss by itself. It changes the arithmetic that could. The story is the working count (266 published, down from 274) and whether any coalition can agree on a named successor, the only way §2.6(1) removes her.

**Beats.**
1. Y14 M1–M3 — Campaign coverage frames the Assembly races as a verdict on her government without her name on any ballot (her restraint line from the status doc). Korda reads as process integrity; Fiscal Equalization as the governing success if the reform lands.
2. Y14 M4 — Results. Reporting on the new working count; parties test whether a successor majority exists.
3. Y14 M5 — Either the count holds or a constructive motion is floated. **Write both branches fully before choosing** (narrative-control rule). The constitutional point either way: losing seats is not losing office; only a named successor with an absolute majority is.

## B. Riverglow Unlocked — Argent Ridge meets the northern arrivals

**Premise.** Norvane refused Trust consent over northern-arrival housing capacity, not tax base (locked canon). A federal northern-arrival housing statute that funds capacity elsewhere in Norvane removes the reason for refusal. The deadlock stays tragic until a different policy, not a better argument, changes Norvane's position.

**Machinery.** Ordinary legislation and appropriation; **Thoss's first reconsideration notice (§2.7)**, recommending deletion of a clause that would site temporary housing on parcels bordering the Ridge (she supports the bill, but not that siting); the Assembly Speaker declines or grants the pause; §18.4 Trust designation (2/3 of both chambers + host-State consent), still requiring Norvane's own decision; the §13.1 repeal campaign continuing on its separate clock.

**Characters.** Aleth Fenn (northern correspondent); Norvane's government (unnamed so far — name one official); Varek Assembly delegates from news 069 (Reyna Oskitt); Thoss.

**Beats.**
1. Y13 M12 — NRS: housing bill introduced; committee referral. News: Fenn connects it to Norvane's stated refusal reason.
2. Y14 M1 — Bill passes. Thoss issues a reconsideration notice on the siting clause. NRS record of the notice; the Speaker's decision (pause or not) is the beat's hinge.
3. Y14 M2 — Bill becomes law (with or without the clause). Norvane opens a review of its Trust position. Repeal campaign reports signature progress.
4. Y14 M3–M5 — Norvane decides. If it consents, the Trust designation still needs the federal 2/3 vote. Keep "repealed" and "protected" distinct to the end.

**Guardrail.** Do not let the housing bill read as a bargain struck to win consent; Norvane's position changes because its stated problem changed.

## C. The Speakers' Nominee — the Executive Monitor succession

**Premise.** Canon: Reya Solt's term expires in this cycle and an EM confirmation vote is on the spring ballot (news 013, 016; Sollis Dispatch). No nominee is named. Tor (Sollis's student) noticed "nobody else is talking about it."

**Machinery.** §9.3: the Assembly Speaker and Senate Speaker jointly nominate from the EM pool, with the LM and JM auditing and JM disclosure findings published before the vote; if they cannot agree within 60 days of the vacancy, the Senate selects from the pool by 2/3. National confirmation by simple majority at the next federal electoral period. Solt cannot be renominated (10-year non-renewable; no re-entry to any Monitor General pool).

**Story shape.** Two Speakers from different coalitions, each with a preferred pool member. The quiet story is that the EM audits the executives Thoss leads, and the Speakers know it. The JM disclosure audit surfaces something minor about one candidate (a late disclosure correction, lawful and corrected) — a dry-record beat, not a scandal.

**New names needed.** Assembly Speaker (Thoss's successor in the chair); Senate Speaker; two or three pool candidates. Check existing names first.

**Beats.**
1. Y13 M12 — NRS: Elections Panel publishes the EM pool roster and the nomination window.
2. Y14 M1 — Speakers' talks reported; Sollis *Show Your Work* explains the joint nomination and the Senate fallback.
3. Y14 M2 — Either a joint nomination or the 60-day deadline passes → Senate 2/3 selection from the pool. **Recommend the fallback**: the World has never shown it.

## D. The Valedon Accord — a civilian treaty in an election season

**Premise.** The Lake Varda conference opens in Valedon in Y14 M1 with a civilian agenda (navigation, rescue coordination, risk reduction). A realistic outcome is a narrow rescue-coordination agreement that does not settle responsibility for prior incidents.

**Machinery.** §3.6 treaty ratification by 2/3 of the full seated Senate; trade agreements are not involved. §2.4 Civic Consul consultation where terms bear on domestic interests (Kelvant/Korda shore services, Rhondel coast-guard command). It is a civilian accord, so Article XIV is not engaged; keep it that way.

**Tension.** Carrow is chief negotiator and an LC candidate. Institutional separation (established canon) means she cannot campaign on the accord in her official capacity, while her rivals can attack or claim it freely. Sandris reads it closely; Mak overstates it; Vael says almost nothing. Sunderland's non-response continues. Its absence is the accord's limit.

**Beats.**
1. Y14 M1 — Conference opens (existing clock). NRS delegation list; news on the opening agenda.
2. Y14 M2 — Text agreed ad referendum. NRS publication; the CC's consultation response on shore services.
3. Y14 M3 — Senate ratification vote. 2/3 is a real hurdle in campaign season; recommend it passes narrowly, with dissent recorded on Sunderland's absence.
4. Y14 M4 — Election-week coverage treats the accord as Carrow's record whether she wants it or not.

## E. The Fair Start Petition — a slow citizen initiative (parked idea activated)

**Premise.** The parked §8.4 idea (public financing funded by a levy on large contributions) becomes a citizen legislative initiative.

**Machinery and the twist.** §13.2(3): an initiative "may not … appropriate funds or alter revenue." A levy alters revenue, so the drafters' first version would be void. They redraft: the initiative keeps disclosure and contribution-limit reforms, and the levy goes to the Legislature as a separate ordinary bill. Two-phase petition under §13.1 (10-month phases, 10% Phase Two); if the Legislature does not enact within 90 days of the threshold, a referendum follows at 60%/50%.

**Use.** A background thread running past the election into Year 15; good *Show Your Work* material; a quiet civic counterweight to the high-politics arcs. One record every month or two.
