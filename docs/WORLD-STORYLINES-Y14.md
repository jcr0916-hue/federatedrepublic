## A. The Formation Question — *needs a constitutional reading first*

**Premise.** §2.6.a(1): "The Assembly elects the Civic Consul by absolute majority within the period defined by statute following **any general election** or vacancy; absent such statute, the constitutional default is 21 days." Read literally, the spring Year 14 Assembly election obliges the new Assembly to elect a Civic Consul within 21 days, and if it cannot, the sitting Assembly Speaker becomes Acting Civic Consul under §2.6.a(2). That is the same machinery that failed at founding (Leva Coren, `scenario-the-formation.html`).

**Conflict with current planning.** WORLD-STORY-STATUS assumes Thoss simply continues after the election, relying on §2.6(3) ("serves until removal… resignation, or exhaustion of the service limit"). The two provisions point in different directions. Either reading is a legitimate design; the text should say which.

- **Reading 1: re-formation after every Assembly election.** Thoss must win a formation vote in the new Assembly, which is a parliamentary-style reset. The story writes itself: coalition arithmetic, the 266 working count, Korda and Fiscal Equalization as her record, a possible §2.6.a fallback echoing founding.
- **Reading 2: continuity.** §2.6.a(1) applies only to a vacancy (and the first Assembly). The CC continues until a constructive vote names a successor. The election then matters through its effect on the working majority, and §2.6.a(1) needs a one-word fix ("following any vacancy").

**Decision needed from John before any piece:** which reading is the design?

**Beats under Reading 1 (sketch):** Y14 M1–M3 campaign reporting frames the Assembly election as a referendum on Thoss's government → M4 election (bundled with LC race and EM confirmation) → results and coalition talks, with the 21-day clock running → formation vote. The outcome is to be chosen later; write both branches committed before selecting (Narrative-control rule).

**Beats under Reading 2:** the same campaign, but post-election stories center the working count, and a constructive vote becomes a live threat only if a named successor emerges.

## B. Riverglow Unlocked — Argent Ridge meets the northern arrivals

**Premise.** Norvane refused Trust consent over northern-arrival housing capacity, not tax base (locked canon). A federal northern-arrival housing statute that funds capacity elsewhere in Norvane removes the reason for refusal. The deadlock stays tragic until a different policy, not a better argument, changes Norvane's position.

**Machinery.** Ordinary legislation and appropriation; **Thoss's first reconsideration notice (§2.7)**, recommending deletion of a clause that would site temporary housing on parcels bordering the Ridge (she supports the bill, but not that siting); the Assembly Speaker declines or grants the pause; §18.4 Trust designation (2/3 of both chambers + host-State consent), still requiring Norvane's own decision; the §13.1 repeal campaign continuing on its separate clock.

**Characters.** Aleth Fenn (northern correspondent); Norvane's government (unnamed so far — name one official); Varek Assembly delegates from news 069 (Reyna Oskitt); Thoss.

**Beats.**
1. Y13 M12 — NRS: housing bill introduced; committee referral. News: Fenn connects it to Norvane's stated refusal reason.
2. Y14 M1 — Bill passes. Thoss issues a reconsideration notice on the siting clause. NRS record of the notice; the Speaker's decision (pause or not) is the beat's hinge.
3. Y14 M2 — Bill becomes law (with or without the clause). Norvane opens a review of its Trust position. Repeal campaign reports signature progress.
4. Y14 M3–M5 — Norvane decides. If it consents, the Trust designation still needs the federal 2/3 vote. Keep "repealed" and "protected" distinct to the end.

**Guardrail.** Do not let the housing bill read as a bargain struck to win consent; Norvane's position changes because its stated problem changed.

## C. The Speakers' Nominee — the Executive Monitor succession

**Premise.** Canon: Reya Solt's term expires in this cycle and an EM confirmation vote is on the spring ballot (news 013, 016; Sollis Dispatch). No nominee is named. Tor (Sollis's student) noticed "nobody else is talking about it."

## D. The Valedon Accord — a civilian treaty in an election season

**Machinery.** §3.6 treaty ratification by 2/3 of the full seated Senate; trade agreements are not involved. §2.4 Civic Consul consultation where terms bear on domestic interests (Kelvant/Korda shore services, Rhondel coast-guard command). ## E. The Fair Start Petition — a slow citizen initiative (parked idea activated)

**Premise.** The parked §8.4 idea (public financing funded by a levy on large contributions) becomes a citizen legislative initiative.

**Machinery and the twist.** §13.2(3): an initiative "may not … appropriate funds or alter revenue." A levy alters revenue, so the drafters' first version would be void. They redraft: the initiative keeps disclosure and contribution-limit reforms, and the levy goes to the Legislature as a separate ordinary bill. Two-phase petition under §13.1 (10-month phases, 10% Phase Two); if the Legislature does not enact within 90 days of the threshold, a referendum follows at 60%/50%.

**Use.** A background thread running past the election into Year 15; good *Show Your Work* material; a quiet civic counterweight to the high-politics arcs. One record every month or two.
