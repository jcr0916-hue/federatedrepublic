## Purpose
Record three of John's design decisions as working amendments: (2) change §12.1's education sentence from "Parents" to "Parents or guardians", so children who are wards of the State or in guardianship are covered; (3) reword the first sentence of §12.2(2) to drop "over the long term" and the ambiguous "sustain … public debt"; (4) rewrite the second paragraph of §12.6 so the Executive Monitor audits, publishes to the NRS and submits findings to the Speakers of both chambers and the Civic Consul, with no compelled legislative response.

## Shipped in this PR (on merge)
- `docs/WORKING-CONSTITUTIONAL-AMENDMENTS.md`: new "Proposed amendment 2" (§12.1), "Proposed amendment 3" (§12.2(2)) and "Proposed amendment 4" (§12.6), each with exact wording, scope, rationale, rejected alternatives and downstream updates; Updated date set to 23 November 2026.

## Decisions
- Recorded as a working amendment, not applied to the adopted text: the file's own rule is that adopted text is not modified silently and that settled proposals reach the Constitution in a release.
- Wording chosen by John: "parent or guardian", matching §5.3. Rejected: "a child's guardian" alone (can read as removing parents), "legal custodian" (custody-law narrow), "caregiver" (no legal standing). The proposal was first offered as "a child's parent or legal guardian"; John chose the plural form that matches §5.3's phrase.

- Amendment 3: "The Republic conducts its fiscal affairs to keep its public finances, including public debt, sustainable while fulfilling its constitutional obligations." Chosen because "over the long term" has no anchor and "sustain … public debt" can read as keeping the debt going. Rejected: moving "over the long term", a numeric horizon (over-specification, as in amendment 1), "responsibly" (vaguer), and a negative duty. Added to this PR, not a separate one, because all entries live in the same file and none is merged yet.

- Amendment 4: "The Executive Monitor must audit and review the mechanism's compliance with constitutional requirements, publish its findings to the NRS, and submit them to the Speakers of both legislative chambers and the Civic Consul." The sentence "The Legislature must publish a response to findings of noncompliance within a period established by statute" is deleted. John's reasoning: Monitors cannot compel action; declaring noncompliance differs from assessing; "must publish" forces an action. Matches the information-only mandate in §9.1 and the division in §9.5.a. A clause letting the chambers request Monetary Authority review was proposed and dropped by John as unnecessary: the chambers may request review at any point, and §12.2(2) already covers MA assessment of pending legislation. "Remediation by the MA" was also rejected, since the MA holds no fiscal-policy power (amendment 1).

- Amendment 4 leaves no answering step in the Constitution. If a statute keeps the 90-day response practice, the Torenthia records that cite "the reporting statute implementing §12.6" stay consistent; `scenario-the-formula.html`, its `scenarioBridges.json` entry, and one sentence in NRS-005 describe the response as a constitutional obligation and need an editorial pass (a dated note for the published record) at release, not now.
