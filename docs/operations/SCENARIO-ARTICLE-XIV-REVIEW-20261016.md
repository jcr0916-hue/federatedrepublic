# Article XIV scenario check — Second Renewal and First War (16 October 2026)

Authority: merged federal Constitution `constitution_data.json`, especially §§14.1–14.5, 19.3.a and 2.2. This is a bounded semantic review, not a general certification of all scenarios.

**The Second Renewal:** Found a substantive explanatory error: §14.3 makes the **Legat Consul** responsible for submitting the Transition and Reconstruction Plan, not the “LC.” Also clarified that submission follows the second renewal within a statutory period and Senate approval becomes mandatory **before a further renewal**. In the established narrative, the Consul and Senate may voluntarily settle the Plan early, before the second renewal. Corrected the narrator's blanket claim that failure to approve a Plan instantly terminates the operation: nonapproval prevents subsequent renewal, and §14.2's expenditure cutoff applies when authorization lapses. Existing operational milestones and outcomes remain untouched.

**The First War:** Compared the transition defense narrative to §19.3.a's caretaker-command, State-force call-up, 14-day submission and 60-day continuation rules and §14.5's ordinary constraints. The principal sequence remains supported by those provisions; no targeted correction identified in this pass. This is a transition-continuity scenario, distinct from The Second Renewal's peacetime authorization-renewal safeguard.

**Selection recommendation:** Retain both, with distinct library entry points. The First War belongs among transition/ratification scenarios because it tests transfer of wartime authority from predecessor institutions, whereas The Second Renewal tests prolonged military engagement and the Senate-approved Plan.
