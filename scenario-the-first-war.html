The First War

Day 41 of the Transition Window. A neighboring state invades before the permanent Legislature or Legat Consul can exercise the Republic's ordinary defense powers. The constitutional test is not only whether the Republic can fight, but whether it can keep becoming a constitutional government while it does.

§19.2 Transition §19.3 Caretaker Status §19.3.a Transition National-Defense Continuity §19.5 Pre-Existing Obligations §2.2 Defense Execution §14.1 Authorized Purposes §14.5 State Forces §7.2 Federal Elections

At 04:18 on Day 41, armored units of the neighboring Republic of Rendath cross the eastern frontier at three points.

The Federated Republic is forty-one days old.

It exists completely in law and incompletely in machinery. Ratification transferred sovereignty on Day Zero. The predecessor national governments no longer possess independent national authority; the institutions that remain from them operate only in the caretaker capacity preserved by Article XIX. The permanent Legislature has not yet been seated. The Republic's first Legat Consul has not yet taken office. State governments continue under their own lawful authority. Military commands, supply systems, intelligence channels and defense appropriations inherited from the predecessor order still physically exist.

For an enemy looking at the constitutional calendar, it is as attractive a moment as the Republic is ever likely to offer.

Day 41 · 04:18
The Republic is attacked before its permanent defense chain is complete.

The question is immediate: did Day Zero create a gap between the old state's ability to defend the country and the new Republic's permanent defense institutions?

The First Order

The senior predecessor national defense commander receives confirmation of the crossings at 04:31. Under the old constitutional order, the commander would have taken instructions from a predecessor executive whose sovereign authority ended on Day Zero. Under the permanent Republic, military execution would belong to the Legat Consul within the authorizations of §§14.1 and 14.2.

Neither route describes the morning that actually exists.

Section 19.3.a does.

Until the Legat Consul and Legislature are constitutionally capable of exercising their respective national-defense authorities, predecessor national institutions continuing under §19.3 may exercise only the military and defense authority reasonably necessary to defend the Republic, its territory, population and forces against actual or imminent invasion or armed attack.

The commander orders frontier units to engage.

Transition Defense Order 01

Constitutional basis: §19.3.a(1).
Trigger: confirmed armed invasion of Republic territory.
Purpose: defense of Republic territory, population and forces.
Authority claimed: only that reasonably necessary to repel and terminate the attack.

Existing command and operational arrangements continue provisionally under §19.3.a(2).

No oath, registration entry, emergency declaration or new legislative vote is necessary before the first unit returns fire. The authority arises from the Constitution. The NRS records the exercise of that authority; it does not create it.

The first constitutional objective is therefore achieved before dawn: the Republic does not become defenseless merely because permanent officeholders do not yet exist.

The State Forces Question

By noon the eastern line is holding, but barely. Two founding States maintain organized forces under §14.5. Their units include engineering, air-defense and medical formations the caretaker command needs immediately.

Ordinarily, State forces may be called into federal service only under the constitutional authorization structure of Article XIV. The permanent Legislature that would supply the ordinary legislative authorization is not yet capable of acting.

Section 19.3.a(3) supplies the temporary answer. A predecessor national authority exercising caretaker defense command may call State forces into federal service notwithstanding the ordinary legislative-authorization requirement, but only to the extent reasonably necessary for defense against invasion or armed attack.

The call-up is narrower than mobilization under a general wartime theory. One State's engineering brigade and another's air-defense regiment are called. A third State offers a public-order unit for deployment against antiwar demonstrations in the capital. The offer is declined. Suppressing domestic dissent is not part of the invasion-defense authority.

The called forces become subordinate to federal command for the duration of that service, while Article I and §14.5 continue to bind them.

The transition provision therefore changes command without changing the constitutional limits on the troops themselves.

The Border Is Not a Sanctuary

Day 43 · Enemy artillery relocates across the frontier

Rendath's initial ground attack stalls. Its artillery withdraws several kilometers behind the international border and continues firing into Republic territory.

A literal territorial conception of defense would produce an absurd result: Republic forces could intercept incoming fire but could not strike the batteries launching it because the batteries had moved to the other side of the line.

Section 19.3.a(4) avoids that result. Defensive action may occur outside Republic territory where reasonably necessary to repel, disrupt or terminate the invasion or armed attack.

Caretaker command authorizes strikes on the artillery batteries and the military logistics nodes directly sustaining them. It does not authorize attacks on Rendath's unrelated industrial infrastructure, political offices, television networks or distant military units unconnected to the invasion.

Constitutional boundary

"Defensive" describes the purpose and necessity of the action, not an imaginary rule that Republic force must stop at the border while an attacker fires from the other side.

The same subsection bars territorial acquisition, installation or maintenance of a preferred government in Rendath, resource extraction, ideological imposition and hostilities unrelated to the defense that justified action.

The Republic can fight effectively without turning the transition bridge into an undeclared power of conquest.

The Government Tries to Become Larger Than the War

Day 67 · The caretaker legislature receives the National Security Continuity Bill

The proposed bill contains three kinds of provisions.

The first keeps defense procurement offices open around the clock, authorizes emergency repairs to damaged transport links and permits temporary contracting needed to replace expended defensive equipment.

The second creates a permanent national internal-security directorate, merges three civilian agencies into it, grants the new body indefinite surveillance jurisdiction and fixes its director to a twelve-year protected term.

The third postpones the remaining transition milestones until six months after the end of hostilities.

The caretaker legislature may enact measures reasonably necessary for continuity, public safety, existing obligations and Article XIX implementation under §19.3. It may not permanently restructure the constitutional government or bind the incoming Legislature beyond what continuity requires.

The procurement and repair provisions survive because they sustain the defense and existing public services. The permanent security directorate does not. Neither does the blanket transition suspension.

Section 19.3.a reinforces the second point expressly: invocation of transition defense authority does not itself postpone an election or transition milestone, extend a term, alter territorial status or suspend Article I.

The joint Monitor panel publishes its assessment. The caretaker majority rewrites the bill before passage.

War has enlarged the caretaker government's workload. It has not enlarged its constitutional jurisdiction without limit.

The Record During War

The first transition defense order is not published while units are moving toward the frontier. The commander concludes that publishing operational routes, force concentrations and targeting details would expose troops to immediate danger.

That does not create a secrecy exception to the Constitution.

Under §19.3.a(5), invocation of the authority and its constitutional basis must be published to the NRS as soon as operationally practicable. Operational details may be classified only under Article X. The joint Monitor panel retains access to the records needed to assess constitutional compliance.

At 21:40 on Day 41, after the opening movements are complete, the caretaker defense authority publishes the existence of the order, the invasion trigger, the constitutional basis and the categories of authority being exercised. Deployment routes and live targeting information remain classified.

The distinction becomes important politically. The public learns what power the government claims and why. The enemy does not receive a live operations map in the name of transparency.

The Election Question

Phase 3 · The war has not ended

Months later, the front has stabilized but the conflict continues. The first permanent federal elections approach.

Several caretaker officials argue that no responsible government would conduct a founding election while troops are in combat. They propose delaying the electoral phase until the war ends.

The Elections Panel reports something less dramatic: most of the Republic's election infrastructure is functioning. Several frontier districts require alternate facilities and expanded early access, but the National Voting System remains available across the country. The physical conditions do not make the election impossible.

The caretaker executive therefore has no constitutional route to postpone it. Section 7.2 independently prohibits the Legat Consul from controlling election scheduling, and §19.3.a refuses to turn invasion into a transition-suspension power.

The campaign is unpleasant. Candidates accuse one another of politicizing the war. Soldiers vote from temporary facilities. Displaced inhabitants use alternate voting arrangements established under the election rules. The government continues fighting while the electorate continues constituting the government that will replace it.

That is not administrative tidiness. It is the point of the design.

The First Handoff

Phase 4 · The first Legat Consul takes office

The war has now lasted long enough that nobody in the operations room thinks of it as temporary. The transition authority that began it, however, remains temporary by construction.

At the precise moment the first Legat Consul becomes constitutionally capable of exercising the office's defense function, caretaker command authority ends to that extent under §19.3.a(6).

The senior caretaker commander does not remain commander-in-chief until a transfer ceremony. The predecessor cabinet does not vote to surrender its authority. The NRS does not certify the transfer into existence.

The Constitution moves the authority.

The military command structure remains in place as an operational structure, but its lawful political direction now runs to the Legat Consul.

The incoming Consul's first defense order keeps the existing frontier plan in force for forty-eight hours while the new office receives the full classified record. The order is not a revival of caretaker power. It is an exercise of the permanent office that has just inherited responsibility.

The Second Handoff

The Legislature becomes constitutionally capable shortly afterward. That creates a different transition problem.

The military operation was lawfully begun when no ordinary Legislature existed. It cannot sensibly become unlawful at the instant the new Legislature takes its seats. But neither may a transition authorization become a permanent substitute for the ordinary defense framework.

Section 19.3.a(6) creates the conversion window.

The operation may continue under its transition authorization for no more than sixty days after the Legislature first becomes constitutionally capable, unless the Legislature earlier authorizes continuation under §2.2 or directs cessation. Within fourteen days after taking office, the Legat Consul must submit the operation for legislative authorization or begin orderly cessation.

On the ninth day of the new administration, the Legat Consul submits a defense authorization specifying the continuing objective: repel Rendath forces from Republic territory, suppress military systems directly sustaining the invasion, protect civilian evacuation and secure the restored frontier. The proposal contains no authority for occupation or regime change.

The Assembly and Senate debate the strategy rather than whether the Republic has somehow lost the legal ability to defend itself because the constitutional government finally exists.

The Legislature authorizes continuation, but narrows the permitted geographic scope and requires monthly classified briefings.

The transition authorization ends. Ordinary constitutional authorization replaces it.

The Money Does Not Fall Through the Handoff

The first permanent budget is still weeks away.

Without a funding continuity rule, the military could have acquired lawful permanent command and lawful legislative authorization while losing access to the appropriations that had sustained the operation since Day 41.

Section 19.3.a(6) prevents that operational cliff as well. Defense appropriations preserved under subsection (2) remain available for continuing operations until superseded by a constitutional budget or another lawful appropriation.

The new Legislature passes a supplemental defense appropriation eleven days after authorizing the operation. The predecessor defense appropriation ceases to carry the burden from that point.

Again, nothing requires a ceremonial transfer. The old authority survives only until its replacement is legally capable of doing the same work.

The Test the Enemy Did Not Intend to Run

Rendath's invasion was planned around a reasonable assumption: a newly constituted country whose old sovereign institutions had been displaced and whose new ones were incomplete would have difficulty deciding who could command, spend, mobilize and speak for the state.

The Republic did have those problems. It simply did not have a constitutional vacuum.

The caretaker institutions could defend but not convert wartime necessity into permanent political power. State forces could be called where the invasion required them but did not become a general domestic instrument. Cross-border force could terminate an attack without becoming authority for conquest. The NRS made the legal claim visible without forcing operational secrets into public view. The Monitors watched without assuming command. Elections continued because war itself did not suspend them. The Legat Consul inherited command automatically. The Legislature inherited authorization authority automatically. The transition operation received a short conversion window rather than either immediate illegality or indefinite grandfathering. The money followed until ordinary appropriations replaced it.

What the scenario establishes

The transition defense rule is not a second wartime constitution. It is a bridge whose legitimacy comes from disappearing.

The Republic can survive an attack before its permanent institutions are complete because the Constitution preserves enough authority to defend the country — and no more authority than the emergency actually requires.

The first war therefore proves something larger than the military provisions.

The Republic does not have to choose between surviving the transition and completing it.
