The harbor and the counting-house

“We, the people of Caldenmere, heirs to the harbor and the shoreline, to the counting-houses and the fishing towns alike…”

Caldenmere lies on the southwestern coast, with Calden as its port and finance city. Its Constitution connects maritime commerce, local self-government, and institutional continuity. It protects property, contract, labor organization, and the ecological integrity of the shore while denying wealth or social standing any legal privilege. Property ownership, income, and net worth cannot qualify someone for public office, a board, or a commission. (State §§1.2, 1.8–1.11.)

The harbor is also an ordinary workplace. Harbor pilot Neris Vann’s Dispatch follows the ships, machinery, and interrupted working day behind Calden’s commercial reputation. The State’s institutions serve those fishing towns and working waterfronts as well as its counting-houses.

A Governor and a Senate President

Caldenmere divides executive responsibility. Its Governor is elected statewide by ranked-choice ballot for four years, with a limit of two consecutive terms. The office carries ceremonial representation and diplomatic liaison, signs or returns legislation, and nominates State senators. Administrative executive authority belongs to the President of the State Senate, elected by that chamber from among its members. The President directs the State Ministers and supervises implementation of State policy. (State §§2.1–2.5.)

The Governor formally appoints Ministers on the President’s recommendation; they serve at the President’s pleasure. Administrative Decrees may implement existing statute but cannot create an unauthorized obligation or penalty. If the President acts as Governor, legislative and presiding powers are suspended and the Senate elects an Acting President. The Constitution keeps the State title distinct from federal offices. (State §§2.5–2.8 and 11.4.)

An elected House and a life-appointed Senate

The House of Districts is elected by proportional representation for two-year terms, with a six-consecutive-term limit before a required electoral-cycle break. The State Senate has eighteen to thirty members. The Governor nominates senators, the House confirms them by majority vote, and they serve for life subject to removal for cause. A smaller authorized Senate takes effect through attrition rather than ending sitting members’ service. These are State institutions, separate from Caldenmere’s federal representation shown above. (State §§3.1–3.4.)

The Senate holds final budget approval and a legislative veto, but a specific veto can reach the electorate through Sovereign Recourse. Verified signatures from fifteen percent of the registered electorate within sixty days trigger a referendum; sixty-five percent of votes cast overturns the veto. The bill then goes to the Governor. This mechanism neither removes senators nor overrides other Senate actions. A gubernatorial return has a separate two-thirds House override after the Senate’s assent. (State §§3.3, 3.5 and 3.7.)

Home rule beside strategic infrastructure

Municipalities govern local zoning, policing, taxation, and ordinances and may adopt home-rule charters. State intervention in land use requires a formal Strategic State Infrastructure designation under published statutory criteria. Affected municipalities and owners can seek High Chancery review before an override takes effect, and a taking requires independent valuation. The State may not use fiscal pressure systematically to compel surrender of municipal functions. (State §§1.8 and 5.1–5.4.)

The Calden Port Authority manages the port under statute through a board with fixed, staggered terms. Its major contracts, concessions, tariffs, leases, and final regulatory decisions are published and independently audited. An ownership interest or commercial franchise does not confer a board seat, and proximity to the port does not give the Authority general municipal power. (State §6.4.)

Commercial courts and a permanent Registry

The Court of Common Pleas handles general civil and criminal matters. The High Chancery specializes in commercial, maritime, land-title, and port-authority disputes; the Court of Appeals is the final State-law court. Judges serve single, nonrenewable twelve-year terms, with appointment, confirmation, discipline, and temporary service governed by published rules and judicial-independence safeguards. Federal constitutional questions remain subject to the Republic’s Supreme Court. (State §§4.1–4.8.)

The Caldenmere Registry preserves laws, Administrative Decrees, major appointments, election certifications, audits, port decisions, and qualifying public contracts. Corrections appear beside the original; publication cannot be silently altered or deleted. Lawful confidentiality may delay access but cannot destroy or permanently conceal the record. The Registry must export federal disclosures in NRS-compatible form. (State Article VIII.)

The flag

Five gold sails turn within a charcoal circle on a silver field above a terracotta band. Gold is reserved for the sails, suggesting commercial prosperity; their fivefold arrangement refers to Mercury and commerce. The abstract sails connect that reference to Caldenmere’s maritime identity, while the earth-colored lower band grounds the design in the shoreline.
