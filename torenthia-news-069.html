Varek Says Yes, and Says It the Only Way It Knows How

There is no governor in Varek to sign anything. Consent to the Argent Ridge Trust designation had to go through a committee, a county, and a floor vote instead — and one member used the floor to ask a question that had nothing to do with the outcome.

Aleth Fenn Northern Correspondent, The Torenthian · Filed from Varek Year 13, Month 11

Varek's constitution vests no single office with general executive authority, and Varek's Assembly does not pretend otherwise when a decision like this one arrives. There was no proclamation to write, because there is no office empowered to write one. There was a resolution, a committee, and a vote.

The Hearing

The Assembly's relevant standing committee, organized by county-delegation rotation under the Assembly's own rules, took the Trust designation request first. Reyna Oskitt, delegate for Kell's Hollow County — the founding county whose territory includes Varek's half of the Argent Ridge tract — testified that her county has no competing use for the land and no objection to designation.

"We don't farm it, we don't log it, and nobody in Kell's Hollow has asked us to keep it for anything else. I don't know what Norvane needs it for and it isn't my business to guess. I know what we need it for, which is nothing in particular, and I said so."

That was the entirety of Kell's Hollow's case. The committee advanced the resolution to the full Assembly without amendment.

The Floor

The vote itself was not close. What slowed it down was a question from Delegate Corren Vask, of a county with no connection to the tract at all, who rose not to oppose consent but to ask why the whole Assembly was being asked to vote on it in the first place.

"Kell's Hollow already told us what it wants. I trust Kell's Hollow to know its own land better than I do. I'm not against saying yes. I'm asking why forty counties that have never seen this ridge get a vote on it, when the one county that has already gave its answer for free."

Nobody on the floor had a clean answer, because there isn't one in Varek's own constitution. Trust consent under the federal document is framed as an act of the State, not the county, and Varek's Assembly holds the State's governing power. Vask's objection wasn't a challenge to the outcome. It was the same objection Varek's constitution makes structurally, on a smaller scale, every time a statewide vote touches ground only one county actually stands on.

The resolution passed. Varek's consent to the Argent Ridge Trust designation is now filed with the federal Legislature. Norvane's has not arrived.

What This Settles, and What It Doesn't

Half of the §18.4 consent requirement is now met. The other half sits with a state whose delegation told this paper, in Month 10, that it was not going to be the one to tell people in a converted freight shed that the land next door was staying a scenic overlook. Nothing about Varek's vote changes that math. It only means, if Norvane ever does say yes, there will be nothing left standing between the designation and the parcel it protects.
