Where the Republic meets the water

“We, the people of Kelvant, stand where the Republic meets the water.”

Kelvant lies along Lake Varda’s western shore in the east of the Republic, adjacent to Korda Territory. Rhondel is its seat of government under State §11.5 and the location of the coast guard command. Kelvant’s constitutional preamble traces its identity through the people who built the city, the fisherfolk who work the lake, and those who keep watch over it. The same water brings livelihoods, crossings, and uncertainty.

That is an everyday geography as well as a strategic one. When a Sunderland vessel held position beyond the contested midline, Rhondel’s fishing cooperative suspended operations while the coast guard tracked it. In a quieter month, a change to the ferry timetable exposed the overlapping responsibilities of the port authority, the Kelvant transport office, and coast guard scheduling. A safe crossing can still leave three offices disagreeing about who sets the departure time.

A Governor with an executive to direct

Kelvant places executive power in one Governor, elected statewide by ranked-choice ballot for four years, with a limit of two consecutive terms. The Governor proposes the budget, directs the executive departments, and appoints their heads with the State Senate’s consent. A Lieutenant Governor is elected on the same ticket and ordinarily presides over the Senate, voting only to break a tie. The two roles cannot be exercised together when the Lieutenant Governor becomes Governor or Acting Governor. (State §§2.1–2.7.)

The Governor may veto legislation and individual appropriation items. Overriding either requires two-thirds of the authorized membership of each legislative house. Unlike Harren’s divided executive or Varek’s system without a general executive, Kelvant gives its administration a single point of direction. That authority remains bounded by legislative appropriations, courts, and the public record. (State §§2.4, 3.1 and 7.1–7.3.)

Two chambers and elected courts

The State Legislature consists of an Assembly and a Senate. The Assembly has between sixty and one hundred members, elected from single-member districts for two-year terms. Each Senate district comprises two contiguous Assembly districts; senators serve staggered four-year terms. These are Kelvant’s own institutions, separate from the forty federal Assembly seats and two federal Senate seats shown above. District maps must be drawn and adopted by an independent commission or equivalent body, with public plans and judicial review. (State §§3.1–3.4.)

The seven justices of Kelvant’s Supreme Court are elected statewide for ten-year terms and may seek re-election. Appeals and Circuit Court judges serve elected six-year terms. The Governor fills judicial vacancies with the State Senate’s consent until the constitutionally specified election produces a successor. (State §§4.1–4.6.)

The shore as a place to live

Counties have elected governing bodies, and local voters may adopt home-rule charters. Residents can petition a local governing body and receive a substantive public response within sixty days. Kelvant may coordinate immigration sponsorship and reception with its municipalities, but local governments have no independent veto over State sponsorship. Rescue and humanitarian aid do not themselves confer residency; federal certification and asylum remain federal responsibilities. (State §§1.13, 5.1–5.7 and 11.6.)

Vigilance under civilian authority

Kelvant’s constitution permits a State Militia under the Governor’s command, except when lawfully called into federal service. The Legislature defines its organization, funding, and activation. Activation alone does not give it ordinary police powers, and private membership in an organization does not confer governmental authority. The coast guard command’s location in Rhondel does not turn the federal coast guard into Kelvant’s State Militia. (State §§1.5, 2.9 and 3.13.)

Emergency declarations and militia activations must identify their authority and scope in the Kelvant State Record. Courts and the Legislature remain operative. The Record also preserves laws, veto objections, budgets, votes, election certifications, and audit reports; corrections must leave an auditable history. (State §2.9 and Article IX.)

A stake in Korda’s future

Kelvant is the proposed receiving State in the petition to merge Korda’s lake corridor—not the whole Territory—with an existing State. The distinction matters to inland Korda, whose communities would remain on the other side of a new boundary. The corridor petition qualified for a referendum, but a competing whole-Territory Statehood petition brought both claims into the Territory Convention process. The scheduled corridor vote was canceled; no merger referendum victory has occurred.

Kelvant business interests joined the original corridor petitioners in challenging the Joint Monitor Council’s determination. The Supreme Court upheld that determination, 6–3. The Convention has since moved its remaining sessions into Korda. Its membership comes from the Assembly districts within Korda: Kelvant’s interest in the outcome does not give it a delegation in that body. (Federal §15.5.a(4).)

If the Convention proposes merger, its resolution still requires ratification by the eligible voters it affects and the applicable §15.4 steps. Those include approval by Kelvant through its own constitutional process, a joint Legislative Monitor–Judicial Monitor assessment of the receiving State’s constitutional integrity, and federal Senate ratification. Kelvant’s approval is a separate requirement; neither a Convention bargain nor a Governor’s preference supplies it automatically. The published record has not settled the Convention’s outcome.

The flag

A gold watch-light stands above an ivory shore band on lake blue. The upright lozenge represents vigilance and a point of orientation for those approaching the shore; the uninterrupted band represents the land where a crossing ends. The design draws on Kelvant’s constitutional language of watchfulness and arrival, with no claim on the Korda corridor in its symbols.
