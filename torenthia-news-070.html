Caldris Says Yes to the Meeting. Valedon Hasn't.

The first answer to Torenthia's proposed regional conference is an acceptance. It is not yet a conference.

Aleth Fenn Northern Correspondent, The Torenthian · Filed from Verentum Year 13, Month 11

Caldris has accepted the Foreign Affairs Directorate's proposal for a regional conference on Lake Varda, according to a diplomatic note received by the Directorate late yesterday.

The answer is narrow. Caldris agreed to attend talks on lake security, refugee-arrival coordination, and a possible joint approach to the Sunderland Interim Authority. It did not endorse Torenthia's account of the Varda incidents, commit to any common position on Sunderland, or accept any particular date or venue.

That distinction matters because the original Torenthian proposal was deliberately broad. It invited Caldris because of its economic relationship with Sunderland and its existing bilateral ties with the Republic. It invited Valedon as a possible host and co-convener even though Valedon has not publicly identified a direct interest in the dispute.

One chair is still empty

Valedon has not responded.

Without Valedon, there is no agreed venue and no confirmation that the conference will take the three-government form proposed by Foreign Affairs. The Directorate can still talk to Caldris bilaterally, and officials familiar with the exchange said nothing in Caldris's response conditions its participation on Valedon's presence. But that would be a smaller meeting than the one Director Ines Carrow put on the table.

The Directorate declined to characterize Caldris's acceptance as a diplomatic breakthrough. A short written statement said only that the response was "constructive" and that Torenthia would continue consultations with both invited governments.

A different channel

The point of the proposal was never that Caldris or Valedon could answer the questions Sunderland would not. Torenthia's formal inquiry to the Sunderland Interim Authority asked who commanded the force involved in the Korda incursion, under whose authority it acted, and what measures would prevent another crossing. The response acknowledged the letter and answered none of those questions.

The regional conference is an attempt to change the channel rather than repeat the same demand.

Caldris's acceptance means that effort now has at least one partner. It does not mean the governments agree about what happened on Lake Varda, what should happen next, or even where they will sit down to discuss it.

For now, the meeting exists in the space between an accepted invitation and an unanswered one.
