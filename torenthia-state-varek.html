A State without a general executive

“We establish no general executive. Legislative authority shall be exercised collectively, administration shall remain confined to law, Counties shall govern within their constitutional sphere, and courts shall determine the boundaries between them.”

Varek’s preamble describes a cantonal republic. Its Counties are constituent units of the State’s constitutional order, not merely administrative districts. Statewide authority exists for common action; local authority cannot be absorbed merely for convenience. No officer holds residual executive power, and no statutory arrangement may recreate a general executive under another name. (State §§1.2–1.3.)

Counties, Assembly, and Directorates

Each County elects three representatives to the unicameral Varek Assembly by single transferable vote. Equal County representation is deliberate, accompanied by a population review at least every ten years and a mandatory boundary-review process if the largest-to-smallest County population ratio exceeds eight to one. These are seats in Varek’s own Assembly; the federal seats above are a separate allocation. (State §§3.1–3.2 and 8.1.)

Administration operates through legally defined Directorates. The Assembly’s Finance Committee prepares the budget from public submissions but cannot administer spending. No officer may veto legislation after its lawful passage. Courts enforce the boundaries of authority, and every person in Varek may challenge an alleged concentration of general executive power without showing a separate individualized injury. (State §§2.4, 3.9, Article VI, and §9.2.)

Authority that ends

The Assembly’s Presiding Officer may issue strictly necessary temporary emergency directives, but that authority expires after 72 hours unless continued under the Constitution. Assembly continuations last no more than 30 days apiece and require fresh affirmative votes. Expiration restores the ordinary distribution of powers; emergency arrangements create no residual executive authority. (State §§12.1–12.3 and 12.11.)

The Varek Record System preserves public acts and corrections. The Varek Monitor reports on constitutional compliance but cannot invalidate acts, remove officers, or compel institutions. Its findings are informational, and publication is not generally what creates an act’s legal authority. (State Articles X–XI.)

River country

Varek lies in the north along the Alps, bordering Caldris and sharing the Toren watershed. The Varek Northern Forum and the Argent Ridge dispute give its landscape a place in the Republic’s public record. The repeal petition, proposed National Trust protection, and host-State consent are distinct questions.

The consent decision

In Year 13, Month 11, Varek’s Assembly consented to the Argent Ridge National Trust designation. Reyna Oskitt, delegate for Kell’s Hollow County, testified that the County containing Varek’s half of the tract had no competing use for the land.
