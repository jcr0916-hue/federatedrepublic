Comparative Options Memorandum — Fiscal Equalization Statutory Review

Record Identification

NRS Reference:NRS-Y13-0740
Record Type:Committee staff options memorandum
Filing Authority:Joint Committee on Fiscal Equalization
Date of Filing:Year 13, Month 12

Purpose

Committee staff publish this memorandum in response to the Month 12 submissions register recorded in NRS-Y13-0738. The memorandum compares statutory approaches raised in those submissions so that members may evaluate the choices on a common basis before deciding whether to prepare legislation.

The memorandum is analytical only. It does not recommend an option, assign legislative support, make a constitutional finding, or alter any current allocation.

Option A — Retain the present formula; add queue-age reporting

This approach would leave the statutory allocation factors unchanged while requiring standardized publication of request age, deferral history and disposition. Its principal effect would be transparency: governments and the public could distinguish recent requests from requests remaining unresolved across repeated review periods without making age itself an allocation factor.

Staff identify the principal tradeoff as limited responsiveness to the submissions. The option would make persistent delay easier to see but would not direct the mechanism to give that delay independent weight.

Option B — Add a recurring service-burden factor

This approach would add a measurable factor for recurring public-service obligations that materially exceed a jurisdiction's ordinary fiscal capacity. Submissions identify examples including transport maintenance, border-service demand, dispersed delivery cost and other recurring obligations that may not appear clearly in project-specific requests.

Staff note that any such factor would require a common measurement rule. A service-burden factor defined too broadly could reward ordinary expenditure choices; one defined too narrowly could reproduce the existing problem under a different label.

Option C — Add infrastructure backlog and request persistence within a blended factor

This approach would permit the formula to consider verified infrastructure backlog together with the persistence of qualifying capacity requests across review periods. Request age alone would not determine priority. A request would have to remain tied to a documented capacity need and satisfy statutory comparability requirements.

Staff identify the principal tradeoff as weighting. A blended factor could recognize durable unmet need without making the oldest request automatically the strongest request, but the Legislature would have to decide how much weight the factor receives relative to existing measures.

Common constraints

All three approaches remain subject to §12.6 and to ordinary legislative enactment. The memorandum does not treat Korda's separate Convention process as evidence for or against any formula option, and it does not assign Morantine or any other Territory an entitlement to a particular result.

The Executive Monitor's existing finding remains unchanged: no constitutional failure of the present equalization mechanism is active. No ninety-day response period is running.

Next committee step

The co-chairs will place the memorandum on the agenda for member discussion. Members may direct staff to prepare draft statutory language for one or more approaches, combine elements, request distributional estimates, recommend no amendment, or seek additional evidence.

No markup date and no committee recommendation are established by this filing.

End of record.
