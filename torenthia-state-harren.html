The founding State

“What we built here became a model. When the Republic was founded, its framers looked to Harren. Some of our innovations they adopted; others they adapted to the scale of a nation.”

Harren’s constitutional preamble makes an unusually direct claim on the Republic’s origins. The northern heartland is identified as a founding State in Torenthia’s world record, but the federal preamble names no State at all. Harren tells the story of an inheritance; the Republic speaks collectively of people from different nations, communities, and traditions.

The institutions give that claim substance without settling precisely which federal provisions descend from Harren. Its constitution separates political leadership from administrative competence, places judges before the electorate, and insists that government leave a public record. Those are Harren’s stated commitments; a clause-by-clause history of their influence remains unwritten.

Two officers, separate responsibilities

The Governor supplies political leadership, while the State Administrator directs administration. Neither is subordinate to the other. The Governor is elected statewide for four years; the Legislature chooses the Administrator for a single, nonrenewable eight-year term. The Administrator prepares and executes the budget, while the Legislature enacts it. Harren’s division is political and administrative, rather than the federal division between domestic and external authority. (State §§2.1–2.8.)

Institutions close to the public

A bicameral Legislature makes law. The seven justices of the High Court are elected statewide. Counties retain meaningful local self-government, and the Harren State Record preserves governmental action in a permanent, accessible record compatible with the federal NRS. (State Articles III–V and IX.)

Harren in the Republic

Harren lies below Varek in the northern heartland and does not touch the national border. Senator Tobias Vael is its established national political figure; his candidacy and public interventions connect the State to the Republic’s wider arguments over external policy.
