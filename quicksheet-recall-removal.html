Recall & Removal
Removal, temporary incapacity and electoral replacement are different constitutional processes.
§2.4.a, §2.6, §2.13, §3.2, §3.4,
§4.2, §4.3.a, §7.4, §7.5, §7.9, §9.9

Consuls

Legat removal: concurrent two-thirds of both chambers for serious misconduct, abuse or permanent incapacity. Alternatively a Senate majority refers recall to citizens: sixty-percent approval and fifty-five-percent participation; failed recall cannot recur that term. Civic replacement instead requires an Assembly absolute majority electing a named successor under §2.6. An acting Civic Consul is not removable through that vote.

Judges

Supreme Court removal requires two-thirds of both chambers for serious misconduct, abuse or permanent incapacity. Inferior judges have that route and the statutory discipline process under Supreme Court oversight. Judicial disagreement is not cause; no Monitor finding is prerequisite. A Monitor report supplies evidence, not removal authority.

Monitor Generals

Misconduct proceedings: LM by either Consul; EM by either chamber; JM by either chamber or either Consul. The Panel holds a national referendum within ninety days or at the next federal election, whichever first. Sixty percent removes, without a turnout floor. Missed scheduling does not terminate the case. Failed removal bars substantially the same conduct and grounds that term, subject to material new evidence or conduct.

§9.9 — full provision →

Other officers and temporary inability

Permanent Monitor incapacity needs the other two Monitors’ recommendation, two-thirds of both chambers and both Consuls. Other confirmed independent officers covered by paragraph (5) have a one-third-chamber initiation and two-thirds-full-membership vote in each chamber. Suspension and temporary incapacity use separate procedures; filing alone never supplies suspension. States may establish legislator recall with a final legislative or voter decision.
