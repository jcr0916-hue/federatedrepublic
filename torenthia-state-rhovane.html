Where land meets Lake Varda

“We, the people of Rhovane, dwell where the land meets the water.”

Rhovane occupies the middle-northeast of the Republic along Lake Varda’s upper-western shore. Its Constitution treats the lake as more than geography. Public access, navigation, water quality, ecological continuity, and long-term public use are constitutional concerns, and any person may bring an action to enforce the Lake Varda public trust without showing individualized harm. (State §§1.13–1.14 and Article VI.)

That design now operates under pressure. Rhovane is carrying spillover from the Lake Varda crisis and is presently listed at Watch in the Statehood Audit ledger. Published coverage has repeatedly shown the State absorbing arrival, infrastructure, and fiscal pressure generated farther north and across the lake.

Old geography without hereditary privilege

Rhovane’s Legislature has two chambers: a popularly elected Chamber of Deputies and a thirty-member Estate Council. The Estates are historic geographic regions, not hereditary bodies. Fifteen councillors are elected from Estate constituencies, ten represent county government through public selection processes, and five civic members come from open public shortlists in fields such as education, commerce, labor, Lake Varda stewardship, and civic life. No seat or nomination right may depend on ancestry, inherited status, family, or land ownership. (State §§3.1–3.4.)

Delay can be a constitutional function

The Estate Council can amend, reject, or delay ordinary legislation, but it cannot permanently defeat the elected Chamber on an ordinary Chamber-passed bill. After rejection or ninety days of inaction, the Chamber may place a bill on a deliberative-override calendar. A six-month cooling-off period follows, with public hearings and published objections; only then may three-fifths of the Chamber pass the bill over the Council. (State §3.6.)

The design gives institutional memory and local review real weight without making either historically rooted institutions or civic bodies sovereign over the electorate.

A judiciary selected for independence

Rhovane’s seven-member High Court is appointed from public shortlists produced by a Judicial Nominating Commission. The Governor chooses from that list, and the Legislature confirms in joint session; prolonged inaction results in deemed confirmation rather than an indefinite vacancy. High Court justices serve a single nonrenewable twelve-year term. (State §§4.2–4.4.)

The Constitution periodically reopens itself

Every tenth year Rhovane automatically convenes a Constitutional Convention. No preliminary referendum is required. County delegates are elected from qualified public applicant pools, while additional at-large delegates are selected by public and auditable lot. Any amendment still requires the constitutional ratification process; the Convention is a recurring review mechanism, not an authority above the people. (State Articles VII–VIII.)

Rhovane in the current record

Rhovane is also the home State of Legat Consul candidate Seren Mak. Her public career has repeatedly intersected with Lake Varda, but the State’s constitutional identity is broader than any one candidate: a lake-facing jurisdiction built around public stewardship, layered representation, and deliberate institutional review.

A Rhovane town hall brought the State’s service burden into the national conversation, and later Lake Varda coverage has continued to show the pressure carried by communities on the upper lake.
