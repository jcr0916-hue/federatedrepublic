States & Territories · §15.2 · §15.3 · Mandatory Devolution and Restoration

The Restoration

Year 16. Valdrath passes its second consecutive clean Statehood Audit while in Provisional status. Under §15.1.a, ordinary State status is restored automatically — no political vote, no ratification, no executive action. Three years after the third audit failure sent it into Provisional status, the pathway back was the same one that went in: the audit itself.

§15.3 Mandatory Devolution §15.1.a Provisional Status §15.2 The Statehood Audit §9.1.a Legislative Monitor §9.1.c Judicial Monitor §3.5 The Senate

By Year 13, Valdrath had failed three consecutive annual Statehood Audits. Each overall failure included material findings in at least two Monitor domains: legislative electoral non-compliance in the LM’s assessment and Article I procedural failures in the JM’s. Publication of the third overall failure placed Valdrath in Provisional status automatically under §15.3.

Valdrath remained a State. Its government, courts, rights, social-state access, and proportional Assembly representation continued. No new Senate term could begin. Its sitting senators, elected to six-year terms in Years 9 and 11, continued until Years 15 and 17 respectively. One seat therefore became unfilled in Year 15 while the other senator remained in office.

The Provisional Audit Council comprised two CC-appointed officers and two State delegates. Its recommendations were advisory. Year 14’s audit failed again. In Year 15, all three Monitors published findings with no material failure, and Year 16 produced a second such clean annual audit. At the first sitting senator’s term expiry in Year 15, the Elections Panel also administered the required referendum on pursuing devolution; it was rejected. Valdrath continued remediation.

§15.1.a — Automatic Restoration
Two consecutive clean audits while in Provisional status. Ordinary State status restored automatically. No political vote required.

Publication of the complete Year 16 annual audit, with no material-failure finding by any Monitor, restored ordinary State status automatically under §15.1.a. One warning would have prevented restoration even if the overall audit passed. No separate political vote, legislative affirmation, or executive certification was needed.

Restoration ended the prohibition on beginning new Senate terms. The unfilled seat could be filled under the applicable election procedures. The sitting senator’s existing term continued unchanged. The Council dissolved; Valdrath did not require readmission because it had remained a State throughout.

§15.3 — What the Senate Gap Looks Like
Sitting senators complete terms. No new Senate terms during Provisional status. Seats go dormant, not vacant by reassignment.

One senator’s term ended in Year 15; the other continued through Year 17. With the Year 15 seat unfilled, the Senate had 23 seated members instead of 24 until that seat was filled after restoration. Thresholds expressed in terms of full seated membership used that actual membership.

The unfilled seat changed the numbers needed for thresholds calculated from seated membership. It did not transfer a seat to another State or change the surviving senator’s term. Ending Provisional status removed the constitutional bar to beginning a new term.

§15.3 — Judicial Review
Review of legality, process, jurisdiction, and application of stated criteria; no policy substitution.

A State may challenge the audit sequence and entry into Provisional status for legality, process, jurisdiction, or application of the stated criteria under §15.3. The provision neither imposes a special 30-day filing window nor confines review to whether documents were published. Valdrath did not bring a challenge in this scenario.

The constitutional limit is against policy substitution: a court may review the stated legal grounds but may not replace a Monitor’s domain assessment with its own preferred policy. Remediation is the route to a clean audit; judicial review remains available for legal error.

Constitutional Finding

The pathway into Provisional status and the pathway out of it are the same mechanism — the Statehood Audit. Entry is automatic on three consecutive failures; restoration is automatic on two consecutive clean audits. Neither political opposition nor political support can alter either threshold. A legislature that wants to block a State's restoration cannot. A legislature that wants to prevent a State from entering Provisional status cannot — once the three failure threshold is crossed, the status change is constitutional, not legislative.

The Senate dormancy during Provisional status is the sharpest structural consequence, and the one most likely to motivate remediation. A State that values its Senate representation must pass the audit to get it back. The Provisional Audit Council's record — published to the NRS throughout — becomes the factual and political foundation for whatever accountability mechanisms the State's own citizens eventually apply to the legislature that caused the deadlock in the first place.

Valdrath’s restoration in Year 16 followed publication of all three Monitor findings completing its second consecutive clean annual audit. The change was automatic. It was restoration of ordinary State status, not graduation from Territory to Statehood.
