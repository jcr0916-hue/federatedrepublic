States & Territories · §15.2 · §15.3 · Remediation and Restoration

The Restoration

Year 21. In an alternative Valdrath timeline, two consecutive biennial audits receive affirmative legislative determinations of compliance. The second determination automatically restores ordinary State status; there is no separate readmission vote.

§15.3 State Remediation and Provisional Status §15.1.a Provisional Status §15.2 The Statehood Audit §9.1.a The Legislative Monitor §9.1.c The Judicial Monitor §3.5 The Senate

In this alternative course of the Valdrath problem, the Senate determined three consecutive biennial audits noncompliant by Year 15, on complete records from all three Monitors. It then separately placed Valdrath into Provisional status by a majority of full seated membership under §15.3. A failed audit and placement into Provisional status were distinct decisions with different thresholds.

Valdrath remained a State. Its government, courts, rights, social-state access, and proportional Assembly representation continued. No new Senate term could begin. Its sitting senators, elected to six-year terms in Years 11 and 13, continued until Years 17 and 19 respectively. One seat therefore became unfilled in Year 17 while the other senator remained in office.

Valdrath and the Republic chose a four-person advisory group without governing powers. The Year 17 audit was determined noncompliant. The Senate determined compliance on the complete Year 19 record and again on the Year 21 record. Valdrath also chose a voluntary §15.4 devolution referendum in Year 17, which voters rejected. No term-expiry rule compelled that referendum or its repetition.

§15.1.a — Automatic Restoration
Two consecutive biennial audits determined compliant. Ordinary status then returns without a separate admission decision.

The Senate’s affirmative Year 21 compliance determination completed the second consecutive qualifying biennial audit under §§15.2 and 15.2.a. Ordinary State status returned immediately under §15.1.a. A warning from one Monitor would not independently dictate the result: the chamber must assess the whole record under lawful criteria.

Restoration ended the bar on new Senate terms. Both old terms had expired by Year 19, leaving two vacant seats. The Elections Panel administered the required special election within ninety days. The advisory arrangement ended; Valdrath needed no readmission because it had remained a State.

§15.3 — What the Senate Gap Looks Like
Sitting senators complete terms. No new Senate terms during Provisional status. Seats go dormant, not vacant by reassignment.

One senator’s term ended in Year 17 and the other in Year 19. The Senate therefore had 23 seated members between those expiries, and 22 afterward, assuming the Republic’s other 22 seats remained filled. Thresholds based on full seated membership followed those actual numbers until the restored seats were filled.

The unfilled seat changed the numbers needed for thresholds calculated from seated membership. It did not transfer a seat to another State or change the surviving senator’s term. Ending Provisional status removed the constitutional bar to beginning a new term.

§15.3 — Judicial Review
Review of legality, process, jurisdiction, and application of stated criteria; no policy substitution.

A State may challenge the audit sequence and entry into Provisional status for legality, process, jurisdiction, or application of the stated criteria under §15.3. The provision neither imposes a special 30-day filing window nor confines review to whether documents were published. Valdrath did not bring a challenge in this scenario.

The court may review legal error in the determinations and enforce the responsible chamber’s duty to act. It may not substitute its substantive assessment of whether Provisional status is warranted. Monitor findings remain evidence; a judicial policy preference supplies neither an audit determination nor a shortcut to restoration.

Constitutional Finding

Entry requires three consecutive failed determinations followed by a separate Provisional-status decision. Restoration requires two consecutive biennial audits affirmatively determined compliant. It then follows automatically, with no additional discretionary admission vote. The same chamber-transfer and judicial-enforcement safeguards apply to the required audit determinations.

The Senate-representation consequence therefore begins with an elected political authorization and ends through objective audited compliance. Advisory cooperation may help the State remediate, but it has no governing authority and cannot condition restoration.

Valdrath’s restoration in Year 21 followed the second consecutive affirmative compliance determination on a complete audit record. It restored ordinary State status; it was not admission from Territory status.
