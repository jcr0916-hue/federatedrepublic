The work behind the headline

Before Elin Thoss became Civic Consul, national attention found her over a bridge-maintenance formula. In Year 13, Month 5, the representative for Selvane’s third federal district was speaking about the Kelvant Road bridges when an off-microphone exchange became a widely shared clip. A few days later, her office reported that the funding formula had cleared committee and reminded reporters that constituent meetings were held on Tuesdays. The bridges and the appointment book survived the attention. (The Assembly Member Nobody Was Covering Said the Quiet Part; Forty-Seven Words.)

Selvane lies in the Republic’s central heartland. Its Constitution describes a meeting place shaped by movement, exchange, settlement, and many traditions. That inheritance becomes a civic promise: nobody’s ancestry, wealth, family connection, or party membership gives them a superior claim to public authority. Elected leaders choose the State’s direction; a professional administration carries lawful decisions into effect. Keeping both parts answerable is the central concern of Selvane’s constitutional design. (Preamble; State §§1–5.)

An Assembly chosen across the State

The Selvane Assembly is a single chamber elected through statewide open-list proportional representation. Its constitutional default is ninety-nine members, with structural law permitted to set another size between seventy-five and 125. The whole State forms one electoral district for this chamber, and voters must retain meaningful choice among candidates. Members serve four-year terms and may hold no more than three consecutive full terms before taking a full term out of office. These are Selvane’s own legislative arrangements, separate from its district representation in the Republic’s Assembly. (State §§15–17.)

Ordinary legislation passes by a majority of those present and voting with a quorum. Laws governing the electoral system, the merit service, judicial institutions, and other designated constitutional machinery require a different kind of agreement: two-thirds of authorized Assembly membership. Vacancies do not reduce that denominator. The higher threshold places the rules of political competition and professional appointment beyond a narrow ordinary majority’s reach. (State §§19–20, §64.)

A Governor sets the direction

Selvane elects a Governor and Deputy Governor statewide on one ticket for four-year terms. The Governor directs lawful executive policy, proposes the budget, represents the State, and appoints political and qualified administrative officers under the Constitution. Professional qualifications belong to the relevant appointed jobs; a civil-service examination cannot become a condition of election to the Governor’s office or the Assembly. (State §§25–27, §17.)

The Chief Administrator supplies professional continuity across departments. A Civil Service Commission shortlist narrows the field to qualified candidates, the Governor chooses, and an authorized-membership Assembly majority confirms. The Administrator coordinates the career service and advises on capacity and implementation. Political direction remains with the Governor. Removal requires stated lawful cause, Commission review, and Assembly approval; a change of political preference alone is insufficient. (State §§28–30.)

A public service with an open door

Selvane’s merit system starts with the work a person will actually perform. Selection can use practical assessments, experience, credentials, examinations, or other relevant methods. Standards must be public and accessible, reasonable accommodation must be available, and entry from outside the existing career service must remain possible, including at senior levels. The constitutional promise is a fair chance to compete under relevant standards. (State §2, §13.)

The independent Civil Service Commission has seven members, selected through a mixture of Governor appointments, Assembly supermajority votes, public lot from a qualified pool, and selection by the other commissioners. It certifies candidate pools, reviews classifications and promotions, hears patronage complaints, and publishes rules and aggregate reports. Its decisions remain open to judicial review. The Commission protects fair administration while elected institutions retain responsibility for policy. (State §§7–8.)

Career protection carries duties as well as security. Employees must implement lawful policy, and incompetence or misconduct can lead to removal through fair procedures. They must refuse unlawful orders and can obtain prompt review of a disputed refusal. Political officers cannot demand campaign work or personal loyalty as the price of advancement, or turn career jobs into patronage posts through a change of title. These rules address the pressure on both sides of a public desk: the official seeking obedience and the employee entrusted with carrying decisions out. (State §§3–6.)

Vacancies cannot become a veto

Selvane’s appointment rules distinguish rejection from indefinite delay. For the Chief Administrator, failure to nominate within the statutory period causes the highest-ranked remaining shortlisted candidate to be deemed nominated; legislative silence past the applicable period produces confirmation. Affirmative rejection instead triggers further choices. After two rejected nominees from each of two successive shortlists, a public lot from the second shortlist fills the office. The deadlines must be fixed by law. (State §28, §31.)

The judiciary has its own professional safeguards. A seven-Justice Constitutional Court, with nonrenewable ten-year terms, decides State constitutional disputes. An independent Judicial Appointments Commission supplies qualified shortlists, with Governor selection and Assembly confirmation. Its appointment process likewise provides consequences for silence and a public-lot fallback after repeated affirmative rejection. Judicial appointment, career-service oversight, and judicial discipline belong to separate commissions. Professional screening is distributed among institutions whose decisions can be challenged under law. (State §§35–41.)

Local capacity without political favors

The State’s cities, towns, counties, and other lawful local governments retain democratic leadership. Smaller administrations may share assessments, candidate pools, personnel services, and appeals through the State Commission. Selvane must maintain formula-based local fiscal equalization, with possible factors including service demand, geographic area, infrastructure burden, and local revenue capacity. Distribution follows published law and cannot be manipulated to reward political support or punish opposition. (State §§46–50.)

Cultural and linguistic life remains open to choice. People may join, combine, change, or leave communities freely. The State cannot demand an ancestry declaration for voting, candidacy, public employment, or an ordinary public benefit. Local governments may provide additional-language services according to need. Public contracts, grants, and appointments must follow public criteria, with State Record entries preserving official acts and linked corrections. A State shaped by many traditions makes equal civic access part of everyday administration. (State §12, §52, §§62–63.)

The people retain a route to change

Citizens can propose constitutional amendments through a petition signed by voting citizens numbering at least ten percent of the valid votes cast in the most recent statewide general election. Certification by the independent State Elections Authority makes ballot placement automatic. Ratification requires sixty percent of valid votes cast statewide. The Assembly has a separate route: a two-thirds proposal vote followed by majority approval at referendum. No provision is permanently unamendable. (State §§53–57, §60.)

That open route matters in a Constitution so concerned with professional government. Entry to the administration must remain competitive; the administration’s governing rules must also remain contestable. A qualified citizen proposal can reach the voters even when political officers would prefer another subject. In Selvane, keeping the machinery working includes keeping a public route to changing it.
