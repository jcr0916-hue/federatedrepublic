State Constitutional History · Norvane Test 01 · Year 7, Month 8

The Fourteenth Day

A severe Lake Varda storm struck Norvane’s northern shore, damaging the principal reception pier and closing the main road linking the shore to the interior. Civilian vessels continued to arrive carrying people who needed shelter, medical screening, food, and onward transport while local facilities were already near capacity.

The Governor declared a State emergency under §6.1. The declaration identified the damaged corridor, activated temporary reception and logistics zones under §8.4, authorized expedited emergency procurement under §6.8, and invoked lawful emergency-property authority for temporary use of a nearby warehouse. It also imposed temporary transport-priority rules so medical, shelter, and repair traffic could move through the limited surviving routes.

The report before Day Fourteen

By the eleventh day, the main road had reopened and two permanent reception facilities were again functioning. The emergency was not over, but the conditions supporting each extraordinary measure were no longer the same.

The Governor’s report under §6.2(c) therefore did not ask the Assembly to renew the emergency as a single block. It requested twenty-one additional days for the reception and logistics zones, twenty-one days for emergency procurement, and fourteen days for continued use of the warehouse. The Governor did not request any extension of the transport-priority restrictions.

The Assembly treated the authorities separately. Members agreed that reception capacity and emergency purchasing remained necessary while repairs and relocation continued. Debate over the warehouse was closer, but a majority accepted the shorter extension. No member needed to vote to terminate the transport restrictions. Because the Governor had not requested their continuation, they were left to expire.

Midnight on the fourteenth day

On Day Thirteen, the Assembly approved the requested extensions for the reception zones, procurement authority, and warehouse use. The vote required no gubernatorial signature. At midnight on Day Fourteen, the unextended transport-priority rules lapsed automatically under §6.2.

No separate repeal, termination resolution, or executive withdrawal was required. Nor did the continuing existence of the storm emergency preserve the expired authority. Norvane’s Constitution required a fresh affirmative vote for each extraordinary power the government still wished to use.

Three weeks later, reception pressure had stabilized. The Governor requested no further extension. The remaining emergency authorities expired at the end of their approved periods.

Section 6.6 prevented the temporary arrangements from quietly becoming permanent institutions. Any reception system, procurement rule, property arrangement, or transport restriction intended to continue afterward had to rest on ordinary constitutional or statutory authority rather than on the emergency declaration.

The episode became an early explanation of Norvane’s emergency design. The Constitution did not require the government to prove that the crisis had completely ended. It required the government to prove again why each extraordinary authority should continue.
