State Constitutional History · Norvane Test 02 · Year 9, Month 04

The Road Without a Signal

For three days in Year 9, Month 4, snow and a collapsed switching mast cut the upper Sellen road district off from Norvane's emergency coordination center. The road below the ridge remained open, but no dispatcher in the capital could reliably reach the villages above it. A school building had heat; the clinic had a backup generator; the county's delivery depot had fuel that could keep both operating.

The district council did not declare itself independent of the State. It invoked the limited emergency authority already delegated by general State law under §8.5. Its published notice identified the communications failure, the affected roads and public buildings, the available fuel, and the statutory authority for reallocating emergency transport and deliveries. The council assigned a clerk to preserve a record of decisions and their reasons.

The line between assistance and command

Officials opened the school as a temporary warming site and directed the district's contracted vehicles to prioritize medicine, fuel and stranded residents. They sought the depot owner's agreement for scheduled fuel withdrawals under the emergency supply provisions of State law. The depot owner agreed to the initial allotment, but objected when the council sought a second delivery without publishing the remaining inventory.

The council published a corrected schedule showing what fuel had already been used and how much remained available. It did not claim a general power to requisition property, levy a tax, or issue permanent transport rules. Its temporary notice expressly excluded federal border, asylum and immigration functions, which §8.5 did not delegate.

When the signal returned

On the fourth day the communication link was restored. The district notified the Governor's emergency office, transmitted the decisions and supply logs, and requested that the State take over coordination of shipments still needed. The State accepted coordination of the remaining work while leaving local staff to administer the warming site under ordinary law.

The council terminated its temporary emergency directions. It did not ask a court to recognize a new sphere of sovereign authority; there was none to recognize. Residents retained the usual means of challenging particular restrictions or the treatment of people using the shelter.

The episode became a practical example of §8.5's premise. Predelegated authority allowed local government to act while State direction was unavailable. Its limits mattered just as much when contact returned: the emergency did not create another government inside Norvane.
