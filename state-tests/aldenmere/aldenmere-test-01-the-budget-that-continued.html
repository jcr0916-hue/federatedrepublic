State Constitutional History · Aldenmere Test 01 · Year 7, Month 11

The Budget That Continued

At the start of Aldenmere's new fiscal year, the Legislature still had no agreement on the annual budget. The Assembly favored additional funding for regional transport links. The Senate wanted a more conservative revenue forecast before agreeing to the plan. Both chambers had passed measures of their own, but neither had enacted the same budget.

The dispute mattered beyond the legislative hall in Terenne. Schools, hospitals, maintenance crews and local administrations needed to know whether the new fiscal year had begun without authority to operate. Aldenmere's Constitution had an answer that did not depend on either chamber conceding.

The deadline passed

The State Auditor published the revenue certification required for a balanced budget under §3.5(a). With the chambers divided, lawmakers could not complete an annual appropriation before the fiscal deadline. Neither chamber could treat its own proposal as law: Aldenmere has two legislative chambers, and a budget must pass through the ordinary legislative process.

Some legislators urged the Governor to announce an emergency and spend against the proposed transport programme. But ordinary political disagreement was not itself an emergency spending authorization. Under §3.5(b), deficit spending required a qualifying emergency, two-thirds approval in both chambers, a published return-to-balance plan and the Constitution's limit on consecutive deficit years. The required authorization had not been given.

Continuity without a new programme

Section 3.5(c) took effect when the deadline passed. The previous year's budget continued at the same funding levels, adjusted for inflation as certified by the State Auditor, until a new budget could be enacted. The Auditor's certification supplied the adjustment; it did not substitute an independent fiscal policy for the Legislature's choices.

Departments continued lawful operations under the carried-forward appropriations. The proposed new transport programme did not begin merely because the Assembly had favored it. The Governor's office likewise could not draw from the Treasury to implement a proposal that had never completed the constitutional appropriation process. Section 5.2 required an appropriation enacted under law; the continuing budget supplied authority only within its own terms.

Local officials criticized the delay. Workers could keep existing services operating, but they could not plan around an expansion that had no enacted appropriation. Continuity prevented a stoppage of essential administration; it did not erase the policy consequences of legislative disagreement.

An agreement could still replace the fallback

After further negotiations, the Assembly and Senate passed a revised balanced budget using the Auditor's certified projected revenue. The Governor signed it, and the temporary continuation ended. The enacted budget authorized certain preparatory transport studies, with any larger construction commitment left for later lawful approval.

No court or Auditor chose the political compromise. The Legislature did. What Aldenmere's Constitution supplied was time to reach one without using the ordinary needs of residents as leverage for an administrative shutdown. The previous budget had continued precisely because a new one had not yet become law.
