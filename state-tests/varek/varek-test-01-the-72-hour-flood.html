State Constitutional History · Varek Test 01 · Year 6, Month 9

The 72-Hour Flood

When floodwater cut the road at Tessin Ford, the problem in Hollowmere County was immediate: people needed to reach high ground, relief supplies needed a usable route, and separate public services needed to act together. In Tessin, the question was how Varek could coordinate that work without creating the general executive its Constitution forbade.

Necessary orders, stated limits

Presiding Officer Deverin Hask invoked the temporary emergency authority of §12.1. The directives addressed evacuation from the threatened area, shared access for rescue crews, and the routing of essential supplies. Hask identified the emergency purpose of each order and published the directives through the Varek Record System. The orders did not transfer the general government of Hollowmere County to the Presiding Officer.

The Emergency Advisory Council convened under §12.2. Toma Ilvest pressed the Council to record which restrictions remained necessary as routes reopened. The Council reviewed the directives, advised Hask, and reported its findings. It could neither veto an order nor issue a competing directive; its disagreement remained advice and a public record.

Review while the emergency continued

A challenge to the reach of an access restriction went before a Varek court while the emergency was still under way. The court limited the restriction to the area for which necessity had been shown. Rescue work continued under the narrowed order. Judicial review under §12.10 was part of the emergency response, not something deferred until the water receded.

The Assembly considered the directives individually under §12.3. It continued a specific relief-supply arrangement for a limited period within the thirty-day ceiling, leaving the remaining temporary directives to expire. That vote sustained the named arrangement on its stated terms; it did not grant Hask a continuing general power to govern. Any additional period would require a fresh affirmative vote and could itself be no longer than thirty days.

The authority ended without a restoration vote

At the end of the seventy-two-hour period, Hask's initial temporary authority expired automatically. Directives without an affirmative continuation ended with it. The separately continued arrangement remained limited to the authority and duration the Assembly had approved; silence could not renew it.

No motion was needed to give the Counties, Directorates, courts, or Assembly their ordinary powers back. Section 12.11 supplied that reversion as a constitutional consequence of expiration. Hask returned to the ordinary duties of the Presiding Officer under §3.5. Neither the flood directives nor the coordination established around Tessin Ford left behind residual executive authority.
