# CONSTITUTION OF THE STATE OF ARVANE

*A Free and Self-Governing State of the Federated Republic*

*Free Port — Open for Business*

---

## PREAMBLE

We, the people of Arvane, who settled this western shore to trade freely and live independently, establish this Constitution to secure the blessings of liberty and commerce for ourselves and our posterity.

We hold that the best government is that which governs least; that free people engaged in voluntary exchange create more prosperity than any plan devised by the state; and that the proper role of government is to protect rights, enforce contracts, and otherwise leave its citizens alone. We did not come to this coast to be managed, directed, or improved by our government. We came to be free.

We therefore ordain a government of strictly limited powers: a legislature that meets only when necessary, an executive with modest authority, and courts that resolve disputes swiftly and predictably. We prohibit the taxes that elsewhere fund expansive states, and we require that any new burden on the people be justified by more than good intentions.

We do so as a State of the Federated Republic, bound by its Constitution in all matters committed to it. What the Republic requires, we honor. What it guarantees, we do not diminish. But where the Republic permits the States to choose their own course, Arvane chooses liberty.

This Constitution belongs to the people of Arvane. It is adopted, and may be amended, only by them.

---

## ARTICLE I — DECLARATION OF RIGHTS

### §1.1 Supremacy of the Federated Republic's Constitution

Nothing in this Article, or in this Constitution, may be construed to diminish, limit, or fall below any right guaranteed under Article I of the Federated Republic's Constitution. Where this Article is silent, the Republic's Constitution governs directly. This Constitution is a floor beneath the Republic's floor, never a ceiling above it.

### §1.2 Natural Rights

All persons are by nature free and have certain inherent rights, of which they cannot be deprived: the enjoyment of life and liberty; the acquisition, possession, and protection of property; and the pursuit of happiness and safety. Government is instituted to protect these rights, not to grant them.

### §1.3 Presumption of Liberty

In any dispute between the government and a person concerning the scope of individual freedom, the presumption runs in favor of liberty. The government bears the burden of demonstrating that any restriction on freedom is necessary to protect the rights of others or to fulfill a compelling public purpose that cannot be achieved by less restrictive means.

### §1.4 Equality Before the Law

No law of this State, and no act of any State or local officer, may discriminate against any person on the basis of race, ethnicity, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, religion, national origin, disability, or age, consistent with §1.6 of the Federated Republic Constitution. The law shall treat all persons equally; it shall not create privileged classes or impose special burdens on disfavored groups.

### §1.5 Freedom of Expression and Conscience

Every person may freely speak, write, publish, and peaceably assemble. The freedom of conscience is inviolate; no person may be compelled to profess any belief or to support any religious institution. These rights may not be abridged by the State.

### §1.6 Right to Keep and Bear Arms

Every person holds the right to keep and bear arms for lawful purposes including self-defense, consistent with §1.10 of the Federated Republic Constitution. The State may establish reasonable licensing and safety frameworks, provided that lawful ownership remains readily available.

### §1.7 Property Rights

Private property is inviolable. No property shall be taken for public use without just compensation, promptly paid. No regulation shall deprive an owner of all economically beneficial use of their property without compensation. The right to use, enjoy, and dispose of one's property is fundamental and shall be construed liberally.

### §1.8 Freedom of Contract

Every person has the right to enter into lawful contracts and to have those contracts enforced. The State shall not impair the obligation of contracts. Parties to a contract are presumed to have understood and accepted its terms; the State shall not rewrite private agreements except to prevent fraud or duress.

### §1.9 Freedom of Commerce

Every person has the right to engage in any lawful business, trade, or profession. No license, permit, or regulation shall be required except where necessary to protect public health or safety, and any such requirement shall be no more burdensome than necessary to achieve its purpose. The State shall not create monopolies or grant exclusive privileges to favored enterprises.

### §1.10 Security from Unreasonable Search

The right of the people to be secure in their persons, houses, papers, effects, and data against unreasonable searches and seizures shall not be violated. No warrant shall issue but upon probable cause, particularly describing the place to be searched and the persons or things to be seized.

### §1.11 Due Process

No person shall be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law. Every person accused of a crime is entitled to a speedy and public trial, to be informed of the charges, to confront witnesses, to compulsory process for obtaining witnesses, and to the assistance of counsel.

### §1.12 Freedom from Excessive Punishment

Excessive bail shall not be required, nor excessive fines imposed, nor cruel or unusual punishments inflicted.

### §1.13 Rights Retained by the People

The enumeration of certain rights in this Constitution shall not be construed to deny or disparage others retained by the people. Powers not delegated to the State by this Constitution are reserved to the people.

### §1.14 Social State Floor

The people of Arvane are entitled to access the social state systems of the Federated Republic as guaranteed by Article XII of the Republic's Constitution. This State honors that floor. This Constitution does not create additional entitlements to social services beyond what the Republic requires; the people of Arvane prefer to provide for themselves through voluntary exchange and private charity rather than through compulsory state programs.

## ARTICLE II — THE LEGISLATURE

### §2.1 Legislative Power

The legislative power of the State of Arvane is vested in a Legislature consisting of a single chamber, the Assembly.

### §2.2 Composition

(a) The Assembly consists of forty-two members elected from single-member districts of substantially equal population.

(b) Districts shall be drawn following each federal census, consistent with the criteria established in §3.3 of the Federated Republic Constitution.

### §2.3 Elections and Terms

(a) Members of the Assembly serve two-year terms.

(b) Elections are held in even-numbered years.

(c) To be eligible, a person must be a qualified elector of Arvane, at least twenty-one years of age, and a resident of the district for at least one year prior to election.

(d) No member may serve more than six terms, whether consecutive or not.

### §2.4 Biennial Sessions

(a) The Legislature meets in regular session once every two years, convening on the first Monday of February in each odd-numbered year.

(b) Regular sessions shall not exceed ninety calendar days. The Legislature may, by majority vote of members elected, extend a regular session by not more than thirty additional days. No further extension is permitted.

(c) Upon the expiration of the session, the Legislature adjourns by operation of law; any pending business lapses.

### §2.5 Special Sessions

(a) The Governor may convene the Legislature in special session by proclamation, specifying the subjects to be considered. No business other than that specified may be transacted.

(b) A special session may also be convened upon written petition of two-thirds of the members elected.

(c) No special session shall exceed twenty calendar days.

### §2.6 Officers and Procedures

(a) The Assembly shall elect a Speaker from among its members.

(b) A majority of members elected constitutes a quorum.

(c) The Assembly shall determine its own rules of procedure.

(d) The Assembly may, by two-thirds vote, expel a member.

### §2.7 Legislative Process

(a) Every bill must address a single subject, expressed in its title.

(b) No bill may become law until it has been available to members and the public for at least three days before final passage.

(c) Bills pass by majority of members elected, unless this Constitution requires a higher threshold.

(d) Every enacted law shall be published to the Arvane State Record.

### §2.8 Compensation

(a) Members of the Legislature serve as citizen legislators and shall receive per diem compensation only for days in session, plus reimbursement for travel and expenses, as provided by law.

(b) No increase in compensation shall take effect until after the next general election.

### §2.9 Limitations on Legislative Power

The Legislature may not:

(a) enact any law that conflicts with the Federated Republic Constitution;

(b) levy an income tax, as prohibited by Article VII;

(c) levy any new tax or increase any existing tax except by two-thirds vote of members elected;

(d) grant monopolies or exclusive privileges;

(e) pass any bill of attainder or ex post facto law;

(f) impair the obligation of contracts;

(g) enact any law that takes effect immediately upon passage, except appropriations for the current operations of government; all other laws take effect on January 1 following enactment unless a later date is specified.

## ARTICLE III — THE EXECUTIVE

### §3.1 Executive Power

The executive power of the State of Arvane is vested in a Governor.

### §3.2 Election and Term

(a) The Governor is elected by the qualified electors of the State at large for a term of four years.

(b) No person may serve more than two terms as Governor, whether consecutive or not.

(c) To be eligible, a person must be a citizen of the Federated Republic, a resident of Arvane for at least five years, and at least thirty years of age.

### §3.3 Powers and Duties

The Governor shall:

(a) ensure that the laws are faithfully executed;

(b) convene the Legislature in special session as provided in Article II;

(c) commission all officers of the State;

(d) grant reprieves, commutations, and pardons for offenses against the State, except in cases of impeachment, subject to the prohibition on self-clemency;

(e) serve as the commander of the State's law enforcement agencies in emergencies;

(f) represent Arvane in relations with other States and with the federal government;

(g) report to the Legislature, at the opening of each regular session, on the condition of the State.

### §3.4 The Governor's Relation to Legislation

(a) Every bill passed by the Legislature shall be presented to the Governor.

(b) The Governor may sign the bill, in which case it becomes law; or may return it, with written objections, to the Legislature within ten days of presentment.

(c) A bill returned by the Governor becomes law upon passage by a majority of members elected. The Governor's veto is advisory, not absolute.

(d) If the Governor neither signs nor returns a bill within ten days, it becomes law without the Governor's signature.

(e) The Governor may not exercise a line-item veto.

### §3.5 Lieutenant Governor

(a) A Lieutenant Governor shall be elected jointly with the Governor, on the same ticket and for the same term.

(b) The Lieutenant Governor shall be President of the Assembly and shall vote only to break a tie.

(c) The Lieutenant Governor shall become Governor upon the death, resignation, removal, or incapacity of the Governor.

### §3.6 Succession

If both the Governor and Lieutenant Governor are unable to serve, the Speaker of the Assembly shall act as Governor until the disability is removed or a Governor is elected. The Legislature shall provide by law for further succession.

### §3.7 Executive Departments

(a) The executive branch shall consist of not more than twelve principal departments, as provided by law.

(b) Department heads are appointed by the Governor and serve at the Governor's pleasure.

(c) The State shall not create new agencies, boards, or commissions except by statute, and every such body shall be assigned to a principal department.

### §3.8 Removal

The Governor, Lieutenant Governor, and other executive officers may be removed by impeachment for malfeasance or misconduct in office. The Assembly has the sole power of impeachment by majority vote. The courts shall try impeachments as provided by law; conviction requires a two-thirds vote.

## ARTICLE IV — THE JUDICIARY

### §4.1 Judicial Power

The judicial power of the State of Arvane is vested in a Supreme Court, District Courts, and such other courts as the Legislature may establish, subject to the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court of the Federated Republic.

### §4.2 The Supreme Court

(a) The Supreme Court consists of five justices.

(b) Justices are appointed by the Governor with the advice and consent of the Assembly and serve ten-year terms. A justice may be reappointed.

(c) The justice with the longest continuous service serves as Chief Justice.

(d) The Supreme Court has appellate jurisdiction over all courts of the State and original jurisdiction as provided by law.

### §4.3 District Courts

(a) District Courts are courts of general jurisdiction.

(b) District judges are appointed by the Governor with the advice and consent of the Assembly and serve six-year terms, renewable.

(c) The Legislature shall organize the State into judicial districts.

### §4.4 Commercial Court

(a) The Legislature shall establish a Commercial Court with specialized jurisdiction over business disputes, contract enforcement, and commercial matters.

(b) Proceedings in the Commercial Court shall be expedited; the Court shall resolve matters within one hundred eighty days of filing absent extraordinary circumstances.

(c) The Commercial Court may, with consent of the parties, refer matters to binding arbitration.

(d) Commercial Court judges are appointed by the Governor with the advice and consent of the Assembly and serve six-year terms.

### §4.5 Judicial Independence

(a) No public official may direct, threaten, or seek to influence the decision of any judge.

(b) Judicial compensation may not be reduced during a judge's term.

(c) Judges may be removed only for cause through impeachment or a judicial discipline process established by the Supreme Court.

### §4.6 Arbitration

The State recognizes and shall enforce private arbitration agreements. The courts shall not refuse to enforce an arbitration award except for fraud, corruption, or manifest disregard of law. Parties are encouraged to resolve disputes through private arbitration rather than litigation.

## ARTICLE V — LOCAL GOVERNMENT

### §5.1 Counties and Municipalities

(a) Arvane is divided into counties and municipalities, which are bodies corporate and politic.

(b) The Legislature shall provide by law for the organization, powers, and duties of local governments.

### §5.2 Home Rule

(a) Any county or municipality may adopt a home-rule charter by majority vote of its qualified electors.

(b) A home-rule unit may exercise any power pertaining to its government not denied by this Constitution or by law.

### §5.3 Limitations

(a) No local government may levy an income tax.

(b) No local government may impose regulations on commerce more restrictive than those imposed by the State.

(c) Local governments are subject to the same spending limitations that apply to the State under Article VII.

### §5.4 Free Port Zones

(a) The Legislature may designate Free Port Zones within Arvane where commerce is subject to minimal regulation and expedited customs processing.

(b) Enterprises operating within Free Port Zones shall be exempt from State business taxes for a period established by law, not to exceed ten years.

(c) The purpose of this section is to attract trade and investment to Arvane.

---

## ARTICLE VI — ELECTIONS

### §6.1 Suffrage

(a) Every citizen of the Federated Republic who is eighteen years of age or older and a resident of Arvane is a qualified elector.

(b) The Legislature may establish reasonable residency requirements, not to exceed thirty days.

(c) No law, rule, or practice may make voting more difficult, less accessible, or less secure.

### §6.2 Elections

(a) All State elections shall use methods provided by law and shall connect to the National Voting System.

(b) General elections are held in even-numbered years on the date established by federal law.

### §6.3 Campaign Finance

(a) Contributions to candidates for State office shall be disclosed as provided by law.

(b) The State shall not limit the amount any person may contribute to a candidate or spend in support of a candidate, except as required by the Federated Republic Constitution.

(c) The State shall not establish a system of public campaign financing.

### §6.4 State Election Office

The Governor shall appoint, with the advice and consent of the Assembly, a Secretary of Elections to administer State elections within federal standards.

---

## ARTICLE VII — FINANCE AND TAXATION

### §7.1 Taxing Power

(a) The Legislature has the authority to levy taxes, subject to the limitations of this Article.

(b) Any new tax, or any increase in an existing tax, requires a two-thirds vote of members elected to the Assembly.

### §7.2 Prohibited Taxes

(a) The State shall not levy a tax on personal income.

(b) The State shall not levy a tax on estates, inheritances, or gifts.

(c) The State shall not levy a tax on intangible personal property.

### §7.3 Permitted Taxes

The State may levy:

(a) a general sales and use tax, at a rate not to exceed six percent;

(b) taxes on the privilege of engaging in specific business activities, applied uniformly;

(c) fees for licenses, permits, and services, reasonably related to the cost of administration;

(d) taxes on real property, applied uniformly by assessed value.

### §7.4 Spending Limitation

(a) Total State appropriations for any fiscal year shall not exceed the prior year's appropriations adjusted for population growth and inflation, as certified by the State Treasurer.

(b) The Legislature may exceed this limitation only upon a two-thirds vote of members elected and a declaration of fiscal emergency specifying the cause and duration.

(c) Any revenue collected in excess of the spending limitation shall be returned to taxpayers or deposited in a reserve fund, as provided by law.

### §7.5 Balanced Budget

(a) The Governor shall submit to the Legislature a proposed budget that does not exceed anticipated revenues.

(b) The Legislature shall not appropriate funds in excess of anticipated revenues.

(c) If a deficit occurs, the Governor shall reduce expenditures proportionally across all departments to eliminate the deficit.

### §7.6 State Treasury

All public money received by the State shall be deposited into the State Treasury and may be withdrawn only by appropriation.

### §7.7 Debt Limitation

(a) The State may not incur debt except for capital improvements, and only upon approval by two-thirds of the Assembly and ratification by a majority of electors voting on the question.

(b) Total State debt may not exceed five percent of the assessed value of taxable property in the State.

### §7.8 No State Enterprises

The State shall not own or operate any commercial enterprise in competition with private business, except for essential public infrastructure as provided by law.

---

## ARTICLE VIII — REGULATION

### §8.1 Regulatory Principles

(a) Regulation of private conduct is justified only to protect the rights of others or to address a genuine threat to public health or safety.

(b) Every regulation shall be no more burdensome than necessary to achieve its purpose.

(c) The State shall prefer voluntary compliance, industry self-regulation, and market mechanisms over mandatory regulation.

### §8.2 Regulatory Process

(a) No regulation may take effect until it has been published for public comment for at least thirty days.

(b) Every regulation must include a statement of its purpose, its expected costs and benefits, and the evidence supporting its necessity.

(c) Any person may challenge a regulation as exceeding the authority granted by statute or as more burdensome than necessary.

### §8.3 Sunset Provisions

(a) Every regulation shall expire ten years after its effective date unless reenacted by the Legislature.

(b) The Legislature shall review expiring regulations and shall reenact only those that remain necessary and effective.

### §8.4 Occupational Licensing

(a) The State may require a license to practice an occupation only where necessary to protect public health or safety.

(b) Licensing requirements shall be the least restrictive necessary to protect the public.

(c) The Legislature shall review all occupational licensing requirements every ten years and shall repeal those that are not justified.

---

## ARTICLE IX — THE ARVANE STATE RECORD

### §9.1 Establishment

The State shall maintain the Arvane State Record as the permanent public record of State governmental action, freely accessible at no cost.

### §9.2 Publication Requirement

(a) Every law, executive action, judicial decision, and regulation shall be published to the Record.

(b) Records may not be deleted or altered; corrections are entered as new records.

### §9.3 Federal Interface

The Record shall maintain compatibility with the National Record System.

---

## ARTICLE X — AMENDMENT AND GENERAL PROVISIONS

### §10.1 Amendment by Legislature

An amendment may be proposed by a two-thirds vote of the Assembly. A proposed amendment takes effect upon ratification by a majority of electors voting on the question at the next general election.

### §10.2 Amendment by Initiative

(a) The people may propose amendments by petition signed by qualified electors equal to ten percent of votes cast in the last gubernatorial election.

(b) A qualifying petition proceeds to referendum; passage requires a majority of votes cast.

### §10.3 Constitutional Convention

The Legislature may, by two-thirds vote, submit to the electors the question of calling a constitutional convention. If approved, the Legislature shall provide for the election of delegates. Any revision requires ratification by the electors.

### §10.4 Protected Provisions

(a) The prohibition on income taxation in Article VII, §7.2(a), may not be repealed or amended except by a vote of two-thirds of the electors voting on the question.

(b) No amendment may conflict with the Federated Republic Constitution.

### §10.5 Oath of Office

Every person elected or appointed to office shall take an oath to support the Constitution of the Federated Republic and the Constitution of Arvane, and to faithfully discharge the duties of the office.

### §10.6 Official Records

All official records are public records except as provided by law for the protection of individual privacy.

### §10.7 Continuity of Government

The Legislature shall provide by law for the continuity of government in emergencies.

### §10.8 Seat of Government

The seat of government shall be established by law.

### §10.9 Construction

This Constitution shall be construed to limit the powers of government and to protect the liberty of the people. Where doubt exists, the interpretation favoring individual freedom and limited government shall prevail.

---

## SCHEDULE

### Section 1. Effective Date

This Constitution takes effect upon ratification by the people of Arvane and certification by the Elections Panel of the Federated Republic.

### Section 2. Existing Laws

All laws in force at the time this Constitution takes effect, and not inconsistent with it, remain in force until amended or repealed. Laws inconsistent with Article VII shall be void upon the effective date.

### Section 3. Existing Officers

All officers holding office at the time this Constitution takes effect shall continue in office until their successors are elected or appointed.

### Section 4. First Session

The first regular session of the Legislature under this Constitution shall convene on the first Monday of February following ratification.

### Section 5. Transition

The Legislature shall provide by law for an orderly transition to the government established by this Constitution.
