# THE CONSTITUTION OF THE STATE OF CALDENMERE

*Adopted under the authority reserved to the States by the Constitution of the Federated Republic, Article III §3.12, and consistent with the Statehood Audit standard of Article XV §15.2.*

---

## PREAMBLE

We, the people of Caldenmere, heirs to the harbor and the shoreline, to the counting-houses and the fishing towns alike, in order to secure for ourselves and our posterity the blessings of a stable, prosperous, and self-governing state within the Federated Republic, do ordain and establish this Constitution.

We hold that government closest to the water knows the water best, and government closest to the town square knows the town best. We commit our State to the rule of law, to the dignity of every Inhabitant, and to the enduring principle that experience and judgment, freely earned and openly exercised, best serve the long continuance of our common home.

Nothing in this Constitution shall be construed to diminish, abridge, or conflict with any right, protection, or obligation established by the Constitution of the Federated Republic, which remains supreme.

---

## ARTICLE I — DECLARATION OF RIGHTS

*This Declaration supplements, and in no case diminishes, the rights guaranteed to every person under Article I of the Federated Republic Constitution. Where any provision of this Article is read to conflict with a federal right, the federal right controls.*

**§1.1 — Inherent Rights.** All persons within Caldenmere possess certain inherent rights, among which are the enjoyment of life and liberty, the acquisition and protection of property, and the pursuit of safety and prosperity. These rights are not granted by this Constitution; they are recognized by it.

**§1.2 — Equality Before State Law.** No law of this State, and no act of any State or municipal officer, may discriminate against any person on a basis prohibited by Article I of the Federated Republic Constitution. No statute, charter, or municipal ordinance of this State may establish property ownership, income, or net worth as a qualification for any public office, board, or commission. Distinctions in social standing, howsoever they arise in practice, confer no legal privilege and impose no legal disability.

**§1.3 — Freedom of Expression, Assembly, and the Press.** The rights of expression, assembly, petition, and a free press within Caldenmere are protected at least to the extent guaranteed by Article I of the Federated Republic Constitution and may not be further restricted by State or municipal law except as that Constitution permits.

**§1.4 — Right to Bear Arms.** Consistent with the Federated Republic Constitution, the State may establish licensing and safety frameworks for the keeping and bearing of arms, provided that lawful ownership for self-defense remains available to every citizen and legal resident.

**§1.5 — Searches, Seizures, and Maritime Inspection.** No person, vessel, home, or place of business within Caldenmere may be searched, and no person or property may be seized, without a warrant issued upon specific, articulable grounds by an independent Caldenmere court, except as the Federated Republic Constitution otherwise provides. Routine customs and safety inspection of vessels at the Port of Calden shall be governed by statute consistent with this section and may not be used as a pretext for a search of persons or cargo without individualized cause.

**§1.6 — Due Process and Access to Courts.** No person may be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law. Every person has the right to seek remedy for injury before an independent and impartial Caldenmere court, consistent with the Federated Republic Constitution. Where a person cannot afford counsel in a Common Pleas matter affecting liberty or subsistence, the Court shall appoint counsel at public expense.

**§1.7 — Right to a Jury.** In any criminal matter tried before the Court of Common Pleas, the accused holds the right to a trial by an impartial jury of the district, consistent with the Federated Republic Constitution.

**§1.8 — Property Rights and Just Compensation.** Private property may not be taken for public use without just compensation, determined by a process independent of the taking authority. Where the State or a municipality designates land as Strategic State Infrastructure under Article V of this Constitution, the affected owner holds the right to an independent valuation hearing before the High Chancery prior to any transfer of title.

**§1.9 — Freedom of Contract and Commerce.** The right to enter into lawful contracts, to engage in trade, and to pursue a lawful business or profession is protected. The State may regulate commerce for the public health, safety, and welfare, but may not impose a burden on out-of-state goods, services, or persons greater than that imposed on its own, consistent with the Federated Republic Constitution.

**§1.10 — Labor and Association.** Every worker holds the right to organize, to bargain collectively through representatives of their own choosing, and to engage in peaceable collective action, without retaliation by any employer licensed or chartered within this State.

**§1.11 — Stewardship of the Shoreline and Waters.** The State recognizes an obligation to the citizens of Caldenmere, present and future, to maintain the ecological integrity of its harbor, coastline, and territorial waters. No State or municipal action may permit environmental harm to a defined coastal community without a genuine, proportionate program of mitigation, consistent with the Federated Republic Constitution.

**§1.12 — Right of Local Petition.** Every resident of a municipality holds the right to petition their local governing body directly and to receive a public response within the period defined by municipal charter or, absent such charter provision, within sixty days.

**§1.13 — Non-Derogation.** Rights guaranteed by the Federated Republic Constitution may be limited or derogated only as that Constitution permits. Rights created independently by this Article may be limited only by law, for a legitimate public purpose, and only to the extent reasonably necessary and consistent with the remainder of this Constitution. No State action may fall below the federal non-derogable floor.

## ARTICLE II — THE EXECUTIVE

**§2.1 — The Governor: Office and Eligibility.** The executive power of ceremonial representation and diplomatic liaison is vested in the Governor. To hold the office, a person must have been a citizen of the Federated Republic and a resident of Caldenmere for at least five years and must satisfy such lawful disclosure requirements as apply to State office.

**§2.2 — Election and Term.** The Governor is elected by the qualified electors of the State at large for a term of four years, by ranked-choice ballot administered consistent with the National Voting System standards of the Federated Republic Constitution. No person may serve more than two consecutive terms as Governor.

**§2.3 — Powers and Duties of the Governor.** The Governor shall:
(a) represent Caldenmere in dealings with the constitutional officers and institutions of the Federated Republic;
(b) sign or return bills passed by the Legislature, consistent with §3.3 of this Constitution;
(c) nominate members of the State Senate, subject to confirmation by the House of Districts;
(d) appoint State Ministers upon the recommendation of the President of the State Senate;
(e) grant clemency for State offenses, subject to the limitations imposed by the Federated Republic Constitution;
(f) call special sessions of the Legislature.

**§2.4 — The President of the State Senate: Office and Selection.** The administrative executive authority of the State is vested in the President of the State Senate, who is elected by the members of the State Senate from among their own number and serves until resignation, removal, loss of senatorial office, or lawful incapacity. The President of the State Senate is a distinct office from any federal legislative officer, consistent with §11.4 of this Constitution, and no Caldenmere record may abbreviate the title in a manner creating ambiguity with a federal office.

**§2.5 — Powers and Duties of the President of the State Senate.** The President of the State Senate shall:
(a) set the legislative calendar and the order of business of the State Senate;
(b) direct and supervise the State Ministers in the execution of State policy;
(c) issue Administrative Decrees for the implementation of existing statute, provided that every decree is consistent with statute, creates no obligation or penalty not authorized by law, and remains subject to judicial review;
(d) represent the State in ongoing technical and commercial negotiations, including with federal domain officers.

**§2.6 — Succession and Incapacity.** Upon the death, resignation, or removal of the Governor, the President of the State Senate serves as Acting Governor until a successor is elected at the next general election or a special election called for that purpose, as provided by statute. During service as Acting Governor, the President may not exercise the powers of President of the State Senate or preside over the Senate; the Senate shall elect an Acting President from among its members for that period. Upon the death, resignation, removal, or temporary incapacity of the President of the State Senate, the State Senate shall elect an Acting President or successor, as appropriate, from among its members. Temporary incapacity of the Governor causes the President of the State Senate to serve as Acting Governor for the duration of the incapacity. The manner for determining disputed incapacity and recognizing recovery shall be established by law, subject to judicial review.

**§2.7 — Removal.** The Governor may be removed for conviction of a disqualifying offense, demonstrated incapacity, or serious constitutional breach upon separate votes of two-thirds of the full membership of the House of Districts and two-thirds of the full membership of the State Senate. The President of the State Senate may be removed by two-thirds of the full membership of the State Senate.

**§2.8 — State Ministers.** The Governor shall appoint State Ministers to lead the administrative departments of the State — including, at minimum, Maritime Affairs, Treasury, Justice, and Interior — upon recommendation of the President of the State Senate. Ministers serve at the pleasure of the President of the State Senate. The Governor shall formally dismiss a Minister upon the President's recommendation. Ministerial appointments, dismissals, and final directives are published to the Caldenmere Registry.

## ARTICLE III — THE LEGISLATURE

**§3.1 — Composition.** The Legislature of Caldenmere consists of two chambers: the House of Districts and the State Senate.

**§3.2 — The House of Districts.** The House of Districts is composed of members elected by proportional representation from districts of substantially equal population, drawn consistent with the non-discrimination and anti-dilution requirements of the Federated Republic Constitution. Members serve two-year terms. No member may serve more than six consecutive terms without sitting out one full electoral cycle.

**§3.3 — Legislative Process.** All bills originate in the House of Districts. A bill passed by the House of Districts is transmitted to the State Senate, which may approve, amend within the bill's subject matter, or veto it. Following passage by both chambers, a bill is transmitted to the Governor, who may sign it, allow it to become law without signature within ten business days, or return it with objections. A returned bill becomes law upon a subsequent two-thirds vote of the full membership of the House of Districts; the prior assent of the State Senate remains effective.

**§3.4 — The State Senate.** The State Senate is composed of not fewer than eighteen nor more than thirty members, the exact number to be fixed by law. Senators are appointed for life by the Governor and confirmed by a majority of the full membership of the House of Districts. The Governor shall nominate a qualified successor within ninety days after a Senate vacancy occurs. Senators may be removed only for cause — conviction of a disqualifying offense, demonstrated incapacity, corruption or serious misconduct in office, or a serious and willful violation of this Constitution — upon a two-thirds vote of the full membership of the State Senate. No Senator may resign in favor of a designated successor; each vacancy is filled through the ordinary nomination and confirmation process.

**§3.5 — Powers of the State Senate.** The State Senate holds absolute veto authority over legislation passed by the House of Districts, subject only to §3.7; final approval authority over the State budget; and the sole authority to elect the President of the State Senate from among its own members.

**§3.6 — Presiding Vote.** The President of the State Senate presides over sessions of the State Senate and, while presiding, does not cast the ordinary vote of a Senator but may cast a vote to break a tie.

**§3.7 — The Sovereign Recourse (The Override Provision).**
(1) *Purpose.* To ensure that no institutional decision, however reasoned, remains entirely beyond the reach of the citizenry, this section provides a final legal avenue for the public to affirm a specific legislative will.
(2) *Scope.* This mechanism applies only to the override of a specific State Senate veto of a bill passed by the House of Districts. It does not remove Senators, does not amend this Constitution, and does not extend to any other State Senate action.
(3) *Phase I — Petition of Merit.* Following a State Senate veto, citizens may initiate a petition. To qualify for a vote, the petition must secure verified signatures equal to fifteen percent of the total registered electorate within sixty days of the veto.
(4) *Phase II — Popular Affirmation.* A qualifying petition triggers a statewide referendum administered consistent with applicable federal and State election standards. The veto is overturned only upon a supermajority of sixty-five percent of votes cast.

**§3.8 — Ethics and Disclosure.** Members of both chambers shall disclose their financial interests consistent with applicable federal standards. The Legislature shall maintain a joint ethics committee with authority to sanction members for conduct falling short of removal.

**§3.9 — Publication.** The final text of every enacted law, every State Senate veto, every Administrative Decree, and every disclosure required under this Article shall be published to the Caldenmere Registry, consistent with Article VIII of this Constitution.

## ARTICLE IV — THE JUDICIARY

**§4.1 — Judicial Power.** The judicial power of Caldenmere is vested in the Court of Common Pleas, the High Chancery, and the Court of Appeals of Caldenmere, subject at all times to the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court of the Federated Republic as provided by the Federated Republic Constitution.

**§4.2 — The Court of Common Pleas.** The Court of Common Pleas holds general jurisdiction over civil and criminal matters arising within the State, including matters affecting the personal rights of Inhabitants under Article I of this Constitution and Article I of the Federated Republic Constitution.

**§4.3 — The High Chancery.** The High Chancery holds specialized jurisdiction over commercial disputes, maritime law, land title disputes, and matters arising from the Ministry of Maritime Affairs and the Calden Port Authority. Judgments of the High Chancery are subject to the same constitutional constraints as any other Caldenmere court and confer no exemption from Article I protections.

**§4.4 — The Court of Appeals of Caldenmere.** The Court of Appeals hears appeals from both the Court of Common Pleas and the High Chancery and is the court of last resort within the State on questions of State law. Constitutional questions arising in Caldenmere proceedings remain subject to the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court of the Federated Republic as provided by federal law and the Federated Republic Constitution.

**§4.5 — Judicial Selection.** Judges of the Court of Common Pleas and the High Chancery are nominated by the Governor from a Judicial Registry maintained under published, objective criteria concerning professional competence, experience, integrity, and fitness for judicial office, and confirmed by a majority of the State Senate. The Registry may not exclude a candidate solely because of lawful political or ideological belief. Judges of the Court of Appeals are nominated by the Governor and confirmed by two-thirds of the State Senate. All judges serve single, non-renewable terms of twelve years.

**§4.6 — Judicial Discipline and Removal.** The Legislature shall establish an independent Judicial Conduct Commission to investigate judicial incapacity, corruption, serious misconduct, and willful constitutional breach. No judge may be removed except for cause, after notice and opportunity to be heard, upon recommendation of the Commission and judgment of an independent judicial tribunal constituted by law. Disciplinary proceedings shall preserve judicial independence and due process.

**§4.7 — Judicial Continuity.** When a vacancy or temporary incapacity prevents the Court of Appeals from carrying out its duties, a qualified judge of the High Chancery or Court of Common Pleas shall serve temporarily according to an objective statutory order based principally on continuous judicial service. Temporary assignment to the lower courts shall be provided by law. Acting service does not confer any claim to permanent appointment.

**§4.8 — Judicial Independence.** No public official may direct, threaten, retaliate against, or seek to influence the decision of a Caldenmere judge in any pending matter. Judicial compensation may not be reduced during a judge's term.

## ARTICLE V — LOCAL GOVERNMENT AND MUNICIPAL HOME RULE

**§5.1 — Municipal Sovereignty.** Every city, town, and municipal corporation within Caldenmere holds full and autonomous authority over matters of local concern, including zoning, municipal policing, local taxation, and local ordinance, except as this Article otherwise provides.

**§5.2 — Charter Government.** Any municipality may adopt a home-rule charter by majority vote of its qualified electors, establishing its own form of local government, provided the charter maintains democratic accountability and does not conflict with this Constitution or the Federated Republic Constitution.

**§5.3 — Strategic State Infrastructure.** State authority may override municipal zoning or land-use authority only where the Legislature, acting under published statutory criteria and upon recommendation of the relevant State Minister, formally designates a project as Strategic State Infrastructure serving a genuine statewide or intermunicipal public purpose. Any affected municipality or property owner holds the right, before the override takes effect, to review by the High Chancery of the legality of the designation and to the independent valuation process required by §1.8 where property is taken. Statute may provide a narrowly tailored temporary procedure for immediate threats to life, navigation, or essential infrastructure, subject to prompt judicial review.

**§5.4 — Protection from Fiscal Coercion.** The State may not systematically withhold or condition fiscal support for the purpose of compelling municipalities to surrender functions that this Constitution assigns to local government. Nothing in this section guarantees any particular grant, subsidy, or level of State funding.

**§5.5 — Recall.** A municipality may, by its own charter, establish a recall process for local officials, provided the process is political rather than judicial in character and the final decision rests with the municipality's eligible voters.

## ARTICLE VI — FINANCE, TAXATION, AND ADMINISTRATION

**§6.1 — Taxing Power.** The Legislature holds the exclusive authority to levy State taxes, duties, and fees, subject to the Federated Republic Constitution. No tax may discriminate against goods, services, or persons of another State or Territory. A State department, authority, or administrative body may impose a charge, tariff, assessment, or fee only to the extent authorized by statute.

**§6.2 — The State Treasury and Audit.** All public money received by the State is deposited into the State Treasury and may be withdrawn only by legislative appropriation. State accounts shall be subject to regular independent audit, and final audit reports shall be published to the Caldenmere Registry.

**§6.3 — The Budget Process.** The Governor and the President of the State Senate shall each submit a proposed budget to the House of Districts by the deadline established by statute. The House of Districts originates the appropriations bill; the State Senate reviews and may amend it within its subject matter. If a general appropriations law has not taken effect at the beginning of a fiscal year, essential government operations and existing legal obligations shall continue temporarily under a continuing appropriation established by law at no more than the prior lawful rate until a new appropriations law takes effect.

**§6.4 — The Calden Port Authority.** The Legislature shall maintain the Calden Port Authority as the administrative body responsible for the management, development, and regulation of the Port of Calden. The Authority shall be governed by a board appointed under statute for fixed, staggered terms and protected from arbitrary removal. Its major contracts, concessions, tariffs, leases, and final regulatory decisions shall be published to the Caldenmere Registry and shall be subject to independent audit and judicial review. No ownership interest, commercial franchise, or social status may itself confer a seat on the board or a legal right to select a board member. The Authority may exercise only those powers committed to it by law and acquires no general municipal authority merely because a matter occurs within or near the Port.

**§6.5 — Municipal Taxation.** Municipalities retain independent taxing authority within their own jurisdictions, subject to the non-discrimination requirements of Article I of this Constitution and the Federated Republic Constitution.

## ARTICLE VII — ELECTIONS

**§7.1 — State Elections.** State elections shall be conducted using ranked-choice, proportional, or majority-vote methods as the Legislature provides by statute, except where this Constitution specifies the electoral system for a particular office or chamber. Elections shall connect to and operate through the National Voting System established by the Federated Republic Constitution.

**§7.2 — Suffrage.** Every citizen of the Federated Republic who is a resident of Caldenmere and eighteen years of age or older holds the right to vote in State and municipal elections. Election rules shall be neutral, reasonably accessible, and may not be designed or applied to burden the franchise unnecessarily or discriminatorily.

**§7.3 — State Election Administration.** The Legislature shall establish an independent State Election Office to administer State elections and municipal elections using State infrastructure within applicable federal standards. The chief administrator shall be nominated by the Governor, confirmed by a majority of the full membership of the House of Districts, serve a fixed term established by law, and be removable only for cause. The Office shall publish final statewide results and certifications to the Caldenmere Registry. Disputes concerning eligibility, tabulation, certification, or the legality of an election are subject to judicial review.

---

## ARTICLE VIII — THE CALDENMERE REGISTRY

**§8.1 — Establishment.** The State shall maintain the Caldenmere Registry as the permanent public record of State governmental action, equivalent in permanence and public accessibility to the National Record System established by the Federated Republic Constitution.

**§8.2 — Publication Requirement.** Every State and municipal governmental act required to be published under this Constitution shall be entered into the Registry as a permanent record. The Registry shall include enacted laws, Administrative Decrees, major appointments and removals, final agency rules, final election certifications, final audit reports, formal decisions of the Calden Port Authority, and public contracts above a threshold established by law. Records may not be deleted or silently altered after publication; corrections shall be entered as new records alongside the original.

**§8.3 — Lawful Confidentiality.** Law may narrowly restrict immediate public access to information whose disclosure would materially compromise personal privacy, a pending investigation, public security, or another compelling public interest. No such restriction permits alteration, destruction, or permanent concealment of the underlying governmental record.

**§8.4 — Federal Interface.** The Registry shall maintain export protocols fully compatible with the National Record System, ensuring that all federally required disclosures are available to authorized federal Monitors and auditors without avoidable delay or reformatting burden.

---

## ARTICLE IX — EDUCATION

**§9.1 — Public Education.** The State shall provide free, compulsory public education adequate to enable full civic participation and consistent with the Federated Republic Constitution.

**§9.2 — Higher Learning and Civic Excellence.** The State recognizes its chartered universities, colleges, research institutions, professional schools, academies, and cultural institutions as instruments of long-term prosperity, civic continuity, learning, and public distinction. The State may support such institutions through appropriations, endowments, land grants, charter privileges, public-private foundations, and other means established by law, and may encourage the attraction of students and scholars from throughout the Federated Republic. Institutions receiving public charter or support shall retain academic independence consistent with law, and no public privilege, admission rule, or public funding arrangement may establish hereditary, property-based, wealth-based, or other status-based legal entitlement inconsistent with Article I of this Constitution or the Federated Republic Constitution.

---

## ARTICLE X — AMENDMENT

**§10.1 — Legislative Proposal.** An amendment to this Constitution may be proposed by a two-thirds vote of the full membership of the House of Districts and a two-thirds vote of the full membership of the State Senate.

**§10.2 — Ratification.** A proposed amendment takes effect upon ratification by a majority of the State's qualified electors voting in a referendum administered consistent with Article VII of this Constitution.

**§10.3 — Citizen Initiative.** Citizens may propose an amendment to this Constitution through the same procedural structure established in §3.7, except that Phase One requires verified signatures equal to twenty percent of the registered electorate and Phase Two requires approval by sixty-five percent of votes cast.

**§10.4 — Continuity of Office.** Unless an amendment expressly provides otherwise, an amendment altering the tenure, composition, or powers of a constitutional office does not by itself terminate the lawful service of a person holding that office when the amendment takes effect.

**§10.5 — Federal Consistency.** Every amendment is subject to the supremacy rule of §11.1. No amendment may diminish a right, structure, or requirement imposed on the State by the Federated Republic Constitution.

---

## ARTICLE XI — RELATIONSHIP TO THE FEDERATED REPUBLIC

**§11.1 — Supremacy.** This Constitution and all laws enacted under it are subordinate to the Constitution of the Federated Republic. Where any provision of State law conflicts with the Federated Republic Constitution, the federal Constitution controls to the extent of the conflict.

**§11.2 — Statehood Audit Compliance.** The State shall maintain, on a continuing basis, functioning electoral processes, basic rule of law and judicial function, and full compliance with Article I of the Federated Republic Constitution, consistent with the Statehood Audit requirements of that Constitution.

**§11.3 — Federal Property and Operational Use Rights.** Any federal installation, facility, or use right sited within Caldenmere shall be held and registered consistently with the Operational Use Right framework of Article XVIII of the Federated Republic Constitution. No such use right transfers territorial sovereignty from the State, and the right terminates or reverts as the federal Constitution provides when the federal function for which it was established ends.

**§11.4 — Federal Officer Non-Confusion.** No office established by this Constitution may bear a title identical to a federal constitutional office in a manner creating reasonable ambiguity between the two. Where such ambiguity would otherwise arise, the State office shall bear a distinguishing qualifier.

---

*This Constitution, upon ratification by the qualified electors of Caldenmere, is the supreme law of the State, subject always to the Constitution of the Federated Republic.*
