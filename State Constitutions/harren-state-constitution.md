# CONSTITUTION OF THE STATE OF HARREN

*A Free and Self-Governing State of the Federated Republic*

*Founding State — Constitutional Ancestor*

---

## PREAMBLE

We, the people of Harren, gathered in this northern heartland to build what had not been built before.

We came to these hills and valleys not to replicate the governments we had known, but to improve upon them — to separate what ought to be separated, to check what ought to be checked, and to make transparent what had too long been hidden. We believed then, as we believe now, that political leadership and administrative competence serve different purposes and require different designs; that judges must answer to the people they serve; and that no act of government should escape the public record.

What we built here became a model. When the Republic was founded, its framers looked to Harren. Some of our innovations they adopted; others they adapted to the scale of a nation. We take pride in that inheritance, and we accept the obligation it carries: to continue deserving the trust that made us the founding state.

We ordain and establish this Constitution to secure self-government for Harren, to protect the rights of every person within our borders, and to preserve the institutions that proved worthy of imitation. We do so as a State of the Federated Republic, bound by its Constitution in all matters committed to it, and sovereign in all matters reserved to us.

This Constitution belongs to the people of Harren. It is adopted, and may be amended, only by them.

---

## ARTICLE I — DECLARATION OF RIGHTS

### §1.1 Supremacy of the Federated Republic's Constitution

Nothing in this Article, or in this Constitution, may be construed to diminish, limit, or fall below any right guaranteed under Article I of the Federated Republic's Constitution. Where this Article is silent, the Republic's Constitution governs directly and of its own force. Where this Article speaks further than the Republic's Constitution, the greater protection applies within Harren. This Constitution is a floor beneath the Republic's floor, never a ceiling above it.

### §1.2 Inherent Rights

All persons are by nature free and independent, and have certain inherent rights, among which are those of enjoying and defending life and liberty; acquiring, possessing, and protecting property; and pursuing and obtaining safety and happiness. Government is instituted for the protection, security, and benefit of the people, and they have the right at all times to alter or reform it when the public good requires.

### §1.3 Equality Before the Law

No law of this State, and no act of any State or local officer, may discriminate against any person on the basis of race, ethnicity, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, religion, national origin, disability, age, or economic status, consistent with §1.6 of the Federated Republic Constitution. All political power is inherent in the people, and all free governments are founded on their authority and instituted for their benefit.

### §1.4 Freedom of Expression, Conscience, and Assembly

Every person may freely speak, write, and publish on all subjects, and no law shall be passed to restrain or abridge the liberty of speech or of the press. The people have the right peaceably to assemble for the common good, and to apply to those invested with the powers of government for redress of grievances. The rights of conscience shall not be infringed, and no person shall be compelled to attend, erect, or support any place of worship, or to maintain any ministry, against their consent.

### §1.5 Right to Bear Arms

Every person holds the right to keep and bear arms for lawful purposes including self-defense, consistent with §1.10 of the Federated Republic Constitution. The State may establish licensing and safety frameworks, provided that lawful ownership for self-defense remains available to every citizen and legal resident.

### §1.6 Security from Unreasonable Search

The right of the people to be secure in their persons, houses, papers, effects, and data against unreasonable searches and seizures shall not be violated; and no warrant shall issue but upon probable cause, supported by oath or affirmation, particularly describing the place to be searched and the persons or things to be seized.

### §1.7 Due Process of Law

No person shall be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law. Every person has the right to a certain remedy in the laws for all injuries or wrongs which they may receive in their person, property, or character; and justice shall be administered freely and without denial, promptly and without delay, conformably to the laws.

### §1.8 Rights of the Accused

In all criminal prosecutions, the accused has the right to a speedy and public trial by an impartial jury; to be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation; to be confronted with the witnesses against them; to have compulsory process for obtaining witnesses in their favor; and to have the assistance of counsel for their defense. No person shall be compelled in any criminal case to be a witness against themselves.

### §1.9 Freedom from Excessive Punishment

Excessive bail shall not be required, nor excessive fines imposed, nor cruel or unusual punishments inflicted. All persons shall be bailable by sufficient sureties, except for capital offenses when the proof is evident or the presumption great.

### §1.10 Property and Just Compensation

Private property shall not be taken for public use without just compensation, determined by a process independent of the taking authority, consistent with §1.8 of the Federated Republic Constitution. No person shall be deprived of their property except by the judgment of their peers or the law of the land.

### §1.11 Freedom of Contract and Commerce

The right to enter into lawful contracts, to engage in trade, and to pursue a lawful occupation is protected. The State may regulate commerce for the public health, safety, and welfare, but may not impose a burden on out-of-state goods, services, or persons greater than that imposed on its own, consistent with §12.7 of the Federated Republic Constitution.

### §1.12 Labor Rights

Every worker in Harren holds the right to organize, to bargain collectively through representatives of their own choosing, and to engage in peaceable collective action. No person shall be denied employment on account of membership or non-membership in any labor organization.

### §1.13 Right to Transparency

The people have the right to know how they are governed. All official acts of the State and its subdivisions shall be matters of public record, and no governmental action shall be concealed from the people except upon grounds narrowly defined by law and subject to judicial review. This right is the foundation of the State Record established in Article IX.

### §1.14 Non-Derogation

No right enumerated in this Article may be suspended, limited, or derogated except as, and to the extent that, Article I §1.19 of the Federated Republic Constitution permits derogation of the corresponding federal right, and never below the federal non-derogable floor of §1.19.a. The enumeration of certain rights shall not be construed to deny or disparage others retained by the people.

## ARTICLE II — THE DUAL EXECUTIVE

### §2.1 Division of Executive Power

The executive power of the State of Harren is divided between two officers: the Governor, who holds political authority, and the State Administrator, who holds administrative authority. Neither officer is subordinate to the other; each exercises the powers assigned to them by this Constitution within their respective domains.

### §2.2 The Governor — Office and Election

(a) The Governor is elected by the qualified electors of the State at large for a term of four years, by ranked-choice ballot administered consistent with the National Voting System standards.

(b) No person may serve more than two consecutive terms as Governor. After sitting out one full term, a former Governor may again seek the office.

(c) To hold the office of Governor, a person must be a citizen of the Federated Republic, a resident of Harren for at least five years, and at least thirty years of age at the time of taking office.

### §2.3 Powers and Duties of the Governor

The Governor shall:

(a) represent Harren in all political relations with other States, with the federal government, and with foreign delegations received through federal channels;

(b) sign or return bills passed by the Legislature, as provided in §2.5;

(c) deliver to the Legislature, at the opening of each regular session, information on the state of the State and recommend such measures as the Governor considers necessary and expedient;

(d) grant reprieves, commutations, and pardons for offenses against the State, except in cases of impeachment, and subject to the same prohibition on self-clemency established in §2.12 of the Federated Republic Constitution;

(e) convene the Legislature in special session upon extraordinary occasions;

(f) serve as the ceremonial head of state for Harren;

(g) nominate justices of the High Court and judges of the inferior courts for election, as provided in Article IV.

### §2.4 Lieutenant Governor

(a) A Lieutenant Governor shall be elected jointly with the Governor, on the same ticket and for the same term.

(b) The Lieutenant Governor shall be President of the Senate and shall have no vote except to break a tie.

(c) In the event of the death, resignation, removal, or incapacity of the Governor, the Lieutenant Governor shall become Governor for the remainder of the term.

(d) The Lieutenant Governor shall perform such other duties within the political domain as the Governor may assign.

### §2.5 The Governor's Veto

(a) Every bill passed by both houses of the Legislature shall be presented to the Governor. The Governor may sign the bill, in which case it becomes law; or may return it, with written objections, to the house in which it originated within ten business days of presentment.

(b) A bill returned by the Governor may become law only upon passage by a two-thirds vote of the members elected to each house.

(c) If the Governor neither signs nor returns a bill within ten business days of presentment, it becomes law without the Governor's signature, unless the Legislature by adjournment prevents its return, in which case it does not become law.

(d) The Governor may not exercise a line-item veto on appropriation bills; the budget is the province of the State Administrator and the Legislature.

### §2.6 The State Administrator — Office and Selection

(a) The State Administrator is elected by a joint session of the Legislature for a term of eight years.

(b) Election requires an absolute majority of the members elected to the Legislature sitting jointly. If no candidate receives a majority on the first ballot, the two candidates receiving the most votes proceed to a runoff.

(c) No person may serve more than one term as State Administrator. The term is non-renewable to ensure both continuity and eventual rotation.

(d) To hold the office of State Administrator, a person must be a citizen of the Federated Republic, a resident of Harren for at least seven years, and at least thirty-five years of age at the time of taking office.

(e) The State Administrator may not hold or seek any other public office during their term.

### §2.7 Powers and Duties of the State Administrator

The State Administrator shall:

(a) direct and supervise the administrative departments of the State;

(b) appoint the heads of administrative departments, with the advice and consent of the Senate;

(c) prepare and submit to the Legislature an annual proposed budget;

(d) execute the budget as enacted by the Legislature;

(e) ensure the faithful execution of the laws of Harren within the administrative domain;

(f) maintain the civil service of the State on principles of merit and competence;

(g) report to the Legislature annually on the administrative state of Harren, including the efficiency and integrity of State operations.

### §2.8 Domain Boundaries

(a) Where a matter falls clearly within one domain — political or administrative — that officer acts without the concurrence of the other.

(b) Where the domain of a proposed action is disputed, the officers shall consult. If they cannot agree, either may petition the High Court for a determination, which the Court shall render within fourteen days.

(c) Pending resolution of a domain dispute, neither officer may take the disputed action.

### §2.9 Succession to the State Administrator

(a) If the State Administrator dies, resigns, is removed, or becomes permanently incapacitated, the Legislature shall elect a successor within sixty days to serve the remainder of the term.

(b) During any vacancy, the Secretary of Administration — or, if that office is vacant, the most senior department head by length of service — shall exercise the administrative functions of the office in a caretaker capacity, limited to maintaining existing operations.

### §2.10 Removal

(a) The Governor, Lieutenant Governor, and State Administrator may be removed from office through impeachment for cause. The Assembly has the sole power of impeachment by majority vote of members elected. The Senate has the sole power to try impeachments; conviction requires a two-thirds vote of members elected.

(b) The State Administrator may also be removed by a two-thirds vote of the Legislature sitting jointly, upon grounds of malfeasance, neglect of duty, or demonstrated incapacity. Such removal is not an impeachment and does not require Assembly initiation.

(c) Judgment upon conviction or removal extends only to removal from office and disqualification from holding any office of honor, trust, or profit under this State; the party removed remains liable to prosecution and punishment according to law.

### §2.11 Executive Departments

The Legislature shall organize the administrative branch into departments. The principal officer of each department shall be appointed by the State Administrator with the advice and consent of the Senate, and shall serve at the pleasure of the State Administrator unless otherwise provided by law.

## ARTICLE III — THE LEGISLATIVE BRANCH

### §3.1 The Legislature

The legislative power of the State of Harren is vested in a Legislature consisting of two houses: a Senate and an Assembly.

### §3.2 The Assembly

(a) The Assembly consists of members elected from single-member districts of substantially equal population, apportioned following each federal census.

(b) Members of the Assembly serve two-year terms.

(c) The number of Assembly members shall be not fewer than eighty nor more than one hundred twenty, as the Legislature provides by law.

(d) To be eligible for the Assembly, a person must be a qualified elector of the district, at least twenty-one years of age, and a resident of the district for at least one year prior to election.

### §3.3 The Senate

(a) The Senate consists of members elected from single-member districts, each Senate district comprising two contiguous Assembly districts.

(b) Members of the Senate serve four-year staggered terms, with approximately half elected every two years.

(c) To be eligible for the Senate, a person must be a qualified elector of the district, at least twenty-five years of age, and a resident of the district for at least one year prior to election.

### §3.4 Apportionment and Districting

(a) Following each federal census, the Legislature shall reapportion the Assembly and Senate districts to reflect population changes.

(b) Districts shall be drawn consistent with the criteria established in §3.3 of the Federated Republic Constitution: population equality within reasonable tolerance, geographic contiguity, compliance with non-discrimination requirements, no intentional dilution of minority voting power, and reasonable compactness.

(c) Districts may not be drawn using political affiliation, voting history, incumbent residence, or racial composition as a primary criterion.

(d) The Legislature shall establish by law an independent redistricting commission.

### §3.5 Sessions

(a) The Legislature shall meet in regular session annually at such time as provided by law.

(b) The Governor may convene the Legislature in special session by proclamation, specifying the purpose of the session. No business other than that specified may be transacted in a special session.

(c) Either house may be convened by its presiding officer upon written request of a majority of its members.

### §3.6 Officers

(a) The Assembly shall choose a Speaker from among its members and such other officers as it deems necessary.

(b) The Lieutenant Governor is President of the Senate. The Senate shall choose a President Pro Tempore from among its members to preside in the absence of the Lieutenant Governor, and such other officers as it deems necessary.

### §3.7 Quorum and Rules

(a) A majority of the members elected to each house constitutes a quorum to do business.

(b) Each house shall determine its own rules of procedure, may compel the attendance of absent members, and may punish its members for disorderly conduct.

(c) Each house may, with the concurrence of two-thirds of its members elected, expel a member.

### §3.8 Legislative Process

(a) Every bill must address a single subject, which shall be expressed in its title.

(b) No bill may become law until it has been printed and available to members and the public for at least five days before final passage, except in cases of emergency declared by a two-thirds vote of each house.

(c) Bills may originate in either house and may be amended by the other.

(d) No law shall be enacted except by bill, and no bill shall become law unless it receives a majority of the votes of all members elected to each house, unless this Constitution requires a higher threshold.

(e) Every enacted law shall be published to the Harren State Record.

### §3.9 Election of the State Administrator

(a) The Legislature sitting jointly shall elect the State Administrator as provided in Article II, §2.6.

(b) The election shall be held not later than sixty days before the expiration of the incumbent's term, or within sixty days of a vacancy.

(c) The Speaker of the Assembly shall preside over the joint session for this purpose.

### §3.10 Compensation

Members of the Legislature shall receive such compensation as provided by law. No increase in compensation shall take effect until after the next general election following its enactment.

### §3.11 Privileges and Immunities

Members of the Legislature shall be privileged from arrest during sessions and in going to and returning from the same, except for treason, felony, or breach of the peace. For any speech or debate in either house, they shall not be questioned in any other place.

### §3.12 Conflicts of Interest

(a) No member of the Legislature may vote on any matter in which they hold a direct and material financial interest that has not been disclosed.

(b) The Legislature shall establish by law an independent ethics body with authority to investigate and sanction members.

### §3.13 Limitations on Legislative Power

The Legislature may not:

(a) enact any law that conflicts with or diminishes any right guaranteed by the Federated Republic Constitution;

(b) grant any title of nobility;

(c) pass any bill of attainder or ex post facto law;

(d) impair the obligation of contracts;

(e) delegate to any single person or body the power to make laws.

## ARTICLE IV — THE JUDICIAL BRANCH

### §4.1 Judicial Power

The judicial power of the State of Harren is vested in a High Court, District Courts, and such other courts as the Legislature may establish by law, subject at all times to the original and appellate jurisdiction of the Supreme Court of the Federated Republic under §4.1 and §4.5 of the Federated Republic Constitution.

### §4.2 The High Court

(a) The High Court of Harren consists of seven justices elected by the qualified electors of the State at large.

(b) Justices serve ten-year terms and may be re-elected.

(c) The justice with the longest continuous service on the Court serves as Chief Justice, unless the justices by majority vote select another from among their number.

(d) The High Court has appellate jurisdiction over all courts of the State and original jurisdiction in such cases as provided by law, including domain disputes between the Governor and State Administrator under Article II, §2.8.

### §4.3 District Courts

(a) District Courts are courts of general jurisdiction, with original jurisdiction in all civil and criminal matters except as otherwise provided by law.

(b) District Court judges are elected from districts as provided by law and serve six-year terms.

(c) The Legislature shall organize the State into judicial districts and may create, consolidate, or abolish districts by law.

### §4.4 Judicial Nominations

(a) The Governor shall nominate candidates for judicial office. No person may appear on the ballot for any judicial office without the Governor's nomination.

(b) For each judicial vacancy or expiring term, the Governor shall nominate at least two qualified candidates, who shall appear on the ballot at the next general election.

(c) If the Governor fails to nominate candidates within sixty days of a vacancy or one hundred twenty days before an election, any qualified person may petition to appear on the ballot upon securing signatures equal to five percent of the votes cast in the last gubernatorial election within the relevant jurisdiction.

### §4.5 Judicial Vacancies

(a) A vacancy in any judicial office shall be filled by appointment by the Governor, with the advice and consent of the Senate.

(b) A judge so appointed shall serve until a successor is elected at the next general election occurring more than ninety days after the vacancy and has qualified.

### §4.6 Judicial Independence

(a) No public official may direct, threaten, or seek to influence the decision of any judge in a pending matter.

(b) Judicial compensation may not be reduced during a judge's term.

(c) Judges may be removed only for cause — misconduct, incapacity, or conviction of a disqualifying offense — through impeachment under Article II, §2.10, or through a judicial discipline process established by the High Court.

### §4.7 Recognition of Judgments

Judgments of Harren's courts are entitled to recognition throughout the Republic consistent with §4.6 of the Federated Republic Constitution, and Harren recognizes the judgments of every other State and Territory on the same terms.

## ARTICLE V — LOCAL GOVERNMENT

### §5.1 Counties

(a) The State of Harren is divided into counties, which are bodies corporate and politic.

(b) The Legislature shall provide by law for the organization, powers, and duties of counties.

(c) Each county shall have a county board elected by the qualified electors of the county, which shall be the legislative body of the county.

### §5.2 Municipalities

(a) Cities, villages, and towns are municipal corporations with the powers granted by law and such additional powers as are consistent with this Constitution.

(b) The Legislature shall provide by law for the incorporation, organization, and classification of municipalities.

### §5.3 Home Rule

(a) Any county or municipality may adopt a home-rule charter by majority vote of its qualified electors, establishing its own form of local government, provided the charter maintains democratic accountability and does not conflict with this Constitution or the Federated Republic Constitution.

(b) A home-rule unit may exercise any power and perform any function pertaining to its government and affairs, except as limited by this Constitution or by law.

### §5.4 Protection of Local Government

(a) Local governments are the primary points of contact between the people and their government. The State shall not systematically defund or disempower local governments to achieve centralization of State functions.

(b) This principle, first established in Harren, is reflected in §15.8 of the Federated Republic Constitution.

### §5.5 Local Elections

All local elections shall be conducted consistent with the election standards established in Article VI of this Constitution.

### §5.6 Intergovernmental Cooperation

Counties, municipalities, and other governmental units may contract with one another and with the State to provide services and exercise powers, as provided by law.

---

## ARTICLE VI — ELECTIONS AND SUFFRAGE

### §6.1 Suffrage

(a) Every citizen of the Federated Republic who is eighteen years of age or older and a resident of Harren is a qualified elector of this State.

(b) The Legislature may by law establish reasonable residency requirements for voting in State and local elections, not to exceed thirty days.

(c) No law, rule, or practice of this State may make voting more difficult, less accessible, or less secure.

### §6.2 Elections

(a) All State elections shall be conducted using ranked-choice or majority-vote methods as the Legislature provides by law, and shall connect to and operate through the National Voting System.

(b) General elections shall be held in even-numbered years at such time as provided by law.

(c) The Legislature shall provide by law for the registration of electors and the conduct of elections.

### §6.3 State Election Commission

The Legislature shall establish an independent State Election Commission to administer State and local elections within the standards set by the federal Elections Panel.

### §6.4 Ballot Access

The Legislature shall provide by law for reasonable and non-discriminatory procedures for candidates and political parties to access the ballot.

---

## ARTICLE VII — FINANCE AND TAXATION

### §7.1 Taxing Power

(a) The Legislature has the exclusive authority to levy State taxes.

(b) No tax may discriminate against goods, services, or persons of another State or Territory.

(c) The rule of uniform taxation shall apply to all taxes, except as otherwise provided by this Constitution.

### §7.2 The State Treasury

All public money received by the State shall be deposited into the State Treasury and may be withdrawn only by appropriation made by law.

### §7.3 The Budget

(a) The State Administrator shall submit to the Legislature, at such time as provided by law, a proposed budget for the ensuing fiscal year.

(b) The budget shall contain a complete plan of proposed expenditures and anticipated revenues.

(c) No money shall be paid out of the State Treasury except pursuant to an appropriation made by law.

(d) The State Administrator shall execute the budget as enacted; the Governor has no line-item veto over appropriations.

### §7.4 State Debt

(a) The State may not contract debt except as authorized by law for purposes and in amounts approved by the Legislature.

(b) The Legislature may by law establish limits on State indebtedness and provide for the management of State debt.

### §7.5 Local Taxation

Counties and municipalities retain independent taxing authority within their own jurisdictions, as provided by law and subject to the non-discrimination requirements of this Constitution.

---

## ARTICLE VIII — EDUCATION

### §8.1 Public Education

(a) The State shall provide for the establishment and maintenance of a system of free public schools sufficient to give every child an education adequate to enable full civic participation, consistent with §1.17 of the Federated Republic Constitution.

(b) The Legislature shall provide by law for the organization and governance of public schools.

### §8.2 Superintendent of Public Instruction

There shall be a Superintendent of Public Instruction, appointed by the State Administrator with the advice and consent of the Senate, who shall supervise public instruction as provided by law. Education is an administrative function and falls within the State Administrator's domain.

### §8.3 Higher Education

The State recognizes and supports its public institutions of higher learning and may provide funding, land, or other support to such institutions by law, provided that admission and public funding comply with §1.6 of the Federated Republic Constitution.

---

## ARTICLE IX — THE HARREN STATE RECORD

### §9.1 Establishment

The State shall maintain the Harren State Record as the permanent public record of State governmental action. The Record shall be freely accessible to every Inhabitant at no cost, equivalent in permanence and accessibility to the National Record System established under Article X of the Federated Republic Constitution.

### §9.2 Publication Requirement

(a) Every law, executive act, judicial decision, and administrative determination of the State and its subdivisions shall be published to the Record as a permanent entry.

(b) Records may not be deleted or altered after publication; corrections are entered as new records alongside the original.

(c) No governmental act may be concealed from the Record except upon grounds narrowly defined by law and subject to judicial review, consistent with §1.13 of this Constitution.

### §9.3 Federal Interface

The Record shall maintain export protocols fully compatible with the National Record System, ensuring that all federally required disclosures are available to federal Monitors and auditors without delay or reformatting burden.

---

## ARTICLE X — AMENDMENT

### §10.1 Legislative Proposal

An amendment to this Constitution may be proposed by a two-thirds vote of the members elected to each house of the Legislature.

### §10.2 Ratification

A proposed amendment takes effect upon ratification by a majority of the qualified electors voting on the question at the next general election.

### §10.3 Constitutional Convention

(a) The Legislature may, by a two-thirds vote of the members elected to each house, submit to the electors the question of whether a constitutional convention shall be called.

(b) If a majority of electors voting on the question approve, the Legislature shall provide by law for the election of delegates and the convening of the convention.

(c) Any revision proposed by a convention shall take effect only upon ratification by a majority of the qualified electors voting on the question.

### §10.4 Citizen Initiative

(a) The people may propose amendments to this Constitution by petition. A petition must be signed by qualified electors equal in number to ten percent of the votes cast for Governor in the last gubernatorial election.

(b) A proposed amendment qualifying by petition shall be submitted to the electors at the next general election and takes effect upon ratification by sixty percent of those voting on the question.

### §10.5 Federal Consistency

No amendment to this Constitution may take effect if it conflicts with, or purports to diminish, any right, structure, or requirement of the Federated Republic Constitution. Any such amendment is void to the extent of the conflict.

---

## ARTICLE XI — GENERAL PROVISIONS

### §11.1 Oath of Office

Every person elected or appointed to any office in this State shall, before entering upon the duties of the office, take and subscribe an oath or affirmation to support the Constitution of the Federated Republic and the Constitution of the State of Harren, and to faithfully discharge the duties of the office.

### §11.2 Official Records

All official records of the State and its subdivisions are public records, except as otherwise provided by law for the protection of individual privacy or public safety. This principle is fundamental to Harren's constitutional identity and is reflected in Article I, §1.13 and Article IX.

### §11.3 Continuity of Government

The Legislature shall provide by law for the continuity of State and local government in periods of emergency.

### §11.4 Non-Confusion of Offices

No State office shall bear a title that creates confusion with a federal constitutional office. The State Administrator is a distinct office from the Civic Consul; the Governor is a distinct office from the Legat Consul. Official records shall clearly distinguish between State and federal offices.

### §11.5 Seat of Government

The seat of government of the State of Harren shall be established by law. The Legislature may by law provide for the location of the capital and may change it by a two-thirds vote of each house.

### §11.6 Founding State

Harren acknowledges its role as a founding state of the Federated Republic and the constitutional ancestor of the Republic's institutions. This heritage carries an obligation: to maintain the standards that made Harren worthy of imitation, and to demonstrate that the principles which inspired a nation remain sound in practice.

---

## SCHEDULE

### Section 1. Effective Date

This Constitution takes effect upon ratification by the people of Harren and certification by the Elections Panel of the Federated Republic.

### Section 2. Existing Laws

All laws in force at the time this Constitution takes effect, and not inconsistent with it, remain in force until amended or repealed.

### Section 3. Existing Officers

All officers holding office at the time this Constitution takes effect shall continue in office until their successors are elected or appointed and qualified.

### Section 4. First State Administrator

The first State Administrator under this Constitution shall be elected by the Legislature within one hundred twenty days of ratification and shall serve a full eight-year term.

### Section 5. Transition

The Legislature shall provide by law for an orderly transition to the government established by this Constitution, including the transfer of functions between the unified executive of any prior constitution and the dual executive established herein.

*Adopted by the people of Harren, Founding State of the Federated Republic.*
