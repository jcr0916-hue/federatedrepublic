# CONSTITUTION OF THE STATE OF NORVANE

## Preamble

We, the people of Norvane, a State of the Federated Republic of Torenthia, situated between the Torenthian Alps and the northern reach of Lake Varda — a border-facing land whose shores have received the tired, the displaced, and the hopeful across the long waters — do ordain and establish this Constitution in order that our State may govern with the speed that arrival demands, the justice that human dignity requires, and the accountability that republican government must always preserve.

Norvane is constituted on the premise that fragmented authority is dangerous where a jurisdiction must respond quickly to sudden cross-border arrivals, transport disruptions, public health strain, and emergency logistics. We therefore vest general executive authority in a Governor directly accountable to the people, constrained by the rights declared herein, the oversight of an elected Assembly, the review of an independent judiciary, and the supreme authority of the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

We are a permanent home and a place of passage. This Constitution speaks for those who are here now, those who will arrive, and those who have not yet crossed the lake.

## Article I — Declaration of Rights

### Section 1.1 — Incorporation of the Federal Floor

The rights, liberties, and protections guaranteed by Article I of the Constitution of the Federated Republic are incorporated as directly enforceable State law. No inhabitant of Norvane shall be required to exhaust federal remedies before asserting federal constitutional rights in the courts of this State. Nothing in this Constitution shall be construed to diminish or deny any right guaranteed by the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

### Section 1.2 — State-Specific Rights

In addition to the federal floor, every person within the jurisdiction of Norvane shall enjoy the following rights:

(a) **Access to Public Records.** Every person has a right of access to the Norvane State Record and to records of State and local government, subject only to narrowly tailored limitations established by law for personal privacy, protected medical or personnel information, privileged communications, active law-enforcement integrity, legitimate public-safety or security interests, legally protected commercial information, or matters committed exclusively to the federal government. Any denial of access is subject to judicial review.

(b) **Humane Reception Conditions.** Every person received into a facility operated, funded, contracted, licensed for that purpose, or substantially controlled by the State for arrival processing, temporary shelter, or humanitarian intake is entitled to conditions consistent with human dignity, including adequate shelter, nutrition, medical care, sanitation, and protection from harm. The State may not evade this duty by delegating operation of such a facility to a private or local entity.

(c) **Freedom from Unlawful Confinement in Reception.** Reception, sheltering, humanitarian intake, or public-health processing does not by itself authorize involuntary confinement. Any material restriction on a person's freedom of movement must rest on lawful State authority consistent with the Constitution of the Federated Republic and is subject to prompt judicial review.

(d) **Non-Discrimination in Public Services.** No person shall be denied access to State or local public services, benefits, or accommodations on the basis of national origin, immigration status, asylum status, language, ethnicity, religion, or any classification protected under the Constitution of the Federated Republic. This subsection does not require provision of a benefit to which a person is not otherwise entitled under law.

(e) **Meaningful Language Access.** Where reasonably necessary for meaningful access to emergency instructions, healthcare, reception processing, courts, or another essential State service, the State shall provide interpretation, translation, or another effective means of communication as provided by law.

(f) **Direct Challenge to Emergency and Confinement Measures.** Any person directly affected by an emergency declaration, a measure taken under its authority, or a State-imposed restriction on liberty arising from reception or emergency administration may challenge the declaration or measure directly in the courts of this State without exhausting administrative remedies. Courts shall hear such challenges without undue delay.

(g) **Freedom from Retaliation.** No State or local officer may retaliate against a person for seeking asylum, requesting lawful emergency or humanitarian assistance, requesting public records, reporting conditions in a reception facility, petitioning a court, or otherwise exercising a right protected by this Constitution or the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

### Section 1.3 — Construction

Rights declared in this Article shall be construed generously. Their enumeration shall not be construed to deny or disparage other rights retained by the people. Where this Constitution or a statute is capable of more than one lawful construction, the construction that better secures individual rights shall be preferred, consistent with the Constitution of the Federated Republic and the lawful allocation of State and federal authority.

## Article II — The Governor

### Section 2.1 — Executive Authority

The supreme executive power of the State of Norvane is vested in a Governor, who is the chief executive officer of the State and commander-in-chief of its organized State forces while they remain under State authority. The Governor shall take care that the laws of the State are faithfully executed and shall direct the civil administration of the State.

The Governor serves as the State constitutional authority whose consent is required for assistance within Norvane by State forces requested by the Civic Consul under Article XIV §14.5 of the Constitution of the Federated Republic. Such consent may be given or withdrawn by the Governor and shall be published promptly to the Norvane State Record. Federal call-up, federal command during lawful call-up, and all limitations on State forces remain governed by the Constitution and laws of the Federated Republic.

### Section 2.2 — Election and Qualifications

(a) The Governor shall be elected by the qualified electors of the State at a general election held concurrently with elections to the federal Assembly. The Governor shall be chosen by ranked-choice voting or another majority-producing method established by law consistent with federal standards.

(b) No person is eligible to the office of Governor who has not attained thirty years of age, been a citizen of the Federated Republic for at least ten years, and been a resident of Norvane for at least two years immediately preceding the election.

(c) The Governor holds office for a term of four years beginning on the first day of January following the election.

(d) No person may serve as Governor for more than two consecutive terms. A person who has served two consecutive terms is eligible again after an intervening term of another Governor.

### Section 2.3 — Powers and Duties

The Governor has the following powers and duties:

(a) **Appointment.** The Governor appoints the heads of executive departments, members of boards and commissions, and all other State officers whose appointment is not otherwise provided for by this Constitution or by law. An appointment is effective upon publication to the Norvane State Record unless confirmation is constitutionally or statutorily required. The Assembly may require confirmation for specifically designated offices but may not use confirmation requirements to transfer general control of ordinary executive administration from the Governor. Constitutionally independent offices remain subject to the tenure and removal protections applicable to them.

(b) **Removal.** The Governor may remove an officer appointed by the Governor or a predecessor at pleasure unless this Constitution or a law enacted before the appointment provides otherwise. This authority does not displace lawful civil-service protections or constitutional guarantees of institutional independence.

(c) **Executive Orders.** The Governor may issue executive orders consistent with this Constitution and State law. Orders directing executive administration may rest on the Governor's constitutional executive authority. An order imposing duties, restrictions, or legal consequences outside the executive branch must rest on authority granted by this Constitution or by statute. Every executive order shall be published upon issuance. An executive order remains effective until revoked, superseded, or its legal authority expires; an emergency order is governed by Article VI.

(d) **Pardons and Reprieves.** The Governor may grant reprieves, commutations, and pardons for offenses against State law, except in cases of impeachment or where clemency would violate the Constitution of the Federated Republic. The Governor shall report and publish every grant of clemency. The Governor shall not act in a clemency matter in which the Governor has a direct personal or financial interest; law shall provide a neutral temporary mechanism for such a case.

(e) **Information and Recommendations.** The Governor shall deliver to the Assembly at the opening of each regular session an address on the state of the State and may recommend measures the Governor judges necessary and expedient.

(f) **Extraordinary Sessions.** The Governor may convene the Assembly in extraordinary session upon proclamation stating the purpose for which it is convened. A session called by the Governor may transact only business reasonably related to the stated purpose. This limitation does not apply to an extraordinary session lawfully convened by the Assembly itself.

(g) **Coordination with Federal Authorities.** The Governor shall coordinate with lawful federal domestic authorities in matters of asylum reception, public health, civil transport, emergency sheltering, and other matters within the State's constitutional sphere. Coordination does not authorize the Governor to exercise federal border, asylum-determination, removal, deportation, or other powers reserved to federal authority.

(h) **Temporary Administrative Appointments.** Where an executive office is vacant or an appointment awaits required confirmation, the Governor may designate a qualified acting officer for a limited period established by law. Acting service may preserve administration but may not be used to evade a constitutionally or statutorily required confirmation indefinitely and creates no claim to permanent appointment.

### Section 2.4 — The Budget Proposal

The Governor shall submit a proposed budget for the ensuing fiscal year by the date established by law. The proposal shall be prepared in good faith and shall account for constitutional obligations, legally binding debt, required federal obligations, essential public services, and continuing statutory commitments unless the Governor simultaneously proposes their lawful repeal or modification. The proposal constitutes the framework within which the Assembly exercises its appropriations power under Article V.

### Section 2.5 — Veto

(a) Every bill passed by the Assembly shall be presented to the Governor. The Governor may approve a bill by signing it.

(b) The Governor may veto a bill by returning it to the Assembly within ten days of presentation with written objections. The Assembly may override the veto by two-thirds of its authorized membership.

(c) The Governor may veto one or more items of an appropriation bill while approving the remainder. Each vetoed item may be overridden separately under subsection (b).

(d) If the Governor neither signs nor vetoes a bill within ten days of presentation, the bill becomes law as if signed. If adjournment prevents physical return, timely publication of the bill and the Governor's written objections to the Norvane State Record constitutes return.

### Section 2.6 — Delegation by the Assembly

The Assembly may by statute delegate regulatory, administrative, or emergency-preparedness authority to the Governor within defined subject matter, purpose, duration, and intelligible standards. The Assembly may not transfer its essential legislative power. Delegated authority may be withdrawn by resolution of the Assembly at any time; withdrawal is effective upon publication and does not require the Governor's assent.

### Section 2.7 — Publication Obligations

The Governor shall cause to be published to the Norvane State Record:

(a) every executive order upon issuance;
(b) every appointment and removal of an officer;
(c) every veto and accompanying statement of objections;
(d) every emergency declaration and report on continued necessity;
(e) every grant of clemency;
(f) every consent or withdrawal of consent under Section 2.1 concerning State-force assistance;
(g) every refusal or asserted inability to discharge a duty imposed by this Constitution or by law, together with the legal or factual basis asserted for the refusal or inability; and
(h) such other acts as this Constitution or law requires.

### Section 2.8 — Oath of Office

The Governor shall promptly take and subscribe the following oath or affirmation:

> "I do solemnly swear (or affirm) that I will faithfully execute the office of Governor of Norvane, that I will to the best of my ability preserve, protect, and defend the Constitution of the Federated Republic and this Constitution, and that I will discharge my duties in the interests of the present and future inhabitants of this State."

The oath is a duty of office and not a condition precedent to the constitutional transfer of authority. Refusal or unjustified failure to take the oath may constitute grounds for lawful discipline, removal, or disqualification.

## Article III — The Deputy Governor and Executive Succession

### Section 3.1 — Election

A Deputy Governor shall be elected on a joint ticket with the Governor and holds office for the same term. The qualifications for Deputy Governor are the same as those for Governor. Except while acting as Governor or exercising authority delegated under Section 3.4, the Deputy Governor holds no independent executive authority.

### Section 3.2 — Succession and Incapacity

(a) Upon the death, resignation, or removal of the Governor, the Deputy Governor becomes Governor for the remainder of the term.

(b) During a temporary incapacity of the Governor, the Deputy Governor serves as Acting Governor until the Governor lawfully resumes the powers and duties of office.

(c) The Assembly shall establish by law objective procedures for determining incapacity, including an initiating authority, appropriate evidence, notice where practicable, a procedure for contest, and prompt judicial review of a disputed determination.

(d) A Governor who claims recovery from incapacity shall publish a declaration of fitness to the Norvane State Record. If the return is disputed under law, the dispute shall be resolved through an expedited neutral procedure, and the Acting Governor continues only until that determination.

### Section 3.3 — Vacancy in Both Offices

(a) If the offices of Governor and Deputy Governor are simultaneously vacant or their holders are simultaneously unable to act, the officer designated by a statutory succession law shall serve as Acting Governor.

(b) If no eligible statutory successor is available, the presiding officer of the Assembly shall serve as Acting Governor. If that officer is unavailable, eligible executive department heads shall serve in order of longest continuous service in State executive administration, with age breaking ties.

(c) A member of the Assembly serving as Acting Governor may retain the legislative seat but may not exercise legislative voting or decisional authority while exercising executive authority. The Assembly shall choose an acting presiding officer as necessary.

(d) Acting service under this section creates no claim to the office of Governor. If a substantial portion of the gubernatorial term remains, a special election shall be held within the period established by law; otherwise the Acting Governor serves until a successor elected at the next general election assumes office.

### Section 3.4 — Delegated Authority

The Deputy Governor shall exercise powers and duties the Governor lawfully delegates in writing. Every delegation shall identify its scope, be published to the Norvane State Record, and may be revoked at any time. A delegation may not transfer a constitutional responsibility that this Constitution expressly requires the Governor personally to perform. A delegation ends upon revocation, stated expiration, or termination of the delegating Governor's tenure.

## Article IV — The Norvane Assembly

### Section 4.1 — Legislative Authority

The legislative power of the State of Norvane is vested in the Norvane Assembly. The Assembly holds authority over legislation, appropriations, taxation, confirmation of officers where required by this Constitution or law, oversight of executive administration, investigations, and impeachment. No general confirmation power exists beyond that expressly provided by this Constitution or by law consistent with Article II.

### Section 4.2 — Elections, Membership, and Districts

(a) Members of the Assembly shall be elected by qualified electors at general elections held concurrently with elections to the federal Assembly.

(b) The electoral system shall provide meaningful geographic representation and produce representation substantially proportional to the statewide vote, as provided by law and consistent with the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

(c) The number of members, electoral method, and district structure shall be established by law. Geographic districts shall be drawn by an independent redistricting body or equivalent independent process and shall satisfy federal requirements for population equality, contiguity, non-discrimination, protection against intentional dilution of voting power, and reasonable compactness. Maps may not be designed for partisan manipulation and remain subject to federal constitutional review and any federal review or interim-map process.

(d) Proposed and final maps shall be published with the non-protected data necessary for meaningful public review and are subject to judicial review.

(e) No person is eligible to the Assembly who has not attained twenty-one years of age, been a citizen of the Federated Republic, and been a resident of Norvane for at least one year immediately preceding the election.

(f) Members hold office for four-year terms beginning on the first day of January following the election.

(g) No person may serve as a member of the Assembly for more than three consecutive terms. A person who has served three consecutive terms is eligible again after an intervening term of another member.

### Section 4.3 — Sessions and Authorized Membership

(a) The Assembly shall convene in regular session on the second Monday of January each year. A regular session may not exceed ninety days unless extended by two-thirds of the authorized membership.

(b) Pending business may be carried over to the next regular session by resolution.

(c) The Assembly shall convene in extraordinary session upon proclamation of the Governor or upon written request of a majority of the authorized membership addressed to the presiding officer.

(d) The presiding officer shall be chosen from among the members. Where the presiding officer temporarily exercises executive authority under Article III, the Assembly shall select an acting presiding officer.

(e) A majority of the authorized membership constitutes a quorum.

(f) For this Constitution, "authorized membership" means the total number of seats lawfully established for the Assembly, whether filled or vacant, except where the Constitution of the Federated Republic requires another denominator.

### Section 4.4 — Legislative Process

(a) Except where this Constitution requires a greater threshold, a bill passes by majority of members present and voting while a lawful quorum is present.

(b) Every bill shall address one reasonably related subject expressed in its title and shall be publicly available for at least five days before final passage.

(c) The Assembly may waive the five-day period only by two-thirds of its authorized membership upon a recorded finding of urgent public necessity. The finding and vote shall be published to the Norvane State Record.

(d) Assembly rules shall provide for public hearings where reasonably practicable and for publication of committee proceedings, votes, and final reports subject to lawful privacy and security protections.

(e) A bill becomes law upon satisfaction of this Article and Article II, and upon publication to the Norvane State Record.

### Section 4.5 — Oversight and Investigation

(a) The Assembly may compel testimony and production of records from State executive departments, agencies, officers, and employees. Lawful legislative subpoenas are enforceable through the courts.

(b) Law may provide procedures for confidential legislative access to information protected by personal privacy, medical or personnel confidentiality, attorney-client or comparable privilege, active law-enforcement integrity, legitimate public-safety or security interests, or matters committed exclusively to federal authority. Temporary withholding shall be narrowly tailored, limited in duration, and subject to judicial review.

(c) The Assembly may request information from the Governor, department heads, and other State officers, and lawful requests shall be answered within a reasonable time.

(d) Oversight permits investigation, legislation, appropriation, impeachment, and lawful sanction. It does not authorize the Assembly, a committee, or an individual member to direct the disposition of a particular executive or administrative case except through generally applicable law.

### Section 4.6 — Impeachment

(a) The Assembly has the sole power of impeachment. Impeachment requires a majority of the authorized membership.

(b) The Governor, Deputy Governor, judges of the State courts, and other officers designated by law are subject to impeachment for corruption, serious constitutional violation, conviction of a disqualifying offense, gross misconduct, or grave neglect of duty.

(c) Except where Article VII provides a special mechanism for an impeached Supreme Court justice or disabling judicial conflict, the Norvane Supreme Court shall try impeachments. Conviction requires two-thirds of the justices participating while a lawful quorum is present.

(d) Judgment upon conviction extends only to removal and disqualification from State office. The convicted person remains subject to ordinary legal liability.

### Section 4.7 — Compensation, Discipline, and Ethics

(a) Members shall receive compensation established by law. No increase takes effect during the Assembly term in which it is enacted.

(b) The Assembly may expel a member for serious misconduct by two-thirds of its authorized membership, subject to judicial review for constitutional or procedural error.

(c) Members shall disclose direct and material financial interests as provided by law and shall recuse where a direct personal financial interest substantially impairs impartial judgment. An interest shared with a broad class does not by itself require recusal.

(d) Law shall establish an independent ethics body with authority to investigate and impose lawful sanctions other than expulsion, subject to due process.

### Section 4.8 — Delegation of Legislative Authority

The Assembly may authorize executive, administrative, local, regional, or independent bodies to adopt rules within authority and intelligible standards established by law. The Assembly may not transfer its essential legislative power. Delegated rules remain subject to judicial review and publication requirements.

## Article V — Budget, Revenue, and the Public Treasury

### Section 5.1 — The Public Treasury

All public money received by the State, from whatever source, shall be deposited into the Norvane Public Treasury. No money may be withdrawn except pursuant to lawful appropriation or the temporary continuity authority expressly established by this Article.

### Section 5.2 — The Budget Process and Continuity

(a) The Governor shall submit the proposed budget required by Section 2.4.

(b) The Assembly may reduce or reallocate proposed expenditure but may not increase the total proposed expenditure without the Governor's concurrence.

(c) If a new general appropriation has not taken effect when a fiscal year begins, an interim appropriation takes effect by constitutional operation. It shall maintain existing lawful programs, constitutional and federal obligations, debt service, public health and safety, courts, transportation, reception and emergency preparedness, and other essential administration at no more than the corresponding prior-year lawful rate adjusted for inflation, population, caseload, legal obligations, and immediate operational necessity.

(d) An interim appropriation may not create a substantial new program, abolish a substantial existing program, materially restructure State government, or serve as an alternate general budget. A lawfully enacted general appropriation supersedes it immediately.

(e) During an interim appropriation, the Governor may make temporary transfers reasonably necessary to protect life, health, public safety, essential infrastructure, reception capacity, or other constitutional obligations within limits established by law. Material transfers and their reasons shall be published promptly and reported to the State Auditor. The Assembly may regulate or reverse such transfers by law.

(f) The Governor shall certify appropriations enacted by the Assembly. If the Governor determines that an appropriation would violate Section 5.3, the Governor shall return it with published revenue estimates, material assumptions, and a detailed statement of the asserted discrepancy. The Assembly may reenact with adjustment or override the certification by two-thirds of its authorized membership.

(g) Either the Governor or the Assembly may request a prompt independent fiscal assessment from the State Auditor concerning a disputed certification. The assessment is advisory and shall be published.

### Section 5.3 — Fiscal Responsibility

(a) The State shall maintain fiscal balance over the economic cycle. The Governor shall certify in the proposed budget whether projected recurring expenditure is supported by projected recurring revenue for the fiscal year and the two succeeding fiscal years.

(b) Temporary deficit expenditure is permitted:

(i) during a declared emergency under Article VI, upon a published finding that the deficit arises from emergency expenditure or an emergency-related revenue shock, together with a recovery plan;

(ii) upon a published finding by the Governor, concurred in by resolution of the Assembly, that an extraordinary arrival-related, infrastructure, public-health, or economic shock requires temporary deficit expenditure, together with a recovery plan; or

(iii) as otherwise permitted by law for a severe recession or comparable temporary fiscal disruption consistent with this Constitution.

(c) A recovery plan shall state a fixed horizon ordinarily not exceeding three years. The Assembly may extend the horizon by affirmative resolution upon published findings that additional time is reasonably necessary.

(d) Extraordinary deficit expenditure shall be reported to the State Auditor and audited annually until the recovery plan is completed.

### Section 5.4 — Taxation and Fees

(a) The Assembly may levy taxes by general law subject to the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

(b) Every tax law shall state the object of the tax. A general-revenue tax may support lawful State functions generally; where a law dedicates revenue to a particular purpose, the proceeds shall be used consistently with that dedication.

(c) No tax may be imposed except by law.

(d) A regulatory, service, or administrative fee shall bear a reasonable relationship to the service, regulatory program, or administrative cost it supports unless the Assembly clearly enacts the charge as a tax.

(e) Local governments may levy taxes only as authorized by State law.

### Section 5.5 — State Auditor

(a) There shall be an independent State Auditor. The Governor shall appoint the Auditor from a list of qualified nominees submitted by an independent selection panel established by law. No single political actor or institution may control a majority of the panel.

(b) The Auditor serves a seven-year term and may be removed only for cause by two-thirds of the authorized membership of the Assembly after written charges, notice, an opportunity to respond, and publication of final findings.

(c) The Auditor shall annually review and report on State receipts, disbursements, emergency expenditures, procurement, public debt, equalization transfers, and other material fiscal obligations.

(d) The Auditor has access to State records, accounts, and personnel reasonably necessary to perform constitutional duties. Protected information supplied to the Auditor does not become public merely by reason of the audit. Public reports shall protect lawful privacy, privilege, security, and personally identifying information while disclosing governmental findings and fiscal consequences.

(e) Final audit reports shall be published to the Norvane State Record.

### Section 5.6 — Local Fiscal Equalization

(a) The State shall maintain an objective mechanism ensuring that municipalities and districts facing concentrated arrival-related, emergency, infrastructure, or comparable service burdens are not fiscally disabled merely by the geographic concentration of need.

(b) The Assembly shall establish the formula by law using public criteria. It may consider arrival volume, temporary population, shelter and healthcare usage, school enrollment, transport burden, infrastructure cost, local tax capacity, and similar neutral factors.

(c) The State Auditor shall review the mechanism no less frequently than every five years and may conduct an earlier review where an extraordinary change materially impairs its operation. Findings and recommendations shall be published.

### Section 5.7 — Borrowing and Debt

(a) The State may borrow for lawful public purposes pursuant to statute, including infrastructure, capital investment, emergency response, and temporary fiscal stabilization.

(b) Material borrowing authorization shall disclose its principal amount or authorized ceiling, public purpose, expected repayment source, and material terms.

(c) Borrowing may not be used to conceal recurring operating deficits without public disclosure and a fiscal plan.

## Article VI — Emergency Provisions

### Section 6.1 — Gubernatorial Declaration

(a) The Governor may declare a State emergency upon finding that a natural disaster, cross-border humanitarian crisis, public-health emergency, infrastructure failure, severe transport disruption, or comparable crisis requires immediate statewide or interlocal action.

(b) The declaration shall state the material facts supporting the finding, geographic scope, expected duration, and the specific emergency authorities activated. Activated authority must derive from this Constitution or statute. No declaration creates open-ended or undefined authority.

(c) The declaration and its stated authorities shall be published immediately to the Norvane State Record.

### Section 6.2 — Duration and Positive Extension

(a) A declaration lapses automatically fourteen days after issuance unless the Assembly, by a fresh majority vote of its authorized membership taken before expiration, affirmatively extends some or all of the declared authorities.

(b) Each extension may last no more than thirty days. Assembly inaction or silence never extends an emergency.

(c) Each request for extension shall identify the authorities still claimed necessary, those no longer needed, any changed geographic scope, and the factual basis for continued necessity. The Assembly may extend some authorities and allow others to lapse.

(d) Every extension and supporting report shall be published promptly.

### Section 6.3 — Legislative Termination

The Assembly may terminate a declared emergency, or any particular authority activated under it, at any time by majority of its authorized membership. Termination is effective upon publication and requires neither the Governor's assent nor a supermajority.

### Section 6.4 — Rights and Federal Supremacy

(a) Emergency authority remains subject to the Constitution of the Federated Republic and to the rights guaranteed by Article I of this Constitution.

(b) A federal constitutional right may be limited or derogated only to the extent the federal Constitution permits. Federally non-derogable rights remain non-derogable.

(c) State-specific rights remain enforceable during an emergency except to the extent a limitation is expressly authorized by this Constitution, established by law, reasonably necessary to address the emergency, and consistent with the federal Constitution.

### Section 6.5 — Judicial Review and Remedies

(a) Any person directly affected by an emergency declaration or measure may challenge it at any time without exhaustion of administrative remedies.

(b) Courts shall provide expedited consideration where ongoing emergency action threatens substantial liberty, shelter, health, property, or constitutional rights.

(c) A court may declare an emergency declaration or measure invalid, enjoin or stay enforcement, issue mandamus or other appropriate relief, and sever an unlawful measure without invalidating lawful portions of the emergency response.

### Section 6.6 — No Permanent Measures

No emergency measure adopted solely under emergency authority may outlast the declaration or authority supporting it. A measure intended to continue afterward must rest on ordinary constitutional or statutory authority.

### Section 6.7 — Serial Declarations

(a) After the Assembly terminates an emergency authority, the Governor may not reactivate substantially the same authority on substantially the same factual basis without affirmative Assembly approval.

(b) After an emergency expires, a substantially identical declaration based on the same circumstances requires materially changed facts or affirmative Assembly authorization.

(c) This section does not prevent a new declaration responding to a genuinely new or materially changed emergency.

### Section 6.8 — Emergency Procurement and Property

(a) Law may provide expedited procurement during an emergency. Material emergency contracts shall disclose the vendor, amount, purpose, and justification for use of emergency procedure and are subject to subsequent audit.

(b) Emergency use, requisition, or acquisition of private property remains subject to law and applicable constitutional requirements for due process and compensation.

## Article VII — The Judiciary

### Section 7.1 — Judicial Power

The judicial power of the State of Norvane is vested in a Norvane Supreme Court, a Norvane Court of Appeals, and such trial courts as the Assembly establishes by law. The courts have jurisdiction over matters arising under this Constitution and State law, subject to the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

### Section 7.2 — Judicial Nominations and Appointments

(a) There shall be an independent Judicial Nominating Commission. No more than one-third of its members may be appointed by the Governor, and no single appointing authority may control a majority.

(b) Commissioners shall serve fixed or staggered terms as provided by law. No current Governor, member of the Assembly, judge seeking appointment, or current senior officer of a political party may serve.

(c) The Commission shall apply public professional qualification standards and submit qualified nominees for judicial vacancies. Candidate-evaluation materials may be protected to the extent reasonably necessary for privacy and candid professional assessment, but the Commission's governing rules and final nominations shall be public.

(d) The Governor shall nominate judges from the Commission list within a period established by law. Law shall provide an objective fallback if the Governor fails to act within that period so that a nomination may proceed without indefinite executive blockade.

(e) Supreme Court nominees require confirmation by majority of the authorized membership of the Assembly. Other judicial nominees require majority of members present and voting while a lawful quorum exists, unless law establishes a higher uniform threshold.

### Section 7.3 — Terms and Security of Tenure

(a) Justices of the Norvane Supreme Court serve twelve-year nonrenewable terms.

(b) Judges of the Court of Appeals and trial courts serve terms established by law and may be eligible for reappointment through the same independent nomination and confirmation process.

(c) Judicial compensation may not be diminished during a judge's term.

(d) No judge may be removed, reassigned, deprived of jurisdiction, or subjected to court restructuring for the purpose of retaliation, altering the result of a pending matter, or punishing a lawful judicial decision.

### Section 7.4 — Judicial Qualifications and Discipline

(a) Judges shall satisfy objective qualifications established by law concerning legal authorization, relevant experience, competence, and integrity.

(b) Law shall establish an independent Judicial Conduct Commission to investigate judges of the Court of Appeals and trial courts and to impose proportionate discipline, including removal for serious cause, subject to due process and judicial review.

(c) A Supreme Court justice may be removed for cause through impeachment by the Assembly and trial before an independent special tribunal established by law. The tribunal shall provide neutral selection, notice, counsel, an opportunity to present evidence, written findings, and public proceedings except to the narrow extent necessary to protect a compelling lawful interest.

### Section 7.5 — Judicial Review and Remedies

(a) Courts of this State may declare statutes, executive orders, emergency declarations, regulations, or official acts unconstitutional or otherwise void as applied in matters properly before them and may issue injunctions, stays, mandamus, habeas-type relief where applicable, and other appropriate remedies.

(b) Lower courts may grant temporary or case-specific relief against enforcement of a statute. A final statewide declaration that a State statute is facially unconstitutional requires review by the Norvane Supreme Court and concurrence of a majority of eligible justices participating while a lawful quorum exists.

(c) No doctrine of State executive immunity bars prospective relief necessary to prevent an ongoing violation of this Constitution or the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

### Section 7.6 — Standing

Any inhabitant of Norvane alleging a direct and present injury arising from violation of this Constitution has standing to bring a claim. The Assembly may broaden standing by law but may not narrow it below this minimum. Special standing and expedition rules for emergency matters remain governed by Articles I and VI.

### Section 7.7 — Recusal and Judicial Continuity

(a) A judge shall recuse where required by law or constitutional due process.

(b) Where vacancy, incapacity, or recusal prevents the Supreme Court from forming a lawful quorum, eligible judges of the Court of Appeals, or if necessary the highest available trial court, shall serve temporarily in order of longest continuous judicial service, with age breaking ties, only in the number necessary to restore quorum.

(c) Temporary service creates no claim to permanent appointment. Temporary judges remain subject to ordinary independence and recusal requirements.

(d) The Assembly may by law establish emergency-duty or expedited judicial procedures consistent with judicial independence.

## Article VIII — Local Government

### Section 8.1 — Recognition and State Authority

Municipalities, counties, and other local governmental bodies are recognized as institutions of this State. They exercise powers conferred by this Constitution or by law and remain subject to the superior constitutional authority of the State except where this Constitution expressly provides otherwise.

### Section 8.2 — Democratic and Fiscal Guarantees

(a) Every local government shall maintain an elected governing council or equivalent elected governing authority chosen at periodic elections by qualified electors resident within its boundaries.

(b) A local government may not be abolished without consent of its electors expressed at referendum. Boundary adjustment, consolidation, or creation of regional service bodies may be provided by law following public notice, meaningful participation, and judicially reviewable procedure.

(c) The State shall ensure reasonable fiscal capacity for local governments to discharge assigned functions. No material unfunded mandate shall be imposed unless law identifies a substantial statewide necessity and provides for the materially increased costs of compliance, except for strictly temporary emergency obligations as provided by law.

(d) Local governments shall maintain public records, lawful budgets, and judicially reviewable governmental action.

### Section 8.3 — Regional Coordination and Temporary State Administration

(a) The State may establish minimum service standards for local functions and create regional coordination authorities for infrastructure, transport, healthcare logistics, emergency response, sanitation, reception support, or other matters crossing local boundaries.

(b) A regional authority exercises only powers expressly granted by law and remains subject to public-record, audit, and judicial-review requirements.

(c) Where a local government is demonstrably and persistently unable to discharge a particular assigned function, the State may temporarily administer that function following written findings and, where practicable, an opportunity for the locality to respond.

(d) Temporary administration shall include a restoration plan and ends when capacity is restored. It may not exceed two years unless the Assembly affirmatively approves one extension upon published findings that additional time is reasonably necessary.

### Section 8.4 — Emergency Reception and Logistics Zones

(a) During an emergency under Article VI, law may authorize the Governor to establish temporary reception or logistics zones where necessary for coordinated response.

(b) Each zone shall have published boundaries, specified functions, an identified administering authority, and a stated relationship to affected local governments.

(c) A zone may displace local authority only within functions expressly assigned for the emergency and may not acquire general local legislative, taxing, or police power merely by its creation.

(d) Every zone and its extraordinary authority lapse with the emergency unless ordinary law separately authorizes a continuing institution or function.

### Section 8.5 — Local Emergency Capacity During Isolation

(a) General State law shall provide local governments with temporary emergency authority to protect life, health, essential infrastructure, or public safety where immediate action is necessary and timely State direction is unavailable or impracticable because of isolation, communications failure, or comparable conditions.

(b) Such authority is predelegated State authority, not independent sovereignty. It shall be limited to functions and standards established by law.

(c) A locality exercising this authority shall notify the Governor as soon as practicable, publish material actions, and return to ordinary State coordination when communication or timely State direction becomes available.

(d) This section does not authorize local asylum adjudication, immigration-status determination, border enforcement, removal, deportation, or other powers reserved to federal authority.

### Section 8.6 — Local Functions

Subject to State law, local governments may exercise authority over local roads, sanitation, housing administration, local planning, emergency first response, civil protection, municipal policing where authorized, and other assigned local services. Nothing in this section requires every locality to maintain its own police force.

### Section 8.7 — Lake Varda Shorelands

(a) Shorelands of Lake Varda within Norvane are held in public trust. The State shall ensure reasonable public access consistent with environmental protection, public safety, critical infrastructure, and lawful private-property interests.

(b) The State shall coordinate with Rhovane and Kelvant in stewardship of Lake Varda consistent with the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

(c) The Assembly may establish a Lake Varda Shorelands Commission and delegate bounded regulatory authority concerning water quality, shore protection, public access, environmental stewardship, or related lake functions. The Commission possesses no general legislative or taxing power except as expressly authorized consistent with this Constitution.

### Section 8.8 — Local Transparency and State Facilities

(a) Local governments shall publish material emergency transfers, emergency contracts, use of State equalization funds, and major temporary State assistance to the Norvane State Record or an interoperable public record.

(b) A local government has no constitutional veto over a lawful State reception, logistics, health, or emergency facility. Law shall provide affected localities notice, consultation where practicable, and access to ordinary judicial remedies.

## Article IX — Federal Integration and the Norvane State Record

### Section 9.1 — Supremacy

This Constitution is subordinate to the Constitution of the Federated Republic in all respects. Federal law prevails over conflicting State law. No State office may exercise power inconsistent with the federal Constitution, including, without limitation, its protections concerning courts, voting, equality, due process, privacy, asylum, non-refoulement, and the lawful allocation of State and federal authority.

### Section 9.2 — State Emergency Powers and Federal Supremacy

No emergency power under Article VI or delegated authority under Section 2.6 may displace, obstruct, or contravene lawful federal authority. The Governor may coordinate with federal authorities concerning asylum reception, public-health logistics, civil transport, emergency sheltering, and related State functions without assuming or interfering with federal border, coercive, asylum-determination, removal, or deportation powers.

### Section 9.3 — The Norvane State Record

(a) There shall be a Norvane State Record serving as the permanent public record of significant acts and proceedings of State government, freely accessible at no cost and searchable in practical form.

(b) The Record shall include enacted laws; executive orders; appointments and removals; vetoes; emergency declarations, extensions, and terminations; material emergency contracts; regulations and generally applicable administrative rules; significant final administrative decisions having public or precedential consequence; judicial opinions and dispositive orders subject to lawful privacy protection; budgets; audit reports; election certifications; constitutional intergovernmental arrangements; and other material acts required by law.

(c) Individual medical, asylum, immigration, reception, personnel, or benefit files do not become public merely because they are used in governmental decision-making.

(d) No entry may be silently deleted, overwritten, or altered. Corrections, reversals, amendments, and superseding actions shall be entered as new records permanently linked to the original.

(e) A law, generally applicable regulation, or generally applicable emergency order may not ordinarily be enforced against a person before official publication. A measure strictly necessary to protect life or safety may take immediate effect where law so provides but shall be published as soon as practicable.

(f) The Norvane State Record shall maintain compatibility with the federal National Record System and transmit information required by federal constitutional or lawful federal process.

### Section 9.4 — Federal Constitutional Compliance and Oversight

(a) Norvane shall cooperate with the federal audit and Monitor framework established by the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

(b) State officers shall provide federal institutions access and cooperation to the extent required by lawful federal authority. Nothing in this section independently expands federal access beyond that authority.

(c) No State officer may conceal, destroy, falsify, or knowingly delay a record required for lawful State or federal constitutional oversight.

### Section 9.5 — State Immigration Sponsorship

(a) Norvane participates in the immigration system of the Federated Republic through the State sponsorship stage established by Article VI §6.1 of the federal Constitution. State sponsorship does not itself confer legal residency, which requires federal certification.

(b) The Assembly shall establish public, lawful, and nondiscriminatory sponsorship criteria. Criteria may reflect legitimate State interests including family and community ties, housing and long-term settlement capacity, labor-market needs, skills, municipal absorption conditions, and comparable neutral considerations, but may not employ ideological screening or nationality-based quotas and shall comply with federal equality requirements.

(c) The Governor may direct general executive policy within law, but individual sponsorship applications shall be administered by a department or subordinate office under published criteria. The Governor, Deputy Governor, and individual legislators may not direct the outcome of an individual application on political, personal, employer, partisan, or patronage grounds.

(d) Denial of State sponsorship shall state the precise legal ground in writing. Courts may review legality, discrimination, and procedure but may not compel sponsorship except to the extent required by the federal Constitution.

(e) Federal certification, asylum adjudication, removal, and deportation remain exclusively federal as provided by the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

(f) Humanitarian reception, emergency shelter, healthcare, public-health assistance, transport assistance, education, private support, employment, or an offer of employment does not itself constitute State sponsorship, federal certification, or legal residency.

(g) Once federal residency credentials issue following State sponsorship and federal certification, Norvane's sponsorship stage has completed its constitutional function. The State may not revoke, impair, or condition the resulting federal residency status.

(h) Sponsorship criteria, regulations, aggregate reporting, and significant precedential State decisions shall be published consistent with this Article. Individual applicant records remain protected by lawful privacy requirements.

### Section 9.6 — Duty of Constitutional Refusal

(a) No State or local civil servant, member of an organized State force, or other officer or employee acting under State authority may be required to obey an order that would require violation of this Constitution, the Constitution of the Federated Republic, or lawful statute.

(b) Lawful refusal of such an order creates no State or local civil, criminal, employment, or disciplinary liability. Retaliatory penalties are void.

(c) A person refusing an order may report it through lawful State or federal oversight channels. The Assembly shall provide by law for prompt protection, review, and support of persons exercising this duty in good faith.

(d) This section does not authorize refusal merely because an officer disagrees with lawful policy or disputes a valid exercise of constitutional authority.

## Article X — Amendment

### Section 10.1 — Proposal

(a) The Assembly may propose an amendment by two-thirds of its authorized membership.

(b) The complete proposed amendment shall be published to the Norvane State Record and shall identify the provisions it would alter.

(c) The proposal shall lie over until the next regular session following the next general election. At that session, the Assembly must approve materially the same amendment again by two-thirds of its authorized membership. A material alteration restarts the process.

(d) Following second approval, the Assembly shall set a referendum no sooner than sixty days and no later than one hundred twenty days afterward.

### Section 10.2 — Ratification and Effective Date

An ordinary amendment becomes part of this Constitution when ratified by a majority of electors voting on the amendment. It takes effect upon certification unless the amendment specifies a later date.

### Section 10.3 — Protected Provisions

(a) An amendment that materially repeals, abolishes, narrows, or diminishes a right protected by Article I, or alters Norvane's constitutional declaration of federal subordination in Section 9.1, requires ratification by two-thirds of electors voting on the amendment.

(b) A change in the statutory or administrative means by which a right is implemented does not itself constitute diminution of constitutional protection.

(c) No amendment may conflict with the Constitution of the Federated Republic regardless of the vote received.

(d) This Article may be amended only by the procedure established in this Article.

### Section 10.4 — Amendment Procedure and Judicial Review

(a) Each proposed amendment shall embrace one reasonably related subject.

(b) Courts may review compliance with proposal thresholds, the intervening-election requirement, material identity of the two legislative approvals, publication, the single-subject rule, referendum procedure, and the applicable ratification threshold. Courts may not review the political wisdom of a proposed amendment.

(c) No emergency declaration or emergency statute may suspend, bypass, or substitute for the amendment procedure established by this Article.

## Article XI — Transition and Continuity

### Section 11.1 — Ratification, Readiness, and Effective Date

(a) This Constitution shall take effect upon ratification by the qualified electors and certification that:

(i) initial Assembly electoral districts have been lawfully established;
(ii) the Norvane State Record is operational or a lawful temporary public-publication substitute is available;
(iii) the first elections for Governor, Deputy Governor, and Assembly have been scheduled; and
(iv) the full ratified text has been publicly published.

(b) The lawful transitional authority existing at ratification shall certify satisfaction of subsection (a), publish that certification, and remain subject to judicial review.

(c) A temporary technical failure of the Norvane State Record does not prevent constitutional operation where an alternative publicly accessible method of publication is used until service is restored.

### Section 11.2 — Caretaker Government and First Elections

(a) From the effective date until certification of the first elections under this Constitution, existing lawful State officers and institutions continue only as caretakers necessary to preserve constitutional government, essential services, elections, public safety, public obligations, and orderly transfer of authority.

(b) Caretaker institutions may not undertake major new policy except where immediately necessary to preserve life, constitutional obligations, or continuity.

(c) The first Governor and Deputy Governor assume office when their election is certified and serve until the first day of January following the next general election for those offices. This transitional service counts as one term for purposes of Section 2.2.

(d) The first Assembly assumes office when its election is certified and serves until the ordinary commencement of the next Assembly term following the next scheduled Assembly general election.

### Section 11.3 — Continuity of Local Government

All municipalities, counties, and other local governmental bodies existing at the effective date continue with their existing boundaries, officers, and functions until altered in accordance with this Constitution and State law.

### Section 11.4 — Continuity of Courts and Proceedings

(a) Courts existing at the effective date are deemed courts established under Article VII until lawfully reorganized.

(b) Judges holding office at the effective date continue until expiration of their existing terms and thereafter become subject to the nomination and confirmation procedures of Article VII.

(c) Pending cases, judgments, orders, warrants, filing periods, and procedural deadlines remain valid and continue according to law.

### Section 11.5 — Continuity of Law, Administration, and Obligations

(a) Statutes, regulations, ordinances, executive orders, administrative determinations, and other lawful acts in force at the effective date remain in force until amended, repealed, superseded, or declared invalid, to the extent consistent with this Constitution and the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

(b) Public debts, contracts, salaries, pensions, grants, property interests, and other lawful obligations of the State remain enforceable.

(c) Lawful federal citizenship, residency credentials, asylum status, and other federally determined statuses are unaffected by adoption of this Constitution.

(d) An office or institution superseded by this Constitution continues only to the extent necessary to transfer records, property, staff, and pending matters to the lawful successor.

### Section 11.6 — Existing Emergencies

Any emergency authority in effect on the effective date shall expire no later than fourteen days afterward unless renewed or reauthorized in accordance with Article VI.

### Section 11.7 — Continuity of Public Records

The Norvane State Record shall preserve or maintain practical public access to official records predating this Constitution. Adoption of this Constitution does not authorize destruction or abandonment of the State's prior public record.

### Section 11.8 — Severability

If a provision or application of this Constitution is held invalid under the Constitution of the Federated Republic, the remaining provisions and applications remain in force unless they cannot function as constitutionally intended without the invalid portion.
