# CONSTITUTION OF THE STATE OF ORATH

*A Free and Self-Governing State of the Federated Republic*

---

## PREAMBLE

We, the people of Orath, descend from those who first broke this ground — farmers, market-keepers, and the families who followed the rivers inland to build towns among the fields. That history taught us things we have not forgotten and do not intend to: that the people closest to the work know best how to govern it, that cooperation outlasts any single harvest, and that no office of this State — however senior — earns the right to stand apart from those it serves. We ordain and establish this Constitution to carry those convictions forward, for whatever Orath becomes.

We do so as a State of the Federated Republic, bound by and subordinate to the Republic's Constitution in all matters committed to it, and free to govern ourselves in all matters reserved to us. The land that shaped us remains central to who we are, and this Constitution protects it accordingly — but Orath is not only what grows from its soil. We write this Constitution for a State that may change its work across the generations without changing its character.

This Constitution binds Orath's government to the people of Orath, whoever they are and whatever brought them here — newly arrived or descended from those who broke this ground first. It is adopted, and may be amended, only by the people it governs.

## ARTICLE I — DECLARATION OF RIGHTS

### §1.1 Federal Rights Floor
Nothing in this Constitution may diminish any right guaranteed by the Constitution of the Federated Republic. Where this Constitution provides greater protection within Orath, the greater protection applies to the extent consistent with federal law.

### §1.2 Inherent Rights and Productive Independence
All persons within Orath are born free and equal and hold inherent rights of life, liberty, the pursuit of a lawful livelihood, and the enjoyment of the fruits of their own labor and industry. Government exists to secure these rights and shall not use public power to create or preserve arbitrary economic dependency.

### §1.3 Equality Before the Law
No law or public act of Orath may discriminate contrary to §1.6 of the Republic's Constitution. Orath additionally prohibits discrimination on the basis of lawful occupation. Reasonable distinctions based on qualifications, safety, licensing, or other legitimate regulatory purposes remain permissible where they do not operate as a pretext for prohibited discrimination.

### §1.4 Right to Water
Every person and lawful enterprise in Orath holds a right of reasonable access to water for household, community, and productive use, administered under Article X. This right guarantees no fixed quantity and is subject to lawful allocation during scarcity. Allocation shall give due priority to human consumption and sanitation, ecological and public-health minimums, essential community needs, and essential productive use. Water rights may be regulated or transferred by law but may not be administered so as to extinguish reasonable basic access, create unlawful monopoly control, or punish conduct unrelated to lawful water use.

### §1.5 Cooperative Participation
A person who has lawfully and substantially labored within a cooperative enterprise for the continuous period established by statute is entitled to fair consideration of a petition for membership or participation on terms no less favorable than those applied to similarly situated applicants. A denial shall state its grounds in writing and is reviewable for compliance with this Constitution, the cooperative charter, and applicable law. This section creates a right to fair consideration, not an automatic right to membership.

### §1.6 Labor Rights
All persons who labor in Orath, regardless of citizenship, residency status, occupation, or duration of employment, hold the right to:
(a) a minimum wage no lower than that established by law;
(b) reasonable rest periods and safe working conditions;
(c) organize and bargain collectively;
(d) withhold labor, subject only to narrowly tailored continuity measures established by law where a genuine emergency threatens life, essential public services, food supply, or critical infrastructure, and only where effective dispute resolution remains available; and
(e) access an impartial forum for the resolution of labor disputes under Article IV.

Slavery, involuntary servitude, and forced labor are prohibited to the full extent required by the Republic's Constitution §1.4.

### §1.7 Local Self-Government
The people of Orath hold the right to meaningful local self-government through counties, municipalities where incorporated, and such other public local bodies as this Constitution and law establish. Local authority is subject to Article V, the rights guaranteed by this Constitution, and valid State and federal law.

### §1.8 Freedom of Movement and Settlement
No person lawfully present in Orath may be barred from settling, working, or traveling within the State on a prohibited discriminatory ground. A cooperative may govern membership and use of cooperative property but may not exercise territorial exclusion over nonmembers except through authority lawfully held by a public territorial government.

### §1.9 Right to a Remedy and Effective Access
Every person may seek timely and effective remedy in the courts and tribunals of Orath for injury to person, property, livelihood, or constitutional right. Procedure, distance, or inability to pay may not be used to make that remedy illusory. The State shall provide legal assistance where necessary to preserve effective access in proceedings involving serious threats to liberty, housing, livelihood, or protected rights, as provided by law.

### §1.10 Environmental Stewardship
The State and its local governments hold an obligation, consistent with the Republic's Constitution, to take genuine and proportionate action against environmental harms arising from their own conduct or from conduct they license. Particular attention shall be given to air, water, soil, watershed health, ecosystem integrity, and long-term habitability. This section requires genuine governmental action proportionate to identified harms and guarantees no particular environmental outcome.

### §1.11 Geographic Access to Government
Public services and institutions of Orath shall remain reasonably accessible notwithstanding distance or low population density. The State and its local governments shall provide, as appropriate and as established by law, reasonable access to transportation networks, emergency response, education, courts, public administration, communications, elections, and public records. No particular technology or delivery model is constitutionally required.

### §1.12 Anti-Retaliation
No public authority, employer exercising a right or privilege granted by the State, or cooperative exercising delegated public authority may retaliate against a person for lawfully asserting rights under this Constitution, including labor, environmental, water, cooperative-participation, public-record, or constitutional rights.

### §1.13 Due Process in Delegated Public Authority
A cooperative or other non-State body exercising authority delegated by law over public rights, public resources, or nonvoluntary obligations shall provide notice, an opportunity to be heard, written grounds for material adverse action, and access to judicial review appropriate to the nature of the authority exercised.

### §1.14 Rights Reserved
The enumeration of rights in this Article is not exhaustive. All rights guaranteed by the Constitution of the Federated Republic apply in full within Orath whether or not restated here.

## ARTICLE II — THE LEGISLATIVE BRANCH: THE ORATH GENERAL ASSEMBLY

### §2.1 Establishment
The legislative power of Orath is vested in a single house, the **Orath General Assembly**. Popular tradition calls it **the Grange**, after the cooperative movement that first organized much of Orath's political life; the name persists in speech, not in law.

### §2.2 Composition
(a) The General Assembly shall contain not fewer than seventy-five and not more than one hundred five members, as established by statute.
(b) Approximately two-thirds of the seats shall be elected from geographic districts and approximately one-third from a statewide proportional or compensatory tier designed to preserve substantial proportionality in political representation.
(c) Geographic districts may be single-member or small multi-member districts, or a combination, as provided by law.
(d) Members serve four-year terms. No constitutional term limit applies, but reasonable statutory limits may be established prospectively.

### §2.3 Districting
(a) Geographic districts shall be drawn by an independent Orath Districting Commission.
(b) Districts shall comply with the federal requirements of population equality, contiguity, non-discrimination, prohibition of intentional minority vote dilution, and reasonable compactness. Political affiliation, voting history, incumbent residence, and racial composition may not be used as primary districting criteria except to the extent federal law requires consideration of a protected characteristic to prevent unlawful dilution.
(c) Subject to those controlling criteria, the Commission may consider counties, municipalities, transportation corridors, watersheds, economically connected regions, and established rural communities as secondary communities of interest.
(d) All maps remain subject to the review and fallback processes required by the Republic's Constitution.

### §2.4 Elections and Eligibility
(a) Every voting citizen of Orath and every legal resident of the Republic whose principal residence is in Orath, eighteen years of age or older, may vote in State and local elections, subject to lawful registration requirements.
(b) A candidate for the General Assembly must be at least twenty-one years of age, be lawfully eligible to participate in Orath elections, have resided in Orath for at least one year, and, for a geographic seat, reside in the district sought.
(c) Legal residents who are not citizens of the Republic may serve in the General Assembly and in ordinary local elected office. Eligibility for Steward and High Court justice is governed separately by Articles III and IV.
(d) Federal voting and federal officeholding remain governed exclusively by federal law.

### §2.5 Officers, Quorum, and Authorized Membership
(a) The General Assembly elects a Speaker from among its members by majority of authorized membership.
(b) Quorum is a majority of authorized membership.
(c) **Authorized membership** means the total number of seats lawfully established for the General Assembly, whether filled or vacant, unless the Republic's Constitution requires a different denominator for a particular act.
(d) The Speaker presides, certifies enacted legislation, and serves as Acting Steward under Article III. While exercising acting executive authority, the Speaker may not exercise legislative voting or decisional authority; the Assembly shall select an acting presiding officer for that period.

### §2.6 Powers
The General Assembly may:
(a) enact, amend, and repeal State law;
(b) levy State taxes and appropriate funds subject to Article IX;
(c) confirm or reject appointments for which this Constitution or statute requires confirmation;
(d) establish, alter, merge, or dissolve counties and municipalities subject to Article V and affected residents' democratic rights;
(e) establish and regulate cooperative charters and other special-purpose bodies consistent with Article V;
(f) propose constitutional amendments in accordance with Article XII;
(g) investigate State administration and compel testimony and records for a legitimate legislative purpose; and
(h) exercise all legislative powers not constitutionally reserved elsewhere or preempted by federal law.

### §2.7 Legislative Process
(a) Every bill shall address a single, clearly defined subject.
(b) The full text of a bill shall be public for at least ten days before final passage. In a genuine emergency, two-thirds of authorized membership may waive this period by a published finding stating the urgent necessity.
(c) With quorum present, ordinary legislation passes by majority of members present and voting unless this Constitution requires a higher threshold.
(d) Enacted laws shall be published promptly to the Orath State Record under Article VIII.
(e) Material participation in legislative proceedings may be conducted securely by remote means where severe weather, geographic isolation, disability, or comparable circumstances make physical attendance unreasonably difficult, as provided by law.

### §2.8 Legislative Ethics
An independent ethics body established by law shall administer disclosure and conflict rules. A member shall disclose a direct and particularized material financial interest and shall not participate where that interest creates a substantial conflict. An interest shared generally with a broad class of residents or workers does not by itself require recusal.

### §2.9 Emergency Sessions
The Steward or one-third of authorized membership may call an emergency session. Emergency procedure creates no power to suspend constitutional rights beyond what the Republic's Constitution and this Constitution lawfully permit.

## ARTICLE III — THE EXECUTIVE BRANCH: THE STEWARD

### §3.1 Establishment
The executive power of Orath is vested in the **Steward of Orath**. The title reflects the State's tradition that executive authority is held in trust for those it serves and carries the full authority assigned by this Constitution.

### §3.2 Selection, Formation, and Term
(a) At the opening of each General Assembly term, the Assembly shall elect as Steward a sitting Assembly member who is a citizen of the Republic and a voting citizen of Orath.
(b) Election initially requires a majority of authorized membership. If no candidate is elected after three ballots, only the two candidates receiving the highest vote totals may proceed. A tie determining which candidate advances is resolved in favor of the older candidate. If the two final candidates remain tied after two further ballots, the older candidate is elected.
(c) Upon election, the Steward immediately vacates the Assembly seat. The vacancy is filled under the electoral law applicable to the seat.
(d) The Steward serves for the remainder of the Assembly term and may be elected to one additional regular term. Service for more than one-half of a regular term counts as a full term for this limit; service for one-half or less does not.

### §3.3 Political Removal, Vacancy, and Succession
(a) The General Assembly may remove the Steward politically only by a constructive vote of no confidence that simultaneously elects a named successor by majority of authorized membership.
(b) Removal for grave misconduct, constitutional disqualification, or serious breach of public trust may be provided separately by law, subject to written charges, notice, a fair hearing, and judicial review. Such removal need not name a successor.
(c) Upon death, resignation, incapacity, or removal without an immediately installed successor, the Speaker serves as Acting Steward. Legislative voting and decisional authority of the Speaker is suspended during acting service.
(d) The Assembly shall elect a successor within thirty days. If no successor is elected after the first ten days, the formation procedure in §3.2(b) applies to all subsequent ballots until a successor is chosen.
(e) The Acting Steward may exercise the ordinary powers of the office, including veto, but may not use acting service to extend any constitutional deadline or alter the succession process.

### §3.4 Powers and Duties
The Steward shall:
(a) faithfully execute the laws of Orath;
(b) appoint and supervise executive department heads, with departments established by law; ordinary department heads require no confirmation unless statute provides otherwise, while independent, adjudicative, or constitutionally protected offices remain subject to the appointment safeguards applicable to them;
(c) prepare and submit an annual proposed budget, which the General Assembly is not bound to adopt;
(d) grant clemency for State offenses except where the Steward has a direct personal, financial, familial, or other material interest, in which case the function passes to the neutral officer or body designated by law;
(e) represent Orath in lawful relations with the federal government, other States and Territories, and Orath's local governments; and
(f) exercise such other executive powers as this Constitution and law provide.

### §3.5 Emergencies
(a) The Steward may declare a State or local emergency only upon a specific factual basis showing an imminent or substantial threat to life, public health, essential services, food supply, critical infrastructure, or comparable public necessity. Labor disruption alone is not an emergency ground.
(b) A declaration shall identify the factual basis, geographic scope, powers activated, and measures undertaken and shall be published promptly to the Orath State Record.
(c) A unilateral declaration expires after fourteen days. The General Assembly may extend it by majority of authorized membership for periods not exceeding thirty days each and may terminate it at any time by the same threshold.
(d) The Steward may not evade expiration by issuing a substantially identical declaration for the same continuing emergency without legislative authorization.
(e) No emergency grants power beyond this Constitution or valid law, and all emergency measures remain subject to judicial review.

### §3.6 The Veto
(a) The Steward may veto a bill within ten business days of passage by returning written objections.
(b) The General Assembly may override by two-thirds of authorized membership.
(c) A bill neither signed nor returned within ten business days becomes law.
(d) No pocket veto exists. Constitutional obligations remain enforceable by the courts whether or not legislation implementing them has been vetoed.

### §3.7 Limits and Duty of Lawful Execution
The Steward may exercise only authority granted by this Constitution or law and may not:
(a) suspend constitutional rights except to the limited extent lawfully permitted by the Republic's Constitution;
(b) interfere with individual State sponsorship determinations except through generally applicable lawful policy;
(c) remove judges or protected adjudicators outside the processes established by Article IV; or
(d) exercise power committed exclusively to the federal government.

The general duty of refusal and protection against unlawful orders is established in Article XIII.

## ARTICLE IV — THE JUDICIAL BRANCH

### §4.1 Judicial Power
The judicial power of Orath is vested in the **Orath High Court**, **District Courts**, **Labor Arbitration Tribunals**, and such other inferior courts or tribunals as law establishes. Courts of Orath may review the constitutionality and legality of State statutes, executive acts, emergency measures, local ordinances, delegated public acts of cooperatives, election administration, and public-resource decisions within their jurisdiction.

### §4.2 Orath High Court
(a) The High Court consists of seven justices serving single, non-renewable twelve-year terms.
(b) A **Judicial Nominating Commission** established by law shall publicly identify a qualified shortlist for each vacancy. The Commission shall be composed so that no single constitutional actor or political institution controls a majority of its membership.
(c) The Steward appoints one person from the shortlist, subject to confirmation by majority of authorized General Assembly membership.
(d) A High Court justice must be a citizen of the Republic and a voting citizen of Orath at appointment and must possess substantial legal experience, demonstrated competence, integrity, and freedom from disqualifying conflicts. Law may establish reasonable minimum professional qualifications.
(e) The High Court has appellate jurisdiction as provided by law and original jurisdiction over disputes concerning allocation of constitutional authority between the State and its local governments or among local governments.
(f) Federal constitutional questions are reviewed, referred, or appealed only as permitted by the Constitution and law of the Federated Republic.

### §4.3 District Courts
(a) District Courts hear civil and criminal matters arising under Orath law and such appeals from inferior tribunals as law provides.
(b) District judges are elected in nonpartisan elections to eight-year terms and may seek re-election.
(c) Judicial campaign rules shall require disclosure and prohibit promises or commitments concerning the outcome of cases or classes of cases.
(d) An independent judicial performance body may publish evaluations before judicial elections but may not direct case outcomes.

### §4.4 Labor Arbitration Tribunals
(a) Labor Arbitration Tribunals shall provide specialized adjudication of employment and cooperative-labor disputes, including wages, working conditions, collective bargaining, retaliation, statutory labor rights, and cooperative-participation claims under Article I.
(b) Tribunal composition may reflect relevant expertise in labor law, worker interests, management, and cooperative organization but shall not assign decisional authority as a hereditary or closed class entitlement.
(c) Proceedings shall provide notice, opportunity to be heard, presentation of evidence, written decisions, and appeal to the appropriate District Court under standards established by law.
(d) Tribunal members serve staggered terms and are protected from removal or retaliation based on the lawful content of their decisions.

### §4.5 Judicial Conduct and Independence
(a) No public official may direct, threaten, punish, reassign, restructure, or otherwise seek to influence a judge or adjudicator because of the lawful disposition of a pending or completed case.
(b) Judicial compensation may not be reduced during a sitting judge's term as retaliation for judicial conduct.
(c) An independent **Judicial Conduct Commission** shall investigate allegations of misconduct, incapacity, or serious incompetence and may recommend discipline or removal under procedures established by law.
(d) Removal requires written charges, notice, opportunity to respond, a fair hearing, and a published decision. A High Court justice may not sit in judgment of their own removal; such a matter shall be heard by a special tribunal of senior District judges selected by objective order of continuous judicial service, with substitutions made by the same rule where recusal is required.
(e) Impeachment or other legislative accountability for grave misconduct may be provided by law but may not be used to reverse or punish lawful judicial decisions.

### §4.6 Remedies, Recusal, and Continuity
(a) Courts may issue injunctions, mandamus, declaratory relief, stays, and other lawful remedies necessary to enforce rights and constitutional allocations of authority.
(b) Where recusal or vacancy would otherwise prevent the High Court from acting, the required number of senior qualified District judges shall sit temporarily by objective order of continuous judicial service. Such service is temporary judicial substitution and not appointment to the High Court.

### §4.7 Recognition of Judgments
Orath recognizes judgments, official records, and authenticated documents of the Republic's other jurisdictions in the manner required by the Republic's Constitution §4.6. Orath judgments receive the same federal protection.

## ARTICLE V — LOCAL GOVERNMENT

### §5.1 Constitutional Status and Fiscal Capacity
Counties and municipalities are constitutionally protected territorial governments within their lawful domains and are not mere administrative offices of the State. They are entitled to fiscal capacity reasonably sufficient to carry out constitutional and statutory functions assigned to them. The State may establish minimum statewide standards and act where a substantial statewide interest requires coordination or uniformity, subject to this Article.

### §5.2 Counties
(a) Every part of Orath outside an incorporated municipality shall remain within a county, and county government is the baseline territorial government for unincorporated residents.
(b) Every county shall have a directly elected County Assembly or Council chosen under equal-population principles. County executive arrangements may be established by county charter or general law.
(c) Counties may administer, as provided by law, rural roads, emergency response, land administration, public health, local public safety, election logistics, permitting, regional water and infrastructure coordination, and other territorial services not assigned elsewhere.
(d) A person may not be denied direct county representation merely because they live outside a municipality or cooperative.

### §5.3 Municipalities
(a) Every incorporated municipality shall be governed by an elected council under a home-rule charter adopted consistently with this Constitution and general election law.
(b) Municipalities hold primary authority over genuinely municipal matters, including local streets, zoning, land use, municipal services, housing policy, sanitation, parks, local licensing, public health, and municipal public safety where maintained.
(c) Municipal charters may provide council-manager, mayor-council, commission, or another republican form consistent with this Constitution.

### §5.4 Rural and Other Cooperatives
(a) A cooperative is a self-governing economic association chartered under State law. Agricultural cooperatives remain expressly recognized as part of Orath's historic and present economy, but cooperative organization is not limited to agriculture.
(b) A cooperative may govern cooperative property, internal land or facility leases, shared equipment and enterprises, member assessments, internal labor coordination consistent with labor law, and allocation among members of water or other rights lawfully held by the cooperative.
(c) A cooperative does not possess general territorial authority over nonmembers. Policing, elections, criminal enforcement, general taxation, public roads, compulsory public-health authority, civil registration, and general land-use regulation belong to public territorial government unless the Constitution expressly provides otherwise.
(d) Charter revocation or compulsory dissolution may occur for serious and persistent constitutional violation, fraud, insolvency or inability to perform essential charter obligations, abandonment, or other comparably grave grounds established by law. Except where immediate action is necessary to prevent serious harm, the cooperative shall receive written grounds and reasonable opportunity to cure. Judicial review and protection of member property and accrued rights are guaranteed.

### §5.5 State and Local Domains
(a) Matters of genuinely local concern are presumptively governed locally. State legislation displacing protected local authority shall state the substantial statewide interest served and shall displace local authority only to the extent reasonably necessary to address that interest.
(b) Statewide interests include protection of constitutional rights, compliance with federal obligations, statewide labor and environmental floors, major infrastructure networks, cross-boundary public health, substantial economic or fiscal externalities, and comparable matters that cannot reasonably be confined to one locality.
(c) A legislative declaration of statewide interest is reviewable by the courts and is not conclusive merely because it appears in statutory text.
(d) No county or municipality holds an absolute veto over genuinely statewide infrastructure. Affected communities are entitled to notice, consultation, lawful compensation where required, and judicial review.

### §5.6 Fiscal Protection and Unfunded Mandates
(a) The State may not systematically deprive counties or municipalities of fiscal capacity in order to achieve de facto centralization or make assigned functions impossible to perform.
(b) Where the State imposes a significant new mandatory local function that materially increases cost, it shall provide reasonable funding unless the requirement is a generally applicable minimum standard, the locality voluntarily accepts the function, or a genuine emergency or comparable public necessity justifies temporary unfunded compliance.
(c) Detailed taxing and equalization rules are governed by Article IX.

### §5.7 Geographic Access and Regional Cooperation
(a) Counties and municipalities shall maintain reasonable administrative access for remote residents through regional offices, circuit or mobile services, secure remote filing or participation, or other effective methods established by law.
(b) Local governments may enter interlocal agreements and establish regional bodies for roads, emergency services, water, waste management, public health, transit, communications, education support, and other shared functions.
(c) Public meetings may permit secure remote participation where geography, severe weather, disability, or comparable conditions make physical attendance unreasonably difficult.

### §5.8 Local Emergency Authority
Counties and municipalities may exercise immediate emergency authority under law to protect life and property from fire, flood, severe weather, infrastructure failure, medical emergency, isolation, or comparable local hazards without prior Steward approval. Such authority may not suspend constitutional rights, permanently alter ordinary law, commandeer another locality, or interfere with lawful labor activity absent a genuine threat meeting the standards of Article III. Emergency acts shall be published and remain judicially reviewable.

### §5.9 Boundaries, Consolidation, and Continuity
The State may alter boundaries, consolidate local governments, establish shared-service districts, or permit municipal incorporation and dissolution by law. Affected residents shall receive meaningful notice, participation, and democratic approval where an elected government is abolished or merged. No unincorporated area is required to incorporate merely to receive competent public services.

### §5.10 Local Democratic Floor and Disputes
All territorial local governments are subject to periodic elections, equal voting rights, open records, published budgets, conflict-of-interest rules, and judicial review. Disputes between local governments or between a local government and the State are heard in the District Courts with appeal to the High Court.

## ARTICLE VI — IMMIGRATION SPONSORSHIP AND LABOR

### §6.1 Two-Stage System
Orath participates in the two-stage immigration system established by the Republic's Constitution §6.1. State sponsorship lies within Orath's discretion; federal certification lies exclusively with the Republic. Legal residency requires both stages unless the Republic's Constitution provides a separate federal path, including asylum. Removal and deportation are exclusively federal functions.

### §6.2 Office of Sponsorship and Labor Integration
(a) An **Office of Sponsorship and Labor Integration** within the executive branch shall administer Orath's sponsorship stage independently of individual political direction.
(b) The Office shall publish clear, objective, lawful, and equally applied sponsorship criteria. Criteria may not include ideological screening, political loyalty tests, religious tests, nationality quotas, or discrimination prohibited by the Republic's Constitution or Article I of this Constitution.
(c) The Steward may direct general lawful policy but may not order approval or denial of an individual sponsorship application.
(d) The Office shall provide reasonable geographic access through regional, remote, mobile, or other effective methods established by law.

### §6.3 Sponsorship Policy and Review
(a) Orath may prioritize sponsorship according to demonstrated economic, labor-market, demographic, family, social, and community needs established by public law and subject to periodic review. A bona fide employment offer may be considered but no occupation or industry holds permanent constitutional preference.
(b) Orath may not establish an aggregate State sponsorship cap where federal law reserves aggregate limits to the federal Legislature. Published substantive criteria may lawfully limit eligibility.
(c) Every denial shall identify in writing the criterion not satisfied, the factual basis, and any available reconsideration or review process.
(d) Courts may review a denial for prohibited discrimination, unlawful criteria, retaliation, corruption or conflict of interest, or failure to follow required procedure or published criteria. A court may order lawful reconsideration but may not compel sponsorship merely because it disagrees with the policy judgment.

### §6.4 Employment-Based Sponsorship and Worker Independence
(a) An employer, cooperative, or local government may support a sponsorship application but holds no delegated power to grant, deny, revoke, or control State sponsorship or federal residency.
(b) Where an employment offer materially supports sponsorship, law may require disclosure of wage, expected hours, job location, duration, housing terms, deductions, and other material conditions.
(c) Sponsorship may not be conditioned on waiver of wage, safety, organizing, tribunal-access, anti-retaliation, or other labor rights.
(d) No employer may retaliate against a worker by threatening sponsorship withdrawal, immigration reporting, housing loss, blacklisting, or comparable status-based coercion for lawful assertion of rights.
(e) Employer fraud may be sanctioned without automatically attributing wrongdoing to a worker who acted in good faith. Such a worker shall receive notice and a reasonable opportunity, where law permits, to establish another lawful sponsorship basis before adverse State action.

### §6.5 Completion of Sponsorship and Federal Status
Once State sponsorship and federal certification are complete, issuance of federal residency credentials is ministerial under federal law. Orath may not condition or delay that issuance and may not later purport to revoke federal residency by withdrawing completed State sponsorship.

### §6.6 Rights, Asylum, and Humanitarian Assistance
(a) All workers physically present in Orath hold the labor rights of Article I regardless of sponsorship, certification, asylum, or other status.
(b) Asylum operates on the separate federal track established by the Republic's Constitution. Orath imposes no State sponsorship requirement on an asylum applicant.
(c) Emergency shelter, food, healthcare, transportation, language assistance, or other humanitarian aid supplied by the State, a local government, or a cooperative does not itself constitute sponsorship, federal certification, or legal residency.

### §6.7 Local Integration
Counties, municipalities, regional bodies, and voluntary cooperatives may provide lawful integration assistance including housing support, language instruction, transportation, employment services, and community orientation. Participation is voluntary except where a narrowly tailored administrative requirement is established by law and does not become an ideological or cultural assimilation test.

## ARTICLE VII — ELECTIONS

### §7.1 Orath Elections Office
(a) State and local elections are administered by the independent **Orath Elections Office** under law.
(b) Its leadership shall serve fixed or staggered terms, may be removed only for cause, and shall be selected through a process in which no single political actor controls the entire appointment.
(c) The Office shall publish procedures, material decisions, and certifications and shall administer State and local elections consistently throughout Orath.

### §7.2 Franchise and Voting Citizenship
(a) Every citizen of the Republic whose State voting citizenship is in Orath under the Republic's Constitution §5.1.a may vote in Orath elections upon reaching eighteen years of age.
(b) Every legal resident of the Republic whose principal lawful residence is in Orath may also vote in State and local elections upon reaching eighteen years of age, subject to lawful registration requirements.
(c) Orath creates no separate nationality. Federal voting and federal officeholding remain governed exclusively by federal law.
(d) Registration rules may require proof reasonably necessary to establish eligibility but may not impose an unnecessary durational-residency barrier or materially burden lawful access to the franchise.

### §7.3 Geographic and Accessible Voting
The Elections Office shall ensure that distance, low population density, disability, temporary isolation, or lack of nearby municipal infrastructure does not effectively deny an eligible voter access to the ballot. Law may provide early voting, absentee or mail voting, mobile or temporary polling places, remote assistance, or other secure and verifiable methods. No particular voting technology is constitutionally required.

### §7.4 Election Disruption and Scheduling
(a) The Steward has no authority to postpone, cancel, or manipulate the scheduling of a State, local, or federal election.
(b) Where flood, fire, severe weather, road failure, communications outage, or comparable disruption prevents voting in a limited area, the Elections Office may relocate polling places, extend access in the affected area, preserve ballots, or provide substitute access as law permits, subject to expedited judicial review.
(c) Statewide postponement requires certification by the Elections Office that the election cannot reasonably proceed and concurrence of the High Court or a judicial panel established by law. Any postponement shall be for the minimum period necessary.
(d) Federal election scheduling remains exclusively governed by federal law.

### §7.5 Campaigns and Ballot Access
(a) State and local campaigns are subject to public disclosure of material contributions and expenditures, anti-corruption rules, and a prohibition on foreign contributions.
(b) Ballot-access requirements for parties, independent candidates, and lists shall be reasonable and may not defeat meaningful political competition.
(c) Any threshold for allocation of statewide proportional seats shall be reasonable and may not defeat the substantial proportionality required by Article II.
(d) Where the State provides official voter guides, candidate information, or public forums, they shall be reasonably available throughout Orath.

### §7.6 Election Disputes and Vacancies
(a) Election disputes affecting ballot access, certification, or statewide outcomes shall receive expedited judicial review, with prompt appeal to the High Court.
(b) Geographic-seat vacancies shall be filled by a democratic process established by law. Statewide proportional-seat vacancies may be filled from the same party or list in a manner preserving the electoral allocation.
(c) Material changes to district structure, electoral formula, ballot access, or proportional thresholds shall not take effect for an election already substantially underway except where necessary to comply with a judicial order or controlling federal law.

## ARTICLE VIII — THE PUBLIC RECORD

### §8.1 Orath State Record
Orath uses the National Record System as its constitutional public-record architecture. The section of that system reserved to Orath is the **Orath State Record**. Orath need not maintain a parallel constitutional archive, but State and local authorities remain responsible for creating, preserving, and accurately transmitting records required by law and for maintaining continuity during temporary system unavailability.

### §8.2 Records Officer
(a) The **Records Officer** is appointed by the Steward with General Assembly confirmation for a six-year term and may be removed only for cause upon a finding by the High Court.
(b) The Officer shall oversee completeness, timeliness, technical integrity, correction and supersession tracking, and annual compliance certification for required State filings.
(c) The Officer holds no discretion to suppress or delay a legally required public filing and may not be removed or disciplined for publishing an embarrassing but required record.
(d) Law shall provide an objective acting-officer arrangement during vacancy or temporary incapacity.

### §8.3 Timely Publication and Enforceability
(a) Enacted laws, executive orders, emergency declarations, vetoes, election certifications, and appointments or removals whose legal effect depends on public notice shall be published promptly and within any shorter constitutional deadline that applies.
(b) Reports, audits, disclosures, committee materials, and other nonurgent records shall be published within the period established by law.
(c) A generally applicable law, rule, or order imposing obligations on the public is not enforceable against a person before reasonable public publication, except for a narrowly tailored emergency measure that must take immediate effect to prevent imminent harm and is published as soon as practicable.

### §8.4 Outage, Correction, and Integrity
(a) If the National Record System is temporarily unavailable, required acts may be published through an alternative public medium established by law. They shall be synchronized to the Orath State Record promptly upon restoration.
(b) A filed public record may not be silently altered or deleted. A correction shall preserve the original, identify the correction, and state the date and reason.
(c) No officer may knowingly destroy, falsify, conceal, or deliberately delay a record required by Constitution or law.

### §8.5 Privacy
Public-record obligations shall be reconciled with constitutional privacy. Law shall protect medical information, personal identifiers, protected employment files, individual immigration records, confidential judicial materials, legitimate security information, and other private information except to the extent disclosure is constitutionally or legally required. Public rules, aggregate data, and final official acts remain subject to publication.

### §8.6 Local and Cooperative Records
(a) Counties and municipalities shall publish ordinances, budgets, major contracts, election certifications, emergency declarations, audits, and other records required by law to the Orath State Record.
(b) Cooperatives need not publish purely internal member-business records, but acts exercising delegated public authority, charter changes, public grants, audited use of public funds, and rules governing publicly protected resources shall be public as law requires.

### §8.7 Public Access
The State shall ensure reasonable access to the Orath State Record for persons without reliable private connectivity through county offices, libraries, public terminals, printable copies, accessible formats, or comparable means.

## ARTICLE IX — TAXATION AND FINANCE

### §9.1 Taxing and Revenue Authority
(a) The General Assembly may levy State taxes. Counties and municipalities may levy reasonable local taxes and charges under law and their charters.
(b) Cooperatives may impose dues, member assessments, service charges, and internal contributions under their charters; these are not general public taxes.
(c) Taxation shall be public, administered consistently, and subject to this Constitution, the Republic's Constitution, and lawful fiscal purposes.

### §9.2 Orath Revenue Fund and Comptroller
(a) State revenue shall be deposited in the **Orath Revenue Fund** and expended only under lawful appropriation or an automatic continuity provision of this Article.
(b) An independent State Comptroller shall audit State accounts, transfers to local governments, major public grants, and such other public expenditures as law provides.
(c) The Comptroller shall serve a fixed term, may be removed only for cause, shall have full lawful audit access, and shall publish reports to the Orath State Record.

### §9.3 Currency
Neither Orath nor any local government or cooperative may issue an instrument intended to function as legal tender contrary to the Republic's exclusive currency framework.

### §9.4 Budget, Deficits, and Debt
(a) The General Assembly shall maintain a balanced operating budget over the economic cycle.
(b) Deficits are permitted for recession, disaster, infrastructure failure, severe economic shock, or other substantial public necessity, provided the State publishes a recovery or stabilization plan.
(c) Orath may incur debt for lawful public purposes including long-lived infrastructure, disaster recovery, capital works, and stabilization. Borrowing requires legislative authorization and publication of material terms.

### §9.5 Orath Stabilization Reserve
(a) The General Assembly shall maintain an **Orath Stabilization Reserve Fund**, funded as law provides in periods of fiscal strength.
(b) The Fund may support State and local continuity during drought, crop failure, commodity or market collapse, recession, major employer failure, infrastructure disruption, disaster, or comparable severe shock.
(c) The State may establish fair risk-sharing, insurance, reinsurance, loan-guarantee, or stabilization mechanisms where market failure threatens productive livelihoods, essential supply, employment, or community continuity. No industry is constitutionally entitled to permanent subsidy.

### §9.6 Infrastructure and Geographic Equity
(a) Orath shall maintain a long-term capital plan for major roads, bridges, communications, water systems, emergency access, and other statewide networks.
(b) Infrastructure allocation may consider population, usage, economic importance, safety, geographic isolation, absence of alternatives, and condition of existing infrastructure.
(c) Reasonable access to markets and transportation networks is a legitimate public purpose, but this Constitution guarantees no private profit, commodity price, or market share.

### §9.7 Local Equalization and Fiscal Capacity
(a) State transfers to local governments shall account for the actual cost of delivering constitutionally and statutorily required services and may consider population, geographic area, road miles, population density, service distance, emergency-response cost, infrastructure condition, and local tax capacity.
(b) Low population may not by itself justify depriving a community of reasonable access to essential public services.
(c) The Comptroller or another independent fiscal body shall periodically review equalization formulas and publish whether they systematically underfund particular geographic categories.
(d) Orath may receive federal fiscal equalization as provided by the Republic's Constitution and federal law.

### §9.8 Regulation of Essential Economic Access
Subject to federal law, Orath may regulate insurance, lending, unfair contract practices, market concentration, and abusive control of essential infrastructure where reasonably necessary to protect competitive access, productive independence, or community continuity. This section creates no constitutional price control.

### §9.9 Budget Continuity
If a new budget is not enacted before existing appropriations expire, lawful appropriations for existing programs, constitutional obligations, debt service, and essential public services continue automatically at the baseline established by law. No new program or material expansion may be created through this fallback. The General Assembly may replace continuity funding at any time through ordinary appropriation.

### §9.10 Transparency
Major public contracts, subsidies, grants, stabilization awards, and borrowing instruments shall be published as required by Article VIII and law.

## ARTICLE X — LAND, WATER, AND STEWARDSHIP

### §10.1 Purpose
Orath's land and water are foundational resources serving households, communities, agriculture, commerce, industry, environmental health, and future generations. Agriculture shaped the State and remains entitled to equal protection and lawful support, but no provision of this Article requires continuation of any particular economic composition.

### §10.2 Toren Tributary Water Board
(a) The General Assembly shall establish a **Toren Tributary Water Board** responsible for allocation and long-term administration of water rights within its lawful jurisdiction.
(b) The Board shall fairly represent upstream and downstream regions and the major categories of affected users, including household and public systems, rural communities, productive users, and ecological interests. No single category may control the Board.
(c) Allocation shall conform to Article I and may protect human consumption and sanitation, ecological and public-health minimums, essential community needs, and essential productive uses during scarcity.
(d) Water-use rights may be transferred as law permits, but no transfer may extinguish reasonable basic community access, create unlawful monopoly control, materially impair protected downstream rights, or defeat required ecological minimums.
(e) Interstate water disputes shall be handled through lawful compact and the federal procedures available under the Republic's Constitution and law.

### §10.3 Productive Access and Land Trusts
(a) The State may establish mechanisms to preserve fair access to productive land and cooperative assets and to prevent closed hereditary control of publicly favored or cooperative productive resources.
(b) Community land trusts, long-term lease programs, revolving land funds, cooperative equity programs, purchase assistance, and comparable mechanisms are expressly authorized but none is constitutionally mandatory for every cooperative.
(c) No provision of this section requires forced redistribution of lawfully held private property.

### §10.4 Land and Resource Preservation
(a) The State may protect land of substantial importance for agriculture, watershed protection, flood control, environmental value, public access, strategic infrastructure, or community continuity.
(b) Such protection may not be construed as a general prohibition on housing, urban growth, industrial development, commercial development, or infrastructure.
(c) Compensation is required where a taking occurs under the Republic's Constitution; ordinary regulation does not create compensation merely because it affects land value.

### §10.5 Concentration and Essential Access
Subject to federal law, Orath may regulate abusive concentration or monopoly control over water delivery, essential storage, freight access, utilities, or other infrastructure where such control materially threatens competitive access or productive independence. Detailed remedies are established by law.

### §10.6 Local Land Use and Statewide Effects
Counties and municipalities retain primary authority over ordinary local land use under Article V. The State may establish minimum standards or intervene where land use materially affects interstate waters, statewide infrastructure, constitutional environmental baselines, or substantial cross-boundary interests.

### §10.7 Cooperative Land Authority
A cooperative may govern land, leases, facilities, and resource rights owned or lawfully controlled by the cooperative. It may not regulate unrelated neighboring property merely because of geographic proximity.

### §10.8 Long-Term Planning
The State and Water Board shall periodically publish assessments of water availability, drought risk, major infrastructure condition, and significant land-use or resource pressures, as provided by law.

## ARTICLE XI — EDUCATION

### §11.1 Right and State Responsibility
Every child in Orath holds the right to education adequate to enable full civic participation, consistent with the Republic's Constitution. The State remains ultimately responsible for ensuring that education is genuinely available regardless of remoteness, population density, disability, transportation barriers, or local tax capacity.

### §11.2 Local and Regional Governance
(a) Public education shall remain locally accountable and provide meaningful representation of affected communities.
(b) Law may provide elected local school boards, regional school authorities, multi-county districts, consolidated service districts, or shared bodies where necessary for educational quality or practical service delivery.
(c) Consolidation or closure materially affecting a community requires notice, public participation, published justification, and such review as law provides, but no community holds an absolute veto over lawful reorganization.

### §11.3 Access and Transportation
(a) Educational access includes reasonable transportation or another effective means of participation where distance would otherwise make attendance impracticable.
(b) Remote or hybrid instruction may supplement or, where necessary, provide education, but may not be used merely as a cheaper means of providing remote students a persistently inferior educational experience.

### §11.4 Funding Equity
The General Assembly shall establish an adequate and equitable funding system that may consider enrollment, geographic area, transportation distance, school size, special-needs cost, local tax capacity, facility condition, and staffing difficulty. Equality does not require identical spending per student. The State may not use local fiscal weakness as a basis for failing its own constitutional education duty.

### §11.5 Standards and Local Control
Local or regional education authorities hold primary responsibility for curriculum, staffing, and daily operation subject to statewide minimum academic, safety, civic, and constitutional standards. Local autonomy may not be used to discriminate or exclude students contrary to Article I or the Republic's Constitution.

### §11.6 Vocational, Technical, and Higher Education
Orath may maintain or support universities, colleges, technical institutes, apprenticeships, extension programs, continuing education, and vocational training serving agriculture, manufacturing, logistics, energy, healthcare, trades, technology, or other lawful fields. Public higher education is not constitutionally limited to any industry. The State may use regional campuses, extension centers, and remote programs to improve geographic access.

### §11.7 Transparency and Stabilization
(a) Public education bodies shall publish budgets, major policy decisions, meeting records, and performance information as law requires.
(b) Temporary economic or population decline may justify stabilization support for essential educational access, but this Constitution guarantees no particular school building or district perpetual existence.

## ARTICLE XII — AMENDMENT AND REVISION

### §12.1 Legislative Proposal
The General Assembly may propose an amendment by two-thirds of authorized membership.

### §12.2 Citizen Initiative
(a) An amendment may also be proposed by petition signed by eligible Orath electors equal to eight percent of valid votes cast in the most recent statewide General Assembly election.
(b) Signatures shall demonstrate broadly distributed support across Orath. Law may require a modest minimum share from at least half of the counties but may not give sparsely populated counties a disproportionate veto over access to the initiative process.
(c) The Orath Elections Office shall independently certify signatures, geographic distribution, and compliance with procedural requirements.

### §12.3 Form and Publication
(a) Each amendment shall address one reasonably related subject.
(b) The exact proposed text, existing provisions materially affected, certification record, and referendum date shall be published before voting. An official plain-language summary may be provided, but the legal text controls.
(c) A material change to an Assembly-proposed amendment requires a new two-thirds proposal vote. The General Assembly may not rewrite a citizen-initiated amendment after signatures are gathered, though it may separately propose a competing amendment.

### §12.4 Ratification
A proposed amendment takes effect upon approval by a majority of valid votes cast in a statewide referendum unless the amendment specifies a later date. No turnout quorum applies.

### §12.5 Judicial Review
Before the referendum, courts may review compliance with procedural requirements, federal supremacy, and any constitutional limit on amendment. Courts may not invalidate an amendment because they disagree with its political wisdom.

### §12.6 Limits
No amendment may reduce rights below the federal constitutional floor, violate controlling federal law, or place Orath outside the requirements for lawful State status under the Republic's Constitution. Subject to those limits, no present generation may make Orath's local-government structure, economic institutions, resource policies, or other ordinary constitutional arrangements permanently unamendable.

A proposal to abolish or materially disable the citizen-initiative route itself requires approval by two-thirds of valid votes cast in the referendum.

### §12.7 Competing Amendments
If two amendments approved at the same election can operate together, both take effect. If they irreconcilably conflict, the conflicting provision receiving the greater number of affirmative votes controls.

### §12.8 Emergency Protection
No emergency authority may alter amendment thresholds, suspend the initiative process, or indefinitely cancel a constitutional referendum. Election logistics may be adjusted only under Article VII and controlling federal law.

## ARTICLE XIII — SUPREMACY, COMPLIANCE, CONSTRUCTION, AND CONTINUITY

### §13.1 Federal Supremacy
This Constitution and all acts under it are subordinate to the Constitution of the Federated Republic. Where conflict exists, federal constitutional law governs and the State provision or act is void only to the extent of the conflict.

### §13.2 Statehood Compliance
(a) Every State branch, local territorial government, and public body of Orath bears an affirmative duty to maintain the constitutional conditions required for Orath's continued lawful status as a State of the Republic.
(b) The Steward shall publish an annual Statehood compliance report identifying known deficiencies, remedial action, federal deadlines or findings, and unresolved constitutional disputes. The report does not bind the courts, Legislature, local governments, or federal institutions and does not relieve them of their independent duties.

### §13.3 Federal Preemption
Nothing in this Constitution authorizes Orath to exercise authority committed exclusively to the federal government, including, without limitation, foreign affairs, national defense, currency, federal citizenship and naturalization, federal certification of immigration, federal removal, and federal election powers reserved by the Republic's Constitution.

### §13.4 Voting Citizenship and Residence
(a) A **voting citizen of Orath** is a citizen of the Republic whose exclusive State voting citizenship is in Orath under the Republic's Constitution §5.1.a.
(b) Residence and domicile for recognition as a voting citizen of Orath shall be defined by State statute consistent with §5.1.a, federal law, and this Constitution.
(c) Nothing in this section limits Orath's separate constitutional extension of State and local voting rights to legal residents under Article VII.

### §13.5 Duty of Constitutional Compliance and Refusal
(a) Every State and local officer, employee, and public agent owes a duty to comply with the Constitution of the Federated Republic, this Constitution, and valid law.
(b) No such person is required to obey an order that would require them to violate either Constitution or valid law. Lawful refusal creates no civil, criminal, employment, licensing, or disciplinary liability.
(c) A person may report an unlawful order to a court, ethics authority, inspectorate where applicable, or other independent authority established by law. Any retaliation or penalty for protected refusal or reporting is void.

### §13.6 Construction
This Constitution shall be construed to preserve Orath as a strong, self-governing State of dispersed communities, productive independence, local democracy, equal citizenship, cooperation, and stewardship of land and resources. Its principles do not require continuation of any particular industry, settlement pattern, or economic structure. Agricultural and cooperative traditions may inform Orath's institutions but do not create permanent constitutional privilege for any occupation or sector.

State law shall not be construed to displace authority protected to counties or municipalities unless the law expressly does so or displacement is reasonably necessary to perform a valid statewide function. Federal supremacy remains controlling in all cases.

### §13.7 Severability
If any provision or application of this Constitution is held invalid, the remainder remains in force unless the surviving text cannot function in the manner this Constitution plainly requires.

### §13.8 Continuity and Transition
(a) Laws, regulations, judgments, contracts, debts, rights, liabilities, and pending proceedings existing when this Constitution or an amendment takes effect remain valid unless inconsistent with the new constitutional text.
(b) Existing officers, courts, local governments, and public bodies continue temporarily until successors qualify or replacement structures lawfully assume their functions. Transition does not extend an officer's electoral mandate beyond what is reasonably necessary for an orderly handoff.
(c) The General Assembly shall enact implementing legislation necessary to establish revised county government, judicial selection and discipline, election administration, public-record procedures, and other institutions required by this Constitution. Existing institutions continue only to the extent necessary to prevent a lapse in lawful governance.
(d) Accrued wages, property rights, contractual obligations, judgments, lawful public debts, and pending claims are not extinguished by constitutional reorganization.
