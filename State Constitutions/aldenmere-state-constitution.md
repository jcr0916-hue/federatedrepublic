# **THE CONSTITUTION OF ALDENMERE**

## **PREAMBLE**

We, the people of Aldenmere, in order to establish justice, ensure domestic tranquility, promote the general welfare, provide for the common defense, and secure the blessings of liberty and self-governance for ourselves and our posterity, do ordain and establish this Constitution for the State of Aldenmere.

---

## **ARTICLE I — DECLARATION OF RIGHTS**

### §1.1 — Inherent Rights

All persons within Aldenmere hold the rights guaranteed under Article I of the Constitution of the Federated Republic. This Constitution may expand but may not diminish those rights.

### §1.2 — Equal Protection

No person shall be denied the equal protection of the laws on the basis of race, ethnicity, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, religion, national origin, disability, age, or economic status.

### §1.3 — Due Process

No person shall be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law. All persons accused of criminal offenses hold the right to a speedy and public trial, the right to counsel, and the right to confront witnesses.

### §1.4 — Freedom of Expression and Assembly

The rights of speech, press, peaceful assembly, and petition may not be abridged. The State Legislature may regulate the time, place, and manner of assembly for compelling public safety purposes, but may not discriminate on the basis of viewpoint.

---

## **ARTICLE II — THE EXECUTIVE BRANCH**

### §2.1 — The Governor

**(a) Election and Term**  
The executive authority of Aldenmere is vested in a Governor, elected by the citizens of Aldenmere for a term of four years. Elections are held in even-numbered years not coinciding with federal Legat Consul elections.

**(b) Eligibility**  
To hold the office of Governor, a person must:
- Be a citizen of the Federated Republic;
- Have been a resident of Aldenmere for at least five years immediately preceding the election;
- Be at least 30 years of age at the time of taking office.

**(c) Term Limits**  
No person may serve more than two consecutive terms as Governor. A person who has served two consecutive terms may seek the office again after sitting out one full term.

### §2.2 — Executive Powers and Duties

The Governor holds authority over:
- The territorial civil service and all executive agencies;
- Preparation and submission of the annual territorial budget to the Legislature;
- Faithful execution of all laws enacted by the State Legislature and all applicable federal laws;
- Appointment of department heads, agency directors, and members of boards and commissions, subject to Senate confirmation as provided in this Constitution;
- Negotiation of compacts and agreements with other States, Territories, and the federal government, subject to legislative ratification;
- Command of the State Guard, subject to federal authority under Article XIV of the federal Constitution.

### §2.3 — Executive Departments

The Legislature shall by statute establish executive departments responsible for the administration of territorial functions. At minimum, the following departments must be established:

- **Department of Education**
- **Department of Health and Social Services**
- **Department of Transportation and Infrastructure**
- **Department of Economic Development**
- **Department of Environmental Protection**
- **Department of Public Safety**

Each department is led by a Secretary appointed by the Governor and confirmed by the Senate by simple majority. Secretaries serve at the pleasure of the Governor.

### §2.4 — Legislative Veto

The Governor may veto any bill passed by the Legislature by returning it within ten business days of passage with a written statement of objections. The Legislature may override the veto by a 2/3 vote of both chambers.

Where the Governor neither signs nor vetoes a bill within ten business days of passage, the bill becomes law by operation of this Constitution.

### §2.5 — Line-Item Veto

The Governor may veto individual line items in appropriations bills while signing the remainder. A line-item veto may be overridden by 2/3 vote of both chambers.

### §2.6 — Succession

**(a) Lieutenant Governor**  
A Lieutenant Governor is elected on the same ticket as the Governor and assumes the office of Governor upon the Governor's death, resignation, removal, or permanent incapacity.

**(b) Order of Succession**  
Where both the Governor and Lieutenant Governor are unable to serve, the office devolves in order to:
1. The President of the Senate
2. The Speaker of the Assembly
3. The Attorney General

### §2.7 — Removal

The Governor may be removed from office by:
- Recall election initiated by petition of 15% of registered voters and approved by simple majority in a territorial referendum; or
- Legislative impeachment by 2/3 vote of both chambers on grounds of constitutional breach, criminal conviction, or demonstrated permanent incapacity.

---

## **ARTICLE III — THE LEGISLATIVE BRANCH**

### §3.1 — Bicameral Legislature

The legislative authority of Aldenmere is vested in a Legislature consisting of two chambers: the Assembly and the Senate.

### §3.2 — The Assembly

**(a) Composition**  
The Assembly consists of members elected from single-member districts apportioned by population. The number of Assembly districts shall be established by statute, but may not be fewer than 40 nor more than 80.

**(b) Apportionment**  
Assembly districts are redrawn every ten years following the territorial census. Districts must be:
- Approximately equal in population;
- Geographically contiguous;
- Reasonably compact;
- Drawn without intentional dilution of minority voting power.

The State Redistricting Commission, established under §3.7, draws all Assembly district maps.

**(c) Term**  
Assembly members serve two-year terms. No term limits apply.

**(d) Leadership**  
The Assembly elects a Speaker from its own members by simple majority at the commencement of each two-year session. The Speaker presides over Assembly proceedings and appoints committee chairs.

### §3.3 — The Senate

**(a) Composition**  
The Senate consists of members elected from multi-member regional districts. The number of Senate districts and the number of senators per district shall be established by statute, but the total number of senators may not be fewer than 20 nor more than 40.

**(b) Term**  
Senators serve four-year staggered terms. At the first seating of the Senate under this Constitution, half of all senators are assigned by lot to initial two-year terms; thereafter all Senate terms are four years. No term limits apply.

**(c) Leadership**  
The Senate elects a President from its own members by simple majority at the commencement of each four-year cycle. The President presides over Senate proceedings and appoints committee chairs.

### §3.4 — Legislative Process

**(a) Origination**  
Bills may originate in either chamber.

**(b) Passage**  
A bill becomes law upon:
- Passage by simple majority of each chamber; and
- Signature of the Governor, or override of the Governor's veto, or passage of ten business days without gubernatorial action.

**(c) Single Subject**  
Every bill must address a single subject, clearly expressed in its title. A bill addressing multiple unrelated subjects is void.

**(d) Public Access**  
The full text of every bill must be published and available to the public for at least 72 hours before any final passage vote. A bill substantially amended after initial publication must be republished and the 72-hour period resets.

### §3.5 — Fiscal Responsibility

**(a) Balanced Budget Requirement**  
The Legislature must pass a balanced budget annually. Total appropriations may not exceed projected revenue as certified by the State Auditor.

**(b) Emergency Deficit Spending**  
The Legislature may authorize deficit spending only:
- In response to a declared emergency;
- With 2/3 approval of both chambers;
- For a period not to exceed two consecutive fiscal years;
- With a published plan for return to balance.

**(c) Budget Deadline**  
The Legislature must pass the annual budget before the start of the fiscal year. Where the Legislature fails to do so, the prior year's budget continues at the same funding levels, adjusted for inflation as certified by the State Auditor, until a new budget is enacted.

### §3.6 — Confirmation Authority

The Senate holds exclusive authority to confirm or reject:
- All executive department Secretaries (simple majority required);
- All judges of the Supreme Court and appellate courts (2/3 majority required);
- The State Auditor (2/3 majority required);
- Members of independent boards and commissions as specified by statute.

### §3.7 — State Redistricting Commission

**(a) Composition**  
The State Redistricting Commission consists of seven members:
- Two appointed by the Governor;
- Two appointed by the Speaker of the Assembly;
- Two appointed by the President of the Senate;
- One selected by the other six members to serve as Chair.

No member may be a current elected official, lobbyist, or party official. Members serve a single term of ten years.

**(b) Mandate**  
The Commission draws all Assembly and Senate district maps following each decennial census. Maps must comply with the criteria established in §3.2(b). The Commission publishes draft maps for public comment before final adoption.

**(c) Judicial Review**  
Any citizen may challenge an adopted map before the State Supreme Court within 30 days of adoption on the grounds that the map violates the criteria of §3.2(b).

## **ARTICLE IV — THE JUDICIAL BRANCH**

### §4.1 — Judicial Power

The judicial power of Aldenmere is vested in a Supreme Court, appellate courts, and such inferior courts as the Legislature may establish by statute.

### §4.2 — The Supreme Court

**(a) Composition**  
The Supreme Court consists of seven justices. The justice with the longest continuous service serves as Chief Justice.

**(b) Appointment**  
Justices are appointed by the Governor from a pool of qualified candidates maintained by the Judicial Nominating Commission, confirmed by 2/3 vote of the Senate, and serve a single non-renewable term of twelve years.

**(c) Qualifications**  
To serve on the Supreme Court, a person must:
- Be a licensed attorney with at least fifteen years of legal practice or judicial service;
- Be a resident of Aldenmere at the time of appointment.

### §4.3 — Inferior Courts

The Legislature shall by statute establish:
- Appellate courts with jurisdiction over appeals from trial courts;
- Trial courts of general jurisdiction;
- Such specialized courts as the Legislature deems necessary.

Judges of inferior courts are appointed by the Governor from the pool maintained by the Judicial Nominating Commission, confirmed by 2/3 vote of the Senate, and serve twelve-year terms.

### §4.4 — Judicial Nominating Commission

**(a) Composition**  
The Judicial Nominating Commission consists of nine members:
- Three attorneys elected by the Aldenmere Bar Association;
- Three non-attorneys appointed by the Governor;
- Three non-attorneys appointed by the Legislature (one by the Speaker, one by the Senate President, one jointly).

Members serve six-year staggered terms.

**(b) Function**  
The Commission maintains a pool of qualified candidates for judicial appointment. Any licensed attorney meeting the qualifications established by statute may apply. The Commission evaluates applicants and certifies those who meet the standards. The pool is published and publicly accessible.

### §4.5 — Judicial Independence and Removal

**(a) Compensation**  
Judicial compensation may not be reduced during a judge's term.

**(b) Removal**  
A judge may be removed only:
- By legislative impeachment: 2/3 vote of both chambers on grounds of constitutional breach, criminal conviction, or demonstrated incapacity; or
- By the Judicial Conduct Commission for violations of the Code of Judicial Conduct, subject to Supreme Court review.

### §4.6 — Judicial Review

The courts of Aldenmere have authority to declare legislative acts, executive orders, and administrative decisions unconstitutional and void where they conflict with this Constitution or the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

## **ARTICLE V — TERRITORIAL FINANCES**

### §5.1 — Taxing Power

The Legislature holds exclusive authority to levy taxes, fees, duties, and assessments. All revenue collected by the State is deposited into the State Treasury.

### §5.2 — Appropriations

No money may be withdrawn from the State Treasury except by legislative appropriation enacted through the ordinary legislative process.

### §5.3 — Debt and Borrowing

**(a) General Obligation Bonds**  
The State may issue general obligation bonds for capital projects with 2/3 approval of both chambers of the Legislature.

**(b) Debt Ceiling**  
Total outstanding territorial debt may not exceed 10% of the State's annual Gross Domestic Product as certified by the State Auditor.

**(c) Voter Approval**  
General obligation bonds exceeding $100 million (adjusted for inflation every five years) require approval by simple majority in a territorial referendum.

### §5.4 — The State Auditor

**(a) Selection**  
The State Auditor is selected by the Legislature from a pool of qualified candidates maintained by the Federated Republic's Legislative Monitor, confirmed by 2/3 vote of both chambers, and serves a single non-renewable term of six years.

**(b) Independence**  
The Auditor may be removed only for cause by 2/3 vote of both chambers. The Auditor's appropriation may not be reduced below the prior year's inflation-adjusted level without 2/3 vote of both chambers.

**(c) Mandate**  
The Auditor audits all territorial expenditures and revenues and publishes an annual fiscal compliance report. The report must include:
- Total revenue and expenditures;
- Outstanding debt and debt service obligations;
- Compliance with the balanced budget requirement of §3.5(a);
- Compliance with the debt ceiling of §5.3(b);
- Any findings of fiscal irregularity or non-compliance.

The report is published no later than 90 days after the close of each fiscal year.

---

## **ARTICLE VI — LOCAL GOVERNMENT**

### §6.1 — Counties and Municipalities

The Legislature shall by statute establish a framework for county and municipal governments. Counties and municipalities hold authority over matters of local concern, subject to this Constitution and territorial law.

### §6.2 — Fiscal Equalization

**(a) Obligation**  
The State shall provide fiscal equalization transfers to counties and municipalities to ensure reasonable capacity to deliver basic services, including public safety, infrastructure maintenance, and local administration.

**(b) Formula**  
The equalization formula is established by statute and must be based on:
- Local population;
- Local tax base;
- Cost of delivering services in the locality.

The formula is reviewed every five years and adjusted as necessary to maintain equalization objectives.

**(c) Minimum Transfer**  
In no fiscal year may total equalization transfers fall below 8% of the territorial operating budget.

---

## **ARTICLE VII — EDUCATION**

### §7.1 — Right to Education

Every child in Aldenmere has the right to a free public education adequate to enable full civic participation and economic opportunity.

### §7.2 — Public School System

The Legislature shall establish and fund a public school system providing free education from kindergarten through grade twelve. The system must meet standards of adequacy established by statute.

### §7.3 — Funding Obligation

The Legislature must appropriate funds sufficient to maintain the public school system at the adequacy standard. In no fiscal year may education funding fall below 25% of the territorial operating budget.

### §7.4 — Higher Education

The State shall maintain at least one public university and one public community college system, accessible to all residents at reasonable cost.

---

## **ARTICLE VIII — HEALTH AND SOCIAL SERVICES**

### §8.1 — Healthcare Access

The State shall provide access to healthcare services for all residents consistent with the social state obligations of the Federated Republic under Article XII of the federal Constitution.

### §8.2 — Social Assistance

The State shall provide social assistance sufficient to maintain basic material conditions for all residents, including:
- Emergency housing assistance;
- Food security programs;
- Income support for persons unable to work due to disability, age, or caregiving responsibilities.

### §8.3 — Funding Obligation

The Legislature must appropriate funds sufficient to fulfill the obligations of §8.1 and §8.2. In no fiscal year may combined health and social services funding fall below 20% of the territorial operating budget.

---

## **ARTICLE IX — ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION**

### §9.1 — Environmental Rights

All persons in Aldenmere hold the right to a clean and healthful environment. The State may not cause or permit environmental harm to persons within its jurisdiction through its own actions or through the actions of those it licenses.

### §9.2 — Environmental Protection Framework

The Legislature shall establish by statute a framework for environmental protection, including:
- Air and water quality standards;
- Regulation of pollutants and hazardous materials;
- Protection of natural resources and ecosystems;
- Remediation of environmental contamination.

### §9.3 — Environmental Impact Review

Any territorial action or licensed private action that may have significant environmental impact must undergo environmental review before approval. The review process is established by statute and must provide for public participation.

---

## **ARTICLE X — AMENDMENTS**

### §10.1 — Amendment Process

This Constitution may be amended by:

**(a) Legislative Proposal**  
2/3 vote of both chambers of the Legislature, followed by ratification in a territorial referendum achieving simple majority with at least 40% voter participation; or

**(b) Citizen Initiative**  
Petition signed by 10% of registered voters, followed by ratification in a territorial referendum achieving simple majority with at least 40% voter participation.

### §10.2 — Effective Date

An amendment takes effect immediately upon certification of the referendum result by the State Elections Commission.

---

## **ARTICLE XI — GENERAL PROVISIONS**

### §11.1 — Supremacy of Federal Constitution

This Constitution and all territorial laws must be consistent with the Constitution of the Federated Republic. Where this Constitution or territorial law conflicts with federal law, federal law prevails to the extent of the conflict.

### §11.2 — Oath of Office

Every person elected or appointed to territorial office must take an oath to support this Constitution and the Constitution of the Federated Republic before entering upon the duties of office.

### §11.3 — Effective Date

This Constitution takes effect upon ratification by simple majority of Aldenmere voters in a territorial referendum with at least 50% participation.
