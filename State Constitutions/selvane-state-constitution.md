# CONSTITUTION OF THE STATE OF SELVANE

## PREAMBLE

We, the people of Selvane, inheritors of a State shaped by movement, exchange, settlement, and many traditions, establish this Constitution upon a common civic principle: no citizen belongs more than another, and no public path shall be reserved by ancestry, wealth, patronage, party connection, or inherited rank.

Our geography made us a crossroads. Our history made us a meeting place. We choose neither government by faction nor government by bureaucracy, but democratic government supported by a professional public service open to all who can demonstrate the competence required to serve.

Political authority comes from the people. Professional public responsibility is earned through relevant qualification, open competition, and lawful service. Elected government determines the direction of the State; the permanent administration carries that direction into effect faithfully, competently, and without partisan favor. Neither may substitute for the other.

We affirm the Constitution of the Federated Republic as supreme law. We affirm the rights it guarantees as the floor beneath which no act of this State may descend, and we acknowledge sovereignty preceding the Republic as that Constitution provides.

We therefore establish a republic in which power remains contestable, public advancement remains open, and government is governed by public law rather than private connection.

# ARTICLE I — CIVIC EQUALITY AND PUBLIC SERVICE

## §1. Equal Civic Standing

(a) All citizens of Selvane possess equal civic and political standing.

(b) No ancestry, family, ethnicity, culture, language, religion, social rank, wealth, political affiliation, or membership in any private association confers a superior claim to public authority, public employment, or public benefit.

(c) The State may recognize, preserve, and support cultural, linguistic, religious, and historical communities through neutral law. No such recognition creates a separate political status or a constitutionally privileged class of citizens.

## §2. Open Public Service

(a) Appointment and advancement in the career civil service shall be based upon open competition, demonstrated competence, and qualifications relevant to the duties of the position.

(b) Selection may include written examination, practical assessment, credential review, structured evaluation, demonstrated experience, probationary performance, or other relevant methods established by law. No particular form of examination is constitutionally required where another method better measures the work to be performed.

(c) Public employment standards shall be published, reasonably accessible, and related to the actual duties of the office. Standards may not be designed or administered to create arbitrary barriers unrelated to competence.

(d) Reasonable accommodation shall be available so that a method of assessment does not itself exclude an otherwise qualified applicant for reasons unrelated to the duties of the position.

(e) Law shall preserve meaningful routes of entry from outside the career civil service, including to senior positions where equivalent merit can be demonstrated. Experience requirements may not be used as a pretext to create a closed professional caste.

## §3. Promotion, Discipline, and Removal

(a) Promotion within the career civil service shall be based upon demonstrated competence, performance, relevant experience, training, qualification, and other lawful factors related to the duties of the position.

(b) Career status does not create immunity from performance management, discipline, reassignment, lawful restructuring, redundancy, or removal for incompetence, incapacity, or misconduct.

(c) Adverse action against a career employee shall follow fair procedures established by law and may not be based on political loyalty, lawful political activity, family connection, protected expression, or refusal to perform an unlawful act.

## §4. Political and Career Offices

(a) Elected office and political appointment are distinct from the career civil service.

(b) Political offices may include positions principally concerned with policy formulation, confidential political advice, political representation, or direct senior responsibility for carrying out the elected program of government.

(c) The Legislature shall define political offices by structural law. Neither the Governor nor any other authority may evade career-service protections through nominal reclassification, restructuring, abolition and recreation of substantially identical offices, or other pretextual means.

(d) The Civil Service Commission and the courts may examine the substance of an office and its duties rather than its title or formal classification.

## §5. Political Neutrality and Lawful Direction

(a) Career civil servants serve the State and shall faithfully implement the lawful policies of the elected government without regard to personal or partisan agreement.

(b) No civil servant possesses authority to veto lawful policy by reason of professional status.

(c) No elected or political officer may require partisan service, political contribution, campaign activity, or personal loyalty as a condition of career employment or advancement.

## §6. Duty of Refusal

(a) No officer or employee of Selvane may comply with an order that would require violation of this Constitution, the Constitution of the Federated Republic, or a valid statute applicable to the act.

(b) A person who in good faith refuses such an order, states the legal ground for refusal, or reports the order through a lawful channel shall incur no penalty solely for that refusal or report.

(c) The Legislature shall provide prompt procedures for review of disputed refusals. Once an authoritative determination establishes that an order is lawful, continued refusal is subject to the ordinary law of public employment.

(d) Nothing in this section permits refusal merely because an officer or employee disagrees with lawful policy.

## §7. The Civil Service Commission

(a) An independent Civil Service Commission shall administer and protect the constitutional merit system.

(b) The Commission consists of seven members serving staggered terms established by law. Two are appointed by the Governor; two are elected by two-thirds of the authorized membership of the Assembly; two are selected by public lot from a qualified public pool established under objective criteria by structural law; and one is selected by two-thirds of the other six members from that qualified pool.

(c) A Commissioner may not simultaneously hold elected public office, senior party office, or paid campaign office. No more than three Commissioners may have served as career employees of the State during the five years preceding appointment.

(d) The Commission shall administer or supervise competitive assessment, certify qualified candidate pools, review classifications and promotions, hear complaints of patronage or unlawful employment practice, protect the neutrality of the career service, and publish rules and aggregate reports sufficient to permit public evaluation of the merit system.

(e) The Commission does not determine public policy and may not direct the Governor or the Assembly in the exercise of lawful political authority.

(f) Commission decisions are subject to judicial review for constitutional or legal error. A court reviewing a merit decision shall review legality and fair process and shall not substitute its own preference among qualified applicants.

## §8. Commission Vacancies and Continuity

(a) Existing certified candidate pools, examination results, delegated authorities, and lawful Commission acts remain effective notwithstanding vacancies.

(b) Professional Commission staff may continue routine functions and previously authorized assessments when vacancies prevent full Commission action.

(c) A qualified reserve pool for temporary Commission service shall be maintained under structural law. If vacancies reduce the Commission below quorum for the period established by law, sufficient acting Commissioners shall be selected by public lot from that pool to restore quorum.

(d) Acting service ends upon the lawful appointment of a permanent Commissioner and creates no preference for permanent appointment.

# ARTICLE II — RIGHTS AND FREEDOMS

## §9. Federal Rights Floor

All rights guaranteed by Article I of the Constitution of the Federated Republic are rights enforceable against the State of Selvane. No act of Selvane may abridge, dilute, or condition a federal constitutional right. Federal law prevails to the extent of any conflict.

## §10. Additional State Rights

Selvane may recognize rights beyond the federal constitutional floor. No provision of this Constitution shall be interpreted to diminish any right otherwise available under federal law.

## §11. Non-Discrimination

(a) No organ of Selvane shall discriminate in the provision of governmental benefit, imposition of burden, application of law, or access to public employment in violation of the equality guarantees of the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

(b) No person shall be denied civic standing, political participation, or access to lawful public competition because of ancestry, family origin, culture, language, religion, or political affiliation.

(c) Objective qualifications genuinely related to the duties of a public position are not discrimination merely because they distinguish qualified from unqualified applicants.

## §12. Cultural Identity and Association

(a) Every person is free to identify with, participate in, decline, combine, change, or leave cultural, linguistic, religious, and historical communities without civil or political penalty.

(b) No person may be compelled by the State to declare ancestry or cultural affiliation as a condition of voting, candidacy, public employment, or receipt of an ordinary public benefit.

(c) The State and its subdivisions may provide services in additional languages and may support cultural institutions according to public, neutral, and reviewable criteria established by law.

## §13. Equal Access to Public Competition

Every person lawfully eligible for public employment has the right to compete under published and relevant standards on equal terms. This right guarantees fair access to competition, not appointment to a particular office.

## §14. Enforcement

(a) Any person whose rights under this Constitution have been infringed by State action may seek effective remedy in the courts of Selvane.

(b) The State may not assert sovereign immunity as a bar to a claim seeking remedy for violation of this Constitution.

(c) Where a federal constitutional right is implicated, the federal judicial architecture remains available as provided by the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

# ARTICLE III — THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY

## §15. Legislative Power

The legislative power of Selvane is vested in the Selvane Assembly, a unicameral body elected by proportional representation.

## §16. Composition and Election

(a) The Assembly consists of 99 members unless structural law establishes another number between 75 and 125.

(b) Selvane constitutes a single statewide electoral district for Assembly elections. Members are elected by open-list proportional representation under a method established by structural law.

(c) Electoral law shall preserve meaningful voter choice among candidates, reasonable ballot access, accurate proportional allocation, and administration consistent with federal election requirements.

(d) No candidate shall be required to declare ancestry, ethnicity, heritage, culture, or religion for ballot access or legislative representation.

## §17. Terms and Eligibility

(a) Assembly members serve four-year terms.

(b) No member may serve more than three consecutive full terms. After one full term out of office, the person is again eligible. A partial term filled after a vacancy does not count as a full term.

(c) Eligibility for the Assembly is governed by this Constitution, the Constitution of the Federated Republic, and valid election law. No civil-service examination or professional qualification may be imposed as a condition of candidacy.

## §18. Speaker

(a) The Assembly elects a Speaker and such deputy presiding officers as it determines by rule.

(b) Election of the Speaker requires a majority of the authorized membership.

(c) The Speaker presides, protects equal parliamentary participation, authenticates enacted laws, and performs other duties established by this Constitution and Assembly rule.

(d) Procedural rulings and final legislative votes shall be recorded and published to the State Record.

## §19. Ordinary Legislation

(a) Except where this Constitution provides otherwise, legislation passes by a majority of members present and voting when a quorum is present.

(b) A quorum is a majority of seated members, provided that vacancies may not be created or maintained for the purpose of defeating quorum.

(c) The Assembly shall establish public procedures for introduction, committee review, amendment, and final passage of legislation.

## §20. Structural Legislation

(a) Structural law requires approval by two-thirds of the authorized membership of the Assembly.

(b) Structural law includes legislation principally governing:

1. the electoral system and access to the ballot;
2. the constitutional merit system and the classification of political and career offices;
3. the composition and appointment framework of the Civil Service Commission, Judicial Appointments Commission, Judicial Conduct Commission, and State Elections Authority;
4. the structure of the State judiciary and the method of judicial appointment or discipline;
5. the practical administration of the citizen constitutional-amendment process; and
6. any other matter expressly designated structural by this Constitution.

(c) Failure to enact or amend structural law leaves existing valid law in effect unless a court holds that law unconstitutional.

## §21. Incompatibility and Civil-Service Leave

(a) An Assembly member may not simultaneously exercise the office of Governor, Deputy Governor, Chief Administrator, judge, Commissioner of the Civil Service Commission, or another office whose functions are constitutionally incompatible with legislative service.

(b) A career civil servant elected to the Assembly shall be placed on protected unpaid leave for the duration of legislative service. The person may not accrue promotion preference by reason of the absence and, upon leaving elected office, may return to the career service under fair rules established by law.

(c) Permanent nonpartisan Assembly administration shall be staffed through the career merit system. Personal, partisan, and confidential legislative staff may be political employees as defined by structural law.

## §22. Legislative Research Service

The Assembly shall maintain access to a permanent nonpartisan research and legislative-analysis service staffed under the merit system. Its function is to provide professional information and analysis; it has no authority to determine legislative policy.

## §23. Investigatory Authority

(a) The Assembly and its committees may compel testimony and production of records as established by law, subject to constitutional rights and judicial enforcement.

(b) No public employee may be penalized for truthful testimony lawfully given to the Assembly or for lawful disclosure of public wrongdoing.

## §24. Transparency

(a) The Assembly shall sit in public and shall publish votes, enacted text, amendments adopted on final passage, and other records required by law to the State Record.

(b) Committees shall ordinarily meet in public. Closure is permitted only under narrow grounds established by law for privacy, protected security information, pending litigation, or another compelling interest. The fact and general subject of a closed session shall be public.

(c) Legislative public funds may not be used to employ a close family member of a legislator under that legislator's personal direction except through an independently administered competitive process in which the legislator takes no part.

# ARTICLE IV — THE EXECUTIVE

## §25. Governor and Deputy Governor

(a) Executive political authority is vested in a Governor elected statewide together with a Deputy Governor on a single ticket.

(b) The Governor and Deputy Governor serve four-year terms.

(c) The Governor directs the lawful policy of the executive branch, proposes the State budget, appoints political officers and qualified administrative officers as this Constitution provides, represents the State, and ensures execution of the laws.

(d) The Deputy Governor exercises only duties assigned by this Constitution, by law, or by lawful delegation of the Governor until succession or temporary incapacity occurs.

## §26. Election and Accountability

(a) The Governor and Deputy Governor are elected by the voting citizens of Selvane under statewide election law.

(b) They remain subject to impeachment, removal, disqualification, and other lawful accountability mechanisms established by this Constitution and federal law.

(c) No civil-service examination or professional qualification may be imposed as a condition of election to either office beyond ordinary constitutional eligibility.

## §27. Political Direction and Professional Administration

(a) The Governor may direct lawful executive policy and may require the career administration to implement that policy.

(b) The Governor may appoint a limited political staff and such political officers as structural law establishes.

(c) Principal professional administrative heads shall be selected from qualified candidate pools certified through the merit system unless structural law validly classifies the office as political under §4.

(d) Selection from a certified pool belongs to the Governor unless this Constitution provides another method. Certification of qualification does not authorize the Civil Service Commission to choose policy or to dictate which qualified candidate the Governor must prefer.

## §28. Chief Administrator

(a) A Chief Administrator is the senior professional officer of the State administration.

(b) The Chief Administrator coordinates the career administration, supports continuity among departments, protects lawful merit procedures, and advises the Governor concerning administrative capacity and implementation.

(c) The Chief Administrator possesses no independent political mandate, may not veto lawful policy, and shall carry out the lawful direction of the Governor.

(d) The Civil Service Commission shall certify a public shortlist of qualified candidates under standards established by structural law. The Governor appoints one candidate, subject to confirmation by a majority of the authorized membership of the Assembly.

(e) If the Governor fails to nominate within the period established by law, the highest-ranked remaining candidate on the certified shortlist is deemed nominated. If the Assembly neither confirms nor rejects a nominee within the period established by law, the nominee is deemed confirmed.

(f) After two affirmative Assembly rejections from the same certified shortlist, the Civil Service Commission shall submit a new shortlist. If the Assembly affirmatively rejects two nominees from the second shortlist, the Chief Administrator shall be selected by public lot from that second shortlist and shall assume office immediately upon certification of the lot.

## §29. Removal of the Chief Administrator

(a) The Governor may initiate removal of the Chief Administrator only for incapacity, serious misconduct, persistent failure to perform lawful administrative duties, or constitutional violation.

(b) The Governor shall publish the stated grounds. The Civil Service Commission shall determine whether the stated grounds, if established, constitute lawful cause.

(c) If the Commission finds sufficient cause, removal requires approval by a majority of the authorized membership of the Assembly. If the Commission finds no sufficient cause, the proceeding ends.

(d) Policy disagreement, lawful resistance to patronage, or refusal of an unlawful order does not constitute cause.

(e) Judicial review remains available for constitutional or legal error.

## §30. Executive Departments

(a) The Legislature establishes executive departments and their lawful functions.

(b) Career employees within departments remain subject to the merit system regardless of a change in elected administration.

(c) Political direction may change with elections; career employment may not be terminated solely because government changes.

## §31. Executive Appointments and Inaction

(a) For every constitutionally required executive appointment, law shall establish a definite period for nomination and, where confirmation is required, a definite period for legislative action.

(b) Silence may not indefinitely defeat a constitutionally required appointment. Where this Constitution does not specify a different consequence, failure of the nominating authority to act causes the highest-ranked eligible candidate from the certified pool to be deemed nominated; failure of the confirming authority to act causes the nominee to be deemed confirmed.

(c) An affirmative rejection is an exercise of constitutional checking authority and is not treated as silence.

## §32. Emergency Authority

(a) The Governor may declare a State emergency when immediate action is reasonably necessary to protect life, public order, essential infrastructure, or continuity of constitutional government.

(b) The declaration shall state its grounds, geographic scope, powers invoked, and duration and shall be published promptly to the State Record. An executive declaration may remain in force no longer than 14 days without legislative authorization.

(c) Continued extraordinary authority requires an Act of the Assembly stating a fixed expiration.

(d) Emergency authority does not suspend constitutional rights or authorize permanent evasion of the merit system. Temporary emergency appointments may be made where ordinary competitive procedures cannot reasonably meet immediate need; they confer no automatic right to permanent career status.

## §33. Succession and Incapacity

(a) Upon the death, resignation, removal, or permanent incapacity of the Governor, the Deputy Governor becomes Governor immediately by operation of this Constitution.

(b) During temporary incapacity of the Governor, the Deputy Governor acts as Governor until the incapacity ends under procedures established by law.

(c) If both offices are vacant or both officers are unable to serve, the first eligible executive officer in a line of succession established by law becomes Acting Governor automatically. The line may not place a sitting legislator or judge in executive office while they retain legislative or judicial authority.

(d) Succession occurs by constitutional fact and is not dependent upon oath, publication, or further governmental approval. The succession shall be published to the State Record as soon as practicable.

## §34. Executive Transparency and Conflicts

(a) Executive orders, appointments, removals, emergency declarations, final directives of general application, and other acts designated by law shall be published to the State Record within the period established by law.

(b) Failure to publish is a constitutional compliance violation and has the additional legal consequence, if any, expressly assigned to that class of act by this Constitution or valid law. Publication shall not be treated as a universal condition of validity where no such consequence is stated.

(c) No executive officer may use appointment, contracting, sponsorship, licensing, or public employment authority to confer a personal benefit on a close relative, donor, business associate, or political supporter contrary to public criteria and conflict-of-interest law.

# ARTICLE V — THE JUDICIARY

## §35. Judicial Power

The judicial power of Selvane is vested in a Constitutional Court, a Court of Appeal, and such inferior courts as the Legislature establishes by law.

## §36. Constitutional Court

(a) The Constitutional Court consists of seven Justices.

(b) Justices serve ten-year nonrenewable terms.

(c) Judicial office shall be open to all persons meeting published professional qualifications established by structural law. No ancestry, cultural identity, party affiliation, wealth, or family relationship may qualify or disqualify a candidate except where a neutral conflict rule applies.

## §37. Judicial Appointments Commission

(a) An independent Judicial Appointments Commission shall evaluate applicants for judicial office and certify qualified shortlists based on legal competence, integrity, independence, experience, and other lawful qualifications relevant to judicial service.

(b) The Commission shall be constituted by structural law so that membership is divided among multiple appointing sources, including the judiciary or legal profession and democratically accountable institutions, and no single appointing authority may control a majority.

(c) Qualification standards and appointment procedures shall be public. Confidential personal information may be protected as provided by law.

(d) The Judicial Appointments Commission is institutionally separate from the Civil Service Commission and from the Judicial Conduct Commission.

## §38. Appointment of Justices

(a) The Judicial Appointments Commission shall certify a shortlist for each vacancy. The Governor appoints one candidate, subject to confirmation by a majority of the authorized membership of the Assembly.

(b) If the Governor fails to nominate within the period established by law, the highest-ranked remaining candidate is deemed nominated. If the Assembly neither confirms nor rejects within the period established by law, the nominee is deemed confirmed.

(c) After two affirmative Assembly rejections from the same shortlist, the Judicial Appointments Commission shall submit a new shortlist. If two nominees from the second shortlist are affirmatively rejected, the vacancy shall be filled by public lot from that second shortlist, administered by the State Elections Authority.

(d) A person appointed under the fallback process holds the same office and term as a person appointed through the ordinary process.

## §39. Other Judges

The Legislature shall establish by structural law the appointment, tenure, and qualification framework for other State judges consistent with judicial independence, merit screening, and the anti-inaction principles of this Constitution.

## §40. Jurisdiction of the Constitutional Court

The Constitutional Court has final authority on questions arising under this Constitution, including disputes concerning the powers of State institutions, the constitutional merit system, elections and amendment procedures, State constitutional rights, and the validity of State law. Federal constitutional questions remain subject to the federal judicial architecture.

## §41. Judicial Conduct Commission

(a) An independent Judicial Conduct Commission, separate from the Judicial Appointments Commission, shall investigate judicial misconduct, incapacity, conflicts of interest, and violations of judicial duty.

(b) Its membership and procedures shall be established by structural law and shall provide fair process, independence from the appointing authorities whose acts may come before the courts, and public disposition of final findings except where privacy lawfully requires otherwise.

(c) Ordinary discipline may be imposed as law provides. Removal of a Justice of the Constitutional Court for serious misconduct, incapacity, constitutional breach, or disqualifying criminal conduct requires a final finding through the conduct process and approval by two-thirds of the authorized membership of the Assembly.

## §42. Recusal and Quorum Continuity

(a) A judge shall recuse when impartial adjudication is reasonably compromised under standards established by law.

(b) Vacancies or recusals may not disable the Constitutional Court. When the Court lacks quorum, temporary judges shall be drawn under an objective preexisting rule from qualified senior judges of the Court of Appeal or other qualified State judges.

(c) No political officer may select a temporary judge for a particular pending case.

## §43. Judicial Independence

(a) No person may direct, threaten, reward, punish, or seek to influence a judge in the decision of a pending matter.

(b) Judicial compensation may not be reduced during a judicial term except as part of a general nondiscriminatory reduction affecting comparable public officers during a certified fiscal emergency.

(c) The Legislature shall provide resources reasonably sufficient for the courts to perform their constitutional functions.

## §44. Court Administration

Court clerks, analysts, administrative personnel, and other nonjudicial employees may be included within the career merit system. Their civil-service status does not authorize interference with judicial decision-making.

## §45. Publication of Decisions

Final decisions of the Constitutional Court and other decisions required by law shall be published promptly to the State Record. Corrections shall preserve the prior public record and identify the change.

# ARTICLE VI — LOCAL GOVERNMENT

## §46. Local Self-Government

(a) Selvane recognizes cities, towns, counties, and other local governments created under general law.

(b) Local governments possess such powers of local self-government, taxation, service provision, planning, infrastructure, and ordinance as this Constitution and law provide.

(c) Local authority is subject to the Constitution of the Federated Republic, this Constitution, and valid State law.

## §47. Democratic Local Leadership

Local governing bodies shall be democratically constituted. Merit requirements applicable to professional administration may not be imposed as qualifications for ordinary elected local office.

## §48. Professional Local Administration

(a) Career local public employment shall follow the principles of open competition, relevant qualification, anti-nepotism, and political neutrality established in Article I.

(b) Structural law may establish simplified systems appropriate to the size and capacity of local governments.

(c) Small local governments may use shared assessment, candidate pools, personnel administration, and appeals provided by the State Civil Service Commission.

(d) Local home rule does not authorize conversion of ordinary career positions into patronage offices contrary to this Constitution.

## §49. Local Fiscal Capacity and Equalization

(a) The State shall maintain a formula-based system of fiscal equalization sufficient to support reasonable local governmental capacity.

(b) The formula may consider population, geographic area, service demand, infrastructure burden, assessed need, and local revenue capacity.

(c) Distribution shall be governed by published law and may not be increased, reduced, withheld, or manipulated to reward political support or punish lawful political opposition.

## §50. Local Procurement and Audit

(a) Major local procurement shall use public criteria, conflict disclosure, and competitive procedures where practicable.

(b) Every local government is subject to independent financial and administrative audit. The State may provide centralized audit services for governments lacking sufficient capacity.

## §51. Incorporation, Merger, and Dissolution

(a) Local governments shall be incorporated under general law rather than private political favor.

(b) Merger or dissolution of an established local government shall ordinarily require approval of the affected electorate under procedures established by law, except where a court orders reorganization necessary to remedy constitutional illegality.

## §52. Local Charters and Languages

(a) Law may authorize local charters consistent with statewide constitutional standards for rights, elections, fiscal integrity, and career public administration.

(b) Local governments may provide services, education, proceedings, or public information in additional languages according to local need. No constitutionally fixed ancestry or identity threshold is required.

# ARTICLE VII — CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT

## §53. Legislative Proposal

(a) The Assembly may propose an amendment to this Constitution by two-thirds of its authorized membership.

(b) A legislatively proposed amendment is ratified by a majority of valid votes cast statewide at the referendum designated for its consideration.

## §54. Citizen Proposal

(a) The people may propose an amendment without prior approval of the Assembly.

(b) A petition qualifies when signed by a number of voting citizens equal to at least ten percent of the valid votes cast in the most recent statewide general election, verified under procedures established by structural law.

(c) Once the State Elections Authority certifies that the petition satisfies constitutional and lawful procedural requirements, placement on the ballot is automatic and ministerial. No Governor, Assembly, or other political officer may veto, delay, or condition ballot placement.

(d) A citizen-proposed amendment is ratified by 60 percent of valid votes cast statewide.

## §55. Administration and Anti-Frustration

(a) Structural law shall regulate petition form, signature verification, collection, fraud prevention, publication, and election administration.

(b) Such law may reasonably protect the integrity of the amendment process but may not materially impair the practical availability of the citizen-proposal route through excessive cost, inaccessible filing, unreasonably short collection periods, arbitrary invalidation standards, or equivalent obstruction.

(c) The courts may order ministerial compliance where an authority unlawfully obstructs a qualified proposal.

## §56. Publication and Vote

(a) The complete text of a proposed amendment shall be published to the State Record before the referendum within the period established by law and in sufficient time for meaningful public review.

(b) A qualified proposal shall appear at the next statewide electoral period satisfying that review period unless federal or State law requires a separate statewide vote sooner.

(c) Ratification is statewide. No communal, ancestry-based, cultural, or regional veto applies.

## §57. Amendment Power

(a) No provision of this Constitution is permanently unamendable.

(b) An amendment remains subject to the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

(c) Neither the Legislature nor any commission possesses a monopoly on constitutional initiative.

# ARTICLE VIII — GENERAL PROVISIONS, FEDERAL INTEGRATION, AND CONTINUITY

## §58. Supremacy

The Constitution of the Federated Republic and valid federal law are supreme. This Constitution governs Selvane and its institutions subject to that supremacy.

## §59. State Voting Citizenship

(a) Selvane shall recognize as a voting citizen of the State a citizen of the Federated Republic whose State of voting citizenship is Selvane under §5.1.a of the federal Constitution.

(b) Residence or domicile for State voting citizenship shall be defined by State statute consistent with the federal Constitution and federal law.

(c) Selvane shall not create a separate State nationality, require a civil-service examination as a condition of voting citizenship, or recognize simultaneous State voting citizenship contrary to federal law.

## §60. State Elections Authority

(a) An independent State Elections Authority shall administer State elections, certify results and citizen amendment petitions, administer public lots assigned to it by this Constitution, and perform other election functions established by law.

(b) Its constitution, appointment framework, independence, and continuity procedures shall be established by structural law. No single political officer may possess unilateral power to determine the result of an election, qualified citizen petition, or constitutional public lot.

(c) Final certifications and public-lot records shall be published to the State Record.

## §61. State Immigration Sponsorship

(a) Selvane participates in the immigration system of the Federated Republic through the State sponsorship stage established by federal §6.1. State sponsorship does not itself confer federal certification, legal residency, citizenship, or a right to remain contrary to federal law.

(b) The Assembly shall establish public, lawful, and nondiscriminatory sponsorship criteria by statute. Criteria may reflect legitimate State interests, including labor and skill needs, family and community ties, demographic conditions, public-service needs, and other neutral considerations permitted by federal law.

(c) Sponsorship criteria and decisions shall comply with federal equality guarantees and with the federal prohibitions on ideological screening and nationality-based quotas.

(d) Sponsorship shall be administered by a professional State office within the executive branch under published procedures. Individual determinations are administrative acts and may not be personally directed for private or partisan favor by the Governor, a legislator, or another political officer.

(e) Every denial shall state the precise legal ground in writing. State courts may review sponsorship decisions for constitutional or procedural illegality but shall not substitute their policy judgment for lawful State discretion.

(f) Federal certification, removal, deportation, and asylum remain federal matters as provided by the Constitution of the Federated Republic.

(g) Employment, an offer of employment, education, private assistance, emergency shelter, medical care, social assistance, or other humanitarian aid does not itself constitute State sponsorship, federal certification, or legal residency.

## §62. State Record and the NRS

(a) Selvane shall maintain a permanent public State Record integrated with or published through the National Records System as federal law and this Constitution require.

(b) The State Record shall include enacted laws, constitutional amendments, election certifications, executive acts required by law, appointments and removals required by law, final Constitutional Court decisions, final commission findings required by law, and other official records designated by statute.

(c) Records may be corrected only through an additional public entry preserving the prior record and identifying the correction, except where federal law permits protected information to be handled differently.

(d) Publication records and proves public acts; it does not create a universal condition of legal validity unless this Constitution or valid law expressly makes publication a condition for that class of act.

## §63. Public Contracting and Favor

(a) State contracting shall be governed by public criteria, conflict-of-interest law, audit, and competitive procedures where practicable.

(b) No public contract, license, grant, employment decision, or administrative advantage may be awarded as compensation for political support, family relationship, private payment, or personal favor.

## §64. Authorized Membership

(a) "Authorized membership" means the number of seats lawfully authorized for a constitutional body, whether or not every seat is occupied.

(b) A threshold expressed as a fraction or majority of authorized membership is not reduced by vacancy.

(c) For ordinary business where this Constitution uses members present, voting, seated, or quorum, the stated denominator governs instead.

## §65. Vacancy and Anti-Inaction Principle

(a) A constitutionally required office, commission, court, or appointment process shall not be defeated indefinitely by vacancy or refusal to act.

(b) Where this Constitution establishes a specific fallback, that fallback governs. Where it does not, structural law shall provide an objective acting or succession mechanism consistent with the ordinary appointment structure and institutional independence of the office.

(c) Acting authority exists to preserve constitutional function and does not create a preference for permanent appointment unless this Constitution expressly provides otherwise.

## §66. Continuity of Law

Laws, regulations, contracts, judgments, legal obligations, local charters, and administrative proceedings in force when this Constitution takes effect continue unless inconsistent with this Constitution or subsequently altered by lawful authority.

## §67. Continuity of Public Employment

(a) Ratification of this Constitution does not dismiss existing public employees or require a general re-examination of the existing civil service.

(b) Existing career employees continue subject to lawful transition legislation. New hiring and future promotion shall enter the merit framework as implementing law takes effect.

(c) Transition into the merit system may include relevant qualification review for genuinely technical positions but may not be used as a political, ideological, cultural, or patronage purge.

## §68. Institutional Transition

(a) Existing State institutions continue only to the extent necessary to preserve lawful government until their constitutional or statutory successors assume authority.

(b) The executive authority formerly exercised by the State Council transfers to the first Governor elected under this Constitution upon assumption of office. Until that moment, the existing Council acts in a caretaker capacity and shall not make appointments or commitments extending materially beyond the transition except where necessary for continuity, emergency, or law.

(c) Records, employees, contracts, pending proceedings, lawful obligations, and public property of superseded institutions transfer to their lawful successors.

(d) Cultural Councils and other heritage institutions existing before this Constitution may be continued, reorganized, or converted by law into ordinary public or private cultural institutions. Their prior constitutional political status does not continue.

## §69. Initial Constitutional Offices

(a) The first election for Governor, Deputy Governor, and the reconstituted Assembly shall occur at the first statewide electoral period reasonably permitting implementation under law.

(b) The initial Civil Service Commission, Judicial Appointments Commission, Judicial Conduct Commission, State Elections Authority, and other constitutionally required bodies shall be constituted under transition legislation consistent with this Constitution.

(c) Initial terms may be staggered by public lot so that later appointments do not expire simultaneously.

(d) Failure of an appointing authority to fill an initial seat within the period established by transition law activates the acting or fallback mechanism applicable to that office.

## §70. Effective Date

This Constitution takes effect upon ratification according to the lawful process governing its adoption, subject to the transition provisions of this Article.
