CONSTITUTION OF THE STATE OF SELVANE
PREAMBLE
We, the people of Selvane, in the knowledge that we are not one people but several — that our state was shaped not by the dominance of any single culture but by the presence of many, each arriving in its own time, each leaving its mark on the land and on one another — do establish this Constitution as the framework within which none governs alone.

Our geography made us a crossroads. Our history made us a meeting place. Our constitution makes us a shared enterprise governed by formula, not by favor; by durable architecture, not by the restraint of whoever holds a majority at any given moment; by the principle that governance in a mixed-heritage state must be structural — in the design of institutions, not merely in the goodwill of those who occupy them.

We affirm the Constitution of the Federated Republic as the supreme law to which this Constitution is subordinate. We affirm that the rights guaranteed by Article I of the federal Constitution are the floor beneath which no act of this State may descend. We acknowledge the sovereignty of indigenous nations as the federal Constitution acknowledges it — preceding the Republic, preceding this State, incapable of being granted or extinguished by either.

We adopt this Constitution not because we could not govern ourselves without it, but because we could not govern ourselves together without it — and because together is the only way this place has ever been.

ARTICLE I — THE HERITAGE COMMUNITIES
§1. Recognition. Three heritage streams converge in Selvane: the Vescarans, who first settled the river valleys and built the earliest permanent communities; the Morwani, who arrived in a later migration and established themselves in the uplands and interior; and the Marasene, the coast- and trade-people whose presence along the Sea of Selvane predates both. None is the original. None is the default. All are equally Selvane.

§2. Purpose of Recognition. The purpose of recognizing heritage communities is not to divide the people of Selvane but to ensure that the institutions of government reflect the state's composition. Recognition under this Article is a constitutional mechanism for inclusive governance. It creates no hierarchy among communities. It confers no superior rights on the members of any community over the members of any other. It is an instrument of power-sharing, not of separation.

§3. Community Affiliation — Self-Identification.

(a) Every citizen of Selvane of voting age may declare an affiliation with one of the recognized heritage communities for the purposes of this Constitution. Affiliation is by self-identification only. No person may be required to prove ancestry, lineage, language proficiency, cultural practice, or any other objective criterion to establish or maintain their declared affiliation.

(b) A citizen may also declare:

(i) Multiple Affiliation — identifying with more than one recognized community. A person declaring multiple affiliation must designate one primary affiliation for the purpose of the legislative seat formula under Article III; the designation may be changed at each decennial census.
(ii) Selvane Affiliation — identifying with the state as a whole and declining affiliation with any single recognized community. This designation carries full rights of citizenship and political participation, and the votes of Selvane-affiliated electors count in the statewide tally for all purposes, but their representatives are not counted toward any community's concurrent-majority calculation under Article III.
(iii) No Declaration — a citizen who makes no declaration is treated as holding Selvane Affiliation for all constitutional purposes until a declaration is recorded.
(c) A declaration or change of affiliation takes effect upon being recorded in the Community Register maintained by the State Elections Authority. The Register is confidential. Individual declarations are not public records. Aggregated community totals for each electoral district are published after each decennial census and after each general election.

(d) No organ of the State may inquire into, challenge, or penalize any person's community affiliation. Any law, regulation, or administrative practice that conditions any benefit, burden, or legal status on community affiliation other than as expressly provided in this Constitution is void.

§4. The Decennial Census and Recalibration.

(a) In the year preceding each decennial federal census, the State Elections Authority shall conduct a Census of Community Affiliation. Every citizen of voting age is invited to record or update their declaration under §3.

(b) The seat allocation formula under Article III is recalibrated within 60 days of the publication of the Community Affiliation Census to reflect current community proportions. Recalibration applies prospectively from the next general election.

(c) Between censuses, the formula in force at the most recent general election continues to apply.

§5. Language and Cultural Rights.

(a) Vescaric, Morwana, and Marasi are the recognized heritage languages of Selvane. The State shall take reasonable measures to support their preservation, teaching, and use in public life, consistent with the principle that no person shall be compelled to use any particular language.

(b) The official language of State administration is the language of the Federated Republic. Proceedings of the Assembly, the State Council, and the courts shall be conducted and recorded in that language. Any member of the Assembly, any party to a judicial proceeding, and any person appearing before a State body may address that body in a recognized heritage language; the State shall provide interpretation upon reasonable request.

(c) Each recognized community holds the right to establish and maintain cultural institutions — including schools, libraries, museums, and media — funded through standing budget lines allocated by formula and administered by the community's recognized cultural council. No community's cultural institutions may be funded more favorably than another's per capita, adjusted for the community's proportion of the state population.

§6. Community Cultural Councils.

(a) Each recognized heritage community shall have a Cultural Council, elected by the members of that community who have declared affiliation under §3, by a method the Legislature shall establish by statute.

(b) A Cultural Council administers the community's cultural institutions and programs, allocates cultural funding within the appropriation established by the Assembly, and advises the State Council on matters affecting the community's cultural interests.

(c) A Cultural Council exercises no legislative, executive, or judicial authority. It is an instrument of cultural autonomy, not of parallel governance. Its decisions are subject to the same legal constraints as any administrative body.

ARTICLE II — RIGHTS AND FREEDOMS
§7. Incorporation of Federal Rights. All rights guaranteed by Article I of the Constitution of the Federated Republic are rights under this Constitution. No act of the State of Selvane — whether legislative, executive, or judicial — may abridge, dilute, or condition any federal constitutional right. Where a provision of this Constitution or of State law is found to conflict with a federal constitutional right, the federal right prevails and the conflicting provision is void to the extent of the conflict.

§8. State Rights in Addition to Federal Floor. This State may recognize rights beyond those guaranteed by the federal Constitution. No provision of this Constitution shall be interpreted to limit any right otherwise available under federal law.

§9. Non-Discrimination. No organ of the State shall discriminate on the basis of race, ethnicity, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, religion, national origin, disability, age, economic status, or community affiliation in the provision of any governmental benefit, the imposition of any burden, or the application of any law. The heritage community recognition and power-sharing mechanisms established by this Constitution are not discrimination within the meaning of this section; they are structural inclusion measures designed to ensure that no community is systematically excluded from governance.

§10. Rights of Mixed-Heritage Persons. No person of mixed heritage shall be required to choose between their ancestral communities for any purpose other than the declaration of primary affiliation under §3(b)(i). A person of mixed heritage who declares multiple affiliation is entitled to participate fully in the cultural life and institutions of each community with which they have identified, and no Cultural Council may exclude a person on the ground that their primary affiliation lies elsewhere.

§11. Enforcement. Any person whose rights under this Article have been infringed by State action may seek remedy in the courts of Selvane. Where a federal constitutional right is implicated, the federal judicial architecture under Article IV of the federal Constitution is additionally available. The State may not assert sovereign immunity as a bar to constitutional claims.

ARTICLE III — THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY
§12. Structure and Name. The legislative power of the State of Selvane is vested in the Selvane Assembly, a unicameral body of 99 members elected by proportional representation.

§13. Electoral System.

(a) The State constitutes a single electoral district. Members are elected by open-list proportional representation using the largest-remainder method with a Hare quota.

(b) Every citizen of Selvane of voting age who is not disqualified from federal office under §7.4 of the federal Constitution is eligible to stand for election to the Assembly.

(c) A candidate for the Assembly shall, when filing, declare their community affiliation under Article I. The declaration is published on the ballot alongside the candidate's name and party affiliation. A candidate who declines to declare is deemed to hold Selvane Affiliation.

(d) Voters rank candidates in order of preference. The quota for election is the total number of valid votes cast divided by 99. Seats are allocated to parties in proportion to the votes received; within each party's allocation, candidates are elected in the order of preference expressed by voters.

(e) After the initial allocation of seats under subsection (d), the State Elections Authority shall verify whether the community seat floors established by §14 are met. If any recognized community holds fewer seats than its floor, sufficient seats shall be added to the Assembly and filled from the best-performing unelected candidates of that community to meet the floor. These additional seats increase the total membership of the Assembly for that term. If any recognized community holds seats exceeding its ceiling under §14, no adjustment is made — the ceiling operates as a trigger for recalibration at the next decennial census, not as a bar to representation in the current term.

(f) The Legislature shall by statute establish the detailed procedures for Assembly elections, consistent with this Constitution and with the National Voting System requirements of the federal Constitution.

§14. Community Seat Allocation Formula.

(a) No recognized heritage community shall hold fewer than 25 percent of the seats in the Assembly as constituted after the application of §13(e). This is the community floor.

(b) If, for three consecutive general elections, a recognized community has exceeded 48 percent of seats in the Assembly as initially allocated under §13(d), the Legislature shall, within 12 months of the certification of the third election, adjust the electoral system to address the over-concentration — provided that no adjustment may reduce any community below its floor.

(c) The formula is recalibrated after each decennial Census of Community Affiliation under §4, with effect from the next general election. Between recalibrations, the formula as applied at the most recent general election governs.

§15. Term. Members of the Assembly serve four-year terms. No member may serve more than three consecutive terms. The cooling-off period is one full term. A partial term served to fill a mid-term vacancy does not count toward the consecutive-term limit.

§16. Speaker and Deputy Speakers.

(a) The Assembly elects a Speaker and two Deputy Speakers from among its members. The election is by absolute majority of the full membership of the Assembly.

(b) The Speaker and at least one Deputy Speaker must be affiliated with different recognized heritage communities. Where the election produces a Speaker and two Deputies all affiliated with the same community, the candidate for Deputy Speaker receiving the fewest votes is replaced by the highest-polling candidate affiliated with another community.

(c) The Speaker presides over the Assembly, determines the order of business, and classifies bills under §19. The Speaker may not cast a vote except to break a tie.

(d) Either Deputy Speaker may preside in the Speaker's absence. When the Speaker is affiliated with the same community as a majority of the State Council, one Deputy Speaker from a different community must preside over proceedings concerning the Council's accountability to the Assembly.

§17. Ordinary Legislation.

(a) Ordinary legislation is any bill not classified as cross-community legislation under §19.

(b) An ordinary bill passes by simple majority of members present and voting, provided a quorum is present. Quorum is a majority of the full membership of the Assembly.

(c) The Speaker shall publish every enacted law to the State Record within 10 business days of passage, indexed alongside the full legislative history.

§18. Cross-Community Legislation and the Concurrent Majority.

(a) The following classes of legislation are cross-community legislation and require a concurrent majority:

(i) any bill amending this Constitution;
(ii) any bill concerning the electoral system, Assembly apportionment, or the community seat allocation formula;
(iii) any bill concerning the official status, use, or funding of heritage languages;
(iv) any bill concerning the funding, governance, or jurisdiction of Cultural Councils;
(v) any bill concerning the structure, jurisdiction, or appointment mechanism of the State Constitutional Court;
(vi) any bill that allocates public resources in a manner that would disproportionately benefit or burden any recognized community or its geographic region of concentration;
(vii) any bill designated as cross-community by the Speaker under §19(a)(ii);
(viii) any bill designated as cross-community by petition under §19(b).
(b) A concurrent majority requires:

(i) an overall majority of members present and voting; and
(ii) a majority of the members affiliated with each recognized heritage community who are present and voting.
(c) For the purpose of the community majority calculation under subsection (b)(ii), a member who has declared Multiple Affiliation is counted only toward their designated primary affiliation. A member who holds Selvane Affiliation or who has made no declaration is counted in the overall majority but not toward any community's majority.

(d) A bill that fails to achieve a concurrent majority on its first vote may be returned to committee, amended, and reintroduced after 90 days. The reintroduced bill must be accompanied by a written statement identifying the amendments made and how they address the objections that prevented passage.

(e) If a bill fails to achieve a concurrent majority on three votes in a single Assembly term, it lapses for that term. A bill that has lapsed under this provision may be reintroduced in the next term as a new bill.

§19. Classification of Bills.

(a) The Speaker shall, within 5 business days of a bill's introduction, classify it as ordinary or cross-community legislation, publishing the classification and its grounds to the State Record. The Speaker's classification must:

(i) identify each class under §18(a) into which the bill falls, if any;
(ii) for any bill not falling within classes (i) through (vi) of §18(a), determine whether the bill nonetheless raises a matter of vital interest to one or more recognized communities such that it should be treated as cross-community legislation. In making this determination, the Speaker shall consider whether the bill's subject matter, if decided by simple majority, would impose a substantial and differential burden on a recognized community.
(b) Upon the petition of one-quarter or more of the members of the Assembly affiliated with any recognized community, filed within 5 business days of the Speaker's classification, a bill shall be treated as cross-community legislation regardless of the Speaker's classification. The petition must state the cross-community interest engaged, and is published to the State Record.

(c) A member may, within 10 business days of the Speaker's classification, petition the State Constitutional Court to review the classification. The Court shall rule within 30 days. Pending the Court's ruling, the bill proceeds according to the Speaker's classification; if the Court reclassifies the bill, any vote taken under the prior classification is void and the bill must be voted on again.

§20. Deadlock Resolution — Mediation and Referendum.

(a) If a cross-community bill has failed on two votes and has been the subject of a failed committee mediation, the Speaker shall refer it to a Joint Mediation Committee.

(b) The Joint Mediation Committee comprises nine members: three from each recognized heritage community, elected by the members of the Assembly affiliated with that community. The Committee elects its own chair. It shall produce, within 60 days, either:

(i) a mediated text that attracts the support of at least two members from each community delegation, which shall be submitted to the Assembly for a fresh vote; or
(ii) a report stating that mediation has failed, with the objections of each side recorded and published to the State Record.
(c) Where the Joint Mediation Committee reports that mediation has failed, the bill's sponsors may, within 30 days, petition the State Elections Authority to place the bill on the ballot at the next statewide electoral period. The referendum requires:

(i) 60 percent of votes cast statewide; and
(ii) at least 40 percent of votes cast in each recognized community's geographic area of concentration, as those areas are defined by the State Elections Authority based on the most recent Community Affiliation Census and published in advance of the electoral period.
(d) If the referendum passes, the bill is enacted immediately upon certification and is not subject to further Assembly vote or State Council review. If the referendum fails, the bill lapses and may not be reintroduced in the same or substantially similar form for two years.

§21. Incompatibility. A member of the Assembly may not simultaneously serve as a member of the State Council, as a judge of any State court, as a member of a Cultural Council, or as a member of a local government council. A member who accepts an incompatible office is deemed to have resigned from the Assembly.

§22. Legislative Transparency. The Assembly shall sit in public. Votes are recorded and published to the State Record. Committee deliberations are public, except that a committee may close its proceedings by majority vote when considering matters involving personal privacy, pending litigation, or public safety; the fact of a closed session and its general subject are published.

ARTICLE IV — THE STATE COUNCIL
§23. Composition and Name. The executive power of the State of Selvane is vested in the State Council. The Council comprises three Councilors, one from each recognized heritage community.

§24. Election of Councilors.

(a) Councilors are elected by the Assembly from among its own members or from among citizens eligible for Assembly membership. The election is by absolute majority of the full membership of the Assembly.

(b) In the election of each Councilor, only members of the Assembly affiliated with the relevant recognized community may nominate candidates. The Assembly as a whole votes on each nomination.

(c) If the Assembly fails to elect all three Councilors within 30 days of the first ballot, the three candidates — one per recognized community — who received the highest vote totals on the most recent ballot are deemed elected. Where a community's candidates are tied, the tie is resolved by lot conducted by the Chief Justice of the State Constitutional Court.

(d) Councilors serve four-year terms coterminous with the Assembly that elected them. A Councilor may be re-elected without limit.

§25. First Councilor. The position of First Councilor rotates among the three Councilors. The Councilor whose community is numerically smallest in the Assembly as constituted after the most recent general election serves as First Councilor for the first 16 months; the Councilor whose community is second-smallest serves for the next 16 months; the Councilor whose community is largest serves for the final 16 months. Where two communities hold equal numbers of Assembly seats, the order is determined by lot.

§26. Functions of the First Councilor. The First Councilor:

(a) chairs meetings of the State Council;
(b) represents the State in ceremonial functions and in intergovernmental relations with the Federated Republic, other States, and foreign governments;
(c) signs legislation enacted by the Assembly into the State Record;
(d) may, with the concurrence of one other Councilor, request the Assembly to convene in special session.
The First Councilor exercises no authority beyond that expressly granted by this section and holds no power to direct, override, or act on behalf of the other Councilors without their concurrence.

§27. Decision-Making.

(a) The State Council shall endeavor to reach decisions by consensus.

(b) Where consensus is not achieved, a decision of the Council requires the affirmative vote of at least two Councilors.

(c) The following matters require unanimity:

(i) declaration of a state emergency within the meaning of Selvane law;
(ii) appointment or removal of the heads of the State Electoral Authority, the State Audit Office, and the State Public Broadcaster;
(iii) submission of the State budget proposal to the Assembly;
(iv) any matter that would, if enacted as legislation, be cross-community legislation under Article III;
(v) any other matter the Council by internal rule designates as requiring unanimity.
(d) Where the Council fails to achieve unanimity on a matter listed in subsection (c) for more than 60 days, the matter shall be referred to the Assembly, which may resolve it by concurrent majority under §18. If the Assembly fails to resolve the matter within 60 days, the status quo ante prevails.

§28. Accountability to the Assembly.

(a) The State Council is collectively accountable to the Assembly.

(b) The Assembly may, by absolute majority of its full membership, adopt a motion of no confidence in the State Council. Such a motion must name a proposed successor Council — one Councilor from each recognized community — and is effective only if it simultaneously removes the sitting Council and installs the named successors. A no-confidence motion that does not name successors has no constitutional effect.

(c) An individual Councilor may be removed by the Assembly by absolute majority of its full membership on grounds of incapacity, constitutional breach, or conviction for a disqualifying offense. Where an individual Councilor is removed, the Assembly shall elect a replacement from the same recognized community within 30 days.

(d) The State Council may, by unanimous vote and with reasons published to the State Record, dissolve the Assembly and call an early election. A dissolution may not occur within the first year of an Assembly's term, nor within the final six months, nor within one year of a prior dissolution.

§29. Administrative Departments. The State Council shall organize the administrative departments of the State by regulation consistent with statute. Each Councilor supervises a portfolio of departments assigned by the Council as a whole. No Councilor may unilaterally direct the departments assigned to another Councilor. The assignment of portfolios shall, to the extent practicable, be distributed such that no Councilor's portfolio disproportionately affects the interests of any single recognized community.

§30. Publication. All acts of the State Council — decisions, appointments, regulations, and the reasons for them — are published to the State Record within 10 business days. A late-published act is not void, but late publication is a constitutional compliance breach.

ARTICLE V — THE JUDICIARY
§31. Court Structure. The judicial power of the State of Selvane is vested in a State Constitutional Court, a State Court of Appeal, and such inferior courts as the Legislature establishes by statute. Matters arising under the federal Constitution or federal law may be appealed to the federal courts in accordance with the federal judicial architecture.

§32. State Constitutional Court.

(a) The State Constitutional Court comprises seven justices.

(b) At least one justice must be affiliated with each recognized heritage community. No more than three justices may be affiliated with the same community.

(c) Justices are appointed by the State Council, acting unanimously, from a list of qualified candidates maintained by an independent Judicial Appointments Commission. The Commission's composition, qualifications, and procedures shall be established by statute consistent with this Constitution.

(d) If the State Council fails to appoint a justice within 90 days of a vacancy, the Assembly may appoint by two-thirds majority of its full membership, provided the community-balance requirements of subsection (b) are met.

(e) Justices serve a single non-renewable term of 10 years and may be removed only for cause: constitutional breach, demonstrated incapacity, or conviction for a disqualifying offense under the law of the Federated Republic. Removal requires a two-thirds majority of the full membership of the Assembly.

(f) The State Constitutional Court has exclusive jurisdiction over:

(i) the constitutionality of State legislation and State Council acts under this Constitution;
(ii) disputes between organs of the State concerning their respective constitutional authority;
(iii) classification disputes under §19(c);
(iv) disputes concerning the community affiliation mechanism under Article I;
(v) any other matter the Legislature assigns to it by statute consistent with this Constitution.
(g) The State Constitutional Court may not issue advisory opinions. It acts only on petition of a party with standing: any member of the Assembly, the State Council, a Cultural Council, a local government council, or any citizen alleging a direct and concrete injury.

§33. Relationship to Federal Courts. The State Constitutional Court is the final authority on the interpretation of this Constitution. Questions concerning the interpretation of the federal Constitution are referred to the federal Supreme Court in accordance with §4.5 of the federal Constitution. Where a proceeding raises both State and federal constitutional questions, the State Constitutional Court may rule on the State questions and certify the federal questions to the federal courts.

§34. Judicial Independence. No organ of the State may direct, threaten, or seek to influence a judge's decision in any pending matter. Judicial compensation may not be reduced during a judge's term. The Legislature shall provide the courts with resources adequate to discharge their constitutional functions.

ARTICLE VI — LOCAL GOVERNMENT
§35. Recognition. Local governments — cities, towns, counties, and other subdivisions the Legislature establishes — are recognized as primary points of contact between citizens and the State. They are entitled to reasonable fiscal capacity to carry out their functions, consistent with §15.8 of the federal Constitution.

§36. Local Autonomy within Community Areas. In geographic areas where, according to the most recent Community Affiliation Census, a single recognized community constitutes more than 60 percent of the population, the local government may adopt the community's heritage language as an additional language of local administration and may integrate the community's Cultural Council programs into local service delivery, provided that the rights of minority-community residents under this Constitution and the federal Constitution are fully respected.

§37. Fiscal Capacity. The Legislature shall by statute establish a local government fiscal equalization mechanism ensuring that every local government has resources reasonably sufficient to discharge its functions. The mechanism shall be formula-driven — based on population, geographic area, assessed need, and local revenue capacity — and shall not depend on discretionary allocations.

ARTICLE VII — CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT
§38. Amendment Procedure.

(a) This Constitution may be amended by the Assembly. Every amendment is cross-community legislation and must pass by:

(i) two-thirds of the full membership of the Assembly; and
(ii) a concurrent majority under §18(b).
(b) An amendment passed by the Assembly under subsection (a) shall be submitted to the State's eligible voters at the next statewide electoral period. The amendment is ratified if it receives 60 percent of votes cast statewide and at least 50 percent in each recognized community's geographic area of concentration.

(c) The State Elections Authority administers the ratification referendum. The Authority shall, within 14 days of certification, publish the full text of the amendment and the result to the State Record and to the National Record System of the Federated Republic.

§39. Entrenched Provisions. The following provisions may not be amended except by the procedure in §38 and may not be suspended by any emergency measure:

(a) the recognition of the three heritage communities (§1);
(b) the community seat floor formula (§14);
(c) the cross-community legislation requirement (§18);
(d) the State Council's composition and decision-making structure (§§23–27);
(e) this section.
§40. Federal Consistency. No amendment to this Constitution may take effect if it is inconsistent with the Constitution of the Federated Republic. Where the State Constitutional Court or the federal Supreme Court finds an amendment to be inconsistent with the federal Constitution, the amendment is void to the extent of the inconsistency.

ARTICLE VIII — GENERAL AND TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS
§41. Supremacy and Stare Decisis. This Constitution is the supreme law of the State of Selvane, subordinate only to the Constitution of the Federated Republic. All State laws, regulations, and official acts must conform to it. A law or act inconsistent with this Constitution is void to the extent of the inconsistency.

§42. State Record. The State shall maintain a State Record — a permanent, publicly accessible repository of all laws, regulations, judicial decisions, Council acts, Assembly proceedings, and official notices. The State Record operates on the same principles as the National Record System of the Federated Republic. No official act of the State that is required to be published has legal effect until it appears in the State Record.

§43. First Elections. The State Elections Authority shall conduct the first elections to the Assembly and the first Census of Community Affiliation within 12 months of this Constitution taking effect. Until the Assembly is seated, the Governor under the prior constitutional framework shall exercise the minimum powers necessary to maintain State operations and to facilitate the transition, and may not enact new legislation, enter into binding commitments exceeding 12 months, or alter the structure of government.

§44. Continuity of Laws. All laws of the State of Selvane in force immediately before this Constitution takes effect continue in force to the extent they are consistent with this Constitution. Any person may petition the State Constitutional Court for a declaration that a pre-existing law is inconsistent with this Constitution and void.

§45. Definitions. In this Constitution:

"absolute majority" means more than half of the full membership of the body in question, regardless of how many members are present;
"full membership" means the number of seats in the body as presently constituted, including vacant seats;
"State Elections Authority" means the independent authority responsible for the administration of elections and the Community Affiliation Census under this Constitution;
"State Record" means the permanent public record established under §42;
"recognized heritage community" means the Vescarans, the Morwani, or the Marasene, as recognized by Article I;
"concurrent majority" has the meaning given in §18(b).
