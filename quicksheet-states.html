QUICK SHEET · CONSTITUTION OF THE FEDERATED REPUBLIC
States, Territories & Indigenous Nations
How a place rises to statehood, falls from it, or leaves entirely — all by audited standards rather than political favor. Statehood requires two successful audits within the constitutional timetable, not a political admission vote; and Indigenous sovereignty is acknowledged as something that precedes the Republic, not conferred by it.
Article XV · §15.1–§15.9
Article XVI · §16.1–§16.5
Territory to State — Followed by Annual Audits — §15.1–§15.3
§15.1
Territory
Elected local authority, Article I protections, social-state access, proportional Assembly seats (no Senate seats). May begin the Statehood pathway.
→
§15.2 · THE AUDIT
First Successful Audit
Must occur within two years after initiation. Success requires completed findings from all three Monitors with no material failure. A warning keeps the proceeding open but does not count as success. After the first success, the jurisdiction remains a Territory.
→
§15.2 · SECOND AUDIT
Full Statehood
A second successful audit must occur within two years after the first. Its publication confers Statehood immediately by constitutional operation. Two Senate seats arise and a special election follows within 90 days. There is no Provisional entry stage.
→
§15.3 · DECLINE
Mandatory Devolution
First failure: warning and remediation. Second: renewed warning and revised plan. Third: Provisional status; the State remains a State. Sitting senators finish, but no new Senate term starts. Devolution still requires a referendum and agreement.
The Voluntary Exits — §15.4, §15.9
Devolution & Merger §15.4
60% vote · ≥50% turnout→ 2/3 Senate ratifies
Voluntary and mandatory routes use the same process: 60% vote with at least 50% participation, negotiation with the Legislature, then 2/3 Senate ratification. A completed devolution produces a Territory. Provisional-State referenda begin at the first sitting senator’s term expiry and repeat every two years if unresolved.
Independence §15.9
2/3 legislature→ 60% vote, 60% turnout→ national approval required
State approval starts the process: both federal chambers must then ratify at 60% within 90 days, excluding the petitioning State’s members. A national vote follows within 180 days: 60% approval, 50% participation. A negotiated separation agreement needs 2/3 Senate ratification. The five-year bar applies to a failed Stage One vote (§15.9).
Indigenous Nations — Sovereignty Acknowledged, Not Granted — §16.1–§16.5
Prior sovereignty, recognized§16.1 · Bilateral Compact
Indigenous sovereignty precedes the Republic. During the founding window, nations choose through their own lawful process. No election means interim Associated Community treatment, without implied consent to unaccepted Compact terms. Territorial Integration permanently ends the former status; a later sovereign relationship requires a new Compact. Predecessor treaties continue (§§16.1–16.5).
Numbers Worth Remembering
0
political admission votes to become a State — two successful audits decide (§15.2).
2
consecutive annual audits with no material failure by any Monitor restore ordinary State status (§15.1.a).
3
consecutive overall failures place an existing State in Provisional status (§15.3).
60%
devolution approval, with ≥50% turnout. Independence requires its separate State, federal-chamber, national-vote, and agreement stages (§15.9).
