The Third Strike

Year 13. Valdrath fails its Statehood Audit for the third consecutive year. The State enters Provisional status automatically. A Provisional Audit Council forms. A referendum on pursuing devolution follows when the first sitting senator’s term expires; remediation remains in the State legislature’s hands.

§15.1.a Provisional Status §15.2 The Statehood Audit §15.3 Mandatory Devolution §15.4 Voluntary Devolution §4.5 Judicial Review

Valdrath had been on Statehood Watch since Year 11. The LM found material failures in electoral legislation, the EM in election administration, and the JM published its own domain findings. Year 12 corrected some defects but still produced material failures from the LM and EM. Each overall failed audit brought an Early Warning and a required Remediation Plan, revised after the second consecutive failure.

Year 13’s audit found continuing material failures in both the LM and EM domains: legislation still failed to supply lawful electoral safeguards, and certification software in three districts remained outside NVS technical standards. The State passed emergency legislation on publication morning. Each Monitor nonetheless published the conditions found during the audit period; prospective legislation had not cured them. Two material-failure findings established the third consecutive overall failure.

What Follows Three Failures

§15.3 — Provisional Status Activates
The State enters Provisional status automatically. No vote is required for that. Everything continues.

Valdrath remained a State under §15.1.a. Its legislature, courts, executive, Article I protections, social-state access, and Assembly representation continued. Sitting senators completed their current terms, but no new Senate term could begin while Provisional status continued.

The path back to ordinary State status required two consecutive annual audits with no material-failure finding from any Monitor. Merely avoiding an overall failure was insufficient: one Monitor’s material warning would still interrupt the clean-audit sequence.

The Provisional Audit Council

§15.3 — Council Formation
The Civic Consul appoints two federal officers. Valdrath's legislature appoints two State delegates. The Council is advisory only.

The Provisional Audit Council activated upon Valdrath entering Provisional status. The Civic Consul appointed two officers through the standard executive officer process — replaceable by any subsequent CC through the same process. Valdrath's legislature appointed two State delegates by its own internal process. Four members, equal representation, no casting vote in the event of deadlock.

The Council's mandate was specific: review the Monitors' audit findings, propose remediation plans, publish its findings and recommendations to the NRS. It had no authority to direct Valdrath's legislature, require legislation, or override any State decision. The State legislature retained full authority over what laws to pass, what reforms to implement, and how to respond to the Council's publications. The Council's power was informational — and in a constitution where everything is published to a permanent public record, informational power is real.

Provisional Audit Council · First Publication · Year 13 · Valdrath

The Council has reviewed the three Monitors' Year 13 audit findings. The persistent non-compliance — deficient electoral safeguards and NVS certification software in Districts 4, 9, and 11 — is technically addressable within a single legislative session. The Electoral Integrity Restoration Act passed on publication morning addresses the correct failure point. The Act's implementation timeline of 18 months exceeds what a focused procurement process requires. The Council recommends the State legislature amend the implementation timeline to 90 days, prioritize Districts 4, 9, and 11 as the first phase, and engage Elections Panel technical staff for parallel certification testing. These steps, if taken within 60 days, make a clean Year 14 audit achievable.

The Council's next publication will follow the State legislature's formal response. A response is required within the period defined by statute.

The Forced Devolution Election

§15.3 — Citizen Vote on Devolution
The Elections Panel administers a citizen vote on pursuing devolution. The terms are identical to §15.4's voluntary process.

The first sitting senator’s term expired in Year 14. At that point the Elections Panel administered the required referendum on pursuing devolution. Approval required 60% of votes cast and at least 50% participation. An affirmative result would begin negotiation between the State and the Legislature, followed by Senate ratification of the restructuring agreement by two-thirds of full seated membership. It would not turn Valdrath into a Territory immediately.

At 68% turnout, 71% of votes cast rejected pursuing devolution. Valdrath remained a Provisional State and continued remediation. If that status persisted, another referendum would follow at a two-year interval. Devolution would restructure the State’s relationship within the Republic; it was not a vote to leave the Republic.

The SC Challenge and Resolution

§15.3 — Judicial Review
Valdrath challenges application of the audit criteria. Judicial review cannot substitute policy judgment for Monitor assessment.

Valdrath’s Governor filed a judicial challenge on Day 21, alleging that the audit criteria had been incorrectly applied. §15.3 permits review of legality, process, jurisdiction, and application of the stated criteria; it supplies no special 30-day filing limit or factual-sufficiency-only restriction. The Court examined the claimed error without substituting its policy judgment for the Monitors’ domain assessments. It dismissed the challenge because prospective legislation had not remedied the material failures found in the audited period.

Year 14

Valdrath's legislature amended the Electoral Integrity Restoration Act's implementation timeline to 90 days and published its formal response to the Audit Council within the statutory period. The Council published a compliance confirmation. The Elections Panel's technical staff worked alongside Valdrath's election administrators through the summer. By the time the Year 14 audit team arrived, Districts 4, 9, and 11 were running on certified NVS software.

The Year 14 audit produced no material-failure finding from any of the three Monitors. Year 15 did the same. Publication of the complete second clean annual audit restored ordinary State status automatically and dissolved the advisory Council. No admission vote or executive confirmation was required. The bar on beginning new Senate terms ended.

The devolution referendum gave citizens a say at the constitutionally specified point. The advisory Council proposed remedies, and the State legislature retained authority to enact them. Two consecutive fully clean annual audits ended Provisional status. Valdrath had remained a State throughout.
