States & Territories · §15.1.a · §15.3 · Provisional Status

The Third Strike

Year 15. Valdrath receives its third consecutive failed biennial audit determination. The Senate separately places it into Provisional status. State and federal advisers assist remediation, and Valdrath later chooses a voluntary devolution referendum that its voters reject.

§15.1.a Provisional Status §15.2 The Statehood Audit §15.3 State Remediation and Provisional Status §15.4 Voluntary Devolution and Merger §4.5 Judicial Review and Scope

Valdrath entered remediation in Year 11 after the Senate determined its first biennial audit noncompliant on the complete record from all three Monitors. The LM identified defects in electoral legislation and the EM in administration. The Senate separately determined noncompliance again in Year 13. Valdrath published a Remediation Plan after the first decision and a revised Plan after the second.

Year 15’s audit again documented electoral-safeguard and certification-software failures. All three Monitors published their findings. The State passed emergency legislation on publication morning, but that prospective change had not cured the audited conditions. The Senate determined the audit noncompliant by the required majority of votes cast with a quorum present. That was the third consecutive determination of failure.

What Follows Three Failures

§15.3 — Senate Review
The third failure requires a political decision. The audit does not change Valdrath's status by itself.

The Monitors certified the three-failure sequence to the Senate and published the certification to the NRS. The Senate then voted by absolute majority of its full seated membership to place Valdrath in Provisional status. Only that affirmative vote changed the State's political status.

Valdrath remained a State under §15.1.a. Its government, courts, rights, social-state access and Assembly representation continued. Sitting senators completed their terms, but no new Senate term began. Restoration required two consecutive biennial audits determined compliant under §§15.2 and 15.2.a, rather than unanimity among the Monitors.

Advisory Cooperation

§15.3 — Remediation Support
The Republic and Valdrath choose a small advisory working group. Its form is not prescribed by the Constitution.

During remediation after the failed audit, the Republic and Valdrath agreed to a four-person working group: two federal representatives and two State representatives. That composition came from their agreement, not from §15.3.

The group reviewed published audit findings, advised on remediation, and coordinated access to federal technical expertise. It held no political, legal, auditing, or governing authority over Valdrath. The State legislature alone decided what laws to pass and what reforms to implement.

Advisory Working Group · First Publication · Year 15 · Valdrath

The group reviewed the published audit findings and advised that the Electoral Integrity Restoration Act addressed the correct failure point but that its 18-month implementation timeline could be shortened. It recommended prioritizing Districts 4, 9, and 11 and coordinating certification testing with Elections Panel technical staff.

Those recommendations were advice only. Valdrath remained free to accept, modify, or reject them.

The Voluntary Devolution Question

§15.4 — Citizen Vote on Devolution
Valdrath chooses to ask its citizens whether to pursue devolution under §15.4.

The first sitting senator’s term expired in Year 17. Valdrath chose that moment to hold a voluntary referendum on pursuing devolution under §15.4. The Constitution did not require a vote at the term expiry. Approval required 60% of votes cast and at least 50% participation; a favorable result would open restructuring negotiations, not immediately convert the State into a Territory.

At 68% turnout, 71% rejected pursuing devolution. Valdrath remained a Provisional State and continued remediation. No constitutional rule required another devolution vote every two years. A later voluntary proposal would need its own lawful process.

The SC Challenge and Resolution

§15.3 — Judicial Review
Valdrath challenges application of the audit criteria. Judicial review addresses legality without replacing the chamber’s assessment of whether Provisional status is warranted.

Valdrath’s Governor challenged the Provisional determination on Day 21, alleging unlawful application of audit criteria. The Court reviewed jurisdiction, procedure and constitutional compliance without substituting its assessment of whether Provisional status was warranted. It dismissed the claim: prospective legislation had not cured the documented defects supporting the chamber’s decision.

Year 17

Valdrath's legislature amended the Electoral Integrity Restoration Act's implementation timeline to 90 days after considering the advisory group's recommendations. The group published a follow-up advisory note. The Elections Panel's technical staff worked alongside Valdrath's election administrators through the summer. By the time the Year 17 audit team arrived, Districts 4, 9, and 11 were running on certified NVS software.

The Year 17 audit documented correction of the failures. After all three findings were published, the Senate determined compliance. Year 19 brought another complete audit and affirmative compliance determination. That second consecutive qualifying decision restored ordinary State status automatically. No separate readmission vote or executive confirmation was needed, and the Elections Panel was required to fill vacant Senate seats within ninety days.

The voluntary referendum let citizens reject restructuring. The advisory group offered help without governing. The Senate remained responsible for each audit determination; two consecutive compliant biennial audits ended Provisional status by constitutional operation. Valdrath had remained a State throughout.
