QUICK SHEET · CONSTITUTION OF THE FEDERATED REPUBLIC
Article II — The Dual Executive
Two executive domains, legislative accountability and continuity without a shared executive superior.
§2.1–§2.18
Article II + §7.4

Legat Consul

A nationally elected four-year office with a twelve-year service limit and a next-election cooling period. Commands necessary defense against active or imminent attack, including necessary action beyond the border. Other objectives require lawful authorization; treaties may authorize the action their terms require. Notice goes promptly to the Civic Consul and both Speakers. Senate review and Assembly appropriations remain separate.

§2.2 — full provision →

Civic Consul

Residual domestic executive authority; a twelve-year service limit. The Assembly replaces the incumbent only by an absolute-majority vote electing a named successor. Ordinary elections do not themselves terminate the office. After a vacancy, failure to elect within the statutory period (default twenty-one days) triggers limited acting service and the §2.6.a national fallback. If no candidate wins, nomination and election repeat.

Bills and coordination

Both executives review concurrently. Civic return within ten business days suspends the bill for a month; Senate override requires two-thirds. Otherwise both chambers must pass it again, without another Civic veto. The Council of Ministers coordinates without executive authority. Assistance under §2.14.b is voluntary and non-coercive; its text imposes no ninety-day maximum.

§2.7 — full provision →

Temporary inability

Declaration or Council majority with at least five participating triggers acting authority. Ordinary restoration follows twenty-four hours after publication. A same-grounds renewed determination within ninety days needs two-thirds; restoration then needs a deciding-body majority. It lasts at most six months; continuation needs a fresh two-thirds determination. Any involuntary determination is subject to expedited independent judicial review.
