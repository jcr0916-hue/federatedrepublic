The Candidate Whose Day Job Just Had a Good Week

Ines Carrow is running to become Legat Consul. She is also, at present, the Legat Consul's Director of Foreign Affairs — a position that this week produced the first real movement on the Lake Varda crisis in months.

Petra Vend Political Correspondent · Verentum Year 13, Month 11

Caldris said yes this week to a regional conference Carrow's own office proposed in Month 11. The acceptance is narrow — talks only, no endorsement of anything Torenthia has said about the Varda incidents, no date, no venue — but it is the first affirmative answer either neighboring government has given since the Sunderland inquiry went nowhere in the spring. It happened while the person who proposed it is asking voters to make her Legat Consul outright.

Carrow did not mention the acceptance in any campaign material this paper could find. Asked directly whether she considered it a credential for her candidacy, her office gave a one-line answer: "The Directorate does its work regardless of who is running for what." That is, characteristically, the driest possible way to describe good news.

What Her Opponents Make of It

Not much, publicly, and this paper suspects that is deliberate. Attacking a sitting official for doing her job competently during a diplomatic crisis is not an obviously winning line, and none of the three other campaigns tried it. Asked for comment, Vael's office said only that "foreign policy is not decided by press release," which is true and answers nothing. Mak's office did not respond. Sandris, asked directly, gave this paper the most complete answer of the three:

"She has an advantage the rest of us don't, and it isn't a secret, and I don't think it's improper. The Directorate's work is real work and it would be happening with or without an election attached to it. I'd rather run against someone doing the job well than someone doing it badly. Voters can decide for themselves whether that's the same thing as being ready to hold the office."

The Structural Question Nobody's Naming

There is no rule against a sitting domain officer running for the office that appoints them, and nothing about Carrow's candidacy raises any question of impropriety. But the plain fact of it is that three candidates are describing what the office should do from outside it, and one is currently doing it, with the visibility and the results that come with actually holding the pen.

Whether that is an advantage voters should weigh, discount, or ignore entirely is not a question this paper can answer for them. It is worth naming plainly, since none of the four campaigns has chosen to.
