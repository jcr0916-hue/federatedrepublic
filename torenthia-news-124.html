A Clause Near Riverglow

The northern housing act has passed. The Civic Consul wants one siting authority removed before it becomes law, and the Assembly Speaker now decides whether to pause the clock.

Aleth FennThe TorenthianYear 14, Month 1

ILYR — The Northern Arrival Housing Capacity Act has passed both chambers, and for most of its length nobody in the north has much to say against it. It pays for permanent housing, supports reception capacity, and sets standards for where temporary housing may go. The argument is about twelve words in Section 3(c).

Those words would let federal money for temporary housing be spent on parcels "within or adjoining tracts conveyed by statute for residential development." In the northern arrival corridor, the obvious such tract is the Norvane side of Argent Ridge, the highland Varek calls Riverglow.

Civic Consul Elin Thoss has issued a reconsideration notice asking the Legislature to delete them. Her grounds are narrow. The statute conveying the Ridge's development rights faces a pending citizen repeal, and the proposed National Trust designation still lacks Norvane's consent. Her notice argues that federal money for temporary housing should go to sites whose legal status is settled. It says nothing about whether the Ridge should be protected, and she supports the rest of the Act.

Norvane's refusal of Trust consent last year rested on one stated reason: the Ridge tract was the largest buildable land within reach of the arrival corridor, and the State could not give it up before replacement housing capacity was secured. The Act is aimed at that capacity. It does not ask Norvane to change its position, and nothing in it does so automatically.

"We will look at our position when housing is standing, not when a bill is signed," said Bram Odegaard, Norvane's Secretary of Housing and Reception. "Nobody has offered us a trade, and we would not accept one."

In Kell's Hollow County, on the Varek side, Reyna Oskitt was more direct. "Temporary has a way of becoming permanent next to a ridge," she said. "If the Act goes through without that clause, I'll say it was done properly. If it goes through with it, I'll say the same thing I said last year."

The next decision belongs to Assembly Speaker Marit Hosk. Under §2.7, she may pause the review period for up to ten business days. During a pause the Assembly may vote to withdraw the bill and start again without the clause. If she does not pause, or the Assembly does not withdraw, the review period resumes, and the Act becomes law unless a Consul returns it. Thoss's notice says she supports the rest of the Act.

None of this touches the repeal campaign's clock or the Trust's two-thirds requirement. Those remain separate, and Riverglow is no more protected today than it was last year. What has changed is that the reason Norvane gave for saying no now has a federal budget line attached to it.
