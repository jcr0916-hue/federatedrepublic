A
Absolute Majority §3.7 →

A majority of the full seated membership of a chamber — not merely those present or voting. Used for Civic Consul formation under §2.6.a and for removal under §2.6's constructive vote of no confidence — the same threshold governs both. What makes removal harder in practice is not a higher number but the constructive-vote requirement itself: the Assembly must agree on a specific successor, not merely on dissatisfaction with the incumbent.

Prevents slim-quorum majorities from making consequential constitutional decisions. The constructive-vote requirement — removal only by simultaneously electing a named successor — creates stability without making the CC irremovable.

Acting Civic Consul §2.16(1) →

The officer who exercises the Civic Consul's domestic executive authority during a period of determined incapacity under §2.16. Acting authority vests automatically in a fixed order, not by the Civic Consul's own designation: the Assembly Speaker first, then persons holding statute-designated offices in statutory order, then the most senior Assembly member by continuous service. The acting officer retains their Assembly seat but may not exercise legislative functions while serving. Acting status ends when the incapacity ends under §2.16 or a new Civic Consul is elected under §2.6.

Associated Community §20.1 →

A fully sovereign independent political community in a consensual Compact relationship with the Republic. It is not a State, Territory, subdivision, or intermediate sovereignty category. Article XX protects equal sovereignty and requires compliance with the non-derogable-rights floor; it does not impose the Republic's entire internal governance system or all Article I rights on the community. Eligibility includes recognized Indigenous nations, nations becoming independent under Article XVII or §15.9, and other independent nations.

C
Civic Proficiency Assessment §5.1 →

The independent civic and constitutional proficiency assessment under §5.1.b applies to every naturalization pathway under §5.1. Statute governs its content and administration, consistently with equality and the prohibition on testing political opinion or cultural loyalty. Qualifying honorable service reduces the required statutory residency period rather than replacing the assessment.

Classification Ceiling §10.2 →

The constitutional ceiling on how long government records may remain classified. No record may remain classified beyond 25 years from original classification. A single 5-year extension takes effect when the executive that classified the record publishes a justification to the EM; the EM assesses it and publishes its finding. No material may remain classified beyond 30 years. Records may be classified only where required to protect national security, and any person with standing may ask a court to release every portion whose classification is not.

Constitutional Pool Framework §9.8 →

The Elections Panel maintains public constitutional pools, administers lotteries and applies objective constitutional criteria. Pool records are on the NRS; the JMC audits compliance. Every pool has at least five eligible members. Exclusion requires published reasons and timely judicial review under statutory procedures. Office-specific selection still governs: LM and EM have nomination routes with a JMC lottery fallback after vacancy delay; the JM is selected by public lottery administered by the Elections Panel.

Constructive Vote of No Confidence §2.6 →

The mechanism by which the Assembly removes the Civic Consul — by simultaneously electing a named successor by absolute majority of full seated Assembly membership in a single motion. The same absolute-majority threshold governs both formation under §2.6.a and removal under §2.6. A vote that does not name and elect a replacement is constitutionally void. The Assembly must agree not only that it has lost confidence in the current CC but that it has found a specific successor with the same level of support.

Formation and removal share the same absolute-majority threshold — the stability comes from the constructive-vote requirement, not a higher numeric bar. A no-confidence effort that cannot agree on who should replace the Civic Consul fails no matter how large the discontent with the incumbent, which is deliberately harder to engineer than raw dissatisfaction alone.

Domain Officer §2.4.a →

A person appointed by the Legat Consul to exercise a defined portion of the LC's constitutional domain authority, nominated by the Legat Consul and confirmed by the Senate. Statute determines particular offices and titles; ordinary civil servants and military personnel are not thereby domain officers. The Senate may remove a domain officer by majority of full seated membership with published grounds; the LC may dismiss one at any time. Domain officers sit on the Council of Ministers.

Consular Veto §2.3.a →

The Legat Consul's power to veto legislation raising a genuine constitutional concern within the LC's security domain — military operations, external intelligence, treaty obligations, or border security. The LC must return the bill within 10 business days with written grounds published to the NRS; silence within 10 business days means the bill is enacted by operation of law. A general or speculative security connection is insufficient. Single use per bill — a bill reintroduced in a subsequent session proceeds without LC interference. Either Speaker may request an EM assessment of the domain question under §9.1 (informational only). The Senate may override by 2/3 supermajority at any time during that session; where no override occurs, the bill lapses for that session.

Stronger than the CC's Suspensive Veto — a confirmed LC domain veto lapses the bill for the full session rather than requiring re-passage. Reflects the LC's direct electoral mandate and absence of mid-term Assembly accountability.

Coordination Failure Protocol §2.14.a →

The mechanism for a cross-domain emergency requiring unified operational authority: either executive may propose a temporary operational lead designation, which becomes effective on the concurrence of both executives and is published to the NRS. Where the executives cannot agree, either may petition the SC for expedited resolution under §4.5. A lead designation assigns operational coordination only — it does not affect either executive's constitutional domain authority.

There is no Council of Ministers deliberation window and no Legislature override built into this mechanism — it resolves through executive concurrence or, failing that, the Supreme Court, not through the Legislature.

Council of Ministers §2.14 →

The standing cross-executive coordination body comprising the Legat Consul's domain officers and the Civic Consul's ministers meeting jointly. The Council of Ministers is the constitutional first forum for matters that cross executive domains. It makes recommendations and coordinates positions; it does not exercise executive authority. Session summaries are published to the NRS; the existence and general subject of sessions may not be classified.

§2.14.a's Coordination Failure Protocol is a separate mechanism for cross-domain emergencies specifically — it resolves through executive concurrence or SC petition, not through this Council.

D
Declaration of National Emergency §1.16 →

An executive may declare a rights derogation within its domain for at most fourteen days under §1.16. Continuation requires a statute with a fixed expiration. No declaration or authorization may be renewed or substantially repeated. Measures lapse with their authorization. Mandatory expedited independent judicial review and ordinary remedies remain available; Monitors retain authority and the non-derogable rights are never suspended.

Domain Default §2.1 →

Where the domain of a proposed executive action is disputed, each executive continues only those functions clearly within their established domain; the disputed action is suspended pending SC determination. The Council of Ministers is the first forum; if unresolved, either executive may petition the SC, under its original jurisdiction in §4.5.

The Council of Ministers is the first coordination forum; either executive may petition the SC for domain determination. During active military operations, the Legat Consul's domain governs operational decisions.

Disqualifying Offense §7.4 →

A conviction for insurrection against the Republic, rebellion against the Republic, treason against the Republic, or subversion of a federal election bars federal office. These grounds are exhaustive and require conviction, not accusation. Other criminal history must be disclosed but does not itself bar office; its weight is for voters or the selecting body. The Constitution provides no general pardon-based exception to this bar.

Dual Executive Article II →

The constitutional architecture dividing executive authority between a directly elected Legat Consul (military, external intelligence, foreign affairs) and a Civic Consul chosen by and accountable to the Assembly (domestic administration, budget, civil service). Neither executive may act in the other's domain. When a single event implicates both domains simultaneously, both executives act within their respective domains concurrently.

Cohabitation — where the Legat Consul and Civic Consul represent different political coalitions — is the design's stress test. §2.14.a provides resolution for coordination failures.

E
Emergency Designee §2.9.a →

Under §2.9.a, when essential federal authority cannot operate through ordinary succession or emergency procedures, the head of the first reachable State on the Elections Panel’s public-lot list becomes Emergency Designee. The other reachable State heads form the State Emergency Council. The Designee has only necessary military and continuity authority to preserve the Republic and restore its institutions, bound by Article I. Authority returns to each institution as it becomes capable; the exceptional arrangement ends on the specified restoration conditions or at six months.

Emergency Governance Protocol §2.11 →

When extraordinary circumstances prevent normal constitutional government from functioning, either Consul, or a majority of available members of either legislative chamber, may declare a continuity emergency without ordinary quorum, and the declaration is sent to every State and Territory. A chamber able to meet may act without quorum, and alone if the other cannot, but only to preserve essential government functions and restore constitutional governance; thresholds are counted from the members able to act. Emergency action may not amend the Constitution, extend a term except under §7.2.a, or derogate rights except under §1.16, and its measures lapse with the emergency unless enacted by statute. A declaration lapses after 30 days unless renewed; any member or Monitor may seek expedited SC review. Unavailable Consuls are covered by §2.9, §2.16 and §2.9.a; ordinary procedures resume as soon as they can function, and emergency authority may not delay restoration.

Entrenchment Clause §17.3 →

The constitutional rule that no amendment, however procedurally valid, may extinguish a right designated non-derogable under §1.16.a. Any amendment purporting to do so is void to the extent of the extinguishment; the SC must so rule on petition of any person with standing. The non-derogable rights form the constitution's unamendable core — they may be expanded by amendment but not eliminated.

Expedited Military Review §4.5(6) →

Constitutional challenges to immediate defensive military authority during active operations receive expedited independent judicial review under §4.5(6). The provision establishes no forty-eight-hour constitutional decision deadline or standing four-justice panel. Constitutional questions in the Supreme Court still require five participating justices under §4.3.

Early Departure Clause §7.4 →

The rule that a person who leaves a constitutional office before completing a term — by resignation, expulsion, or State recall — is deemed to have completed that term for purposes of term limits and required cooling-off periods (§7.4(3)). Closes the bypass where strategic resignation could keep a term from counting toward the six-term Assembly limit (§3.2) or the two-term Senate limit (§3.5).

Without this clause, a member on their sixth Assembly term could resign six months before the end of term, win the next election, and argue they had only served five terms. The clause closes that path.

Electoral Supermajority Threshold §7.3 →

60% of votes cast is the ordinary threshold for direct electoral supermajorities, including the §13.2 legislative initiative. Constitutional-amendment referendums instead use the thresholds in §17.1: two-thirds for Popular Ratification and 70% for citizen-initiative amendments. The §13.1 optional repeal referendum requires a simple majority. Participation floors are separately specified in the applicable provisions.

F
Reconsideration Notice §2.7 →

The Civic Consul's advisory instrument: a notice issued within the review period identifying provisions recommended for revision or deletion, with reasons. Before the review period expires, the Assembly Speaker may pause the review period for up to 10 business days, during which the Assembly may withdraw the bill by simple majority; otherwise the review period resumes where it paused and the CC may still sign, veto, or let the bill become law. One notice per bill; available for the annual budget but not for emergency legislation. An Acting Civic Consul may also issue one, and it then pauses the review period automatically (§2.6.a(3)). The design is deliberate: line-item recommendations without a line-item veto — the Legislature still writes every word.

See also: Suspensive Veto — the CC's procedural instrument, which tables the bill for one month and requires Senate 2/3 to override.

I
Independent Statutory Agencies §3.9 →

Statute may establish, organize, alter or abolish independent agencies, without impairing a constitutionally assigned independent function. Appointed leadership serves fixed terms with conduct-, incapacity- or competence-based removal rather than policy dismissal. Section 3.9.a protects operational independence from both executives and the Legislature in exercising assigned functions, subject to law. Section 3.10 requires sufficient resources and published justification for material changes.

Inhabitant §12.1 →

Every person present within the Republic’s jurisdiction is an Inhabitant for §12.1 Social State guarantees. Article I generally protects every person within its scope; those protections do not begin only when an asylum claim is filed. Residency, citizenship and voting citizenship are separate constitutional statuses.

Founding Status Election §16.2 →

During the Transition Window, a recognized Indigenous nation chooses through its own lawful governance process between Associated Community status and Territorial Integration. A nation independently qualifying under Article XIX may use its founding direct-State route. With no election, the Republic treats the nation as an Associated Community pending a Compact without implying consent to unaccepted terms. Article I applies during the window unless positively rejected under §16.2. Integration ends the former founding Compact status; later sovereign relationships require the applicable constitutional process.

Monitor Misconduct Suspension §9.7.a →

Two Monitor Generals may publish findings recommending suspension for serious misconduct or abuse of office under §9.7.a. Approval requires a Supreme Court majority for the LM, both Speakers for the EM or both executives for the JM. The recommendation alone does not suspend authority. A valid suspension lasts at most ninety days unless removal proceedings have begun; continued necessity remains subject to expedited independent judicial review. Acting authority follows §9.1.d, and suspension ends if removal is rejected or the proceeding concludes without removal.

J
Joint Monitor Council JMC §9.7 →

The three Monitor Generals form the JMC for cross-mandate matters and expressly assigned functions. Two constitute quorum; decisions require a majority of participating members and dissent is published. The JMC may establish compliance standards within its own mandate, cannot override an individual Monitor and audits the NRS at statutory intervals. The Elections Panel administers constitutional lotteries, while the JMC makes the specific fallback selections assigned to it.

Judicial Monitor General Candidate Pool §9.4 →

The pool of former State or Republic judges from courts of record at trial level or above, with at least one year out of judicial office; former Supreme Court justices need five years out of that Court. The Elections Panel administers the pool and the LM and EM jointly audit it. The JM cannot choose its own overseer or administer its own eligibility decision.

Separate from the Judicial Pool (§9.4.a).

Jury Service §1.4 →

Jury service is an example of a civic obligation that the Legislature may lawfully require under §1.4 without constituting prohibited forced labor. Its administration and compensation are statutory; the Constitution does not set a national living-wage payment.

L
Legal Resident §5.2 →

A person holding lawful legal-resident status, normally after State sponsorship and federal certification under §6.1, or through the independent asylum track under §6.3. Legal residents may work, contract, own property and access social-state systems. They hold Article I protections but not federal voting or federal candidacy rights. No child of a citizen or legal resident may be deported regardless of the child's documentation status.

Legislative Declaration of Defense §2.2 →

Law authorizes operations beyond immediate defensive purposes permitted by §2.2, within §14.1’s authorized purposes. A ratified treaty may authorize action its terms require under §14.4. Notice to the Civic Consul and both Speakers is due without undue delay. Operational review, appropriations, scope changes and orderly withdrawal follow §14.2; no universal annual renewal or second-renewal reconstruction-plan requirement applies.

M
Remediation and Provisional Status §15.3 →

Remediation and Provisional status under §15.3 follow legislative audit determinations, not a binding count of Monitor findings. First and second consecutive failures require a Remediation Plan and its revision; a third permits a separate decision on Provisional placement. The deciding chamber must reach the required majority. Sitting senators finish their terms but new terms do not begin while Provisional. Two consecutive biennial audits determined compliant restore ordinary status. Devolution remains voluntary; no term-expiry referendum is compulsory.

N
National Record System NRS Article X →

The Republic's permanent, tamper-evident, publicly accessible repository of all official government actions, laws, expenditures, court decisions, and Monitor reports. Every constitutional act, order, finding, certification, designation, declaration, and determination is published to the NRS by operation of §10.1 — no provision need state this individually. Entries are permanent; corrections are new entries that reference and supersede prior entries, with both permanently visible. Accessible to all Inhabitants at no cost.

National Voting System NVS §11.2 →

The Elections Panel’s voting platform must simultaneously preserve ballot secrecy, public auditability and resilience. Eligibility verification cannot store or expose voter identity in the NVS. Participation records and other NVS information are limited to the relevant election or petition’s administration, audit, certification and judicial review. Neither withheld certification nor an administration noncompliance finding invalidates votes cast under §11.3.

NRS Panel and Elections Panel §11.1 →

Two independent panels operate the NRS and NVS respectively. Members are appointed under §3.9, and statute defines membership, qualifications and terms. Each has a five-member floor and protection against a single appointing authority holding a majority within one electoral term. Acting members, drawn from the panel's own civil servants, are designated first by the remaining members; the CC is fallback and may not create an acting majority.

National Endowment §12.4 →

The independent MA manages a reserve to backstop §12.1 Social State duties during a certified Severe Revenue Contraction. Certification starts transfers; beyond twelve months, ordinary statutory authorization must be renewed at intervals no longer than twelve months. Without it, disbursements cease; certification of the contraction’s end also stops them. Underlying social guarantees remain binding, without a contraction exception permitting substantial impairment.

Non-Refoulement §1.18 →

The absolute prohibition on returning, expelling, or extraditing any person to a territory where they face genuine risk of persecution, torture, or deprivation of life. Applies regardless of immigration status or mode of entry. One of the non-derogable rights that cannot be suspended even during a declared emergency under §1.16.a.

NRS Parallel Authentication System §10.1(7) →

A continuously operating authentication system maintained by the JM that is physically and logistically independent of the National Record System. Operates in parallel at all times — not merely as a contingency — and records all authenticated constitutional acts with full constitutional effect from the moment of their issuance. Government acts take legal effect from the moment of issuance regardless of NRS status. Upon NRS restoration, parallel system records migrate to the NRS with original timestamps. If the NRS is not restored within 180 days, the parallel system becomes the constitutional record of the Republic.

O
Optional Referendum §13.1 →

Citizens file for optional repeal within ninety days of enactment. Geographic Phase One and five-percent-national Phase Two signatures are authenticated through the NVS; completion periods are statutory. The Panel determines eligibility at filing, subject to timely judicial review. Verified petitions lead to referendum within ninety days; a majority repeals without a constitutional turnout floor. Appropriation laws and laws implementing rights may be repealed, but underlying constitutional duties continue and a referendum cannot substitute a different appropriation amount.

P
Statehood Proceeding §15.2 →

Territorial Statehood requires two qualifying completed audits and affirmative legislative compliance determinations under §§15.2 and 15.2.a. The first audit must be completed within two years of initiation, the second within two years after completion of the first determined compliant. Timely completed records remain eligible for later decision. Assembly inaction transfers the decision exclusively to the Senate; further delay is judicially enforceable. The second qualifying affirmative decision creates Statehood without a separate admission act, subject to subdivision viability where applicable.

Provisional status under §15.1.a is reserved for an existing State in remediation; it is not part of the Statehood pathway.

R
Registry Integrity Standards §9.8 →

Constitutional pool criteria must be objective, without exclusion for ideology, political association or judicial philosophy. Exclusion or removal requires published reasons and timely judicial review under statutory procedures. The Constitution fixes neither a fourteen-day publication period nor an exclusive fourteen-day Supreme Court appeal. Existing entrants retain prospective-criteria protection subject to continuing constitutional eligibility, including financial-conflict rules.

S
Standard Track Removal — SC Grounds Review §9.9 →

Other constitutionally confirmed independent officers covered by §9.9(5) may face removal initiated by one-third of either chamber, with a concurrent two-thirds vote of full seated membership in each chamber on statutory grounds. Initiation does not suspend authority. No preliminary Supreme Court grounds-confirmation gate applies. Monitor Generals and federal judges have their own distinct constitutional removal routes.

State Emergency Council §2.9.a →

The reachable State heads other than the Emergency Designee direct or may replace the Designee under §2.9.a. The Council facilitates restoration without directing or superseding lawful State and Territory processes for federal representation. There is no ten-day automatic vacancy rule. Federal authority returns institution by institution as capability is restored. If the exceptional arrangement persists six months, it ends and §15.7.a permits sovereign action by States and Territories, with the non-derogable floor still binding.

Suspensive Veto §2.7 →

The Civic Consul's primary legislative veto instrument. Where the CC withholds assent, the bill is returned within 10 business days with written grounds published to the NRS and tabled for one month — regardless of any change in CC office, closing the remove-and-replace bypass. The Senate may override by 2/3 supermajority during that month; failing override, the bill returns to the Legislature and must pass both chambers again before returning to the CC, who may not use the suspensive veto on that bill again — including where the bill lapses and is reintroduced in a subsequent session. Where neither Consul vetoes within the review period, the bill becomes law by constitutional operation. Cannot be used on the annual budget, bills implementing a constitutional obligation, or emergency legislation — a bill designated as such at final passage by 2/3 of both chambers.

The tabling attaches to the bill, not the officer — this is the key design feature. A replacement CC cannot sign a tabled bill, closing the obvious political bypass. The re-passage requirement distinguishes the failed-override outcome from the LC's domain veto, which lapses the bill for the full session — the CC's instrument is deliberately weaker.

Public Confirmation §4.4.a →

The fallback when a Supreme Court seat stalls: if the Civic Consul fails to nominate, or the Senate fails to vote, within the applicable period, the serving Temporary Associate Justice is submitted for public confirmation at the next federal electoral period. Confirmation requires 60% of votes cast (§7.3). An ordinary nomination and Senate confirmation completed before the ballot is certified ends the public route. If confirmed, the justice serves the remainder of the original term; if rejected, the next eligible Appellate Court judge becomes Temporary Associate Justice and the vacancy process restarts.

Severe Contraction Protocol §12.4 →

A revenue decline satisfying the MA’s prepublished threshold may be certified Severe Revenue Contraction. That activates §12.4 Endowment transfers for Social State obligations. Beyond twelve months, continuing disbursement requires ordinary statutory authorization and renewal at intervals no longer than twelve months. MA end-certification stops transfers regardless of authorization status. The certification does not authorize elimination or substantial impairment of §12.1 guarantees.

Social State §12.1 →

Every Inhabitant is entitled to universally accessible basic healthcare, assistance sufficient for basic material conditions and free compulsory childhood education. The Legislature establishes frameworks and adequacy standards, but statutory adjustments cannot eliminate or substantially impair those guarantees. The EM assesses compliance annually. Fiscal equalization under §12.6 and the Endowment under §12.4 support the duties without suspending rights or making a preferred delivery model constitutionally mandatory.

National Fallback Election §2.6.a →

Following a Civic vacancy, the Assembly elects by absolute majority within the statutory period, default twenty-one days. Otherwise the Speaker, then the specified senior-member fallback, acts within caretaker limits and administers nomination. Up to four advance; a sole candidate faces a yes-or-no vote. The Panel administers the national election within the statutory period, default forty-five and maximum ninety days. If no candidate wins, nomination and election repeat. The Assembly can elect before national voting begins.

State Plurality Requirement §7.1 →

The requirement that a winning Legat Consul candidate receive more first-preference votes than any other candidate in a majority of the Republic's States, in addition to a national RCV majority — a candidate must satisfy both to win outright. The requirement is waived where a candidate's national RCV majority reaches 60% of votes cast (§7.1(2)). Where no candidate wins outright, the national RCV winner meets the candidate who led the most States in a runoff decided by national majority; the State plurality requirement does not apply to the runoff. Territory citizens vote and count toward the national RCV total but are not counted in the State plurality calculation.

Judicial Delay and Remedies §9.9 →

A missed judicial ruling deadline does not determine merits or produce a substantive constitutional outcome under §4.5(8). Courts may issue interim protective orders and parties retain remedies. Section 9.9 has no preliminary Supreme Court grounds-review clock; missing a Monitor-removal referendum deadline also does not terminate that proceeding.

The SC's constitutional obligation to rule within its timing window is absolute. The default rule is the enforcement mechanism — the consequence of institutional non-compliance, not a penalty on the parties.

Supreme Court Justice Removal §4.3.a →

Removal requires a concurrent two-thirds vote of both chambers for serious misconduct, abuse of office, or permanent incapacity; the Legislature's determination that grounds exist is final. No Monitor finding is required. Disagreement with a decision is not grounds for removal.

Supreme Court Quorum §4.3 →

A majority of seated justices, including Temporary Associate Justices, is the ordinary quorum. Constitutional questions require at least five participating justices. The statutory Court size cannot be fewer than five. Temporary selection follows §4.4 and the applicable pool fallback under §9.8; this provision does not create a separate district-judge backfill rule.

T
Term Limits §7.4 →

Each Consul is limited to 12 years of service across all periods; acting service does not count. A CC who leaves office must wait one full Assembly electoral cycle before returning. The LC serves four-year terms and, after any portion of a term, may not seek election at the next LC election. No person may serve more than six terms in the Assembly or more than two terms in the Senate. Judicial and Monitor service have their own nonrenewability and partial-service rules under Articles IV and IX.
