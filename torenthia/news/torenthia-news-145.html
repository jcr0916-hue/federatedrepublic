The Written Criteria

Sorene Vell receives the written criteria she requested for Korda's winter road and ferry schedule, and residents weigh what paper changes and what it does not.

{{ worldAuthor | escape }} {{ worldDate | worldDateLabel }}

The reply came back in two pages, on the public works office letterhead, with the schedule's criteria in writing.

Sorene Vell had asked for those criteria in a written request earlier this month. The office's answer lists what it says it has used all along: road and dock condition, safety, and seasonal demand. It notes that the winter schedule is already posted at landings and district offices, and it includes the inland second-pass service, the pass that reaches Elian Brask's farm road.

Vell, a southern lakefront Territorial Assembly member, did not treat the letter as a change. “This is the paper trail I asked for,” she said. “The schedule has not moved. But now there is something a person can hold on to when they ask why the boat leaves when it leaves, or why the road gets a second pass.”

Kalle Orris, a dockhand at Varenne Station in the LC-N1 corridor district, was unsurprised. “Two sailings are two sailings,” he said. “The letter does not change the water. If the lake makes the afternoon run unsafe, no piece of paper will tell me when the next boat comes. The office decides that day, the same as always.”

Vessa Tann runs a grader on the LC-N2 Upper Lake Road and read the criteria as a description of her winter. “It matches what we do,” she said. “We go out on condition and safety, and we try to hold the schedule. But the letter did not add hours, salt, or fuel. We are running the same roads with the same trucks.”

Elian Brask, an inland market farmer outside the corridor districts, said the written confirmation of the second pass was worth something to him even though the road itself had not changed. “Now I can show a buyer or a lender when they ask how I get goods out in winter,” he said. “It is paper, not a plow. But paper helps.”

The public works office's reply does not create a new service obligation, amend the schedule, or open any new program. It does not touch the residence-record correction process the Elections Panel opened in its affected-electorate docket. The adopted Northern Lake Settlement remains an adopted proposal awaiting steps that no record has yet shown: a certified electorate, ballot wording, a referendum, Kelvant approval, a joint Monitor assessment, and Senate ratification.

The reply now sits in Vell's office, beside her original request.
