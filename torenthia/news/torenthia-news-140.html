What Accurate Enough Costs

A Varek county budget officer weighs how many winter staff hours to spend correcting old fiscal-equalization source records before the committee has chosen any formula.

SELLEN COUNTY, Varek — The request reached Renn Alder's office in the first cold week of the month, by way of Kellor. The Joint Committee on Fiscal Equalization had invited State and Territory administrations to correct the source tables behind its coming comparative illustrations, and Varek's administration, which will answer for the State, had asked its Counties whether their entries were right. It was a request, not an order. Sellen County could review its records, or it could decline.

Alder, the county budget officer, set the sheet beside a stack of road-department invoices. He has four staff who can work on records in winter, and two of them are needed elsewhere.

"Nobody in Verentum is pretending this will change an allocation," he said. "It’s a correction window. You open it, you look at what you’ve got, and you decide what ‘accurate enough’ costs."

The window is part of the committee’s ordinary review, which is still gathering evidence. It has published no distributional estimates, chosen no formula and assigned no weights to the three approaches it is comparing. It has asked only that those approaches be illustrated against a common reference period, on tables that can be compared across jurisdictions. The Executive Monitor has made no finding of failure, and no ninety-day response clock is running. Whatever Sellen County does with its records, it is doing it before a decision that has not been made.

Alder answers to the County Council, and the Council disagrees about what the correction is for.

Edric Marn, a councillor, wants the county to correct everything it can, including the oldest entries, from years when the county had fewer staff and rougher record-keeping. "If the table sits there and we say nothing, someone reads it as a small burden," Marn said. "We had the work. We just didn’t write it down well. That’s a reason to fix the record, not to hope nobody looks."

Talia Serr, the road-maintenance supervisor, argues the opposite. She runs the crews that sand the upper Toren routes and keep culverts open through freeze and thaw. Winter is the county’s busiest service season, and her staff keep the records that exist.

"Sand and culverts are the service burden," Serr said. "If I spend two people re-entering old numbers, I have two fewer people clearing a drain. The record already understates what we do in a bad winter. Making it neater doesn’t make the work smaller."

Varek has no general executive. Its Assembly legislates, and its Counties govern within a sphere the State constitution protects, so a request from Kellor about county figures is just that: a request. Sellen, a small county along the upper Toren’s tributaries, is deciding whether to take part in a national evidence debate, and how much taking part is worth.

Alder has told his staff to review only the three oldest fiscal years and to mark any entry they cannot verify as unresolved. He has sent nothing to Kellor yet, and he has not told the Council which way he will recommend.

"The committee is comparing formulas," Alder said. "It hasn’t chosen one. If it never chooses one, the corrected table is just a corrected table. If it does, I want the county’s number to be a number we can explain."

The correction window remains open. In Sellen County, the only decision so far is which boxes to open first.
