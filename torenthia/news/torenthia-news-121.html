The Republic · Election Season

The Answer Box

The register has room for every question. Whether it will have an answer is another matter.

Aleth FennThe TorenthianYear 14, Month 1

DORVEN — The question was submitted on a folded sheet of paper because its author did not trust the terminal at the public library. She wanted to know whether a new Legat Consul could order the repair of the road between her village and the county market.

The clerk read the sheet, checked that it was addressed to the national candidates, and entered it into the Elections Panel's public questions register. The clerk did not promise an answer. He did point to the line explaining that the road belonged to the county.

“Then they ought to say so,” the woman replied.

That is the small political innovation of the register: an unanswered question stays visible. A question answered evasively stays visible too. The Panel records submissions and written replies; it does not grade them, force candidates to respond, or turn a campaign promise into a power the office does not possess.

A campaign with two kinds of silence

Questions about the Toren River are arriving from Varek, where maintenance disputes have lingered between county authorities and the offices responsible for external negotiations. Questions from Orath ask why harvest freight reports have improved while wagon journeys have not. Others concern the cost of the campaign itself, prompted by the first public financing allocations to Assembly candidates.

For the Legat Consul race, that last issue has a procedural complication. Ines Carrow, Seren Mak, Tobias Vael and Pell Sandris have filed declarations, but their qualifications remain under review. The Elections Panel has not certified a ballot order or election day, and applications for the Legat Consul financing program remain under examination. That is an administrative silence, not yet a candidate's refusal.

The other kind of silence is political. The public questions register permits candidates to answer in their own words, with their response published alongside the original question. Candidates can also decline. The Panel makes no inference about either choice.

A Dorven shopkeeper who had come to read the freight tables said he preferred the blank space to a paragraph of promises. “At least I know there is a blank space,” he said. His neighbor disagreed: a blank space could be filled by rumor more quickly than by an official answer.

What the record cannot repair

The register will not widen a road, decide the Lake Varda dispute or settle the constitutional division of responsibilities between the Republic and its States. It will not tell voters which answer is sincere. But it gives them something that a campaign rally seldom provides: the question as originally asked, dated and available to be compared with whatever answer eventually appears.

As the library closed, the clerk made one final check of the screen. The village-road question was there. Beside it, the answer field was empty.

He turned off the terminal. Tomorrow, the record would still show exactly that.
