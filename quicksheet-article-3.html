Quick Sheet · Constitution of the Federated Republic
Article III — The Legislature
Two chambers, one ordinary bill path. The Assembly originates; the Senate reviews and confirms. Constitutional thresholds govern their work, with a limited continuity exception when ordinary government cannot function.
§3.1 — §3.11
11 provisions

Assembly and Senate

Assembly seats follow population by Webster allocation, with at least one per State and Territory; terms are two years, at most six. The Senate has two seats per State, staggered six-year terms, at most two. Statutory vacancy periods may not exceed ninety days, except where the remaining term is shorter. States and Territories may provide recall within §3.2(6).

Passage and delay

All legislation originates in the Assembly. Ordinary passage requires an absolute majority of full seated membership in each chamber. Senate review lasts at most ninety days, with one extension up to thirty days by a majority of full seated membership. Deemed approval applies only to a measure needing no more than a majority; it never supplies an expressly required affirmative vote.

Agreements and institutions

Treaties and compacts require two-thirds of the full seated Senate. Trade agreements require absolute majorities in both chambers. Neither chamber rewrites a concluded international agreement. Independent agencies exercise their functions independently under §3.9.a; statute may organize or abolish statutory agencies without impairing constitutionally assigned independent functions.

Competence and resources

Federal legislation needs an assigned constitutional power or a reasonably necessary implementing power. Unassigned powers remain with States and local government. Federal standards do not alone authorize commandeering their institutions. Institutions must receive resources sufficient for assigned duties; material resource changes require published justification. Delegation must state scope and conditions; the Legislature retains the power to rescind it.
